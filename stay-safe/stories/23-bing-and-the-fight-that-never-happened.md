# Story 23: Bīng and the Fight That Never Happened

**Main Character:** Bīng (Shaved Ice)
**Guest Characters:** Magnus Meltsworth, Auntie Hù, Herschel the Walrus, Sergeant Solveig Snowmane, Boba, Finnegan Flipsworth
**Setting:** Herschel's Cocoa Hut on a packed Saturday night after the big downhill race — steamy windows, a crackling fire, and one very loud guest in the corner
**Theme:** A fight nobody had is a fight nobody lost. Leaving isn't losing — it's the whole game.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** **算了** (suàn le) = forget it / let it go
**Safety Skill:** Fight avoidance — words are never a reason to hit; walk away, leave the place, and don't stay where trouble is brewing

---

Bīng's Grand Toppings Tower was, in Bīng's own humble opinion, THE MOST BEAUTIFUL THING IN THE HISTORY OF THINGS.

It stood four feet tall on the counter of Herschel's Cocoa Hut. It had eleven layers. It had mango and red bean and mochi balls and a spiral of condensed milk that went around it like a staircase, and on the very top, a tiny paper flag that said MORE IS MORE, because of course it did. Bīng — a towering, glittering mountain of fluffy shaved ice in sunglasses — stood beside it like a proud parent at a graduation.

"BEHOLD," said Bīng.

"It's dripping on my fish," said Barnaby.

"THAT'S NOT DRIPPING. THAT'S *GLISTENING.*"

Yēguǒ drifted past in his flip-flops, said "Duuude," with such feeling that a single mochi ball rolled off in sheer admiration. Bīng screamed. Boba bounced up and put it back. It was a wonderful Saturday night.

The Cocoa Hut was packed, because the big downhill race had just finished and the entire valley had crammed inside to warm up. The windows were steamed. The fire crackled. Herschel the Walrus moved behind the counter like a ship in a harbor, pouring cocoa into an endless river of mugs. In the far corner, on his ancient wooden skis "with character," sat Magnus Meltsworth — a big grey-muzzled polar bear with the face of a grump and, everyone knew, the center of a marshmallow.

And at the table right next to Magnus sat the Loud Guest.

---

Nobody knew the Loud Guest. He'd come up for the race. He was a large bear in a very expensive jacket, and he had been drinking Herschel's *spiced* cocoa — the grown-ups-only kind, the kind that makes your voice get bigger and your brain get smaller — for about two hours.

"HEY," he said, to the whole room. "Hey. Whose pile of garbage is that on the counter?"

The Cocoa Hut got a little quieter.

Bīng's sunglasses slipped one inch down his nose.

"I'm sorry," said Bīng, in the dangerous, quiet voice of a very dramatic person who is about to stop being quiet. "Did you say *garbage?*"

"Yeah. That." The Loud Guest waved his mug at the Tower. Cocoa slopped. "Looks like somebody dropped a fruit salad on a snowbank. Looks like a *landfill* with a *flag* on it."

"IT'S A TOWER," said Bīng, and a small drip ran down his side, which is what happens to shaved ice when its temperature rises. "IT HAS ELEVEN LAYERS. IT HAS *ARCHITECTURE.*"

"It has *problems.*" The Loud Guest laughed at his own joke, too long and too loud, and looked around the room for people to laugh with him. Nobody did. That made him louder. "Hey, who *made* you? Was it a snowblower? Did a snowblower have a bad day?"

Now, here's a thing about Bīng. Everything was THE BEST or THE WORST. There was no middle. And right now his whole glittering body was sliding fast from THE BEST NIGHT EVER toward THE WORST, and the slide made him *melt.* Drip. Drip. A little puddle spreading on the floor.

"Boba," Bīng hissed. "Hold my sunglasses."

"Why?" said Boba.

"BECAUSE I'M ABOUT TO—"

That was when Magnus Meltsworth stood up.

---

Magnus did not stand up quickly. He was old, and his skis were older, and he did everything as if he'd been asked to do it against his will. But when he finally reached his full height, the Loud Guest's table went into shadow.

"That's enough," Magnus rumbled.

The Loud Guest looked up, and up. And because the spiced cocoa had made his brain small, he grinned.

"Oh, here we go. The bodyguard. You gonna do something about it, Grandpa?"

Magnus's paws — very large paws — closed slowly.

Bīng, still dripping, felt a wild surge of joy. THIS WAS IT. THIS WAS THE MOMENT. A giant bear was about to defend his HONOR and his ARCHITECTURE and it was going to be MAGNIFICENT and—

"Mm," said a small voice.

Auntie Hù was sitting on the edge of the counter, next to the Tower, peeling a tea egg. She had been there the whole time. She had not looked up once, and she did not look up now.

"Magnus," she said. "What do you get if you win?"

Magnus paused. "…What?"

"Say you win. Say you knock him flat. Best case. What do you get?"

Magnus thought about it honestly, because he was a grump but not a liar. "…Satisfaction."

"Mm. And?"

"And… he stops talking."

"And," said Auntie Hù, still peeling, "Solveig comes. Because somebody in this room already has their phone out — put it *away,* Finnegan — and it doesn't matter who started it, she has to take you both. So: a night in a cell. Then a bill from a lawyer, which is more money than these skis have ever been worth. Then a judge asking why a grown bear hit a man over *shaved ice.*" She finally looked up. "And Bīng, standing right there, watching every second of it, learning that this is how it's done."

The Cocoa Hut was very quiet now.

"What do you get if you *leave?*"

Magnus stood there for a long, long moment, a mountain deciding whether to be a mountain.

"…Cocoa," he said at last.

"Mm."

"Fine." He sat back down. He picked up his mug. He glowered into it. "*Fine.* But this is my one noble act for the week."

---

Bīng could not believe it.

"THAT'S IT?" he whispered. "He gets to call my Tower a *LANDFILL* and we just — we just *SIT?*"

"No," said Auntie Hù. "We don't sit either. We *go.*" She hopped off the counter. "Bīng. Boba. Finnegan. Magnus. Bring the Tower. We're leaving."

"LEAVING? BUT THAT'S — that's *LOSING!*"

Auntie Hù stopped. She looked at Bīng over the top of his own sunglasses, which Boba was still holding.

"Let me tell you the one rule that matters more than every other rule I know," she said. "**You cannot lose a fight you are not in.** Say it."

"…You can't lose a fight you're not in."

"Words are not a reason to hit. Not insults. Not about your mother. Not about your toppings. Not *anything.* Words don't break bones. Fists do — and the law knows the difference even if your feelings don't." She started walking toward the door, and the others, without quite deciding to, followed. "There's an old saying: don't be in stupid places, with stupid people, at stupid times. Right now this is a stupid place. That man is being stupid people. And it's getting to be a stupid time. So we take our beautiful tower and we *go.*"

"But what if he *follows* us?" said Boba.

"Then he's the one who made it a fight, and *that* changes everything. But he won't. Men like that don't want a fight. They want a *show.* Take away the audience and he's just a bear talking to a wall."

Bīng picked up his Tower — carefully, all eleven layers — and looked back at the Loud Guest, who was already yelling something at a coat rack.

"**算了** (suàn le)," Bīng muttered. "Forget it. Let it go."

And then, because he was Bīng, and because dramatic was the only way he knew how to be, he decided that if he was going to leave, he was going to leave *MAGNIFICENTLY.*

He drew himself up to his full glittering height. He took his sunglasses back from Boba and put them on, slowly, with one hand. He hoisted the Tower onto his shoulder like a champion's trophy. He turned, so the flag caught the firelight.

"Goodnight, Frostpeak," he announced, to the entire Cocoa Hut. "You have been a *WONDERFUL* audience. This concludes tonight's exhibition of *ARCHITECTURE.*"

And he walked out the door with his chin in the air and eleven layers of dessert balanced perfectly, dripping only a little, while the whole room burst into applause.

Behind him, Finnegan tripped on the doormat, and that got applause too.

---

Outside, the night was cold and clean and full of stars, and the sound from the hut faded to a warm murmur behind the steamed-up glass.

Magnus stood on the porch with his mug, glowering at the aurora as if it owed him money.

"That," he said, "was the hardest thing I've done all winter."

"Mm. That's why it's the noble act," said Auntie Hù.

"I *wanted* to hit him."

"Of course you did. Wanting's free. *Doing* is what costs." She sat down on the porch step. "Here's what nobody tells kids, Bīng, so listen. Your ego — your pride, the part that goes hot when somebody laughs at you — that is the most *expensive* thing you own. It'll buy fights you don't need. It'll sell you nights in cells and years of regret at a discount. Every fight ever started in a place like that started with somebody's ego saying *I can't let him talk to me like that.*" She shrugged. "You can. You can let him talk. Talk is *free.* And then you take your tower and go home, and tomorrow you're still beautiful and he's still got a headache."

Bīng thought about this. It was, he had to admit, a very good speech, though it would have been better in ALL CAPS.

"So leaving," he said slowly, "isn't losing."

"Leaving is the *whole game.* The best fighters I ever knew — real ones, not movie ones — were the best at *not fighting.* Anybody can throw a punch. It takes a grown-up to walk out a door."

Through the window, they watched Herschel come around the counter, all seven hundred pounds of him, and put one enormous flipper on the Loud Guest's shoulder. Sergeant Solveig Snowmane was already standing at the door. Nobody hit anybody. The Loud Guest was simply, gently, and *completely* escorted out into the snow, where he stood blinking and quiet and suddenly much smaller.

"See?" said Auntie Hù. "It got handled. It just didn't get handled by *us.* That's allowed. That's usually *better.*"

---

The Tower, it turned out, survived the whole adventure with ten layers intact. The eleventh had melted into Bīng's shoulder, but he decided that was a feature.

"IT'S A *SCAR,*" he told Boba, back at the Boba on Ice stand, very late. "A BATTLE SCAR. From the battle I *DIDN'T HAVE.*"

"Duuude," said Yēguǒ from his hammock, half asleep. "That's the most zen thing you've ever said."

"I KNOW."

Magnus had walked them all the way back, grumbling the entire time about the cold, the stars being too bright, and the general injustice of nobility. At the door he stopped.

"Bīng."

"Yes?"

"The tower wasn't garbage." He scowled at the snow. "It was… fine. It was a fine tower."

From Magnus Meltsworth, this was roughly the same as a parade.

"THANK YOU," said Bīng, and dripped a little, but only from happiness this time.

He fell asleep with his sunglasses on, ten layers tall, feeling like the best kind of tired — the kind you get from *not* doing something very hard, which, it turns out, is harder than doing it.

## Goodnight Blessing

Let the hot feeling melt, little one. It was only ice — it was always only ice.
You can't lose a fight you're not in, and tonight you're not in any.
Walk out of the noisy room in your mind. Close the door softly behind you.
The stars are quiet. Be quiet with them. Sleep.

## 🛡️ What We Learned

- **You can't lose a fight you're not in.** Leaving isn't losing. Leaving is the whole game.
- **Words are never a reason to hit.** Not insults, not about your mom, not about your toppings. The law knows the difference between words and fists, even when your feelings don't.
- **Don't be in stupid places with stupid people at stupid times.** If a fight is brewing, *leave the place* — don't stay to watch, don't stay to film.
- **Never argue with someone who's had too much of anything.** Their brain is small right now. You can't win, and you don't need to.
- **Ask: "What do I get if I win?"** Usually: nothing but trouble. **"What do I get if I leave?"** Usually: cocoa and a normal tomorrow.
- **Your ego is the most expensive thing you own.** Don't let it buy you a fight.
- **Let the grown-ups in charge handle it** — the owner, the staff, the police. It gets handled *better* when it isn't you.
