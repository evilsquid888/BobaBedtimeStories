# Story 17: The Knock at the Cottage Door

**Main Character:** Hóng Dòu (Red Bean)
**Guest Characters:** Willa Wobble, Willa's mom (Mrs. Wobble), Sergeant Solveig Snowmane, Herschel the Walrus
**Setting:** Willa's little iceberg cottage on the edge of Frostpeak Valley, one snowy evening while the grown-ups are all at the lodge — a warm kitchen, a pot of red-bean soup, and one front door
**Theme:** A locked door is a locked door. You never have to open it, you never have to answer it, and you never, ever have to say you're alone.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** **不開門** (bù kāi mén) = don't open the door
**Safety Skill:** Home alone — never open, talk through the door if at all, never say you're alone, call a parent, get out the other way if someone tries to get in

---

Willa's mom left a list.

It was forty rules long. It was taped to the refrigerator, and it went all the way down the refrigerator, and then it went across the floor, and then it went up the side of the stove, where rule thirty-eight had begun, very slightly, to toast.

"Rule one," read Hóng Dòu, adjusting the tiny ladle she carried everywhere like a walking stick. "*Feed Willa.* Well. Obviously." She stirred the pot of red-bean soup that was bubbling gently on the stove and filling the whole iceberg cottage with a smell like a warm blanket. "Rule two: *Feed Willa again if she says she's not hungry.* Ha! A woman after my own heart. Rule three: *Do not let Willa snowboard off the roof.*"

"That was ONE time," said Willa, from the couch.

"Rule four: *Not even the low part of the roof.*"

"It's a very low roof!"

Hóng Dòu was a small, plump, deep-burgundy bean with a white stripe down her middle and the kind of warmth that made every room she was in feel like a kitchen. She had been in Frostpeak for two weeks with the Boba on Ice crew, and in those two weeks she had fed every penguin, three polar bears, a walrus, a narwhal (through the ice-hole, with a very long spoon), and a snowman, who did not eat it but appreciated the gesture. Tonight she had volunteered to stay with Willa while Mrs. Wobble helped run the lodge's Big Snowy Bingo Night, and she had brought soup, because of course she had brought soup.

"Rule thirty-nine," she read, leaning over the stove and squinting at the toasty part. "*If anyone knocks—*"

And then someone knocked.

---

It was a big knock. Three thumps, friendly and firm. *Thump-thump-thump.*

Willa sat up on the couch. Hóng Dòu put down her ladle.

"Package for Wobble!" called a man's voice, cheerful, through the door.

"Oh, a package!" said Willa, and swung her little feet off the couch.

"Sit," said Hóng Dòu.

She said it the way she'd say *have you eaten?* — gently, and in a way that left absolutely no room to argue. Willa sat.

"Did your mother order a package?"

"I… don't know?"

"Hm." Hóng Dòu waddled — she did not walk; a bean does not walk — over to the refrigerator and peered at the bottom of the list. Rule thirty-nine, in Mrs. Wobble's neat handwriting, said: *If anyone knocks, do NOT open the door. Not for anybody. Call me.*

"Rule thirty-nine," Hóng Dòu said aloud, with great satisfaction. "Your mother is a wise woman."

"Package for Wobble!" the voice repeated. "Just need a signature!"

Hóng Dòu did not go to the door. She went, instead, to the little side table where a phone sat next to a card that said LODGE — MOM — HERSCHEL — SOLVEIG in big letters, and she picked up the phone, and she held it in her stripe like a shield.

"**不開門** (bù kāi mén)," she said to Willa quietly. "*Don't open the door.* That's all the rule is, little one. Nice voice, mean voice, package, no package — the door stays shut. You don't have to be clever. You just have to be *shut.*"

"Should we say something?"

"We don't *have* to. That's the first thing. Nobody can make you answer a door." She considered. "But he can hear us moving around. So." She raised her voice, and it came out in her best grandmotherly bellow, the one she used to call the tangyuan in for dinner. "*Where's it from, dear?*"

A pause. Just a little one. Just long enough.

"Uh — the lodge! From the lodge gift shop!"

Hóng Dòu's eyebrows, which were really just two slightly darker spots on her bean, went up.

"Does the gift shop deliver?" she whispered.

"It's *forty steps* from here," Willa whispered back. "Herschel just… *hands* you things."

"Mm." Hóng Dòu looked at the door as if it had said something rude. "A *real* delivery person leaves the package on the step, dear. They don't need a signature from a penguin. They don't need the door open. They *leave it* and they *go.*" She raised her voice again. "*Leave it on the step, please! Thank you!*"

Silence.

Then: "I really do need a signature."

"Then bring it back tomorrow when there are grown-ups here!" called Hóng Dòu, and then, quieter, to herself, "*Ai-ya.* There is no package."

---

Here is a thing that Hóng Dòu knew, that Willa was about to learn, and that you should tuck in your pocket for later.

People who want to get into a house have a *script.* It goes in steps, like a recipe. First: something nice and normal that makes you want to open the door. *A package!* If that doesn't work, second: something *urgent*, so you feel like you have to. And if *that* doesn't work — third — a question, sneaky and small, to find out if anyone big is home.

The man outside went straight to step two.

*Thump-thump-thump.*

"Ma'am? Sorry to bother you again." The voice had changed. It was still friendly, but now it was friendly-and-worried, like a nurse. "I'm actually with the pipe company. We've got a report of a leak under this whole row of cottages. I need to come in and check your pipes real quick or the water's going to freeze up and burst. Won't take two minutes."

Willa's eyes went very wide. "A *leak?*"

"Willa." Hóng Dòu didn't look away from the door. "Did your mother tell you a pipe man was coming tonight?"

"…No."

"Did she write it on the list?" Hóng Dòu checked. Rule forty was *Bedtime is nine, not nine-fifteen, I will know.* "No. She did not." She turned to Willa and spoke very clearly. "*Real* workers — pipe, power, anything — have an *appointment.* Your mother knows they're coming. Your mother is *here* when they come. A worker who shows up at night, when the grown-ups are out, with no appointment, saying *hurry, let me in?*" She shook her whole bean. "That's not a pipe man. That's a *script.* Uniform, clipboard, hat with a logo on it — doesn't matter. The door stays shut. You call your mother and *she* decides."

"But what if there really IS a leak?"

"Then the pipes will be *very wet* tomorrow," said Hóng Dòu, "and we will all be *alive* to mop them. **不開門.**"

Willa laughed — a tiny, startled laugh — and some of the tightness went out of her shoulders.

Hóng Dòu pressed MOM on the phone.

---

It rang. It rang. Somewhere across the valley, Big Snowy Bingo Night was very loud, and Mrs. Wobble's phone was in her coat, and her coat was on a hook, and a walrus was calling "B-NINE!" through a megaphone.

*Thump-thump-thump.*

"Ma'am?" said the voice. Step three. Soft now. Sweet. "Hey — is your mom home? I could just explain it to her. When's she getting back?"

And Willa — who was ten, and honest, and had been raised to answer grown-ups — opened her beak to say *she's at the lodge till late* —

Hóng Dòu's tiny ladle came up like a traffic officer's hand. *Stop.*

"Never," she whispered, and it was the fiercest whisper Willa had ever heard from anyone, let alone a bean. "*Never* tell anyone you're alone. Not on the phone, not through a door, not to a nice voice, not to a mean one. You don't lie about a *lot* of things, little one. But you may lie about *this.* Every time. It's not a fib. It's a *lock.*"

Then she turned to the door, took a great big grandmotherly breath, and put on the voice — the one that every bean, grain, and dumpling in the Taiwan crew knew and feared, the voice of a woman who has cooked for forty years and will *not* be having any nonsense.

"*Ai-ya!*" she bellowed. "My HUSBAND is NAPPING, and he is very GRUMPY when he wakes up, and if you make him get out of his chair he will be grumpy at YOU, not me! Come back TOMORROW like a normal person!"

Willa stared at her.

Hóng Dòu did not have a husband. Hóng Dòu had never had a husband. Hóng Dòu had a ladle, a pot, and the moral authority of four thousand years of grandmothers, and she was using every bit of it.

Silence outside.

A long one.

Then footsteps, in the snow, going *away.*

---

Neither of them moved for a full minute. Hóng Dòu stood with the phone in her stripe and her ladle in the air. Willa sat with her flippers pressed together. The soup bubbled, cheerfully, as if nothing at all had happened.

"Is he gone?" Willa whispered.

"I think so. We're not going to look."

"Why not?"

"Because looking means going *to* the door," said Hóng Dòu, "and we are not going to the door. Come here." She led Willa — quietly, away from the front of the cottage — to the kitchen, where the little back door was, the one that led to the snow path, the one that led to the Powderpuffs' cottage forty steps over. "If he had tried the handle. If he had pushed. If he had *not* gone away — this is what we do. We don't argue. We don't hide in the bathroom. We go out *this* door, fast and quiet, straight to a neighbor, and we call Solveig from *there.* You understand?"

"Out the other way," said Willa. "To the Powderpuffs. Call Solveig."

"Mm. Out the other way." Hóng Dòu patted the back door as if it were a good dog. "A house has two doors for a *reason,* little one. One to keep shut, and one to leave by."

The phone rang in her stripe. She nearly dropped it.

"HELLO — yes — Mrs. Wobble — yes, she's fine, she's *right here,* she is eating soup, or she will be in a moment. Listen to me. There was a man."

---

Mrs. Wobble did not finish Bingo Night. Mrs. Wobble was home in nine minutes, which was faster than any penguin had ever crossed the valley, including the ones on snowboards. She held Willa for a long time. Then she called Sergeant Solveig, who came and walked all around the cottage with a flashlight and looked at the footprints in the snow and wrote things down in her notebook.

"Any idea who he was?" Mrs. Wobble asked.

"None," said Solveig honestly. "Tracks go to the road and stop. Could be anybody."

"So we'll never know."

"Probably not." Solveig closed her notebook. She crouched down until she was eye to eye with Willa, which for a polar bear took a while. "And I want you to hear me say that that's *fine.* You don't need to know who he was. You don't need to know if he was bad. You needed the door shut and the phone in your hand, and that's exactly what you had. That's the whole job." She looked at Hóng Dòu. "Ma'am. *My husband is napping.* That was a beautiful piece of work."

"Forty years of practice," said Hóng Dòu modestly. "Have you eaten?"

---

When the sergeant had gone, and the door was locked again — checked twice, and then a third time, by Willa herself, standing on a stool — Mrs. Wobble sat down at the kitchen table with a pen and pulled the long, long list off the refrigerator.

She smoothed it out. She found the bottom, past the slightly toasted part, past *bedtime is nine.* And she wrote, in her neat handwriting:

*Rule 41: You did everything right.*

Then she stuck it back on the refrigerator, where it hung all the way down and across the floor and up the stove, and where Willa would see it every morning for the rest of the winter.

Hóng Dòu ladled out three bowls of red-bean soup, sweet and dark and steaming. She pushed one to Mrs. Wobble, and one to Willa, and kept the smallest for herself, because that is what grandmothers do.

"Everyone needs a little sweetness," she said. "Especially after a night like that. *Eat.*"

They ate. Outside, the snow came down soft over the footprints until there weren't any footprints, only snow. Inside, the pot ticked as it cooled, the refrigerator hummed, and the door — just a door again, a plain wooden door with a plain brass lock — stayed exactly the way it was supposed to.

Shut.

## Goodnight Blessing

The door is locked, little one. I checked. You checked. The bean checked.
Nobody's coming in tonight but sleep, and sleep already knows the code word.
Warm soup in your belly, warm rule on the fridge: *you did everything right.*
Let the snow cover the footprints. Close your eyes. Shut.

## 🛡️ What We Learned

- **Never open the door** when you're home without a grown-up. Not for a package, not for a uniform, not for a friendly voice. **不開門.**
- **You don't have to answer at all.** Nobody can make you talk to a door. If you do talk, talk *through* it.
- **Never say you're alone.** "My mom can't come to the door right now." You're allowed to fib about this — it's not a lie, it's a lock.
- **Real deliveries leave the package.** Real workers have an appointment your parents know about, and your parents are home for it. No appointment + "hurry, let me in" = a script, not a worker.
- **Call a parent first.** Keep the phone list by the door: parent, neighbor, a trusted grown-up, 911.
- **If someone tries to get in, get out** — the *other* door, fast and quiet, straight to a neighbor, and call 911 from there.
- **You don't need to know who it was.** Door shut, phone in hand — that's the whole job.
