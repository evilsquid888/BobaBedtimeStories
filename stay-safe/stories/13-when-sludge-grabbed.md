# Story 13: When Sludge Grabbed

**Main Character:** Zhīma (Sesame ball)
**Guest Characters:** Bjorn Bigpaws, Willa Wobble, Sludge, Nora Snowmane, Auntie Hù, Finnegan Flipsworth
**Setting:** The Frostpeak rail line — a long row of snow-packed practice rails behind the lodge — on a grey, blustery afternoon, and later Nora Snowmane's office with the blanket chair
**Theme:** If someone puts hands on you, you may get free. Then you stop. Getting away is the win; getting even is a second bully.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** **夠了** (gòu le) = that's enough
**Safety Skill:** Physical bullies — enough force to break free, then stop; go to an adult; tell the whole truth first

---

Zhīma had a rule about danger, and the rule was: *get there first.*

"We roll NOW!" he shouted, and rolled — off the lodge steps, down the packed-snow path, straight through Bīng's carefully raked topping garden and out the other side wearing a mango slice like a hat.

He was a sesame ball. Perfectly round, golden-fried, crunchy all over with a coat of white sesame seeds that sparkled when the sun came out, which today it did not. He was hollow inside and sweet in the middle, and he had never in his life seen a problem and thought *someone else should handle that.* He saw a problem and thought *ROLL.*

"Where's the fire?" called Finnegan, who was upside down in a snowbank for reasons he would explain later.

"NO FIRE!" Zhīma shouted back. "I'm PRACTICING! For when there IS one!"

He hit the rail line at full speed, bounced off the first rail, ricocheted off the second, and came to rest, spinning gently, in a puddle.

A large, blue, drippy puddle.

"Well, well," said the puddle. "Look who rolled in."

---

Sludge was a slush puppy — an oversized cup of blue-raspberry slush that had been left in the sun too long and gone mean about it. He dripped. He sneered. He left puddles wherever he went, and today he'd left one right at the foot of the rail line, where he was leaning on the third rail with his drippy fists and watching Willa Wobble try to land a board-slide.

Willa was small. Willa was concentrating. Willa had finally, *finally* got her tiny purple board onto the rail, and she was sliding, and she was *doing it—*

Sludge stuck out one slushy arm and shoved her off.

She hit the snow with a small *oof* and lay there a moment, blinking.

"Oops," said Sludge. "You wobbled."

Zhīma stopped spinning.

Now, here is a thing you should know. Two days earlier, in the lodge cocoa line, Zhīma had watched Bùdīng use his boundary voice on this very slush puppy, and he had thought it was *magnificent*, and he had been waiting ever since for a chance to try it. So he rolled up — not fast, not into anything, just *up* — planted himself between Sludge and Willa, and said it exactly the way Auntie Hù had taught them.

"Stop. Don't touch her."

Feet planted. Eyes up. No jokes.

"Or what?" said Sludge.

"Or—" Zhīma faltered. There hadn't been an *or what* in the practice. "Or I'll tell Nora."

"Nora's up the hill," said Sludge, and grinned a slushy grin, and grabbed Zhīma by the sesame.

---

Let's be clear about this, because it matters: Zhīma had done everything right.

He'd used his words. He'd used his voice. He'd stood his ground. And it hadn't worked — because sometimes, with some people, it doesn't. Words are the *first* thing you try, and they're the *best* thing you try, and most of the time they're the *only* thing you need. But not this time. This time a drippy blue fist the size of a snow shovel had closed around a sesame ball and lifted him clean off the ground, and Nora *was* up the hill, and Willa was too small, and Finnegan was still upside down.

"Let go," said Zhīma.

"Make me."

And Zhīma thought — very fast, the way you think when your feet aren't touching anything — *Auntie Hù said something about this.*

She had. Not two days ago, on the rental-boot bench, after Bùdīng's magnificent voice had worked so well. She'd said: *Sometimes it doesn't work. Sometimes they grab you anyway. And then the rules change — a little. Not a lot. A little.*

*If someone puts hands on you, you may get them off you.*

*Not to win. Not to teach them a lesson. To get FREE and get AWAY. That's the whole job. Enough to break the grip — a stomp, a twist, a shove, a shout — and then the second you're loose, you STOP, and you go straight to a grown-up. Not one more hit. Not one.*

*Because the moment you hit back for revenge, you're not defending yourself anymore. You're just the other bully.*

Zhīma looked at the fist. He looked at the thumb. Auntie Hù had said something about thumbs, too.

---

"**夠了** (gòu le)!" Zhīma yelled — *that's enough* — and did three things at once.

He *twisted*, hard, toward Sludge's thumb — because a thumb is the weakest part of any grip, and a round thing that twists toward it tends to pop right out. He *stomped* his crunchy golden foot down on the top of Sludge's slushy one, which made a noise like a boot in a puddle. And he *shouted* — not words this time, just a big round sesame-ball noise, the kind of noise that carries all the way up a hill to wherever a polar bear might be.

Sludge yelped. The grip broke. Zhīma dropped, hit the snow, bounced once—

—and was *free.*

And here is the moment. Here is the whole story, right here, in one breath.

Every single thing in Zhīma's golden, crunchy, roll-first body said: *AGAIN.* Sludge was off balance. Sludge was holding his stomped foot and hopping. Sludge was *right there*, wobbling, wide open, and Zhīma had never in his life let a wide-open anything go un-rolled-into. *We roll NOW. Get there first. He shoved WILLA. One good hit, just one—*

He was already rolling. He could feel it. The snow was hissing under him.

A shadow fell across the rail line.

It was a very large shadow. It had ears.

"I did not fall," said Bjorn Bigpaws, from somewhere far above, in his deep and dignified voice. "I descended with style."

He had, in fact, fallen — down the entire back slope, on his forest-green skis, having heard a sesame-ball noise and come at once. There was snow in both ears. He did not brush it out. He looked down at Zhīma, mid-roll, one inch from Sludge's hopping foot.

"Also," said Bjorn. "**Stop.**"

Zhīma stopped.

It was the hardest thing he had ever done. Harder than the twist. Harder than the stomp. His whole body was leaning forward like a dog on a leash, and he made it *stay*, and the effort of it made his sesame seeds rattle.

Sludge, still hopping, still holding his foot, took one step back to steady himself — onto the exact patch of ice he'd been dripping on all afternoon — and his slushy feet went out from under him, and he sat down with enormous dignity in a snowbank, all by himself, and stayed there.

Nobody laughed. Well. Finnegan laughed, from inside his own snowbank, but he didn't know what he was laughing at.

---

"Up the hill," said Bjorn. "Both of you. Now."

"He shoved Willa first—" Zhīma started.

"Then you will say so. To Nora. First, and fast, and all of it." Bjorn scooped Willa gently onto his shoulder, where she sat blinking, and started up the slope on his skis with the particular slow dignity of a bear who has snow in his ears and has decided not to mention it. "The first whole story is the one grown-ups believe, small one. Get there first."

Zhīma, who had spent his entire life getting there first, had never once thought of *that* as the race.

Behind them, Sludge climbed out of the snowbank, dripping, and did not follow. Bjorn had known he wouldn't. Bullies do not enjoy hills with polar bears at the top.

---

Nora Snowmane's office had exactly one chair with a quilt on it, and today Zhīma sat in it, and Willa sat on the arm, and Bjorn stood in the doorway because he did not fit anywhere else.

"Sit," said Nora, which he already was. "Cocoa first. Then feelings."

But Zhīma had learned something on the way up the hill, and he couldn't wait for the cocoa.

"He shoved Willa off the rail," he said, fast, all in one breath. "I told him to stop and it didn't work. He grabbed me by the sesame and picked me up. I twisted and I stomped his foot and I yelled and I got loose. And then—" He stopped.

Nora waited. Nora was very good at waiting.

"And then I wanted to hit him," said Zhīma, small. "He was right there. He was wobbling. I was *rolling.* I was going to. If Bjorn hadn't said stop, I—" His seeds rattled. "I would have. And that's the truth. I'm telling you the whole thing because Bjorn said the whole thing is what you tell."

"Bjorn is right," said Nora, and handed him the cocoa, and did not look even slightly surprised. "Zhīma. Listen to me. Did you hit him?"

"…No."

"Then here's what happened today. Somebody put hands on you. You got free — with just enough, not one bit more — and then you *stopped*, even though every part of you wanted to keep going. And then you came straight up the hill and told me the whole truth, including the ugly part, before anybody else could tell me a different one." She sat back. "That's not a *close call*. That's the *whole thing*, done right. Wanting to hit back isn't the test. *Everybody* wants to hit back. Not doing it — that's the win."

"It didn't feel like winning," said Zhīma. "It felt like losing a race I was already in."

"Mm," said Auntie Hù, who had appeared in the doorway beside Bjorn's knee at some point, as she did. "That's exactly what it feels like. Every time. It never gets more comfortable. It just gets more *yours.*"

---

They talked about Sludge, too. Nora would talk to him — and to whoever was supposed to be looking after him — because a slush puppy who shoves small penguins off rails needs a grown-up as much as the penguin does. That wasn't Zhīma's job. That had never been Zhīma's job.

"What *is* my job?" he asked, on the way out, cocoa gone, quilt folded.

Bjorn considered this with great seriousness, snow still in both ears.

"Get free," he said. "Get away. Get to us. Get there first with the truth." He paused. "And perhaps, small one, for the next while — practice rolling *toward* things that are not on fire."

"That's *four* jobs."

"You are a very round person. You can hold four."

---

That night Zhīma didn't make it back to his bunk. He fell asleep on the warm iron rim of Auntie Hù's tea-egg pot, where she let the small ones curl up on cold nights, with his sesame seeds finally, finally still.

He thought about the twist toward the thumb, which had worked. He thought about the stomp, which had *really* worked. He thought about the shout that had carried up a whole hill and brought a polar bear down it. Those were the loud parts, and they were good.

But the part he kept coming back to, the part that hummed in him like a struck bell, was the quiet one. The one inch. The *stay.*

It turned out you could be the boldest thing in the valley and still have a brake. It turned out the brake was the boldest part.

"Roll with it," he murmured to the dark, and for the first time in his life, he meant *stop*.

## Goodnight Blessing

You are brave, little one, and you are strong — and tonight you don't need either one.
Let your hands go loose. Let your feet go still. The race is over and you got there first.
If anyone ever grabs you, you know what to do — and then you know when to stop.
Nothing is holding you now but the blanket. Sleep.

## 🛡️ What We Learned

- **Words first.** Boundary voice — "Stop. Don't touch me." — is always the first thing you try, and most of the time it works.
- **If someone puts hands on you, you may get them off.** Getting free is allowed. That is not fighting; that is *leaving*.
- **Enough to break the grip:** twist toward the thumb (the weak spot of any grab), stomp the foot, shove, and *yell* so grown-ups hear.
- **The second you're free — STOP.** Not one more hit. Revenge turns the kid who was grabbed into the other bully.
- **Get away, get to a grown-up.** Don't stay to argue. Bullies don't follow you up a hill toward a polar bear.
- **Tell the whole truth first and fast** — including the part you're not proud of. The first complete story is the one grown-ups believe.
- **Wanting to hit back is normal.** Not doing it is the win. It never gets comfortable; it just gets more *yours.*
