# Story 6: Sludge and the Words That Melt

**Main Character:** Bùdīng (Pudding)
**Guest Characters:** Finnegan Flipsworth, Sludge (new), Nora Snowmane, Auntie Hù, Herschel the Walrus, Teddy & Tilda Powderpuff
**Setting:** The Frostpeak lodge on a grey, sleety afternoon — the cocoa line, the rental-boot bench, and the long hallway to Nora's office
**Theme:** A bully is a fire that eats reactions. Starve it, stand with your friends, and tell a grown-up — that's not tattling, that's reporting.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** **停** (tíng) = stop
**Safety Skill:** Verbal bullies — the boundary voice; don't feed it; stay in the herd; reporting ≠ tattling

---

Bùdīng had been in Frostpeak Valley for exactly nine days, and in that time he had fallen down eleven times, wobbled into a snowbank twice, and been mistaken for a very small lamp once, by a seal, in the dark.

"I'm fine!" he told the seal. "I'm fine! I'm totally fine! …I'm wobbling."

He was a caramel pudding. He was golden on top with dark caramel dripping down his sides, and he wobbled — always, constantly, even standing still, even *asleep*. He left tiny caramel footprints wherever he went, which made him extremely easy to follow and extremely hard to lose, which was the story of his life.

But this morning, Bùdīng had done something brave. He had put on rental snowboard boots. Both of them. By himself.

They were, as it happens, on the wrong feet. He didn't know that yet. He clomped into the lodge cocoa line with the proud, careful walk of a pudding who has finally figured out footwear, wobbled to a stop behind Finnegan Flipsworth, and said, "Good morning! I have *boots.*"

And that's when the puddle spoke.

---

"Look at Jell-O Legs," said a voice like a slushie being poured too fast.

Bùdīng turned around.

Behind him — all around him, actually, because he was leaking — stood the biggest cup of blue-raspberry slush that Bùdīng had ever seen. It was as tall as a bear cub and twice as wide, and it was *melting*: blue drips ran down its sides and pooled around it in a spreading puddle the color of a bad idea. It had two drippy fists and a sneer you could have hung a coat on.

"Jell-O Legs," said the slush again, louder, so the whole line could hear. "Boots on backwards. Wobbling like a *baby*. Look at him! He's *shaking!*"

And here is the thing: the line laughed.

Not everyone. Not meanly, even. But a laugh went down the cocoa line like a ripple in a pond, and Finnegan Flipsworth — chaotic, loyal, loud Finnegan — laughed too, a big honking rockhopper "HA!" before his brain had even caught up with his beak.

Bùdīng looked down at his boots. They *were* on backwards.

His wobble, which had been a little wobble, became a medium wobble. His eyes went wide and shiny. One single caramel tear rolled down his side and landed — *plip* — on the wrong-footed boot.

"I'm fine," he whispered. "I'm fine. I'm totally—"

"Aww, he's gonna CRY," crowed the slush, and stomped a drippy foot, and the puddle spread. "What are you gonna do about it, Jell-O Legs? Huh? *What are you gonna do about it?*"

Bùdīng, who had no idea what he was going to do about it, did the only thing he knew how to do. He wobbled harder. Three wobbles, a hiccup, and a second tear.

It was, if we're honest, a *fantastic* reaction. It was the reaction of a lifetime.

The slush puppy beamed like it had been fed.

---

Finnegan stopped laughing about half a second after he'd started, and the feeling that replaced it was so awful that he actually put a flipper over his own beak, as if he could stuff the "HA!" back in.

He couldn't. It was out. It was in the air, and Bùdīng had heard it, and Bùdīng was wobbling and *crying* and looking at his backwards boots, and the puddle was laughing, and Finnegan — who could launch himself off a forty-foot jump without a second thought — could not for the life of him figure out what to *do*.

So he did nothing. He stared at his own feet, which were on the right way, and felt like the worst penguin in Frostpeak Valley.

"Sludge," said a voice.

It wasn't loud. It was the voice of a tea egg who was sitting on the cocoa counter with her arms folded and had been watching the entire time.

"Cocoa's ready," said Auntie Hù. "Take it and go."

The slush puppy — Sludge, apparently — snatched his cocoa, sloshed a last "*Jell-O Legs*" over his shoulder, and dripped away down the hall, leaving a blue trail that Herschel would be mopping for the next hour.

The line went quiet. Somebody coughed.

Bùdīng sat down on the rental-boot bench, very slowly, so he wouldn't wobble off it, and looked at his boots.

---

Finnegan sat down next to him. It took him three tries to start talking.

"I laughed," he said finally.

"I know."

"I didn't *mean*—it just—it came out, I didn't even *think*, and then it was OUT, and I—" Finnegan's voice went high and cracked. "I'm sorry. I'm really, really sorry. That was the worst thing I've ever done and I once snowboarded through a wedding."

Bùdīng sniffed. "Through a *wedding?*"

"They were very understanding. The cake was not." Finnegan slumped. "Bùdīng. Why does he *do* that?"

"Mm," said Auntie Hù, who had climbed down off the counter and was now sitting on the bench between them, which was a very small distance for a very large lesson. "Good question. Let me ask you both a better one. When Sludge said all that — what did he *get?*"

Bùdīng thought. "He got… everyone laughing."

"What else?"

"He got me crying."

"What else?"

Bùdīng looked down at himself. He was still wobbling — a little less now, but still. "He got… *this*," he said. "The wobble. The tears. The hiccup."

"Mm." Auntie Hù nodded slowly. "A bully isn't after your boots. He's after your *reaction.* It's food to him. Every wobble, every tear, every *I'm fine I'm fine* — that's a snack. You gave him a *feast*, little pudding. You gave him a nine-course meal with dessert."

"I'm the dessert," said Bùdīng miserably.

"You are," agreed Auntie Hù, "and that's a fine thing to be. But not for *him.*"

---

"So what do I do?" said Bùdīng. "Not react? I *can't* not react. I'm a pudding. Reacting is my whole—" he wobbled to demonstrate "—thing."

"You don't stop reacting. You change what you feed him." Auntie Hù held up one small marbled finger. "**The boundary voice.** Three parts. Feet planted — you've got boots, use them. Eyes up — look at his face, not your feet. Plain words. *'Stop. I don't like that.'* That's it. That's the whole thing."

"That's *it?*"

"That's it. No jokes — a joke tells him you're playing. No begging — *please stop* tells him he's winning. No insults back — the second you call him Slushy McDripface, you're in *his* game, and he's better at it than you." She shrugged. "*Stop. I don't like that.* Flat. Boring. Like reading a sign. **停** (tíng). It means *stop*, and it's the least interesting word in the world, which is why it works."

"And then?"

"Then you don't stand there alone." She jabbed a finger at Finnegan. "Bullies pick off the lonely one. Every time. The wobbly pudding by himself on the bench — that's the target. The wobbly pudding with a loud penguin next to him? Less fun. Stay in the herd."

Finnegan sat up. "I can be a herd. I'm *great* at being a herd. I'm basically a stampede."

"Mm. And *then*—" Auntie Hù looked at Bùdīng "—you tell a grown-up. Nora. Herschel. Me."

Bùdīng's wobble came back all at once. "That's *tattling.*"

"Is it?" Auntie Hù tilted her head. "Let me ask you. If you told Nora *Sludge took the last marshmallow and I wanted it* — that's telling to get *him in trouble.* That's tattling. But if you tell Nora *Sludge is calling me names every day and I don't feel safe in the cocoa line* — who's that for?"

Bùdīng thought. "…Me?"

"That's for *you.* To get *you safe.* That's called **reporting**, and it's not tattling, it's what grown-ups are *for.* We can't fix what we don't know about. You wouldn't call the fire department tattling on a fire."

"Well," said Finnegan, "*technically*—"

"Finnegan."

"Sorry."

---

The trouble with lessons is that you have to actually *use* them, usually sooner than you'd like.

Sludge came back at four o'clock. Of course he did. Bùdīng was still on the bench — boots now on the correct feet, thank you very much — and Finnegan was next to him, and Sludge came dripping down the hallway with a fresh cocoa and a fresh sneer.

"Jell-O Legs," he said. "Still here? Still *shaking?* What are you gonna—"

Bùdīng stood up.

It was not graceful. Nothing Bùdīng did was graceful. But he planted both boots, right feet, on the floor, and he made his eyes go *up* — off his boots, off the puddle, right to Sludge's drippy blue face — and he opened his mouth and said:

"stop."

It came out as a squeak. A tiny caramel squeak, like a mouse that had stepped on something.

Sludge grinned. "What was *that?*"

Bùdīng's wobble was enormous. It was a category-five wobble. But he did not sit down, and he did not say *I'm fine*, and he did not look away. He took a breath all the way down to his caramel bottom, and *something happened* — the wobble went down through his boots and into the floor and came back up as *sound*.

"**STOP,**" said Bùdīng. "**I don't like that.**"

It boomed. It actually *boomed.* It bounced off the rental-boot lockers and rattled a ski pole off its hook. Three penguins in the cocoa line turned around. Bùdīng himself looked more surprised than anyone.

Sludge's grin flickered. "Oh, *big* words, Jell-O—"

"WOOOHOOO—" said Finnegan, leaping up beside Bùdīng, flippers wide, and then catching himself. "I mean. Um. Yeah." He put his flippers down. He planted his feet, right way round. He looked Sludge in the face, and said, as flatly and boringly as a rockhopper penguin has ever said anything: "Stop. He doesn't like that. I don't either."

Two of them. Shoulder to shoulder. Neither one crying. Neither one laughing. Neither one giving him a single thing.

Sludge stood there and dripped. He opened his mouth. He waited for the wobble, the tear, the *I'm fine.*

None came.

"…Whatever," he said, and it came out smaller than he wanted. "*Whatever.* Boring." And he sloshed away down the hall, and for the first time all day, the puddle he left behind him was just a puddle, and nobody laughed at anything.

---

"I have to go tell Nora now," said Bùdīng, when his wobble had settled down to merely gigantic.

"Do you want me to come?" said Finnegan.

"I'm a herd," said Bùdīng. "Herds come."

So they went. Down the long hallway, past the fire, past Bjorn reading a very large newspaper, to the door with the hand-lettered sign that said NORA, and Bùdīng knocked, and went in, and told her.

He told her about Jell-O Legs and the laughing line and the two visits and the boots and the boom. He told her he wasn't telling to get Sludge in trouble. He was telling because he didn't feel safe in the cocoa line, and he wanted to feel safe in the cocoa line, because the cocoa was *really good.*

Nora Snowmane listened to every word with her big white paws folded. When he was done, she didn't say *are you sure* or *boys will be boys* or *just ignore him.*

She said: "Thank you. I'm so glad you told me. That's exactly right."

And then she said, "Cocoa first. Then I'll go have a word with a slush puppy."

Bùdīng let out a breath he'd been holding since nine o'clock that morning. His wobble dropped to a mere ripple.

"I'm fine," he said, and this time, he actually was.

---

Here is one last thing about that day, which Bùdīng didn't see, and which you should.

Late that night, when the lodge was dark and the fire was down to embers, Nora Snowmane went looking for a puddle. She found it in the empty rental room, sitting by the boot dryer, dripping in the dark.

"Sludge," she said.

He didn't turn around. "Come to yell?"

"No." Nora sat down on the bench — the same bench — and looked at him for a long moment. "You're melting, you know."

"I *know* I'm melting. I'm *slush.* That's the whole—"

"Has anyone ever told you," said Nora gently, "that you're allowed to refreeze?"

The puddle stopped spreading. Just for a second.

Sludge didn't answer. But he didn't leave, either, and Nora sat with him a while in the quiet, and that is where this story sets him down — not fixed, not finished, but not alone in the dark. That's a different story. We'll get to it.

## Goodnight Blessing

Feet planted, eyes up, little one — and now, eyes closed.
Your voice was loud when it needed to be, so it gets to be quiet all night long.
No one to stop tonight but the day itself, which is stopping on its own.
You're in the herd. The herd is asleep. Sleep too.

## 🛡️ What We Learned

- **Bullies eat reactions.** Tears, wobbles, begging, jokes, yelling back — that's all food. Stop feeding it and the fire goes out.
- **The boundary voice:** feet planted, eyes on their face, plain flat words — "**Stop. I don't like that.**" No jokes, no *please*, no insults back. Boring works.
- **Stay in the herd.** Bullies pick off the lonely one. Stand next to a friend; be the friend someone stands next to.
- **If you laughed and wish you hadn't** — say sorry, then go stand beside the person. That fixes it faster than feeling terrible.
- **Reporting is not tattling.** Tattling = telling to get someone *in trouble.* Reporting = telling to get someone *safe.* Reporting is what grown-ups are for.
- **Tell a grown-up who listens.** If you don't feel safe somewhere — even the cocoa line — that's worth a report.
