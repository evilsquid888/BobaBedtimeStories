# Story 26: The Gun Up Close

**Main Character:** Bjorn Bigpaws (Polar Bear)
**Guest Characters:** Coach Rosa the Flamingo, Finnegan Flipsworth, Piper Paddlefoot, Tico the Toucan, Auntie Hù, Boba, Willa Wobble
**Setting:** The Pink Belt Academy in the lodge's boot-drying room on a grey, quiet afternoon — and later, the rim of Auntie Hù's tea-egg pot
**Theme:** A wallet is a thing. A phone is a thing. You are not a thing. Give up things — and know the one time you don't.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** **讓開** (ràngkāi) = get out of the way / step aside
**Safety Skill:** A gun up close — comply and give up things first; fight only if you're being taken; get off the line, redirect, control, run; never point it at yourself

---

> *A note for the grown-up reading this: this story talks about a gun pointed at a person. A blue rubber training gun stands in for it, nobody is hurt, and the loudest lesson is "give up things." The moves are described the way a coach says them — as principles with one cue each — not as a manual. Read it together, earlier in the evening the first time.*

---

Every lesson at the Pink Belt Academy began the same way: with Tico the Toucan.

"LADIES AND GENTLE-PENGUINS! BEARS OF ALL SIZES! ONE SLOTH! Welcome to the boot-drying room, home of the PINK BELT ACADEMY, where everyone starts pink and everyone STAYS pink! Today's lesson — brought to you by SHRIMP — is—"

He stopped.

Coach Rosa the Flamingo had not raised a wing for silence. She had not blown her tiny whistle. She was simply standing on one leg in the middle of the room. She had set something down on the boot bench in front of her, very gently, and stepped back from it.

It was a training gun. Blue rubber. About the size of a mitten. Everyone in Frostpeak knew that blue rubber meant *pretend*, the same way orange meant *toy*. But Rosa hadn't given it a fanfare. She hadn't given it a sombrero. She hadn't done a bit.

Tico closed his enormous beak.

Finnegan Flipsworth, who had come in doing a cartwheel, finished the cartwheel and sat down without being asked.

"Señora Pistola Azul," said Rosa quietly. "Sit. Two feet down. Nobody touches her yet."

And the whole academy — penguins, bears, a tapioca pearl, a sloth — noticed that when Coach Rosa was quiet, the room got quieter than when she shouted.

---

"Here is the first thing," said Rosa, "and it is the loudest thing, so I will say it softly."

She held up one pink wing.

"If someone shows you a gun and wants your wallet — give him the wallet. Your phone — give him the phone. Your snowboard, Piper, your beautiful board — you give it. Hands up, open, slow. You say, *okay, okay, it's yours.* You let him walk away. You do not chase. You do not argue. You do not say *that was a gift from my grandmother.* And when he is gone, you find a grown-up and you call Solveig."

"But it's *mine,*" said Piper.

"It is yours. It is also a *thing.* A wallet is a thing. A truck is a thing. A glittery blue board with silver snowflakes—" she looked at it, and Piper hugged it "—is a *very nice* thing. You are not a thing. Things can be bought again. You cannot."

Boba's tiny arm went up. "Auntie Hù says the best fight is the one you're not in."

"Auntie Hù is correct. She is always correct. It is exhausting." Rosa's other leg came down; she stood, for once, on two. "Giving up the wallet *is* not being in the fight. That is the whole trick. He wants a thing. You give a thing. There is no fight. Then shrimp."

Nobody laughed at *then shrimp.* Not this time. Rosa nodded, as if that were correct too.

---

"So when do you *ever* fight a gun?" Bjorn Bigpaws asked from the back, where he took up most of the wall.

Rosa looked at him for a long moment. Then at the small ones. Then back at him.

"You fight a gun," she said, "only when you believe you are about to be shot *anyway.* Not for a wallet. Not for a phone. Only when giving up things has stopped working — when he says *get in the car.* When he tries to take you somewhere. When he tries to tie you." Her voice stayed level. "Then the gun has stopped being a *robbery* and become the other thing. And then — and *only* then — you go."

Willa Wobble, very small on the bench, said: "Go how?"

"Like this. *Tranquilo* — watch first. Bjorn, you're the one big enough. Everybody else, this is a *watching* lesson today. Your version comes after."

Bjorn stood up. The boot-drying room, which had felt roomy, suddenly did not.

Rosa picked up Señora Pistola Azul, walked to Bjorn, and pointed the blue rubber muzzle at his enormous white chest from two feet away. The academy held its breath. Finnegan held Piper's flipper. Piper let him.

"Give me your wallet," said Rosa flatly.

"Here," said Bjorn, and mimed handing it over, hands open, slow.

"Good. Now get in the truck."

And Bjorn *moved.*

---

Here's what it looked like, and it took about as long as a sneeze.

One step — *sideways.* Not back. Not forward. Sideways, so the blue rubber was suddenly pointing at the wall where a polar bear had just been.

"**讓開** (ràngkāi)," said Rosa over the top of it, calm as cocoa. "*Get off the line.* The line is where the gun is looking. One step and it is looking at nothing."

Bjorn's paw came across and *pushed* — not grabbed, pushed — the blue muzzle away from every single body in the room, toward the empty corner with the boot dryer.

"Redirect. Away from *everyone.* Not up. Not at Finnegan. The corner."

Then he was *in* — close, chest to wing, both great paws wrapped around the training gun and Rosa's wing together.

"Both hands. You are not holding a gun. You are holding *the whole arm.*"

A knee — soft, slow, a *demonstration* knee — toward where a person's middle would be.

"Closest weapon, closest target. Once. Maybe twice."

And then the blue rubber was in Bjorn's paw, and Bjorn was three enormous steps back. He held it *pointed at the floor*, his other paw open, his eyes already sweeping the room.

"Take it. Step back *far.* Never point it at yourself. Never put your finger on the trigger. And you *scan* — because there is never just one problem. Then when Solveig comes, you put it *down*, you put your hands *up*, and you let her be the one holding it." Rosa flexed her wing. "Also — ow. Nicely done."

"I did not fight," said Bjorn, with dignity. "I *relocated a gun.*"

"With style," said Rosa. "Yes."

---

"MY TURN," said Finnegan, who had been vibrating.

"Your turn is the *small* version—"

"I KNOW THE MOVES. I WATCHED. Step, push, in, knee, take—"

Rosa considered him. Then she reached under the boot bench and pulled out a second training gun. This one was *foam.* Bright pink. It squeaked.

"Señorita Espuma," she announced. "For rockhoppers. Piper, you're the bad guy. Point it at his chest. Finnegan — off the line. *¡Dale!*"

Piper pointed. Finnegan stepped.

Forward.

The foam muzzle bonked him gently between the eyes. *Squeak.*

"Off the *LINE,* Finnegan."

"I AM the line!"

"You are *on* the line. That is the opposite. Again."

Piper pointed. Finnegan stepped — sideways, this time, beautifully — then reached across to redirect and pushed the foam gun *toward his own face.* *Squeak.*

"Away from *everyone,* Finnegan. Everyone includes *you.*"

"THAT'S NOT FAIR, I'm the closest one!"

Third try. He stepped off the line. He pushed the foam away, toward the corner. He burst in. He wrapped both flippers around Piper's wing. He looked up at her, triumphant, forgot the knee, forgot the take, forgot everything, and hugged her.

*Squeak,* said the foam gun, squashed between them.

The academy lost it. Even Rosa. Even Bjorn, who laughed like a rockslide. Tico fell off his perch announcing "AND IT'S A HUG, FOLKS, A DEVASTATING HUG."

"That," said Rosa, wiping one pink eye, "was *almost* Krav Maga."

---

When everyone had breathed again, Rosa did the thing she did when the joke was over: she sat down. All the way down, on the boot bench, both legs folded, eye level with Willa and Boba and the cubs.

"Now the truth," she said. "Bjorn did that because Bjorn is a seven-foot bear who is going to train it a hundred more times. Grown-ups train this for *years.* Solveig trains it. Your parents probably do not, and that is fine." She looked at each small face. "You are not going to take a gun away from a grown-up. I will not pretend you are. So here is *your* version, and it is three things."

She held up one pink feather.

"*Off the line.* If someone points a gun at you and it stops being about a wallet — one step sideways, so it's looking at nothing."

Two feathers.

"*Run zigzag to cover.* Not straight. Not toward him. Away, crooked, fast, toward the biggest solid thing you can find — a brick wall, the engine end of a car — and *around* it."

Three.

"*Scream words.* You know this one. Auntie Hù taught you. *HELP. CALL 911. THIS IS NOT MY DAD.* Words turn heads."

"That's it?" said Boba.

"That's it. Off the line, zigzag, words. Three things a small person can actually *do.*" She stood back up, one leg again. "Everything Bjorn did — you'll learn it when you're big, if you want, from a coach in a real class with real mats. Not from a story. The story just tells you it *exists.*"

---

Late that afternoon, Bjorn and Rosa sat on the rim of Auntie Hù's tea-egg pot — well, Rosa sat; Bjorn sat *beside* the pot, since the rim would not have survived him — and watched the market lanterns come on.

"They were scared," said Bjorn. "The little ones. Even with the blue."

"Mm," said Auntie Hù, from inside a cloud of steam.

"Good," said Rosa. "Scared is the correct amount. Not terrified. Not brave. *Scared and clear.*" She tucked her head under one wing, then remembered she was talking and un-tucked it. "Bjorn. What did I tell them first?"

"Give up the wallet."

"And what did I tell them last?"

"Off the line, zigzag, words."

"And in the middle?"

Bjorn thought. "That most of them will never need any of it."

"Mm," said Auntie Hù again, which was as close as she came to applause.

"That," said Rosa, "is the whole class. Everything else is decoration." She looked at the lanterns, and then far across the ice, at the small pink foam gun sticking out of Finnegan's jacket pocket. He had smuggled it out to practice on a snowman. "Also the hug. The hug was decoration. Good decoration."

---

Boba found Rosa at the end of the night, alone on the ice, one leg up, so still she might have been a very tall pink lamppost.

"Coach Rosa? Are you asleep?"

"Always. Also no. What."

"I keep thinking about it. The gun. Do you think I'll ever—"

"No."

"But—"

"Boba. Listen to me." She didn't open her eyes. "You will walk ten thousand roads. Auntie Hù told you this. On nine thousand nine hundred and ninety-nine of them, nobody shows you anything but a smile and a cocoa. You will probably never need what I taught today. Not the wallet. Not the line. None of it."

"Then why—"

"*Learn it anyway.*" One pink eye opened. "Because *probably* is not *never.* Because the one road where you need it, you will not get a warning. And because a small person who knows three things is a completely different person from a small person who knows nothing — even if they never use them. They stand differently. They *sleep* differently." The eye closed. "You will sleep fine tonight. Watch."

"How do you know?"

"Because you know three things. Off the line. Zigzag. Words." A long pause. Her leg didn't waver. "Then shrimp."

Boba bounced back to the stand, very softly, and climbed into his bunk, and thought about it for exactly as long as it took to discover that Rosa was right.

## Goodnight Blessing

Give up the small things, little one — the day, the worries, the last of the light.
Keep the big thing. Keep *you.*
You know three things now, and you will probably never need them, and that is the very best kind of knowing.
Off the line. Off the day. Off to sleep.

## 🛡️ What We Learned

- **Give up things.** If someone with a gun wants your wallet, phone, or board — give it, hands up, slow. Let them leave. Then find a grown-up and call 911. A wallet is a *thing*. You are not.
- **You only ever fight a gun if you believe you're about to be shot anyway** — when it stops being about things and becomes *get in the car*, *come with me*, or being tied up.
- **Grown-ups train the disarm for years.** Bjorn's version — off the line, redirect the barrel away from *everyone*, both hands on the arm, knee, take it, step back far, never point it at yourself, never touch the trigger, put it down when police come — is for a real class with a real coach, not a story.
- **The kid's version is three things:** *Off the line* (one step sideways). *Run zigzag to cover* (a brick wall, the engine end of a car). *Scream words* (HELP! CALL 911! THIS IS NOT MY DAD!).
- **Scared and clear** is the right amount. Not terrified. Not "brave."
- **You will probably never need this. Learn it anyway.** Probably is not never — and knowing three things changes how you stand and how you sleep.
