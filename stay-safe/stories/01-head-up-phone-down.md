# Story 1: Head Up, Phone Down

**Main Character:** Aiyu (Aiyu jelly)
**Guest Characters:** Willa Wobble, Auntie Hù (new), Finnegan Flipsworth, Bīng, Boba, Sergeant Solveig Snowmane (new)
**Setting:** Opening night of the Frostpeak Winter Night Market — the Boba on Ice stand, Herschel's Cocoa Hut, and Auntie Hù's tea-egg pot, under strings of paper lanterns and a sky full of snow
**Theme:** Paying attention is the first and best kind of safety — and it can be a game.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** **注意** (zhùyì) = pay attention
**Safety Skill:** Situational awareness — Aiyu's Four Coats (White, Yellow, Orange, Red)

---

The first thing that happened on opening night of the Frostpeak Winter Night Market was that Bīng's topping tower fell on a penguin.

It was a very tall tower. It had mango, red bean, three kinds of mochi, a waterfall of condensed milk, and a small paper flag that said MORE IS MORE. It had been the pride of the brand-new **Boba on Ice** stand for almost eleven seconds. And then Finnegan Flipsworth came through the grand-opening banner at full speed, shouting "WOOOHOOO—wait, how do I stop?!" and the answer turned out to be: with his face, in the tower.

"MY MASTERPIECE!" wailed Bīng.

"MY EVERYTHING!" wailed Finnegan, from underneath the red bean.

Boba bounced up onto the counter. "Okay!" he said. "Okay, okay! That's the opening-night bad luck used up! It's all good from here! Let's BOUNCE!"

And truly, it was a wonderful night. The Taiwan dessert crew had come all the way across the ocean to run a boba stand for one winter season in Frostpeak Valley, where penguins snowboard and polar bears ski and the snow squeaks under your feet like a small happy mouse. Paper lanterns swung on strings between the lodge and the frozen lake. The air smelled like cocoa and sugar and pine. Somewhere a narwhal was doing a dramatic spin through a hole in the ice, just in case anyone was watching.

Almost nobody was watching. That, as it turned out, was the whole story.

---

Aiyu was in charge of the tip jar.

She was a small, wobbly cube of pale golden jelly, so clear that you could see the lanterns right through her. Tiny lemon-seed freckles floated inside her. When she got nervous she jiggled, and tonight she was jiggling so hard the lemon seeds inside her were getting seasick.

The crowd was *enormous*. Penguins, bears, seals, a fox, a walrus in a very small hat. Everyone was pushing gently toward the fire-dancers on the lake. Everyone was looking at their phones, or their cocoa, or the sky.

Somebody bumped Aiyu. The tip jar rocked.

Somebody else bumped her. The tip jar tipped.

Aiyu grabbed it with both wobbly arms, hugged it to her chest, and discovered that she had absolutely no idea who had bumped her, or where they'd gone, or which way the stand was anymore. The crowd had simply closed over her like water.

"Mm," said a voice by her knee.

Aiyu looked down.

Beside the boba stand sat an iron pot the size of a bathtub, steaming gently, full of dark brown tea eggs. And on the rim of the pot, watching the crowd with her arms folded, sat a tea egg.

She was small and dense and brown, and her shell was cracked all over in fine dark lines — a marbled map of everywhere she had ever been. She wore a padded vest with too many pockets. Over the pot hung a hand-painted sign: **ATTITUDE. SKILLS. PLAN.**

"You're Aiyu," said the tea egg. "From the boba stand. I'm Auntie Hù. I sell eggs and I mind the crowd."

"Nobody minds the crowd," said Aiyu. "The crowd minds itself."

"Mm," said Auntie Hù. It was the sound of someone deciding whether to teach you something. "Tell me. When they bumped you — which coat were you wearing?"

Aiyu looked down at herself. She was a jelly. She had never worn a coat in her life.

"I don't understand," she said, honestly, because Aiyu could not say a thing she didn't mean. You could see right through her. That was how you knew.

"Good," said Auntie Hù. "Sit. I'll show you."

---

Auntie Hù did not lecture. Aiyu waited for the lecture and it never came. Instead the tea egg pointed with one small marbled arm at Willa Wobble, who was standing three steps away with enormous fluffy ear-muffs on, staring at her phone, humming.

"Willa," said Auntie Hù. "What color is Bīng's flag?"

Willa didn't hear. Willa didn't look up. A seal walked between Willa and the stand carrying a fish the size of a sled, and Willa did not notice the seal, or the fish, or the sled.

"That," said Auntie Hù, "is a **white coat**. Asleep to the world. Eyes down. Ears closed. If a snowball came, she'd meet it with her face." She said this kindly. "Everyone wears the white coat sometimes. In bed, it's the right coat. In a crowd of ten thousand strangers, it's the wrong coat."

"What's the right coat?"

"**Yellow.** Head up. Eyes moving. Relaxed — not scared, not stiff, just *looking*. Where's the exit? Who's behind me? Who's watching the crowd instead of the show?" Auntie Hù tapped the sign. "The yellow coat is where you live. It's your house. You should be comfortable there."

"And then?"

"**Orange.** Something's off. You don't know what yet. You don't panic. You make a plan: *if that happens, I do this.* And **red** is when you do it." She shrugged. "That's all. Four coats. Most nights you wear yellow all evening and nothing happens, and you go home, and that's a *good* night. **注意** (zhùyì) — pay attention. It's not a chore. It's a game you get to win every single day."

Aiyu thought about this. She thought about the bump and the tip jar and the crowd closing over her.

"I was wearing the white coat," she said.

"Mm," said Auntie Hù, and Aiyu understood, all at once, that *Mm* meant *yes, and I'm glad you said so.*

---

"Let's play," said Auntie Hù.

"Play what?"

"Three exits. Go."

Aiyu looked around. It was strange, at first. Her eyes wanted to go back down to the jar. She made them go up.

"The lodge doors," she said. "The gap between the cocoa hut and the tea-egg pot. And… the lake path, past the fire-dancers."

"Four," said Auntie Hù.

"There isn't a—" Aiyu turned. Behind the stand, between two stacked crates of tapioca, was a slot just wide enough for a small jelly to slide through. "Oh."

"Small people have small exits. Learn yours. The bears have to use the door like everybody else. Who's behind you?"

Aiyu didn't turn around. She *thought* about the last thirty seconds instead. "A polar bear with twin cubs. A fox. Herschel, carrying a tray."

"Mm. Now the hard one. Who's watching the crowd instead of the show?"

And here is where the strangest and most wonderful thing happened.

Aiyu had spent her whole life being the see-through one. People looked *at* her and saw whatever was behind her. It had always felt like a small sadness. But now she let her eyes go soft and wide — the way the lanterns went soft when you looked at them through her — and she discovered she could do the same thing to a crowd. She could look *through* it. The show was over there. Everyone's faces pointed at the fire-dancers, glowing orange, mouths open. Everyone's faces except—

One.

A lodge guest in a nice grey coat, two rows back. His face pointed the wrong way. Not at the fire. At *handbags.* At the pocket of a penguin's parka. At the open zipper on a bear's backpack. His eyes moved the way Aiyu's eyes were moving now, except he wasn't playing a game. He was shopping.

Aiyu's jelly went cold.

"Orange," she whispered.

"Mm," said Auntie Hù, very quietly. "What's your plan?"

---

Now, here is a thing you should know about Aiyu. Every brave story she'd ever read said the hero *confronts* the villain. Marches up. Points. Shouts *STOP, THIEF!*

She was a jelly cube the size of a teacup. He was a grown-up in a grey coat.

"My plan," said Aiyu slowly, "is not to go over there."

"Good plan."

"My plan is to tell you."

"You just did."

"And you tell…?"

Auntie Hù turned her head one inch to the left, where a very large, very white polar bear in a navy jacket with a silver snowflake badge was drinking cocoa and looking, it seemed, at nothing at all.

"Sergeant Solveig," said Auntie Hù, not raising her voice even slightly. "Grey coat. Two rows back. Shopping."

"Saw him," said Sergeant Solveig, and set her cocoa down.

What happened next was the least dramatic thing Aiyu had ever seen, and it was the best thing.

The sergeant walked slowly. She stopped beside the man in the grey coat. She said something Aiyu couldn't hear. The man's shoulders went up, then down. And then the two of them walked together, quietly, toward the lodge doors, and the fire-dancers finished their dance, and the crowd went *oooooh*, and not one single penguin ever knew that anything had happened at all.

"That's it?" said Aiyu.

"That's it," said Auntie Hù. "No shouting. No chasing. You saw it, you told the right person, the right person handled it. That is what winning looks like. It looks like *nothing*." She pulled a tea egg from the pot and handed it to Aiyu, still warm. "Most heroes are very boring. That's how you know they're good at it."

Aiyu held the egg. It was the first prize she had ever won for *looking.*

---

Willa came over eventually, one ear-muff pushed up, blinking.

"Did I miss something?" she asked. "I feel like I missed something."

"You were wearing the white coat," said Aiyu, and then, because she was honest and also kind, "I was too. Before."

Willa looked down at her tiny purple board, and her phone, and her enormous muffs. "I like the white coat," she admitted. "It's quiet in there."

"Yellow's quiet too," said Auntie Hù. "It's just quiet with the lights on."

Willa thought about that for a long moment. Then she pushed the other ear-muff up, and looked around the market — really looked — at the lanterns and the crowd and the lake and the place where a seal was, at that very moment, stealing a pretzel.

"Oh," said Willa softly. "There's a lot out here."

"The smallest step is still a step," said Auntie Hù, who had clearly been reading up on penguins.

---

Naturally, Finnegan wanted to try.

"YELLOW COAT," he announced, climbing out of the wreckage of Bīng's tower with a mochi ball stuck to his head. "I'm in it. I'm LIVING in it. Head up! Eyes moving! I see EVERYTHING!" He whipped his head left. "Lantern!" Right. "Bear!" Up. "SNOW!" Down. "PRETZEL—"

He lunged for the pretzel. The pretzel was in the seal's mouth, on the far side of Herschel's cocoa vat. Finnegan was not.

There was a splash. There was a long, marshmallowy silence.

"I see," said Finnegan, from inside the cocoa, "that I have made a mistake."

"He's not wrong," said Boba, peering in. "He *did* see everything."

"He saw everything except the *vat*," said Bīng, "which is LITERALLY the BIGGEST thing here."

"Yellow coat," said Auntie Hù, fishing him out with a ladle, "also means watching where your own feet are going." She looked at the dripping penguin. "We'll work on it."

---

Late that night, when the lanterns were dimmed and the crowd had melted away up the mountain, Aiyu sat on the rim of the tea-egg pot with her friends and watched the last of the fire die down on the lake.

She noticed things. She noticed Bīng, rebuilding his tower for the fourth time, smaller and sturdier and still with the flag. She noticed Boba, asleep on the counter with his arms around the tip jar — which was full. She noticed Willa, both ear-muffs off, counting exits on her flippers and getting to five, because she'd found one Aiyu had missed.

She noticed, as she got sleepy, that her eyes wanted to close. And she let them. Because that's the other half of the game Auntie Hù never quite said out loud: eyes that have *looked* all evening are allowed to rest all night.

"Which coat now?" murmured Willa.

"White," said Aiyu, yawning. "The good kind."

"Mm," said Auntie Hù, and turned off the lantern.

## Goodnight Blessing

Head up all day, little one — and now, head down on the pillow.
You looked at the world tonight, so the world can look after itself for a while.
Four exits, five friends, one warm egg.
Put on the white coat, the good kind. Sleep.

## 🛡️ What We Learned

- **The Four Coats:** White = asleep to the world (phone down, headphones on). Yellow = head up, looking around, relaxed. Orange = something's off, make a plan. Red = do the plan.
- **Live in Yellow** when you're out in the world. It's not scary — it's just having the lights on.
- **Know your exits.** Everywhere you go, find two or three ways out. Small people have small exits.
- **Judge faces by direction:** at a show, everyone looks at the show. Someone looking at pockets and bags is worth noticing.
- **Notice, then tell** the right grown-up. You don't confront. Winning looks like *nothing happening.*
- **Phone down, head up** in crowds, at bus stops, walking. You can't see with your face in a screen.

## 💬 Talk About It

1. Which coat do you usually wear when we're at the store? On the walk to school? In your room?
2. Right now, where you're sitting — can you name two ways out of this room?
3. If you noticed someone acting strange at a crowded place, who is the right grown-up to tell?
