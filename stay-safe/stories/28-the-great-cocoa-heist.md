# Story 28: The Great Cocoa Heist

**Main Character:** Aiyu (Aiyu jelly)
**Guest Characters:** Taro, Tico the Toucan, Estrella the Sea Turtle, Barnaby Beaksworth, Coach Rosa the Flamingo, Herschel the Walrus, Sergeant Solveig Snowmane, Boba, Finnegan Flipsworth
**Setting:** Herschel's Cocoa Hut on a grey Sunday afternoon, closed to the public for two hours and re-opened as the scene of the Pink Belt Academy's most dramatic drill
**Theme:** When you can't fight and you can't run, the bravest thing you can be is small, calm, and patient — and the smartest thing you can be is watching.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** **等** (děng) = wait
**Safety Skill:** Hostage and robbery situations — stay calm, comply, be grey, observe, wait for the moment; when police come, get DOWN with open hands

---

The robber came through the Cocoa Hut door at exactly two o'clock, and the first thing he did was announce himself.

"THE ROBBER," said the robber, "HAS ARRIVED."

He was a toucan in a black knit cap that did not fit over his enormous rainbow beak, so it sat on top of his head like a very small sad hat. He had a paper bag marked LOOT, and, tucked under one wing, a stack of little cards that said *RATE YOUR ROBBERY EXPERIENCE 1–5*.

"Tico," said Coach Rosa the Flamingo, from her spot on one leg by the fireplace. "You are not supposed to *announce* yourself."

"But how will they know it's a robbery?"

"They will know, Tico."

"Beak the news!" said Tico, delighted, then lowered his voice to a growl that came out as a squawk. "I mean. THIS IS A ROBBERY. Also, if you have a moment afterward—" he held up a card "—a five is really helpful."

Aiyu, sitting at a corner table with a mug of cocoa she had been told not to drink, thought this was going to be the silliest afternoon of her life.

She was half right.

---

Here is how the afternoon had started.

Coach Rosa — seven feet of pink flamingo, a headband, a whistle, and the Pink Belt Academy in the lodge's boot-drying room — had ended Friday's lesson with: "Sunday. Two o'clock. Cocoa Hut. We are doing a *heist.*"

"A what?" said Boba.

"A pretend one. **Tranquilo** (*trahn-KEE-lo*) — *easy, easy.* Herschel says yes. Sergeant Solveig will be the police. Tico will be the robber because he asked forty times. And you will be the people who were just having cocoa when something bad walked in."

"Do we burst?" said Finnegan. "Do we knee?"

"No burst. No knee." Rosa lowered her head until her long pink neck made a question mark. "Today's lesson is the hardest one I teach. Today you learn to do *nothing.* Beautifully."

So here they all were. Aiyu in the corner with Taro, who had settled into his chair the way a purple mountain settles into a valley. Estrella the sea turtle at the next table, so still she might have been furniture. Barnaby at the counter with dried fish, because it was a drill, but it was also two o'clock. Boba and Finnegan by the window. Herschel polishing a mug with tremendous seriousness.

"HANDS WHERE I CAN SEE THEM," squawked Tico. "And the cocoa. All of it. In the bag."

"The bag is paper," said Herschel.

"IN THE BAG, WALRUS."

---

Here is the thing about Aiyu.

She was the see-through one — a wobble of amber jelly you could watch the fire flicker through — and since Auntie Hù's Four Coats, she'd learned to turn that around. She could see *through* things. Crowds. Patterns. A room.

So while Finnegan vibrated like a kettle, and Boba tried very hard not to bounce (he bounced anyway, about a quarter of an inch, which for Boba is sitting still), Aiyu did the thing she did. She looked.

*One robber — he keeps glancing at the door like he's waiting for someone, but nobody comes.* *Black hat, LOOT bag, cards. Nervous — keeps fixing the hat. The hat is losing.* *Two doors: the front, and the kitchen one behind Herschel.* *Thick oak counter. Stone wall around the fireplace.* *He's between us and the front door. He is NOT between us and the kitchen.*

She didn't stare — Rosa had said Friday, *staring is a challenge, and you are not challenging anyone today.* She let her eyes rest near the LOOT bag and saw everything else out the sides of them, the way you watch a snowstorm without looking at any one flake.

Beside her, Taro — dusty purple, reading glasses on the end of his nose — was *breathing.* Slowly. In, out. It was contagious. Her nervous jiggle went down a notch. Then another.

"Good things," Taro murmured, so quietly it was barely a sound, "take time to steam."

---

"YOU," said Tico, pointing his beak at Finnegan. "You look like a HERO. Are you a HERO?"

Finnegan, who had been half out of his seat for two full minutes, froze. Every feather on his crest wanted to burst across the room, knock the tiny hat off that toucan, and be *magnificent.*

"Finnegan," said Rosa, very softly, from the fireplace. "What is a hero, in this room, right now?"

Finnegan thought about it. "…The one who gets everybody hurt?"

"*Mm.*" Rosa had picked that up from Auntie Hù. "The one who gets everybody hurt. Sit. Be *grey.*"

"What's grey?"

"Grey is the color nobody remembers. Not loud, not brave, not staring, not crying, not *special.* Grey is a chair. Be a chair, Finnegan."

Finnegan sat. Flippers flat on the table. He was, for possibly the first time in his life, a chair.

"Excellent," said Tico. "Very grey. Five stars." He turned. "WALRUS. The cocoa."

Herschel ladled cocoa into the paper bag, which immediately began to sag. "It's not going *well.*"

"That's a LOOT problem, not a ROBBER problem."

---

Barnaby Beaksworth, who had been quietly eating fish at the counter this whole time, cleared his throat.

"Excuse me. Mr. Robber."

Tico spun around. "WHAT."

"Would you like a fish?" Barnaby held one up. "You seem stressed. There's always enough fish for one more. Even a robber."

Rosa's eyebrow — flamingos do have them, if you look — went up. "Barnaby. Explain what you are doing."

"Being *human,*" said Barnaby. "You said Friday. Soft voice. Use a name. Ask for water. Offer a thing. A scared, angry person gets a little less scared and angry if you treat him like a person." He looked at Tico. "You want the fish or not?"

Tico ate the fish, looked extremely happy for about one second, then remembered he was a robber and scowled, which on a toucan is mostly beak.

"*Mm,*" said Rosa. "Good. Barnaby is right and Barnaby is also about to be wrong. Barnaby — do you *trust* him now?"

"He ate my fish."

"Do you *trust* him."

Barnaby looked at the toucan, and at the LOOT bag leaking cocoa onto Herschel's floor, and thought about it honestly, because he always did.

"…No," he said. "He's still the robber."

"*That's* the lesson. You be human *to* him. You do not *believe* him. Kindness is not the same as trust." Rosa shifted her weight — still on one leg. "Nobody in this room is his friend. Everybody in this room is polite."

---

"Okay!" said Tico, consulting a crumpled paper. "Now the robber gets NERVOUS and makes everybody MOVE. Everybody! Against the — Rosa, where do I put them?"

"Wherever you want, Tico. You're the robber."

"Against the WALL!"

And here is where Aiyu did the second smart thing of the afternoon.

Everybody got up — slowly, hands visible, no sudden moves. And Aiyu, small and see-through, drifted — not fast, not obviously — to the end of the line nearest the fireplace. The one with the stone wall. The one six steps from the kitchen door.

She sat down low, behind Taro, who was large, and Estrella, who was larger and whose shell was extremely thick. Nobody noticed. That was the point.

"Aiyu," said Rosa. "Tell the room what you just did."

"It's stone," said Aiyu. "It's closest to the kitchen door. And Estrella's in front of me. He said *against the wall.* He didn't say *which part.*"

"Nobody ever does," said Rosa. "When they let you choose *anything* — where to sit, where to stand — you choose low, near something solid, near a way out. Quietly. Like it's an accident." She looked at Estrella, who had not moved a flipper in twenty minutes. "Estrella. Are you alive?"

"Follow the moon home," said Estrella, without opening her eyes, which was a yes.

---

Here is the hardest part. Rosa had warned them.

After the wall, nothing happened.

Tico paced. He fixed his hat. The hat slid off. He fixed it again. He handed out two more rating cards. And the room *waited*, and it was the longest ten minutes Aiyu had ever sat through, because every part of her wanted to *do something.*

*The kitchen door is right there. He's looking the other way. If I went now—*

Taro's hand — big, purple, dusty — came to rest on the table beside her. Not on her. Just beside her. Steady.

"**等** (děng)," he said. "Wait."

"But he's not looking—"

"He is not looking. He is not *gone.* Those are different." Taro's voice was a warm rumble. "A door six steps away is a door you *know about.* Keep knowing about it. When the moment comes — a real one, not a wish — you'll be ready. Until then—" he breathed, in, out "—you steam."

"What does a real moment look like?"

"He leaves the room. Something loud happens that is not you. Someone whose job it is comes through a door." Taro's glasses caught the fire. "It does not look like *he turned his head for a second.* That's a trap you build for yourself."

Aiyu sat. She steamed. She knew about the door. She did not go.

---

And then something loud happened that was not her.

The front door of the Cocoa Hut opened, and a voice came through it that Aiyu knew — level, slow, enormous.

"FROSTPEAK POLICE. EVERYBODY DOWN. HANDS OUT WHERE I CAN SEE THEM."

Sergeant Solveig Snowmane filled the doorway.

And this was the part Rosa had said mattered *most*, more than the wall, more than the waiting: *what you do when the good guys come.*

Because every part of you wants to jump up and run *toward* them. Point at the robber. Be a hero *now* that it's safe.

*Don't,* Rosa had said. *They see a room full of shapes, and one of them is bad, and they have half a second to figure out which. You get DOWN. Hands open. You do exactly what they say — even if they treat you like the robber for a minute. That minute is them keeping everyone alive. Give it to them.*

Aiyu got down. Flat. Hands out. Estrella had, in some sense, been down the whole time. Taro lowered himself like a landslide. Boba went flat, which for a round person is an achievement. Finnegan hit the floor so fast he bounced.

"YOU," said Solveig, pointing at Finnegan. "Don't move."

"I'm not the robber!" Finnegan squeaked.

"Don't. Move."

Finnegan didn't move. It was, Rosa would tell him later, *perfect.*

The only one standing was a toucan in a tiny hat, holding a leaking paper bag.

"Oh," said Tico. "Right. That's me." He raised both wings. "THE ROBBER SURRENDERS. Also — Sergeant — if you have a moment afterward—"

"*Tico.*"

---

Herschel made real cocoa afterward. The robber sat in the corner reading his rating cards. ("Four stars. *Four.* 'Robber talked too much.' Who *wrote* this?" "Everyone, Tico.")

Rosa stood before the fire on one leg and did her not-quite-a-speech.

"Today nobody burst. Nobody kneed. Nobody was a hero. And every one of you is going home. That's the whole lesson. Stay calm. Do what they say. Be grey. Watch everything — Aiyu, how many doors?"

"Two. He never stood between us and the kitchen."

"How many robbers?"

"One. He kept looking at the door like he expected another."

"What was he wearing?"

"A hat that doesn't fit. And the beak."

"That," said Rosa, "is what the police need afterward. Not a hero. A *witness.* Someone small and quiet who *saw.*" She turned. "Barnaby. Human?"

"Human."

"Trust?"

"Never."

"Taro. When do you move?"

"When the moment is real. Not when I wish it were."

"And when the police come?"

The whole room, together, from the floor where several of them were still lying for fun: "*DOWN. Hands open.*"

"**¡Dale!**" said Rosa — *that's it, go!* — and blew her whistle once, softly. "…And then, shrimp."

Barnaby had, of course, brought shrimp.

---

Aiyu walked back to the boba stand with Taro as the snow came down soft over the lake. She was tired in a strange way — not from doing anything, but from *not* doing anything, on purpose, for a very long time. Somewhere behind them, Finnegan was still being a chair, in case Rosa was grading.

"Taro," she said. "It felt like I did nothing."

"You did the hardest thing there is," said Taro. "You waited, with your eyes open. Most people can run. Fewer can fight. Almost nobody can *wait* — and the ones who can, go home."

Aiyu thought about the fireplace, and the door she'd known about the whole time, and the word *děng.*

"Good things take time to steam," she said.

"They do," said Taro. "And so do you."

That night Aiyu slept the way a jelly cube sleeps when it has held perfectly still for an hour and finally, finally doesn't have to — soft, and clear, and all the way through.

## Goodnight Blessing

Be small tonight, little one. Be still. Be grey as a pillow, as a chair, as a soft dark room.
You don't have to be brave right now. You don't have to watch the door.
The grown-ups have the doors. You have the blanket.
Wait for morning. It always comes. Sleep.

## 🛡️ What We Learned

- **If you're ever caught in a robbery or held somewhere:** stay calm, do what you're told, and keep your hands where they can be seen. No sudden moves.
- **Be grey.** Not the loud one, not the hero, not the one who stares. Be a chair. The one who tries to be a hero is the one who gets people hurt.
- **Watch everything** without staring: how many of them, what they look like, what they say, where the doors are. Afterward, police need a *witness*, not a hero.
- **Be human, but don't trust.** A soft voice, a name, offering something small can calm a scary person down. It never makes them your friend.
- **If you get to choose where to be:** sit low, near something solid, near a way out — quietly, like it's an accident.
- **等 (děng) — wait.** A real moment is when they leave, or something loud happens that isn't you, or the police arrive. "He looked away for a second" is not a moment.
- **When police come: get DOWN, hands open, don't run toward them.** Do exactly what they say — even if they treat you like a suspect for a minute. That minute keeps everyone alive.
