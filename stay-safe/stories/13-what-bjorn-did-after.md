# Story 13: What Bjorn Did After

**Main Character:** Bjorn Bigpaws (Polar Bear)
**Guest Characters:** Willa Wobble, Sergeant Solveig Snowmane, Counselor Kumquat (new), Cleo Coldwater, Barnaby Beaksworth, Auntie Hù, Herschel the Walrus
**Setting:** The Frostpeak rink at the bottom of the race run, on the afternoon of the Valley Downhill Cup — and afterward, a bench outside the lodge with two cocoas and one very small briefcase
**Theme:** Stopping a bad thing is only half the job. What you do in the ten minutes after is the other half.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** **報警** (bàojǐng) = call the police
**Safety Skill:** Lawful self-defense — only as much as it takes, stop when the threat stops, never chase, call 911 first, give your full statement with a lawyer

---

Bjorn Bigpaws did not fall at the finish line of the Valley Downhill Cup. He descended with style.

It was, to be fair, a *lot* of style. His forest-green skis went one way, his enormous white body went another, and a spray of snow went up so high that Captain Prism the narwhal said "Magnificent," and meant it. Bjorn came to rest sitting upright on the rink with his goggles on backward and his dignity, somehow, intact.

"Third place!" shouted Cleo Coldwater, sketching furiously. "Bjorn, you got *third!*"

"I was aiming for third," said Bjorn, who had been aiming for first.

Willa Wobble came in fourth, half a second behind him, on a purple snowboard the size of a dinner plate. She had never placed in a real race before in her life. She was so happy she forgot to stop and skidded gently into Bjorn's lap.

"Fourth," she whispered. "Bjorn. *Fourth.*"

"The smallest step," said Bjorn gravely, adjusting his backward goggles, "is apparently very fast."

And that would have been the whole story — a nice one, about a bear and a penguin and a snow spray — if the racer who came in *fifth* had been a better sport.

---

He was a grown-up. That's the first thing to know. A big lodge guest on rented skis, red in the face from losing, who had told everyone at breakfast that he was going to win. By the time he reached the rink he'd decided whose fault fifth place was.

"*You,*" he said, pointing a ski pole at Willa. "You cut me off at the second gate. You *cut me off.*"

"I — I didn't—" said Willa. She hadn't. Everyone had seen. Cleo had *drawn* it.

"Little cheater," said the man, and he skated up, and he shoved her.

It wasn't a bump. It was a two-handed shove, the kind that puts a small penguin flat on her back on the ice with the wind knocked out of her.

The whole rink went quiet the way a room does when something has just gone wrong.

Bjorn stood up.

Here is a thing you should know about Bjorn Bigpaws. He was the biggest creature in Frostpeak Valley by a wide margin. He could have picked up that man, skis and all, and put him on the roof of the lodge. And every bit of him — every bear-shaped inch — *wanted to.*

He didn't. He stepped, instead, between the man and Willa. Just that. One big white wall, standing on the ice.

"**夠了** (gòu le)," said Bjorn quietly. "That's enough. Walk away."

"Or *what*, teddy bear?"

And the man swung his fist at Bjorn's face.

---

What happened next took about two seconds, and the story is going to slow it down, because the two seconds are not the important part but you should see them anyway.

Bjorn caught the man's arm — not hard, just *stopped* it, the way you'd catch a door swinging shut. He pushed, once, with his shoulder. The man's rented skis did what rented skis do, which is go in two directions at the same time, and he sat down on the ice, hard, and Bjorn put one great paw flat on his chest and held him there.

"Stay down," said Bjorn.

The man stayed down, breathing hard and saying words the story won't repeat. The fight had gone out of him the way air goes out of a balloon.

And here is the important part. Here is the *whole story*, right here:

Bjorn took his paw off.

He stood up. He stepped back — two full steps, three — until there was a good wide stretch of ice between them. He did not push the man again to make a point. He did not hold him down a few extra seconds because it would feel good. When the man scrambled up and skated off toward the parking lot, still shouting over his shoulder, Bjorn did not follow. Not one step.

"He's *leaving!*" said Barnaby, outraged, waving a fish. "Bjorn! He's getting *away!*"

"Let him," said Bjorn.

"But—"

"Barnaby," said Bjorn, without looking away from Willa, who was sitting up now, "the moment I chase him, *I'm* the one attacking *him.*" He took off his backward goggles at last and knelt down. "Willa. Look at me. Are you hurt?"

"I'm okay," said Willa, shakily. "I'm okay. He just — he *shoved* me."

"He did," said Bjorn. And then he pulled out his phone and did the thing that turned out to be the hardest thing he did all day.

He called 911 himself — **報警** (bàojǐng), *call the police* — *first.* Before the man could.

---

"Frostpeak emergency."

Bjorn's voice was shaking a little, which surprised him. His paws were shaking too. He kept them where he could see them.

"My name is Bjorn Bigpaws. I'm at the Frostpeak rink, bottom of the race run. A man attacked a child and then swung at me. I stopped him. He's leaving on foot toward the parking lot — tall, red jacket, rented skis. I need police, and I think the child needs to be looked at. I'll give a full statement."

"Are you hurt, sir?"

"No."

"Is the attacker still there?"

"No. He left. I didn't follow."

"Stay where you are. Officers are on the way."

He hung up. Cleo, beside him, had stopped drawing and was staring at him.

"You called it in on *yourself,*" she said.

"I called it in on *him,*" said Bjorn. "Cleo, in a few minutes an officer is going to ask what happened. You *saw*. Barnaby saw. Both of you stay right here." He looked up. "And Herschel's roof camera was pointed at the finish line the whole time. Somebody tell him not to touch it."

"Why does it matter who called?" asked Willa in a small voice.

Bjorn hesitated, because he wasn't sure how to explain it. He didn't have to. Somebody else did.

---

"Because," said a very small, very sharp voice near Bjorn's left boot, "the first one to call is the *victim.* The one who runs off is the *suspect.* And right now, out there in that parking lot, a large angry man is figuring out how to call the police on *you.*"

Everyone looked down.

Standing on the ice was a kumquat.

She was a glossy orange kumquat about the size of a large marble, and she wore tiny round glasses, and she carried a black briefcase the size of a matchbox. She had, apparently, been in the cocoa line the entire time.

"Counselor Kumquat," she said, and stuck out a hand roughly the size of a sesame seed. "Public defender. I saw everything, which is lucky for you, because *I* can't be your witness, but I can be your lawyer, and you're going to need one in about—" she checked a watch that nobody could see "—four minutes. Say less. Call me. Actually you don't have to call me, I'm right here."

"I don't need a lawyer," said Bjorn, baffled. "I didn't *do* anything. He swung at *me.*"

"Mm-hm. And you're going to say that, exactly like that, when the sergeant walks up?" Kumquat pushed her glasses up. "Bjorn. Big fella. You put a grown man on the ice and held him there. You did it *right* — I watched you, I'd frame it — but you *did it.* And the most dangerous thing in the world right now is a nice bear with a good heart *explaining* — with his paws shaking, ten seconds after his heart was going a thousand beats a minute. That is how good bears get in trouble."

---

Sergeant Solveig Snowmane arrived exactly when Kumquat said she would.

She skated up slowly, the way she did everything, and looked at the scene — Willa on the ice, Bjorn standing three steps away with his paws open and visible, two penguin witnesses, and one kumquat.

"Bjorn," she said.

"Sergeant."

"Tell me what happened."

And Bjorn Bigpaws, who two minutes earlier would have told her *everything* in a big shaky rush, looked at the kumquat by his boot and said, instead, the five things she had just drilled into him.

"A man attacked Willa — shoved her down. Then he swung at me. I stopped him. He left toward the parking lot, I didn't follow. I called it in." He pointed. "Cleo and Barnaby saw the whole thing. Herschel's roof camera was on the finish line." He took a breath. "I'll give you a full statement, Sergeant. With my lawyer present."

There was a pause.

Willa held her breath. She was *sure* this was the moment the sergeant would narrow her eyes and say *why do you need a lawyer if you didn't do anything?*

Solveig nodded.

"Good," she said. "That's the smart thing. That's what I'd tell my own cubs to say." She wrote something in her notebook. "Slow hands, honest words, and a lawyer for the details. You did this exactly right, Bjorn. Sit down before you fall down."

Bjorn sat down.

"Wait," said Willa. "*You're* telling him it's okay to not talk to you?"

"I'm telling him," said Solveig, "that I want the *true* story, and the true story comes out better *slow.*" She looked over at the parking lot, where a man in a red jacket was, at that very moment, waving at another officer and pointing furiously at the rink. "Excuse me. Somebody's about to tell me a *different* story. I'd like to hear it."

---

Later — after the man in the red jacket had told his different story, and Cleo's sketch and Herschel's camera had told the *true* one, and he'd been driven away to answer for what he'd done — Bjorn sat on the bench outside the lodge with two cocoas and a kumquat.

"I still don't understand," he said. "I was *right.* Why did being right need a lawyer?"

Kumquat sipped from a thimble.

"Let me ask you something. How many times did he swing at you?"

"Once."

"Cleo's drawing says twice. Herschel's camera says twice. He swung, you caught it, he tried again on the way down." She let that sit. "And how far away were you from Willa when you stepped in?"

"Right beside her."

"Eight feet. Camera." Kumquat set down the thimble. "You're not lying, Bjorn. You're *remembering wrong.* Everybody does. When something scary happens, your body pours a bucket of fizzy juice into your brain to help you move fast, and the juice is *terrible* for memory. Order gets scrambled. Distances shrink. Numbers go funny. You'd have said *once, right beside her* to the sergeant, with your whole honest heart — and then the camera would say *twice, eight feet* — and now the nice bear has a *story that doesn't match.* You see?"

Bjorn saw.

"A lawyer isn't for hiding," said Kumquat. "A lawyer is a *translator.* You tell me what you remember, slowly, when the juice has worn off. I help you tell the sergeant the *true* thing *carefully*, so it comes out the way it actually happened. Innocent people need that *more* than guilty ones do. Guilty ones already know what happened." She shrugged her tiny shoulders. "The rules here in the valley — and in most of America — say you can protect yourself or somebody else, only while it's happening, only as much as it takes, and you stop the second it stops. You did all three. My *job* is making sure the paperwork agrees with you. Ask a grown-up how it works where you live. It's a little different everywhere. The stopping part isn't."

---

Willa found them there as the sun went down. Nora had declared her bruised, brave, and in need of a very large dinner. She climbed up between the bear and the kumquat and swung her feet.

"Bjorn," she said. "Thank you for standing in front of me."

"You're welcome."

"And thank you for… stopping. After." She thought about it. "I think that was harder."

Bjorn looked out at the rink, where the last light was going gold on the ice. "It was," he admitted. "Every part of me wanted one more push. Just one. To make a point."

"What stopped you?"

"Auntie Hù says the fight isn't over when the punching stops. It's over when you're *safe* — and when the paperwork's done." He picked up his cocoa. "I could win the punching part easily. I'm a bear. But the paperwork part? A bear who gives one more push loses that part every time."

"So you didn't fight," said Willa.

"I did not fight," said Bjorn Bigpaws, with enormous dignity. "I stood between. With *style.*"

Kumquat, from the far end of the bench, did not look up from her thimble.

"Say less," she said.

## Goodnight Blessing

Big paws, little one — and gentle ones.
Strong enough to stand in front. Wise enough to step back.
The fight is over. The paperwork is done. Everyone is safe.
Put your hands where you can see them — on the blanket, soft and open. Sleep.

## 🛡️ What We Learned

- **You may protect yourself or someone else** — but only while the danger is happening or clearly about to, and **only as much as it takes** to make it stop.
- **Stop when the threat stops.** Not one more push. The moment they're down or leaving, you step back. Extra hits turn you from the protector into the attacker.
- **Never chase.** The one who follows becomes the aggressor. Let them go — that's what the police are for.
- **Call 911 first.** The first caller is usually written down as the victim. The one who leaves is the suspect. Don't let the bad guy make the call before you.
- **Say the basics, then stop:** "I was attacked at [place]. I need police and medical. I'll give a full statement." Point out **witnesses** and **cameras**. Don't narrate, don't apologize, never say "I wanted to."
- **For details: "I'll give a full statement with my lawyer."** That's not suspicious — a good officer will tell you it's smart. Scared brains remember wrong (order, distance, how many times). A lawyer helps you tell the *true* story *carefully*.
- The rules are a little different everywhere. **Ask a grown-up how they work where you live.** The stopping part is the same everywhere.
