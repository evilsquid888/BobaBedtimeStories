# Story 6: The Turtle: Protect Your Head

**Main Character:** Flan the Coconut Flan (coconut custard) and Estrella the Sea Turtle
**Guest Characters:** Bùdīng, Coach Rosa the Flamingo, Herschel the Walrus, Tico the Toucan, Teddy & Tilda Powderpuff, Auntie Hù
**Setting:** The Pink Belt Academy in the lodge's boot-drying room, then the snowy back deck of Herschel's Cocoa Hut, where a snowball cannon has been built "for science"
**Theme:** Everything else heals. Your head is the one thing you can't replace — so you cover it, and you keep moving.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** **保護** (bǎohù) = protect
**Safety Skill:** The shell — forearms frame the head, chin down, elbows in; move while covered toward the exit; never turn your back, never freeze

---

When Flan met Bùdīng, the two of them wobbled at each other for a full minute without saying a word.

Flan was a coconut flan, fresh off the mail-boat from Costa Rica that very morning — a jiggly, pale-gold dome of custard glistening under a caramel drizzle, flecked with toasted coconut. Bùdīng was a caramel pudding — golden on top, dark caramel dripping down his sides, and wobbling, always, even standing still. They stood in the doorway of the boot-drying room and looked at each other the way two people look when each has spent his whole life thinking he was the only one.

"You…" said Flan.

"You…" said Bùdīng.

"You *wobble.*"

"I'm fine! I'm totally fine! …I'm wobbling."

"*Steady, steady,*" whispered Flan, in awe. "*Ooh, wobbly.*"

Then a snowball came through the open window and hit Flan directly on top of the head, and his whole dome snapped forward, back, left, right, and around like a bobblehead on a bumpy road.

"FOR SCIENCE!" shouted Herschel the Walrus, from somewhere outside.

---

The snowball had come from a cannon.

The cannon was made of a stovepipe, two sled runners, a bicycle pump, and Herschel's enormous enthusiasm. It sat on the back deck of the Cocoa Hut, pointed at the boot-room window, and it could launch a snowball about as hard as a mean fifth-grader could throw one — which was exactly what Coach Rosa had asked for.

"*¡Perfecto!*" said Rosa, which means *perfect*, and which she said to most things. She was a seven-foot pink flamingo standing on one leg in the snow with a pink headband and a tiny whistle, and she had been awake for nearly the whole demonstration. "Flan. Flan, come here. How's your head?"

Flan wobbled out onto the deck. His head was still going. "It's — it's *steady*, it's — ooh. Wobbly."

"Mm-hm. That's the lesson." Rosa pointed a wing at him. "Today, my little custards, we learn the most important thing I will ever teach you. More important than knees. More important than shrimp. *Almost.*" She tapped the side of her own long pink head with one feather. "**Protect this.**"

"My head?" said Flan.

"Everybody's head. Everything else on you heals. A bumped knee heals. A scraped arm heals. A wobbled *dome*—" she poked him gently and he jiggled for eleven seconds "—heals. But the thing inside your head is the one thing you get *one* of, and there is no shop that sells another. **保護** (bǎohù). *Protect.* Everything you learn in this academy is really just a fancy way of protecting your head and getting it home."

"How?" said Bùdīng, who had crept out onto the deck behind Flan and was wobbling in sympathy.

Rosa smiled and looked out at the frozen lake.

"I brought an expert."

---

Estrella came up out of the ice-hole the way she did everything — slowly, gracefully, as if the whole world had agreed to wait for her. She was a green sea turtle with a mossy shell patterned like a scatter of stars, and kind ancient eyes, and she had come north on the mail-boat in a tank of warm water because she had heard there were children who needed to learn about shells.

"Follow the moon home," she said, which was how she said hello.

"Estrella," said Rosa. "Show them."

The turtle didn't say anything. She simply looked at Herschel. Herschel, delighted, pumped the bicycle pump nine times and fired.

The snowball flew straight at Estrella's face.

Estrella's head went *in.*

Not far. Not dramatically. Her head simply drew back inside the rim of her shell. The snowball hit the shell with a soft *pomf* and fell apart into powder. Then Estrella's head came back out, and she blinked, and said:

"The face is *inside.*"

Flan's dome stopped wobbling for the first time all morning. He was too amazed to wobble.

"A turtle doesn't get hit in the face," said Estrella, "because the face is not where the hitting is. The shell is where the hitting is. And the shell does not mind."

"But I don't *have* a shell," said Flan. "I'm custard."

"Everyone has a shell," said Estrella. "You just have to *make* it."

---

Here is how you make a shell. Coach Rosa taught it with her wings, and it's the same for a custard, a pudding, a penguin, or you.

"**Hands up.**" Rosa's wings came up, and Flan's wobbly arms came up. "Higher. *On* your head. One hand on the back of your skull, one on the side, like you're holding your own head so it doesn't roll away."

"It might," said Flan honestly.

"**Forearms in front.**" Her wings came together in front of her face like two pink pillars. "Frame your face with your arms. Elbows *in* — pointing down, close together. That's the front wall."

"**Chin down.**" She tucked her long neck. "Your chin hides behind your arms. Your eyes peek over the top. You can still *see.* You *must* still see."

"**Elbows in front of your belly.**" The bottom of the wall.

Flan stood there on the deck, arms up, hands on his own head, elbows tucked, chin down, peeking out from between his forearms. He looked like a very small, very nervous, very golden turtle.

He also looked like a bobblehead who had finally been given something to hold on to.

"Herschel," said Rosa. "Gently. For science."

*Pomf.*

The snowball hit Flan's forearms and fell apart into powder.

Flan's head did not snap forward. It did not snap back. His dome jiggled — a little, a *custard* amount — and then settled. Behind his own arms, in the small warm cave he'd made, his head was *fine.*

"I…" said Flan. "I didn't wobble. I mean I *did.* But my head didn't *go* anywhere."

"The shell wobbles *with* you," said Estrella softly. "That is what a shell is for."

---

Naturally, Bùdīng had to try.

"I'm fine," he announced, planting his boots, hands up, elbows in, chin down, eyes peeking out. "I'm fine. I'm *actually* fine. I'm in a *shell.*"

*Pomf.* Snowball. Forearms. Powder.

"I'm FINE!" Bùdīng shrieked, delighted, still wobbling like a pudding on a paint shaker but with his entire head safe and sound inside a fortress of his own arms. "Look at me! I'm wobbling and I'm FINE! Both things! At the same time!"

Then Teddy and Tilda Powderpuff wanted to try, and made a *double* shell by standing back to back with their arms up. Tico the Toucan flew down from the roof to announce it — "AND IN THE SHELL, FOLKS, TWO CUBS, ONE DEFENSE, ZERO FACES" — and Herschel got so excited he fired three snowballs at once. One of them hit Tico, who did not have his shell up, and whose beak went *bonk* on the railing.

"Ow," said Tico. "Beak the news: that's why you cover."

"Mm," said Auntie Hù, from the rail, where she had been the entire time and nobody had noticed, which was her preferred way of being anywhere. "The bird learns."

---

"Now," said Rosa, when everyone had a shell and the deck was covered in snowball powder. "The hard part."

She put her own shell up — wings framing her long pink face — and then she did something none of them expected. She *walked.*

Slowly, with her shell up, she walked across the deck toward the lodge door, while Herschel fired snowballs at her one after another. *Pomf. Pomf. Pomf.* They hit her wings. They hit her shoulders. She did not stop. She did not turn around. She did not drop her wings to swat at them. She walked, shell up, chin down, eyes peeking forward, until she reached the door. *Then*, and only then, she dropped her wings, turned her head sharply left and right, and stepped through.

"That," she said, from the doorway, "is the whole thing."

"Walking?" said Flan.

"*Moving.*" Rosa came back and crouched down to custard height. "Here's what happens to most people when something scary comes at their head. They freeze. Or they turn their back and curl up. Or they put their arms *out* to push it away and leave their whole face open." She shook her head. "No. Shell up, and *go.* Toward the door. Toward the grown-up. Toward the light. A covered head that is standing still is a covered head that gets hit *again.* A covered head that is *moving* is a head that gets home."

"Never turn your back?" said Bùdīng.

"Never. The back of your head has no arms in front of it. You keep your face toward the trouble — *behind your shell* — and you back up, or you angle away, and you *keep going.* And when you get where you're going—" she turned her head sharply left, right "—you *look around.* Scan. Then you're done."

"*Tranquilo,*" she added, which means *easy, calm*, and she said it to Flan specifically, because he had started wobbling again just thinking about it. "It's a shell. Turtles do it every day and they don't even have arms. You have *arms.* You're ahead."

---

They practiced until the light went gold. Flan walked the deck with his shell up while Herschel plinked snowballs off his forearms and Rosa called "*¡Dale!* Keep going!" from the rail. Each time he reached the door he dropped his arms, scanned left, scanned right, and stepped through. And each time he stepped through, he was a little less wobbly and a little more *Flan.*

On the last run, Herschel — who could not help himself — fired the cannon at *maximum science*, and the snowball that hit Flan's forearms was a big one, and his whole custard body shivered like a bell.

His head didn't move an inch.

"STEADY," said Flan, from inside his shell, in a voice he'd never used before. "Steady… steady…" He reached the door. He dropped his arms. He scanned. "…*ooh.*" He grinned an enormous caramel grin. "*Not* wobbly."

Estrella, down in the ice-hole, closed her ancient eyes with pleasure.

"…And then," said Coach Rosa, "*shrimp.*"

There was, in fact, shrimp. Barnaby had a bucket. Nobody asked where it came from.

---

That night Flan lay in a hammock on the mail-boat, which rocked gently in its hole in the lake ice. Bùdīng was in the next hammock over — he'd asked to sleep aboard, and nobody had asked why. The two of them wobbled softly in the dark, not from fear, just from being who they were.

"Bùdīng?" whispered Flan.

"Mm-hm?"

"I always thought the wobble was the problem. Like — if I could just be *steady*, I'd be safe."

"Me too."

"But it wasn't the wobble. It was my *head* going wherever the wobble went." Flan put his hands, very slowly, on his own head in the dark — one on the back, one on the side. "Now I've got somewhere to put it."

"The shell wobbles with you," said Bùdīng, quoting a sea turtle, which was a sentence he had never expected to say in his life.

"**保護,**" said Flan sleepily. "*Protect.*"

And he took his hands off his head, because you don't need a shell in bed — the pillow is the softest shell there is — and folded his arms across his custard chest, and wobbled once, gently, and slept.

## Goodnight Blessing

Hands down now, little one. The pillow is your shell tonight.
Everything else heals — but this head, this one head, we keep it safe, and it is safe.
Chin down. Eyes closed. Nothing coming.
Wobble if you want to. The blanket wobbles with you. Sleep.

## 🛡️ What We Learned

- **Your head is the one thing you can't replace.** Everything else heals. Protect your head first.
- **The shell:** one hand on the back of your head, one on the side; forearms up framing your face; elbows tucked in and down; chin down; eyes peeking over the top so you can still see.
- **A turtle doesn't get hit in the face because the face is inside.** Make the hitting land on your arms, not your head.
- **Shell up and MOVE** — toward the door, the grown-up, the light. A covered head that stands still gets hit again. A covered head that's moving gets home.
- **Never turn your back** — the back of your head has no arms in front of it. Keep your face toward the trouble, behind your shell, and angle away.
- **Don't freeze, don't curl up, don't reach out to push** — all three leave your head open.
- **When you get there, SCAN.** Look left, look right. Then you're done.
