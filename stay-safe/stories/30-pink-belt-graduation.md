# Story 30: Pink Belt Graduation

**Main Character:** Everyone — Boba narrates, Coach Rosa the Flamingo runs the show
**Guest Characters:** Auntie Hù, the whole Taiwan crew, the whole Frostpeak crew (Piper, Barnaby, Willa, Finnegan, Cleo, Bjorn, Nora, Solveig, Teddy & Tilda, Magnus, Aurora, Herschel), the whole Costa Rica crew (Tato, Coco, Tico, Slowy, Estrella, Gallo & Pinto, Cajeta, Flan, Lala, Don Café), Counselor Kumquat
**Setting:** The last dusk of the season — the frozen lake below the Frostpeak lodge, sixteen snow-and-lantern stations in a line across the ice, and two boats waiting at the dock: the mail-boat home to Pura Vida Town, and the Taiwan crew's ferry
**Theme:** You don't graduate from being safe. You just get to carry it lightly.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** **畢業** (bìyè) = graduate
**Safety Skill:** The whole Krav track in one run — stance, escapes, the shell, knees, the rack, bursting, distance, the fence, the station, cover, the pop-up, the handle, the ties, off the line, be grey, SCAN

---

The Boba on Ice sign was down. The stand was boxed. The tapioca vat was empty — Boba and Piper had refilled it *and* emptied it, which Piper called the most pointless noble act in valley history.

And yet, on the last dusk of the season, nobody had gone to the boats.

Because across the frozen lake, from the lodge steps to the far dock, someone had built sixteen little stations out of snow and lanterns and rope and, in one case, a cocoa chest. Each had a hand-painted sign with one line on it. And at the first station, asleep on one leg with a pink headband slipping over one eye, stood a seven-foot flamingo.

"Is she—" whispered Willa.

"She's asleep," said Boba.

"Standing up?"

"It's a flamingo thing."

Tico the Toucan flew a low circle and announced, at a volume that woke three seals and a narwhal: "LADIES AND GENTLE-CREATURES! WELCOME TO THE FIRST, LAST, AND ONLY PINK BELT ACADEMY GRADUATION! IN THIS CORNER — EVERYBODY!"

Coach Rosa woke up mid-sentence, the way she always did.

"—and *then* shrimp. Where am I. Excellent." She blinked at the assembled crowd — penguins, bears, desserts, a sloth, a very small kumquat — and clapped her wings once. "**¡Dale!**" (*DAH-lay* — *let's go!*) "You are all graduating tonight. Nobody asked me. I decided."

"What belt do we get?" asked Finnegan.

"Pink."

"We already *have* pink."

"Then you already graduated. Congratulations. Line up."

---

The idea was simple: run station to station, do the thing on the sign, Rosa blows her tiny whistle, move on. Sixteen stations. One long winter, folded small enough to run through in ten minutes.

The reality was that forty-one people tried to run it at the same time.

**Station 1 — TWO FEET DOWN. HANDS UP.**

Everyone planted their feet and put their hands up like they were saying *whoa.* Bīng did it so dramatically his sunglasses fell off. Rosa, inspecting, stopped in front of Slowy the Sloth, who was on one leg.

"Two feet, Slowy."

"Puuura… viiida…" said Slowy, and lowered the second leg over the course of about nine seconds.

"Mm," said Auntie Hù, from the rim of her tea-egg pot, which had somehow been dragged onto the ice. The *good* Mm.

**Station 2 — AGAINST THE THUMB.**

Mochi, the designated attacker, grabbed every wrist that went past and apologized to each. "Sorry! Sorry! Go against the thumb — SORRY!" Cajeta bounced out of her grip without touching the ice. Willa dropped, rolled, and came up on the far side. "Small people go down and out!" — the loudest thing she'd said all winter. Nora Snowmane put a paw over her heart.

**Station 3 — SHELL.**

Estrella the Sea Turtle stood beside Herschel's snowball cannon. "Forearms up. Chin down. Keep moving." *Thoomp.* Forty-one shelled heads walked through a blizzard of snowballs and not one face got hit. Flan wobbled the whole way saying "Steady — I'm *steady!*" in pure astonishment, and Bùdīng beside him said "I'm fine. I'm actually fine," and for the first time all season everyone believed him instantly.

---

**Station 4 — HEAVY KNEES.**

Señor Saco, the heavy bag, waited in his sombrero. Tato the Patacón, exactly the right height, gave him three knees and a hammer-fist and then — the part Rosa watched for — *stopped.* Switch off. Scan. Run.

"Smash now, worry never!" said Tato, and Señor Saco's sombrero fell off, and everyone bowed to it, because that was the rule now.

**Station 5 — A RACK IS A "DON'T."**

Just an empty wooden rack, and Magnus Meltsworth beside it with his arms folded, glowering at the sunset.

"What do we do?" asked Teddy.

"Nothing," rumbled Magnus. "You *don't.* That's the station. Walk past it."

"That's *it?*"

"That's the whole technique. Zero moves." He looked, briefly, at his own paws. "I locked mine up. Both cabins. Don't tell anyone."

"We didn't mean to hear that—" said Tilda.

"—but we're telling everyone," said Teddy, and they ran.

**Station 6 — BURST.**

Herschel's pool noodle swung. Zhīma, *born* for this station, went in along the arm like a golden cannonball, wrapped, kneed, took the noodle, threw it forty feet, scanned. Behind him Bīng burst, tripped on his cape, and collapsed with tremendous dignity.

"THE CAPE," said Bīng, from the ice, "REMAINS A MISTAKE."

**Station 7 — DISTANCE.**

Coco the Granizado held Señor Cuchillo, the rubber knife. The sign said RUN. Everybody ran. Piper held her glittery board up like a shield the whole way, shouting "Stay frosty and FAR!" — and Coco, who'd invented the line, looked prouder than a shaved ice has any right to.

---

**Station 8 — THE FENCE.**

Rosa herself, in a fake mustache, being a Guy On The Street. "Hey. What are *you* looking at?"

Mango's hands came up, palms out, chest height. "Nothing, man, we're good, we're leaving." Gallo and Pinto grabbed him by both arms — *out,* not in — and went sideways through a gap in the snowbanks. The mustache fell off. "**Tranquilo**" (*trahn-KEE-lo* — *easy, easy*), Rosa said to nobody, and stuck it back on for the next one.

**Station 9 — DON'T GO HOME. GO TO THE STATION.**

Dìguā's charcoal truck sat on the ice with Sergeant Solveig leaning against it, drinking cocoa. You ran up, did *not* get out of it (confusing, since you weren't in it), honked the goose horn once — *HWAAANK* — and Solveig said, "I heard you coming."

Don Café, in the passenger seat, murmured "Good things brew slowly" every time the horn went off. It went off forty-one times. He did not tire of it.

**Station 10 — COVER, NOT CONCEALMENT.**

Tato on the left. Lala Tres Leches on the right. *Thoomp.* Everyone behind Tato stayed dry. Everyone behind Lala got gently, thoroughly soaked — "Soak it all in!" — and nobody minded, because the lesson worked *every single time.*

Slowy stood in the open and said "Hmm." A snowball landed on his head and stayed there like a hat.

"Slowy," said Rosa.

"I was… deciding…"

"Decide *behind the patacón.*"

---

**Station 11 — POP UP.**

Yēguǒ had chalked a blue wave on the ice. You lay on it, shelled your head, feet toward the imaginary attacker, kicked twice, and — one hand, one foot, hips up, back leg through — *popped.*

"Duuude," said Yēguǒ, to everyone. "That's the whole ocean right there."

Slowy lay down and everyone, unasked, stopped and waited. Four minutes. Perfect. Rosa cried one pink tear and blamed the wind.

**Station 12 — FIND THE HANDLE.**

Señor Baúl, the cocoa chest, lid shut, Bǎi on top like a queen. "Bet you can find the glow handle with your eyes closed." Each person did. "*That* was a good dare." Herschel, from a distance: "I want my chest back." He did not get it.

**Station 13 — SLAM THE TIES.**

Finnegan had been duct-taped into a burrito again. Nobody knew how. Cleo sketched it. "Arms up. Slam down and out," said Rosa, and Finnegan popped free on the first try and was so surprised he sat down. "I did it *again!*" — as though it were a different achievement.

---

**Station 14 — OFF THE LINE.**

This one, Rosa ran quietly.

Bjorn held Señora Pistola Azul, the blue rubber training gun, pointed at the ice. Nobody announced anything. You walked up, hands up, slow, and gave Bjorn your imaginary wallet — "a wallet is a thing" — and then Rosa said softly, "Now the other one," and you took *one step sideways* off the line and ran zigzag to the snowbank. That was the whole station for the kids.

Finnegan stepped off the line, got confused, and stepped back onto it.

"Finnegan."

"I AM the line—"

"Finnegan."

He stepped off. He ran. Bjorn lowered the blue gun and put it in his pocket and said, in his deep and dignified voice, "You will probably never need this. Learn it anyway."

"Then shrimp," said Rosa, and the station was over.

**Station 15 — BE GREY.**

Tico was the robber. He kept announcing it. "THE ROBBER — that's me — DEMANDS COCOA AND ALSO A REVIEW." You sat low, near the exit, hands visible, as boring as you had ever been. Aiyu sat so still that Tico lost track of her. "WHERE DID THE JELLY GO?" She was two feet in front of him. Taro closed his eyes and appeared to fall asleep, which was also a technique.

Barnaby offered the robber a fish. "There's always enough for one more," he said, and Tico broke character completely and ate it.

**Station 16 — SCAN.**

The last station was a lantern on a pole at the edge of the dock, and the sign said one word.

You ran up. You stopped. You turned your head — left, right, all the way around, like a meerkat, like a jelly cube at a night market, like a pearl who had once run the wrong way up a one-way lane on purpose — and looked for more trouble, and there wasn't any, and looked for the exit, and it was a boat.

And then you were done.

---

They ran it badly and joyfully, an hour instead of ten minutes, and when the last of them — Slowy, naturally — reached the lantern and scanned so slowly the sun finished setting while he did it, the whole lake cheered until snow fell off the lodge roof.

Rosa stood at Station 16 to meet them.

Nobody said anything.

Everybody noticed.

She was standing on two legs.

She had stood on one leg all winter — on the ice, in the boot room, asleep mid-sentence, kneeing Señor Saco across the lake. Now, with the valley in front of her and the boats behind, Coach Rosa stood on two feet, planted, hands up like *whoa*, and looked at forty-one graduates, and did not fall asleep even a little.

Auntie Hù climbed down off the rim of her pot. She walked across the ice — small, brown, dense, cracked like a map — and stopped in front of the flamingo, who had to bend almost in half to see her.

"Mm," said Auntie Hù.

"Then shrimp," said Coach Rosa.

Auntie Hù reached into one of her many pockets and took out a tea egg, still warm, and held it up. Rosa reached into her headband — nobody knew there was anything in her headband — and took out one perfect pink shrimp, and held it down.

They traded.

"I teach them not to be there," said Hù.

"I teach them what to do if they are."

"We are the same egg."

"We are the same shrimp." Rosa straightened up, all seven feet, and put the tea egg in her headband, where it would apparently live now. "**Feliz graduación**" (*feh-LEES grah-doo-ah-SYOHN* — *happy graduation*). "**畢業** (bìyè). You have all graduated. From what, I don't know. Pink forever. Go get on your boats."

---

The mail-boat left first.

The Costa Rica crew crowded the rail — Gallo and Pinto side by side, Tato's arm around Coco, Lala still dripping, Flan wobbling happily, Cajeta bouncing on a coil of rope. Tico perched on the mast and did *not* announce anything, which was how everyone knew he was sad. Slowy boarded last. It took a while.

As the boat crunched out through the ice under a hundred swaying lanterns, they began to sing — something slow in Spanish with *pura vida* in the chorus — so clearly that the penguins on the dock hummed along without knowing a word.

Rosa stood at the bow beside Estrella. On two legs. Then, as the boat reached the dark water beyond the ice, she lifted one foot, tucked it up, and — mid-wave — fell asleep.

"That's the flamingo thing," said Boba, quietly, to Willa.

"I know," said Willa. "I'll miss it."

---

The ferry left last.

The Taiwan crew didn't crowd the rail; they were too tired. They found the blankets Herschel had loaded without telling anyone and lay down in a heap on the warm boards over the engine, and let the lanterns of Frostpeak slide away behind them.

On the dock, growing smaller: a huge white bear, a small blue penguin with her ear-muffs off, a tea egg on the rim of a pot, and a walrus in a hat that had shrunk in the wash, all waving.

Boba was the last one awake. He usually was.

He lay on his back on the deck with his tiny arms folded behind his head and looked up, and the sky was doing the thing it did over the lake at dusk, which was purple, then deep blue, then stars.

He thought about the season. Coats, and code words, and a van, and a gear room, and a lockdown, and a kumquat. A flamingo on one leg, a patacón who came back crunchier, a sloth who got there eventually. All the things he knew now that he hadn't when the banner went up — and how none of them felt heavy. They felt like a coat. The yellow kind. The kind you live in.

And then, without deciding to — out of pure habit — he lifted his head and looked around the deck. Left. Right. All the way around. Friends, blankets, dark water, one lantern, no trouble, an exit that was a whole ocean.

Scan complete.

"Mm," Boba murmured, in nobody's voice but his own, and closed his enormous eyes, and slept.

## Goodnight Blessing

Two feet down, little one — and now, two feet up under the blanket.
Hands up all day; hands folded now.
Attitude, skills, plan — tucked away, like an egg in a headband, like a shrimp in a pocket.
You know the coats. You know the words. You know the handle glows.
So run when you can, fight only when you must, and always, always look around —
then sleep.

## 🛡️ What We Learned

- **Rule zero never changes:** the best fight is the one you're not in. Run first. Fight only when trapped.
- **Two feet down, hands up, chin down, eyes up** — a stance that looks polite and works like a wall.
- **The switch:** ON all at once when you're trapped; OFF the instant you're free. Then run.
- **Hard parts to soft parts, only as much as it takes**, then go.
- **Distance and things-between are your friends**; a wallet is a thing; a truck is a thing; you are not a thing.
- **A rack is a "don't."** Guns get left alone, rooms get left, grown-ups get told.
- **SCAN.** After everything — look for more trouble, look for the exit, then go home.
