# Story 18: Cover vs. Concealment

**Main Character:** Coco the Granizado (Costa Rican shaved ice)
**Guest Characters:** Tato the Patacón, Lala the Tres Leches, Slowy the Sloth, Coach Rosa the Flamingo, Tico the Toucan, Herschel the Walrus, Auntie Hù, Boba, Finnegan Flipsworth
**Setting:** The frozen lake behind the Frostpeak lodge on a bright, squeaky-cold morning — Coach Rosa's Pink Belt Academy has moved outside for the day, and Herschel has built something "for science"
**Theme:** Not everything that hides you protects you. Know the difference before you ever need to.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** **躲** (duǒ) = take cover / hide
**Safety Skill:** If shooting ever starts near you — get low, get moving, get behind COVER (stops things), not CONCEALMENT (only hides you); hands visible when police arrive

---

> *A note for the grown-up reading this: this story says, once and plainly, what to do if shooting ever starts nearby. Snowballs stand in for everything else. Nothing frightening is shown — it's a very silly morning on a frozen lake — but the one plain sentence is the point, so read it together.*

---

Herschel the Walrus had built a cannon.

"For science," he said, patting it.

It was made of a cocoa drum, a bicycle pump, three feet of stovepipe, and the lodge's second-best ladle. It sat on the frozen lake on a little sled, full of snowballs.

Coach Rosa the Flamingo — seven feet of pink, one leg, a headband, and a whistle — looked prouder of it than Herschel did. "The most beautiful teaching tool I have ever seen. *¡Dale!*" — which is Costa Rican for *go on, do it!* — "Fire one."

Herschel pumped. The cannon went *THOOMP.*

The snowball crossed the entire lake in under a second and took the head off a snowman by the far shore. The head sailed another forty feet and took the head off a *second* snowman.

There was a respectful silence.

"Okay," said Rosa. "Everybody line up. Nobody stand where the snowmen were."

---

Coco the Granizado did not want to line up.

Coco was a tall, fluffy mound of rainbow-syruped shaved ice in a paper cone, dusted with powdered milk, wearing tiny round sunglasses. His whole personality was *cool.* He said "tuanis" about everything — *cool, all good, mae* — and had never once been in a hurry.

He was, at this moment, dripping slightly. Not from heat. From the cannon.

"Stay frosty," he told himself. "Stay frosty, stay sweet."

"You are melting, my friend," said Tato the Patacón, who was standing next to him, flat and golden and unbothered. Tato was a twice-fried, twice-smashed disc of green plantain with crispy crinkled edges. He had been flattened by a wooden press, then flattened *again*, and had come out crunchier both times. Very little in the world could worry him.

"I'm not melting," said Coco. "I'm *glistening.*"

"Bīng says that too," said Boba helpfully.

Tico the Toucan landed on the cannon barrel, beak like a rainbow banana. "LADIES AND GENTLE-PENGUINS!" he bellowed, because Tico had never said anything quietly in his life. "IN *THIS* CORNER — A PATACÓN! And in *THAT* corner —" he swung his enormous beak toward a soft pale-gold square of cake standing shyly at the end of the line, dripping sweet milk onto the ice — "A *CAKE!*"

Lala the Tres Leches looked around, as if there might be another cake.

"I'm honored?" she said.

---

"Sit," said Rosa, and everybody sat, and she said the serious thing first — that was how Rosa did it: the serious thing first, then a whole morning of silly to hold it in place.

"Here is the one plain sentence," she said. "If shooting ever starts near you — a street, a market, a parking lot, anywhere — you get low, you get *moving*, and you get behind something that *stops* things. Not something that *hides* you. Something that *stops.* That is the whole lesson. Everything else today is snowballs."

Nobody said anything. Even Tico.

"It is rare," said Rosa, more gently. "You know who told me that? The egg." She nodded at Auntie Hù, on a crate at the edge of the ice with a thermos. "*Rare is not never.* So we practice. Tranquilo — *easy.* We practice with snow. Now. Who knows the difference between *cover* and *concealment?*"

Silence.

"Nobody? Excellent. Tato. Lala. Walk out there. Stand still."

Tato walked out and stood. Lala followed, dripping a little trail of milk, and smoothed her whipped-cream top nervously.

"Herschel," said Rosa. "*¡Dale!*"

*THOOMP.*

---

The snowball hit Tato square in the middle.

And *stopped.*

It didn't bounce. It didn't go through. It hit that twice-smashed golden disc and simply — ended. A puff of snow. Tato blinked once.

"Smash now," said Tato, mildly, "worry never."

"THAT," roared Rosa, "IS *COVER!*"

*THOOMP.*

The second snowball hit Lala.

It did not stop.

It went in one side of that soft, milk-soaked square of cake and — gently, politely, like a guest wiping their feet — right out the other side, and hit the ice behind her with a soft *plump.* Lala was fine. Completely fine. Also considerably wetter, which for a tres leches cake is saying something.

"…Soak it all in," said Lala faintly.

"AND *THAT,*" roared Rosa, "IS *CONCEALMENT!*"

Tico could not contain himself. "THE CAKE IS DOWN — no, the cake is UP — the cake is FINE, folks, but the SNOWBALL WENT *THROUGH!*"

"Somebody get her a towel," said Herschel.

---

Rosa hopped over and put a wing on each of them.

"Look carefully. Tato is dense. He has been through the press twice and he has *substance.* When the snowball meets him, the snowball loses. That is cover." She turned. "Lala is *beautiful.* She *hides* the ice behind her very nicely. But the snowball doesn't care what it can see. It goes where it's going. That is concealment. It hides you. It does not *stop anything.*"

"So what's cover?" said Coco. "In real life. Not — not patacones."

"Good. Say it back to me as I go." Rosa held up her feathers one at a time. "A brick wall. A concrete wall. The *engine end* of a car — the heavy front, where the motor is, down behind the front wheel."

"The engine end of a car," repeated Coco.

"*Not* the doors. Car doors look like cover in movies. They are Lala." Another feather. "A thick tree trunk. A concrete planter. The thick end of things."

"And concealment?"

"Car *doors.* Tables. Bushes. A wooden fence. Thin inside walls. A snowbank. A snowman, as we have learned. All of those *hide* you. None of them *stop* anything. Hiding beats the open — but if you can get to *cover*, you get to cover."

"How do you know which is which?" asked Boba. "In a hurry?"

"You ask yourself one question," said Rosa. "*Would I hide behind this from a truck?*" She let that sit. "A bush? No. A table? No. A brick wall? Yes. The front of a parked car? Yes. That's your answer. **躲** (duǒ) — *take cover.* Not *hide.* *Cover.*"

---

"Now the *moving* part," said Rosa, "because standing still behind cover is only half a plan. Slowy!"

Everyone turned.

Slowy the Sloth was standing in the exact middle of the lake, in the open, with nothing near him but sky, looking at the cannon with a gentle, thoughtful expression.

"Hmmmmm," said Slowy.

"Slowy," said Rosa, "what are you doing?"

"Thiiiinking," said Slowy, "about… which… way…"

*THOOMP.*

The snowball went past his ear at full speed — it only *felt* slow, because Slowy had not moved even a little — and took a leaf off a branch forty feet behind him.

"…to go," finished Slowy.

"Everybody look at Slowy. Slowy is doing it *exactly wrong.* Slowy, come here. Slowly. You have no choice."

It took a while.

"Here is the rule. You do not stand in the open and think. You get *low* — like your knees forgot how to be knees — and you *move.* Away from the sound, toward the far exit. In short bursts, *between* cover. Tato to Tato to Tato. Behind one, look for the next, go. You do not cross the open in a straight line like a very brave, very silly sloth."

"I… was… deciding," said Slowy, with dignity.

"Decide *behind Tato,*" said Rosa.

---

They practiced the whole rest of the morning, and it was — everyone agreed — the best morning of the Pink Belt Academy so far, which was saying something after Señor Saco's sombrero.

Herschel set up cover across the lake: two upturned sleds packed solid with ice, a stack of frozen cocoa drums, the lodge's old iron stove, and — in the very middle, arms folded, grinning — Tato. And concealment: a row of snowmen, a sheet on a clothesline, a cardboard box, and a bush Finnegan had brought from somewhere and would not explain.

"When I blow the whistle," said Rosa, "get low, get moving, get behind *cover.* Not concealment. If I catch you behind the box, you are Lala. Herschel will fire the whole time. *¡Dale!*"

*Whistle.*

*THOOMP. THOOMP. THOOMP.*

Glorious chaos. Boba went so low he was basically rolling, and pinged sled to drum to stove like he'd been born for it. Finnegan went straight behind his own bush, was immediately hit, and shouted "IT'S CONCEALMENT, I *KNEW* IT" from inside a cloud of snow. Tico refused to get low on principle — "A TOUCAN DOES NOT CROUCH" — took one in the beak, and did not stop announcing. Lala got behind the iron stove and had the best morning of anyone.

And Coco —

Coco froze.

Not *cool*-froze. Actually froze. He made the first cocoa drum fine — then a snowball hit it right in front of his face with a *BANG*, and every rainbow crystal in him locked up solid, and he could not make himself move.

"I can't," he whispered. "Rosa. I can't. It's — it's loud."

---

"Coco," said a calm voice, very close.

Tato had come over, flat and golden and unhurried, and put himself between Coco and the cannon like a door closing.

"Look at me," said Tato.

Coco looked.

"What am I?"

"…Cover."

"Mm-hm. So. You are behind cover. You are okay right now. Right *now* you are okay. Say it."

"Right now I'm okay."

"Good. Now — where's the next one? Don't move. Just look."

Coco looked past Tato's crispy shoulder. The upturned sled. Twelve feet. Solid ice.

"The sled."

"When Herschel pumps, he can't fire. *Sssk, sssk, sssk* — that's your moment. Low, fast, sled. You don't have to be brave. You have to be *behind things.* Ready?"

*Sssk, sssk, sssk.*

"NOW," said Tato, and Coco *went* — low, fast, twelve feet of rainbow ice moving like it had somewhere to be — and slid in behind the sled a half-second before the *THOOMP*, and Tato slid in beside him, and Coco discovered that he was laughing.

"I did it."

"You did it. Now the stove."

"Stay frosty," said Coco, and this time he meant it the right way, "and *stay behind Tato.*"

---

Rosa blew the whistle three times, which meant *done*, and everyone came out from behind everything, and then she taught the very last part, which was the smallest and the most important.

"When it's over and the police come — and they *will* come — you do not run at them. You do not jump up and shout *I'm a good guy.* They don't know that yet. They are looking for the one dangerous person, and they have to look at *everyone* until they find him."

She held both wings up, open, feathers spread.

"So you come out *slow.* Hands *open*, hands *up.* Nothing in them — not a stick, not a phone, not anything you found on the ground. And you do exactly what they say, even if it's *get down*, even if it feels rude, even if you did everything right. *They don't know that yet.* You help them know it. Hands open. Slow. Then shrimp."

"Then shrimp," repeated the whole academy solemnly, which was the traditional way a Rosa lesson ended, and Barnaby produced a bag of them from somewhere, which was the traditional way it *actually* ended.

---

Late in the afternoon, with the cannon rolled away and the snowmen given new heads, Coco sat at the edge of the ice beside Tato and watched the sun go down pink. He had refrozen. He glistened, appropriately.

"Tato?"

"Mm."

"How do you *not* freeze up? When it's loud?"

Tato thought about it, flat and steady.

"I've been smashed twice. Both times I thought I was done. Both times I came out *more* than I went in." He shrugged, which for a patacón is a wobble of the edges. "So when something hits me now, I don't think *oh no.* I think *okay. What's next.* You don't have to be brave, Coco. You just have to know where the next Tato is."

Coco leaned against him. It was a very good place to lean. Nothing was getting through.

"Tuanis," he said. "That's cool."

"Smash now," Tato agreed. "Worry never."

Behind them Auntie Hù screwed the lid back on her thermos, and looked at the lake full of cover and concealment and snowmen with new heads, and said, "Mm," and Rosa, asleep on one leg beside her, said, "…then shrimp," without waking up at all.

## Goodnight Blessing

Lie down low, little one — the good kind of low, the pillow kind.
Nothing to hide from tonight. Nothing to stop.
Your bed is the thickest, warmest, most solid cover there is, and you're already behind it.
Stay frosty. Stay sweet. Sleep.

## 🛡️ What We Learned

- **The one plain sentence:** if shooting ever starts near you — get low, get moving, get behind something that *stops* things.
- **Cover stops.** Brick and concrete walls, the *engine end* of a car (down by the front wheel), a thick tree trunk, stone planters. Ask: *would I hide behind this from a truck?*
- **Concealment only hides.** Car doors, tables, bushes, wooden fences, thin inside walls, snowbanks. Better than the open — but get to cover if you can.
- **Get low, get moving, get away from the sound.** Toward the far exit. Don't stand and think in the open.
- **Move in short bursts** — cover to cover — and don't peek, don't pick anything up.
- **When police arrive:** come out slow, hands open and up, nothing in them. Do exactly what they say, even if it feels rude. They don't know you're okay yet — help them know it.
- **Rare is not never.** That's why we practice — with snowballs.

## 💬 Talk About It

1. In our living room right now — what's cover and what's concealment?
2. If you were in a parking lot, where's the cover on a car?
3. Why do you think the police say "hands up" to *everybody* at first — even the people who did everything right?
