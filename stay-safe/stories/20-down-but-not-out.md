# Story 20: Down But Not Out

**Main Character:** Yēguǒ (Coconut jelly)
**Guest Characters:** Slowy the Sloth, Zhīma, Coach Rosa the Flamingo, Tico the Toucan, Tato the Patacón, Herschel the Walrus
**Setting:** The frozen lake below the Frostpeak lodge on a bright, glittering afternoon — Yēguǒ's ice-surfing spot — and then the boot-drying room of the Pink Belt Academy
**Theme:** Never choose the ground. But if you land there anyway, getting up is a thing you already know how to do.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** **起來** (qǐlái) = get up
**Safety Skill:** Ground survival — never go to the ground on purpose; if you're down: shell the head, feet toward him, kick the knees, and pop up like a surfer the second he steps back

---

Yēguǒ was surfing the lake.

There were no waves. The lake was frozen flat as a plate. This did not bother Yēguǒ in the slightest.

He was a coconut jelly — a bouncy, translucent white cube with a faint tropical shimmer, tiny flip-flops on his feet, and a shell necklace that clicked when he moved. He smelled, very faintly, of sunscreen, even here, even in the snow. He had borrowed Piper's snowboard, waxed it with coconut oil "for the vibes," and was lying belly-down on it, sliding across the ice at one mile per hour, paddling with his arms.

"Duuuude," he said to nobody. "Here it comes."

Nothing came. He popped up anyway — hands flat, hips high, back foot swinging through, standing in one smooth motion — rode the board for four glorious feet, hit a bump of snow, and wiped out spectacularly, sliding on his back in a slow spin with his flip-flops in the air.

"Whoa," he said, gazing at the sky. "Gnarly."

Then he popped up again. Flat, hips, foot, stand. Slid. Wiped out. Popped up.

From the shore, a seven-foot pink flamingo standing on one leg watched him with her eyes narrowed to slits.

"Do that again," said Coach Rosa.

---

"Which part?" said Yēguǒ, upside down.

"The part where you fall on your back on the ice and then, one second later, you are standing."

"Oh, the pop-up? Bro, that's just surfing. Wave knocks you down, you pop up. Go with the flow."

"Show me. Slowly."

Yēguǒ lay back down on the ice. "Okay, so. You're down, right? Belly or back, whatever. You get one hand flat" — he planted a hand — "and one foot flat" — he planted the opposite foot — "and you push your hips up, way up, like the ice is lava, and your back leg swings *through*—" the leg came through under him like a pendulum "—and then you're just… up. Standing. Ready for the wave."

He was standing. It had taken about a second and a half.

Rosa did not say anything for a long moment. Then she blew her tiny whistle — one short, piercing *PEEP* — and shouted at the top of her considerable lungs, "*¡DALE!* Everybody! Academy! Now! The jelly has a *technique!*"

"I have a *what?*" said Yēguǒ.

"*¡Dale!* means *go!* Go go go! Boot room! Bring the sloth!"

---

The Pink Belt Academy was the lodge's boot-drying room, which smelled like warm rubber and had foam mats over the floor drain. Señor Saco the heavy bag hung in the corner in his sombrero.

Tico the Toucan was standing on Señor Saco's head with his enormous rainbow beak, announcing.

"LADIES AND GENTLE-PENGUINS! Tonight's lesson! In THIS corner — a COCONUT! In THAT corner — the FLOOR! Beak the news!"

"The floor is not in a corner, Tico," said Rosa. "The floor is everywhere. That is the problem with the floor."

She hopped onto the mat and looked at the assembled academy: Yēguǒ, Zhīma the Sesame Ball, Tato the Patacón, a handful of penguins, and — arriving last, having left the kitchen six minutes ago to travel forty feet — Slowy the Sloth.

"Puuura…" said Slowy, "…viiida."

"Sit, Slowy. You will be important later. Everybody. First rule tonight, and it is the most important, so I say it before the shrimp." Rosa held up one pink wing. "**You never choose the ground.**"

"But surfing—" said Yēguǒ.

"Surfing is *water.* Water is soft. I am talking about the street. The sidewalk. The ice. A parking lot." She stamped one flat pink foot on the mat. "Here is what is on the ground in a fight: concrete. Your head. And the *feet* of the bad man's *friends*, because bad men come with friends, and friends kick. And here is what is *not* on the ground: the exit. In movies they wrestle for ten minutes. In real life, *tranquilo* — easy — you stay UP. Up is where running lives."

"So the lesson is don't fall down?" said Zhīma.

"The lesson," said Rosa, "is that you *will* fall down anyway. Ice is ice. Bad men push. And when you are down, you do not lie there like a starfish. You do four things, and then you are up. And the jelly has just shown me the fourth thing is something *everybody already knows.*"

---

Rosa pointed at Tato. "You. Be the bad man."

Tato, who was a twice-fried smashed plantain about as tall as a coffee mug and roughly as threatening, cracked his crispy knuckles. "Smash now, worry never," he said gravely, and pushed Yēguǒ over.

Yēguǒ fell on his back on the mat with a soft *bloop.*

"Thing one," said Rosa. "**Shell.**" Yēguǒ's forearms came up around his head — the turtle Estrella had taught them, elbows in, chin tucked. "Good. The head is the one thing you cannot replace. Thing two — **feet toward him.**"

Yēguǒ spun on his back like a turtle on a table until his flip-flops pointed at Tato.

"Why the feet?" asked a penguin.

"Because legs are *strong* — the biggest muscles you own. From down there, your legs are a *fence* — he cannot get to your head without coming past your feet. Thing three — **kick.** Not the air. The knees. The shins. Like riding a bicycle, *fast*, one after the other — *dale, dale, dale.* Not to win. To make him *step back.*"

Tato, very seriously, stepped toward the flip-flops. Yēguǒ bicycle-kicked. Tato — who had been smashed twice in a fryer and did not scare easily — took one step back out of pure respect.

"And *there,*" said Rosa, and her voice dropped, and the room got quiet. "The instant he steps back. That is your whole life, right there. That one second. You do not wait. You do not think. You—"

"Pop up," said Yēguǒ.

"You pop up. **起來** (qǐlái). *Get up.* Hand down, foot down, hips up, back leg through, STAND. And then you *run.* And then—"

"SCAN," said everyone, turning their heads like meerkats.

"Then scan. Then shrimp. Yēguǒ. Show them."

Yēguǒ lay back down. He shelled. He turned his feet. He kicked three times at nothing. Tato stepped back on cue.

And the coconut jelly did the thing he'd done ten thousand times on ten thousand waves — hand, foot, hips like the ice was lava, leg swinging through — and was standing, in a surfer's crouch, arms out, ready for a wave that wasn't coming.

The room stared.

"Bro," said Yēguǒ, looking at his own feet. "I've been training for this my *whole life* and nobody *told* me."

---

Now, when a room full of friends learns a new move, everyone immediately wants to try it, and everyone immediately does it wrong.

Zhīma the Sesame Ball went first, because Zhīma always went first. He fell down enthusiastically, shelled, kicked, and popped up so hard that he kept going — straight up, off the mat, into Señor Saco, off Señor Saco, and into the boot rack, where he came to rest inside a size-fourteen polar bear boot.

"We roll NOW," he said, from inside the boot.

"You roll *later,*" said Rosa. "First you *stand.*"

A penguin got his hand and foot on the same side and fell over like a dropped book. Another popped up beautifully, forgot to run, and just stood there smiling until Rosa said "Mae — you are standing in the *fight.* Standing is not the finish. *Running* is the finish."

Tato tried it and discovered that a patacón is very flat and therefore very good at lying down and very bad at having hips.

"I don't have hips," he reported.

"Everyone has hips," said Rosa.

"I have *edges.*"

"Then push up with your *edges*, mae. Crunchy things stand too."

He did. It was ugly. It worked. Rosa nodded once and said, "Mm," which she had learned from Auntie Hù and used only when something was truly good.

And then it was Slowy's turn.

---

Slowy the Sloth had been lying on his back for most of the lesson — nobody had pushed him; he'd been slowly lowering himself to sit and had simply kept going.

"Slowy," said Rosa. "Shell."

Slowy's long, mossy arms began to rise toward his head.

They were still rising when Tico flew a bored lap of the room. Still rising when Herschel the Walrus stuck his head in to offer cocoa, and the cocoa came, and was drunk. When the arms finally arrived and his chin tucked down, a full minute had passed.

"Puuura…" said Slowy, "…shell."

"Feet toward him."

Slowy began to rotate, like the hour hand of a clock. Tato sat down and waited. A penguin fell asleep. Somewhere a narwhal did a dramatic spin through a hole in the lake, gave up on anyone noticing, and went home.

But here is the thing, and it is the thing that made the whole academy go quiet: Slowy was not doing it *wrong.*

His shell was perfect — elbows in, chin down, eyes up. His feet came around, slow as a sunrise, until they pointed straight at Tato. His bicycle kick was three slow, deliberate pushes through the air, each one exactly aimed at a knee. Tato, honoring the drill, stepped back.

And Slowy the Sloth — very, very slowly — put down one hand. Put down one foot. Raised his hips, in a long, patient, mossy arc, as if the floor were lava that was in absolutely no hurry. Swung his back leg through like a pendulum in a grandfather clock.

And stood.

It had taken four minutes. It was the most perfect get-up anyone in the room had ever seen. Nothing skipped. Nothing wobbled.

"Puuura…" said Slowy, standing tall, "…viiida."

Then he turned his head, slow, to the left. Slow, to the right. And *scanned.*

Nobody said anything.

Coach Rosa the Flamingo was standing on one leg, perfectly still, and down her long pink cheek ran one single, glistening, pink tear.

"That," she whispered, "is the most beautiful thing I have ever seen. Speed is nothing. *Order* is everything. Get the order right slow, and one day, when you need it, it will be fast." She wiped the tear with the tip of a wing. "The ground," she said, "is for shrimp."

Then she went and got some shrimp.

---

Later, when the mats were rolled up and the cocoa was gone and Zhīma had been extracted from the boot, Yēguǒ and Slowy sat together on the dock with their feet — flip-flops and claws — dangling over the frozen lake.

"Slowy," said Yēguǒ. "Real talk. How come you did it perfect and the fast guys all messed up?"

Slowy considered this for a while. The sun moved.

"Because…" he said at last, "…I have fallen down… many, many times. Out of many trees. And every time… I found out… the getting up… is just the falling… backwards."

Yēguǒ blinked. Then he grinned, his whole translucent cube lighting up with the last of the sunset.

"Dude. *Dude.* That's it. That's the whole thing." He lay back on the dock and looked at the sky, arms out, the way he'd lain on the ice a hundred times that afternoon. "Falling's just standing up in advance."

"…Puuura…" Slowy agreed, "…viiida."

They stayed there a while. The lake glowed pink, then blue. Yēguǒ thought about how you never choose the ground — and how, even so, he wasn't scared of it anymore. The way down and the way up were the same shape. Hand, foot, hips, leg. Wave after wave.

Go with the flow, bro. And when the flow puts you flat on your back —

**起來.** Get up.

---

That night Yēguǒ slept on a pile of boot-room mats that smelled like warm rubber and coconut oil, which he declared "the vibe." Slowy slept hanging from the boot rack, which took him twenty minutes to climb and which he called "…the… penthouse."

Just before he drifted off, Yēguǒ heard a soft *thump* from the mat beside him, then a shuffle, then a small proud voice: "Popped up. Ready for the wave."

It was Zhīma, in the dark, practicing. Down. Hand, foot, hips, leg. Up. Down. Up.

"Bro," Yēguǒ murmured, "the wave's asleep. You can be asleep too."

A pause. Then the soft sound of a sesame ball lying down on purpose, at last, and staying there.

## Goodnight Blessing

Lie down, little one — on purpose, this time, with nothing to get up for.
You know the way down. You know the way up. They're the same shape, and you carry both.
Tonight the ground is soft, and the wave is asleep, and so are you.
Go with the flow. Sleep.

## 🛡️ What We Learned

- **Never choose the ground.** In a real fight the ground has concrete, your head, and other people's feet — and no exits. You can't run from down there. Stay up whenever you can.
- **If you fall anyway, do four things, in order:** shell your head · feet toward him · kick the knees · pop up.
- **Shell:** forearms around your head, elbows in, chin tucked. The head is the one thing you can't replace.
- **Feet toward him:** your legs are the strongest thing you own. From the ground they're a fence he has to get past.
- **Kick like a bicycle** — fast, at knees and shins — not to win, but to make him *step back.*
- **The second he steps back, pop up:** one hand down, one foot down, hips high, back leg swings through, stand. It's exactly a surfer's pop-up. Then RUN. Then SCAN.
- **Order beats speed.** Learn it slow and right. Slowy did it in four minutes and it was perfect.
