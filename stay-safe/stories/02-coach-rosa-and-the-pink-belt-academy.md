# Story 2: Coach Rosa and the Pink Belt Academy

**Main Character:** Tato the Patacón (twice-fried plantain) and Coach Rosa the Flamingo (new)
**Guest Characters:** Tico the Toucan, Gallo & Pinto, Coco the Granizado, Boba, Auntie Hù, Finnegan Flipsworth, Barnaby Beaksworth, Herschel the Walrus
**Setting:** The frozen-lake dock where the mail-boat from Pura Vida Town arrives, then the Frostpeak lodge's boot-drying room — freshly renamed the Pink Belt Academy
**Theme:** The best fight is the one you're not in. But if you're ever trapped, know how to stand, know how to switch on — and know how to switch off.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** **準備** (zhǔnbèi) = ready
**Safety Skill:** Krav Maga basics — fighting stance, hands up, the switch (on when trapped, off when free), heavy knees and palms (hard parts to soft parts), and SCAN

---

The mail-boat from Pura Vida Town arrived by crunching straight through the lake ice, which is not how mail-boats are supposed to arrive, and the whole of Frostpeak Valley turned out to watch it do it.

"LADIES AND GENTLE-PENGUINS!" bellowed a toucan from the bow, wings spread, rainbow beak pointed at the sky like a trumpet. "Straight from the sunny shores of Costa Rica — the *only* rice grain who has ever fallen off a boat twice — GAAAAALLO!"

"Rise and pinto!" said a small white rice grain in a palm hat, and fell off the boat.

"And his partner, the bean who catches him — PINTOOOO!"

A small black bean with a tiny wooden spoon leaned over the rail, hooked Gallo by the hat, and hauled him back up. "Better together, *mae,*" he said calmly, as if this happened every day, which it did.

"Stay frosty," said Coco Granizado, a mound of rainbow shaved ice in sunglasses, regarding the snow with deep professional approval.

"The plantain who got SMASHED TWICE and came back CRUNCHIER — TATOOOO!"

A short, sturdy, golden disc with crinkled edges thumped down the gangplank and said, with great satisfaction, "Smash now, worry never."

"And FINALLY," said the toucan — Tico, who had never once said a sentence at normal volume — "the reason we are all here. Seven feet of pink. The coach. The legend. Coach — ROSA!"

Everyone looked at the boat.

At the very back stood a flamingo. She was enormous, brilliantly pink, wearing a pink headband, standing on one leg with her head tucked under her wing — and completely asleep.

---

"Is she… okay?" whispered Boba, who had bounced down to the dock with the whole Boba on Ice crew.

"She's *sleeping,*" said Tico proudly. "Standing up. On one leg. She once slept through a volcano."

"A *small* volcano," said Coco.

"MEDIUM."

The flamingo's head came up out of her wing so fast that Finnegan fell over.

"—and THEN, *shrimp,*" she announced, finishing a sentence nobody had heard the beginning of. She looked around at the snow, the lodge, the penguins. "Where am I. Excellent. Is this the academy?"

"It's a dock," said Boba.

"Then we will *make* it the academy." She swung her second leg down — *click* — and stood on two feet like a lamppost deciding to be a person. "I am Coach Rosa. I teach **Krav Maga.** It is what you do when running did not work. Is there a room nobody wants?"

There was: the boot-drying room — warm, low, smelling permanently of wet sock. By suppertime it had a hand-painted sign over the door.

**PINK BELT ACADEMY**

"Everyone starts pink," said Rosa, standing under it with her whistle. "Everyone stays pink. There is no other belt."

Boba raised a tiny arm. "What's the belt *after* pink?"

"Shrimp."

"That's not a—"

"*Shrimp,*" said Rosa, and blew the whistle.

---

Before the first lesson, however, there was a meeting. Rosa had heard there was a tea egg in this valley who minded the crowd, and before she taught anyone anything, she walked down to the night market, folded herself in half, and looked into the steaming pot beside the boba stand.

Auntie Hù looked back. A seven-foot pink bird and a small cracked brown egg, both with arms folded, both wearing an expression that said *I have seen some things.*

"You teach them not to be there," said Rosa.

"Mm," said Auntie Hù.

"I teach them what to do if they are."

"Mm."

"We are," said Rosa, "the same egg."

Auntie Hù handed up a tea egg, still warm. "Rule zero. Before anything you teach. The best fight is the one you're not in."

"Rule zero," Rosa agreed solemnly, tucking the egg into her headband for later. "*Then* the other rules. And then—"

"Shrimp," said Auntie Hù, who caught on fast.

Rosa went very still on her one leg. "*Mm,*" she said — her first one — and it was the beginning of a beautiful friendship.

---

The first class had eleven students, one heavy bag, and a toucan.

"IN THIS CORNER," bellowed Tico, self-appointed announcer, "eighty pounds of sawdust — the undefeated, the SOMBRERO'D — SEÑOR SACOOOO!"

The heavy bag had a marker face and a sombrero. Everyone bowed to it, because it seemed rude not to.

"Lesson one," said Rosa. "Before you can do *anything*, you must be **準備** (zhǔnbèi) — *ready.* Ready is a shape. Watch."

She stood on one leg.

"Like this?" said Finnegan, wobbling onto one foot.

"NO. That is *sleeping.* One leg is for sleeping. *Two* feet are for—" She put her second foot down. "**Two feet down.** Not side by side like a penguin waiting for a bus. One a little forward. Knees soft. Now — **hands up.**"

Everyone put their fists up like boxers on a poster.

"No. That says *I want to fight.* You want to *look* like you don't, while being completely ready to. Hands up like this—" she opened both wings, palms out, chest height, "—like you are saying *whoa. Tranquilo.* Easy, easy. It looks polite. It *is* polite. It is also a *wall.* Chin down. Eyes up. **Two feet down. Hands up.**" Everyone waited. "And *then—*"

"Shrimp!" said Tico.

"*Later,* Tico."

Boba, in the front row, was holding the shape so hard he vibrated. "How long do we stay like this?"

"Until you don't need to. Most of the time you stand like this for two seconds, the other person decides you are not worth it, and you have won a fight nobody saw. The *best* kind." She glanced at the shelf by the door, where a small tea egg had appeared, arms folded. "Rule zero."

"Mm," said the shelf.

---

"Now," said Rosa. "The part nobody likes to talk about. The switch."

The room got quieter.

"Sometimes running does not work. Sometimes somebody *has* you, and nobody is coming." She let that sit for a moment. "For that, you have a switch inside you. Right now it is OFF. It is off all day, your whole life, probably. But the *instant* you are trapped, you turn it ON — and then you are not polite, you are not small. You are all at once, and ugly, and *loud.* I need a volunteer."

Tato thumped forward.

He was the shortest one in the room and the densest, and he had the calm of a plantain who has already been through the worst thing twice and come out crunchier.

"Tato," said Rosa. "Tico is going to grab you. You are going to be *friendly.* And then, when I say, you are going to turn the switch on."

Tico put on a pair of oven mitts, for safety, and grabbed Tato by both crinkled shoulders. "GOT YOU, PLANTAIN."

"Hola," said Tato pleasantly.

"*Switch,*" said Rosa.

And Tato *changed.*

One second there was a friendly golden disc saying *hola.* The next there was a *wall of noise.* Tato roared — a huge flat plantain roar — grabbed Tico's shoulders right back, and pulled *down.* His short sturdy knee came up into the padded middle of the toucan's flight vest. *Whump.* *Whump.* Then he *shoved*, and Tico stumbled backward into Señor Saco, who swung gently and lost his sombrero.

*WHISTLE.*

Tato stopped.

Instantly. Mid-motion. Hands down, face back to friendly. He looked at Tico, sprawled against the bag, and said with real concern, "You okay, *mae?*"

"I'M GREAT," said Tico from the floor. "I'M ANNOUNCING FROM DOWN HERE NOW."

"*That,*" said Rosa, "is the switch. ON the instant you are trapped. OFF the instant you are free. Not one second longer. Did you see him stop?"

"He stopped so *fast,*" whispered Boba.

"Because the switch is not anger. Anger has no off. The switch is a *tool.* You use it, you put it *down*, you *run.* Everyone: what does Tato do next?"

"…Shrimp?" said Finnegan.

"He *RUNS,*" said Rosa. "And then, yes. Fine. Shrimp."

---

"The knee," said Rosa, when the sombrero had been restored, "is the most important thing in this room, and I include myself."

Standing on one leg — of course — she drove her other knee into Señor Saco so hard the recoil hopped her a foot sideways. Kneed again. Hopped again. By the fourth knee she had hopped clear out into the hall, still on one leg, still kneeing the air.

"IS SHE COMING BACK?" said Tico.

She came back.

"When somebody has hold of you *up close* — too close to run — the **knee** is the biggest, hardest weapon you own, and it is *right next to* all their softest places. You do not have to be strong or tall." She looked at Tato, who was neither. "In fact—"

Tato, experimentally, raised one knee. It was at *exactly* the height of Señor Saco's middle.

"Oh," said Tato. "Oh, *smash now.*"

"**Closest weapon to the closest target.** Hard parts of you—" she slapped a knee, an elbow, the heel of her wing, "—to soft parts of them. Knee to the thigh or the middle. Heel of the hand *up*, under the chin. Bottom of the fist *down*, like a hammer. Two or three. Not ten. Then *push*, then *run.*"

Boba was frowning. "What about punching? Like in movies?"

"Look at your hand."

Boba looked at his hand. It was approximately the size of a sesame seed.

"Now look at Bjorn's head."

Bjorn, who had wedged himself into the doorway to watch, obligingly presented his head. It was approximately the size of a boulder.

"*No fists to faces,*" said Rosa. "Small hands break on big heads. Knees do not break. Palms do not break. Save your hands. You will need them later, for shrimp."

---

The last thing Rosa taught that night was the thing she'd teach every night, forever.

"When it is over — when you are free, when you have run — what do you do?"

"Go home?" said Gallo.

"Before that."

"Tell a grown-up?" said Pinto.

"Before *that.*"

Silence.

"**SCAN,**" said Rosa, and turned her long pink neck all the way left, all the way right, and back again, slow and deliberate, like a lighthouse. "For *more* trouble — was he alone? For the *way out* — where is the light, the people? The fight you just finished is never the one that gets you. It's the one you didn't see because you were busy feeling proud. Everyone. Scan."

Eleven students turned their heads left, then right, then left, like a field of meerkats.

Finnegan scanned so hard he kept going, spun a full circle, and fell into Señor Saco. The sombrero came off again.

Everyone bowed to it.

"Good," said Rosa, and blew the whistle. "That is lesson one. *Two feet down. Hands up.* The switch — on when trapped, off when free. Knees. Scan. And now—"

"*SHRIMP!*" roared the entire room.

And Barnaby Beaksworth, who had been waiting in the hall for exactly this moment, wheeled in a bucket of actual shrimp — because there is always enough fish for one more, and shrimp are basically fish — and this was, he felt, the first Krav Maga class in history to end correctly.

---

Late that night, Rosa stood on the lodge porch on one leg, watching the snow. Auntie Hù climbed onto the railing beside her.

"They'll never need most of it," said Auntie Hù finally.

"No," Rosa agreed. "Rule zero does the work. You do the work." She tucked her head halfway under her wing. "But the day one of them *does* need it, I want their hands to already know where to go. Not deciding. Just… *ready.*"

"Mm."

"Mm," said Rosa, who had learned it in one afternoon and considered it the finest word in any language.

Down in the boot-drying room, Tato slept sitting up against Señor Saco, one knee still slightly raised, smiling the smile of a plantain who has found out what he was smashed for. Out on the ice the mail-boat rocked in its hole, the stars came out over the Pink Belt Academy, and nobody in Frostpeak Valley had needed the switch tonight — which, Rosa would have told you, was the entire point.

## Goodnight Blessing

Two feet down, little one — and now, two feet up, under the blanket.
Hands up all day, if they needed to be. Hands open now.
Your switch is off. It's allowed to be off. Tonight there's nothing to be ready for but dreams.
Scan once — the room is yours. Good.
And then… sleep.

## 🛡️ What We Learned

- **Rule zero:** the best fight is the one you're not in. Run first. Krav Maga is for *when running didn't work.*
- **Ready is a shape:** two feet down (one a little forward, knees soft), hands up open like *whoa, easy*, chin down, eyes up. It looks polite. It's a wall.
- **The switch:** OFF your whole life — until the instant you're trapped and can't get away. Then ON: all at once, loud, ugly. **OFF the second you're free.** Then RUN.
- **Closest weapon to the closest target:** knees, palm-heel, hammer-fist, elbows — *hard* parts of you to *soft* parts of them. Two or three, not ten.
- **No fists to faces.** Small hands break on big heads.
- **SCAN:** after everything, turn your head — left, right — for more trouble and for the way out. *Then* run, *then* tell a grown-up.
- **Everyone stays pink.** You never graduate from being careful.
