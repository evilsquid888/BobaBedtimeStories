# Story 5: Secrets Are Not Surprises

**Main Character:** Willa Wobble (Little Blue Penguin) and Aiyu (Aiyu jelly)
**Guest Characters:** Nora Snowmane, Auntie Hù, Sergeant Solveig Snowmane, Herschel the Walrus
**Setting:** The Frostpeak lodge over one snowy week — the cocoa line, the gear room, and Nora Snowmane's little office with the blanket chair
**Theme:** Your body is yours. No grown-up gets to make rules about it that your parents don't know. It is never, ever the kid's fault.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** **身體** (shēntǐ) = body
**Safety Skill:** Body safety — the Swimsuit Rule, secrets vs. surprises, grooming red flags, tell and keep telling

---

> *A note for the grown-up reading this: this story names sexual abuse plainly, in words a ten-year-old can understand. Nothing graphic happens. Read it together, earlier in the evening the first time, and be ready to stop for questions.*

---

The new lodge helper was, everyone agreed, the most fun grown-up in Frostpeak Valley.

He remembered every single person's cocoa order. He could juggle three snowballs and a ski pole. He laughed at Finnegan's jokes — *all* of Finnegan's jokes, even the one about the fish that was just the word "fish" said louder and louder. He called Bjorn "Your Majesty" and Bjorn *loved* it. By the end of his first day, the cubs were following him around like ducklings.

And on his second day, he noticed Willa Wobble.

Now, you need to understand something about Willa. She was a Little Blue Penguin, which is the smallest kind of penguin there is. She rode a tiny purple snowboard. She stood at the back of every group photo and the end of every line. Most days she felt roughly as visible as a snowflake in a snowstorm, and she'd made a kind of peace with that. The smallest step is still a step, she'd tell herself. Quietly. Where no one could hear.

So when the most fun grown-up in the valley crouched down in the cocoa line and said, "Hey — you're the one with the *legendary* board, right? Willa? I've heard about you," it felt like the sun coming out.

"You've… heard about me?" said Willa.

"Are you kidding? Everybody has." He winked. "Extra marshmallows for the legend. Don't tell the others."

She floated through the rest of that day.

---

Aiyu noticed on the third day.

Aiyu was a small cube of pale golden jelly, so clear that you could see right through her — and lately, since Auntie Hù's game at the night market, she'd been learning that she could see through *other* things too. Crowds. Patterns. The shape a week makes when you look at it all at once.

The shape of this week went like this:

Monday: extra marshmallows, *don't tell the others.*

Tuesday: a brand-new pair of purple mittens, just for Willa, from the lodge gift shop. "Our secret, okay? People get jealous."

Wednesday: "You're so mature for your age, you know that? You're not like the other kids. You *get* things."

Thursday: "Hey, stick around after the others go — I could use a hand in the gear room. It'll be fun, just us."

And Thursday, also: a hand on Willa's back, in the cocoa line. Low. Lingering. The kind of touch that wasn't a pat and wasn't a push and wasn't anything Willa had a word for, except that her stomach went tight and small like a fist, and she thought, very fast and very quiet: *He's nice. He's* nice. *I'm the one who's being weird.*

Aiyu, three places back in the line, watched Willa's whole tiny body go still.

And Aiyu thought: *Orange.*

---

She didn't march up to anyone. She'd learned that much. Instead she found Willa on the bench outside the gear room at the end of the day, kicking her feet, wearing the new purple mittens and not looking happy about them.

Aiyu sat down. For a while neither of them said anything.

"Can I ask you something?" said Aiyu finally. "And you don't have to answer. I'm just — I see through things. It's what I do. And I saw something this week and I want to check if I'm wrong."

"Okay," said Willa, very small.

"When he's around," said Aiyu, "does your stomach go tight?"

Willa didn't say anything for a long time.

"Mine would," said Aiyu. "That's all. Mine would."

"He's *nice,*" said Willa, and her voice cracked right down the middle. "He's the nicest one. He *noticed* me. Nobody notices me. And the mittens are so *soft,* and he said I'm mature, and — and I don't want to be *rude* —"

"Willa."

"—and I didn't tell anyone about the marshmallows because he asked me not to and now it's like I'm *in* it—"

"Willa. Listen. You're allowed to be rude to someone who's breaking the rules on you."

Willa stopped.

"What rules?" she whispered.

Aiyu, who could not tell a lie, and who had listened very carefully to Nora Snowmane at last year's Safety Week, told her.

---

Here is what Aiyu told her, and here is what this story is going to tell you plainly, because you're old enough, and because knowing it is one of the strongest things you can carry.

Some grown-ups — not many, but some — try to touch kids' private parts. Or get kids to touch theirs. Or look at theirs. Or show kids pictures of that. **Private parts** means the parts a swimsuit covers. That is called **sexual abuse**. It is a crime. The grown-up who does it knows it's a crime, which is why he needs it to be a *secret.*

And here's the thing that the movies get wrong: it is almost never a stranger in a dark alley. It is almost always someone the kid already *knows.* Someone fun. Someone trusted. Someone who spent a whole week being the nicest grown-up in the valley — because being nice is *how it starts.*

The pattern has a shape, and Aiyu had seen it:

**Gifts** that are just for you. **Secrets** — *don't tell the others, don't tell your parents, they wouldn't get it.* **Special** — *you're so mature, you're not like the other kids.* **Alone** — *stick around after, just us.* And then **touching** that makes your stomach go tight.

"You didn't do anything wrong," said Aiyu. "Not one thing. Not the marshmallows. Not the mittens. Not the not-telling. He's the grown-up. He *made* it a secret on purpose so you'd feel like you were in it with him. That's the trick. It's *his* trick. It's not yours."

Willa was crying now, quietly, into the soft purple mittens.

"What do I do?"

"Two rules," said Aiyu. "Nora taught them. **The Swimsuit Rule:** the parts a swimsuit covers are yours. Nobody touches them, looks at them, or asks about them. Not a teacher, not a coach, not a fun lodge helper, not *anybody* — except a doctor, with your parent right there. And **the Secrets Rule:** surprises are okay. A surprise ends in telling — a birthday cake gets *eaten*, everybody finds out. A **secret** that's supposed to last forever, that your parents can't know? That is a red flag. Every single time. No grown-up ever, ever needs a kid to keep a secret from their parents."

"But I'd have to be so *rude,*" said Willa. "I'd have to say no to a *grown-up.*"

"Yes," said Aiyu. "Yes. That's exactly right. You'd have to be *so* rude. And it would be the best thing you ever did."

---

Friday.

The others left the gear room, laughing, kicking snow off their boards. Willa was the last one, because she was always the last one, because she was small and the mittens were new and she'd been *asked.*

"There she is," said the helper, warm as cocoa. "My legend. Give me a hand with these bindings?" And the door drifted, just a little, toward shut.

Willa's stomach went tight like a fist.

*He's nice,* said a voice in her head.

*You're allowed to be rude to someone who's breaking the rules on you,* said Aiyu's.

*The smallest step is still a step,* said her own.

Willa Wobble, the smallest penguin in Frostpeak Valley, put both feet flat on the floor and said, in a voice that came out much bigger than she was:

"**No.** I'm leaving."

She did not say *sorry.* She did not say *thanks anyway.* She did not explain. She did not wait to see his face. She turned around, pushed the door all the way open with her whole tiny body, and walked — not ran, *walked*, though her heart was going like a drumline — straight down the hall, past the cocoa line, past Bjorn and the cubs and the fire, to a door with a hand-lettered sign that said NORA.

She knocked once and went in.

---

Nora Snowmane was a big white polar bear with a voice like a warm blanket, and her office had exactly one chair, and the chair had a quilt on it. She looked up. She looked at Willa's face. She got up and closed the door behind her, gently, and said the thing she always said.

"Sit. Cocoa first. Then feelings."

But before the cocoa, before anything, while Willa was still standing there shaking in her new purple mittens, Nora crouched down to her level and said four things very slowly and very clearly, because she knew they were the four things that mattered most:

"I believe you."

"You did nothing wrong."

"Thank you for telling me."

"You are safe now."

And *then* the cocoa.

Willa told her all of it, out of order, in pieces, the marshmallows and the mittens and *mature* and the hand on her back and *just us* and the door drifting shut. Nora listened to every word. She did not say *are you sure.* She did not say *maybe he didn't mean it.* She did not say *he's such a nice man.* She said, "Mm-hm," and "Okay," and "That was a red flag, wasn't it? You're right. It was."

When Willa was finished, Nora picked up the phone and called her sister.

---

Sergeant Solveig Snowmane came within the hour. What she said to the lodge helper, and where he went after, the story doesn't need to follow. He did not work at the lodge that night, or ever again, and he would answer to the law for what he'd done and tried to do. Herschel walked the whole length of the lodge and back with his whiskers bristling like a broom. That is the last the story has to say about him.

It has more to say about Willa.

"I ruined it," she said to Nora, when it was very late, and the cocoa was gone, and she was wrapped in the quilt. "Everybody *loved* him. The cubs. Bjorn. I ruined everything for everybody."

Nora shook her great white head.

"Willa. Listen to me. Who broke the rules?"

"…He did."

"Who made it a secret?"

"He did."

"Who's the grown-up?"

"He is."

"Then whose fault is it?"

Willa didn't answer. So Nora answered for her.

"His. All of it. Every bit of it. You did not ruin anything. You *stopped* something. Those are opposites." She tucked the quilt tighter. "And I'll tell you something else. If you'd been too scared to tell me — if you'd waited a month, or a year — it *still* wouldn't have been your fault. Kids freeze. Kids get confused. Kids like the mittens. None of that changes who broke the rules. You tell when you can. And if the first grown-up doesn't listen, you tell another one. And another one. You keep telling until somebody does."

"You listened the first time."

"I did," said Nora. "That's my job. That's *every* grown-up's job. The ones who don't do it are wrong, not you."

---

Aiyu was waiting on the bench outside when Willa came out.

Willa sat down. Aiyu didn't say anything. She just sat there, being clear.

"You were right," said Willa finally. "About my stomach."

"I'm sorry I was right."

"I'm not." Willa looked down at the purple mittens. Then she took them off, folded them, and set them on the bench between her and Aiyu, and left them there. "I've got my own mittens," she said. "They're ugly. I love them."

**身體** (shēntǐ) means *body.* It's a small word for a big thing. It's the only one you get, and it's yours — all of it, every inch, always, and it was yours before anyone ever noticed you, and it'll be yours long after.

That night Willa Wobble slept in her little iceberg cottage with the lamp on, and she slept *deep* — the kind of deep, still sleep that only comes after you've been braver than you knew you could be. And the shame that had sat in her stomach all week like a fist got up, quietly, and went to sit where it belonged, which was on somebody else.

Your body is yours, little one. All of it. Every inch. Always.
Nobody gets to make rules about it that we don't know.
If your stomach ever goes tight — tell me. I'll believe you. First time, every time.
Tonight there's nothing to hold but the blanket. Let go of everything else. Sleep.

## 🛡️ What We Learned

- **The Swimsuit Rule:** the parts a swimsuit covers are private. Nobody touches, looks, or asks — not a coach, not a teacher, not a family friend. Only a doctor, with your parent there.
- **Sexual abuse** is when a grown-up (or older kid) touches a kid's private parts, has the kid touch theirs, or shows them pictures of that. It's a crime. It's almost always someone the kid *knows.*
- **Secrets are not surprises.** A surprise ends in telling. A secret from your parents that's supposed to last forever is a red flag — *every time.* No grown-up ever needs a kid to keep a secret from their parents.
- **Grooming has a shape:** special gifts → secrets → "you're so mature" → alone time → touching. If you see the pattern, tell.
- **Your stomach is a smoke alarm.** Tight stomach around someone = tell a trusted grown-up. You don't need proof.
- **Be rude. Be bold.** You may say "NO. I'm leaving." to *any* grown-up about your own body — no sorry, no explaining — and walk out of any room.
- **It is never the kid's fault.** Not if you froze, not if you liked the gifts, not if you didn't tell right away. Tell when you can. If the first grown-up doesn't listen, tell another. Keep telling.
