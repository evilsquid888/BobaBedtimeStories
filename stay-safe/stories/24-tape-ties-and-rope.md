# Story 24: Tape, Ties, and Rope

**Main Character:** Lala the Tres Leches (Milk-soaked sponge cake)
**Guest Characters:** Finnegan Flipsworth, Cleo Coldwater, Coach Rosa the Flamingo, Tico the Toucan, Herschel the Walrus, Auntie Hù
**Setting:** The Pink Belt Academy — the lodge's boot-drying room, warm and steamy, with a wall of dripping boots and a hand-painted PINK BELT sign — on a sleety afternoon
**Theme:** Slack is a secret. Stay calm, keep your hands where the slack lives, and no tie stays tied forever.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** **鬆** (sōng) = loose / slack
**Safety Skill:** Getting out of zip ties, duct tape, and rope — hands in front, make slack, slam, wiggle, then run

---

Nobody could remember who had taped Finnegan into a burrito.

He was standing in the middle of the boot-drying room, wrapped from neck to knee in silver duct tape, with his beak sticking out the top and his flippers pinned to his sides and an expression of deep, philosophical calm.

"It was for a drill," said Finnegan.

"Which drill?" said Cleo Coldwater, who was already sketching him.

"The burrito drill."

"There's no burrito drill."

"Well," said Finnegan, "there is *now.*"

Coach Rosa the Flamingo, seven feet of pink standing on exactly one leg, opened one eye. She had been asleep. She was very often asleep, standing up, in the middle of things. "Who did this?" she asked.

Everyone looked at everyone. Tico the Toucan, who announced every drill at the Academy like a title fight and had a beak the size of a canoe paddle, looked at the ceiling.

"I may have *announced* it," said Tico. "I did not *do* it. There was a roll of tape. There was a penguin. Things *happened.*"

"Beak the news," muttered Cleo, still drawing.

And then a soft, dreamy voice from the doorway said: "Oh, poor thing. Hold still. I'll get you out."

---

Lala the Tres Leches was a soft square of pale-gold sponge cake, soaked all the way through with three kinds of milk, topped with a wobbly cloud of whipped cream and one cinnamon dot. She glistened. She dripped a little wherever she went, leaving tiny milky footprints. She gasped at beautiful things and swooned at sad ones, and Finnegan-the-burrito struck her as both.

"Soak it all in," she said gently, and hugged him.

Now, here is a thing about duct tape: it is very, very sticky — right up until it gets wet. And here is a thing about Lala: she was the wettest cake in the Northern Hemisphere.

There was a soft *shlorp.* The tape went dark and slick where she'd touched it. Finnegan wiggled. A strip peeled. Then another. Twenty seconds later he stepped out of a silver puddle like a rockhopper penguin being born, dripping milk and covered in cinnamon.

"WOOOHOOO!" said Finnegan. "I'm FREE! Lala, you're a *genius!*"

"I only hugged him," said Lala, blushing a shade of caramel.

Coach Rosa, still on one leg, said, "Mm. Good. That is *a* lesson." She raised one long pink wing. "It is not *the* lesson. Sit down, everybody. Tico — announce it."

Tico cleared his throat, which took a while.

"LADIES AND GENTLE-BIRDS. IN THE PINK CORNER — EVERYBODY. IN THE OTHER CORNER — *A ROLL OF TAPE.* THIS IS…" He paused for effect. "…*THE DRILL WE MEANT TO DO.*"

---

"One thing before the tape," said Rosa, "and it's the only thing that matters, so I'll say it standing up."

"You're always standing up," said Finnegan.

"*Wide awake,*" said Rosa, and opened both eyes at once, and everybody sat up straighter, because that almost never happened. "If anyone ever tries to tie your hands — *ever* — you will not be able to stop them. You are small. So you do not fight the tying. You fight the *tightness.*"

She held her wings out in front of her, wrists together.

"You ask — you *offer* — to have your hands in front. *Tranquilo.* That means *easy, easy.* 'Please, in front. I'll hold still.' Wrists together. Palms facing each other, like a prayer. Elbows in close to your ribs." She squeezed her wings tight against her body. "You know what lives right *here,* between two wrists pressed together?"

"Fear?" said Finnegan.

"**鬆** (sōng)," said Rosa. "*Slack.* Loose. Room. When you're tied like this, you have a secret space between your hands that nobody can see. And when you relax your shoulders and turn your wrists—" she rolled them "—the space gets *bigger.* Behind your back you have nothing. In front, you have a secret. *That's* the lesson. Everything else is just what to do with the secret."

Lala clasped her own soft hands together, palms in, elbows tight, and gasped. "There's a little *room* in here!"

"There's always a little room in there," said Rosa. "*¡Dale!* Pass out the ties." (*¡Dale!* is what Rosa says instead of *please,* and also instead of *now.*)

---

The zip ties were bright pink, because of course they were.

"Zip ties are the scariest," said Rosa, "because they look like nothing. A little plastic strip. Like something off a bag of bread. Finnegan. Hold out your wrists. Cleo, tie him. Gently. This is the *only* time you tie a friend, and it is because a flamingo told you to."

Cleo looped the tie over Finnegan's flippers and pulled it snug.

"Now here is the strange part," said Rosa, "and it will feel *wrong.* Finnegan — *tighten it more.*"

"Tighten it?!"

"Grab the tail with your beak and pull. A loose tie slides around. A *tight* tie holds still — and I need the little plastic lock to be sitting right *here—*" she tapped the spot between his wrists "—in the middle. Not on the side. In the middle, facing you."

Finnegan pulled it tight with his beak. The lock clicked into place between his wrists.

"Now. Arms up. All the way. Like you're about to cheer."

Finnegan raised his tied flippers above his head. He looked like a penguin about to do a very small, very confused wave.

"And now," said Rosa, "you bring them *down.* Hard. Fast. Right into your own belly. And as you do it, your elbows go *out* and *back* — like a chicken. Like *wings.*" She flapped her own to demonstrate, which on a flamingo was enormous. "The lock is the weak spot. Your belly is the anvil. *¡Dale!*"

Finnegan took a breath. He brought his flippers down—

*Snap.*

The pink tie flew off in two pieces and landed in a boot.

Finnegan stared at his free flippers. He stared at the boot. Then, very slowly, he sat down on the floor.

"Are you all right?" said Lala, rushing over.

"I did it on the *first try,*" said Finnegan faintly. "I have never done *anything* on the first try. I once took eleven tries to *sit in a chair.*"

Cleo drew a small explosion coming out of the boot. Underneath, she wrote: *Panel 4: The Chicken Wing.*

---

"Not everybody can do the slam," said Rosa. "Some people are too small, or their arms are tied too tight, or they're scared and the slam won't come. So here's the quiet way."

She held up a shoelace.

"You put one end in your teeth, or under your foot, and you run the string back and forth *through* the tie, like a saw. Plastic against string. String wins — it takes a minute, and it hurts your wrist a little, and it *works.* Tico. You're the tie."

"I am *not* the tie."

"You are the tie."

Tico held a zip tie in his enormous beak, stiff as a board, while Lala — who was gentle, and had all the time in the world — sawed a shoelace across it. *Zzt. Zzt. Zzt.* Her whipped cream wobbled with concentration. On the fortieth stroke the tie parted, and Tico dropped it and announced, unprompted, "*SHE'S THROUGH, FOLKS. THE CROWD IS IN TEARS.*"

"The crowd is *three people and a walrus,*" said Cleo.

Herschel, who had come in for a mop, waved.

"Duct tape is easier," Rosa went on. "Same idea, no lock. Arms up—" Finnegan's arms went up so fast he nearly fell over "—slam *down* and *out,* like you're ripping open a present. Tape is strong going *around* and weak going *sideways.*

"Rope—" she picked up a length of it "—is the oldest one, so it has the oldest trick. When they're tying you, you puff up your chest like a proud pigeon and you *wiggle.* Little wiggles. Bored wiggles. Then when they're done you let your breath out and go small, and there's your **鬆** — a whole rope's worth of slack, right there, for free."

"What if there's tape on your mouth?" asked Lala, very quietly. It was the first time anybody had asked the real question underneath all the others.

Rosa looked at her for a moment. Then she answered it plainly, the way she answered everything.

"You work your jaw. Slowly. Side to side, up and down, like you're chewing a very tough shrimp. Tape hates a moving thing. It lets go a little at a time. And you *breathe through your nose,* and you stay calm, because calm is the whole secret. Panic pulls everything tighter. Calm makes room." She tucked her second leg back up and balanced, effortless. "Most of you will go your whole lives and never meet a zip tie that isn't holding a bread bag shut. Good. Know this anyway — an Academy is for knowing things you hope stay useless."

---

Cleo's comic had eleven panels by the time the sleet stopped.

Panel 1 was the burrito. Panel 2 was Lala's hug, drawn with little hearts and a *shlorp.* Panel 4 was the Chicken Wing and the exploding boot. Panel 7 was Tico as a zip tie, which he had not consented to and loved. Panel 9 was Rosa with both eyes open, which Cleo had drawn extra large, because it was rare. Panel 11 was just two words, in Cleo's neat slanted hand:

*Slack lives in front.*

"Every slope tells a story," said Cleo, tucking her pencil behind her ear. "This one's called *How to Untie a Penguin.*"

"I'd read that," said Finnegan.

"You *are* that."

Lala sat by the boot dryer with her hands clasped in front of her — palms in, elbows close — and turned her wrists very slowly, feeling the little secret space open and close.

"Coach Rosa?" she said. "I'm a cake. I'm not fast. I'm not strong. I'm mostly *milk.*"

"Mm," said a voice from the doorway. Auntie Hù had been standing there for some time, in the way tea eggs have of arriving before anyone notices.

"So I thought I couldn't do any of this. But the slack part — the staying-calm part — the wiggling part—" She looked up. "That's just *soaking it in,* isn't it? That's the thing I'm already good at."

Rosa, who had been about to fall asleep, opened one eye and looked at the cake for a long, quiet moment.

"Lala," she said. "That is the most Krav Maga thing anyone has said in this room all winter."

Lala glistened.

"…And then," said Rosa, closing the eye, "*shrimp.*"

Barnaby, from the hallway, produced shrimp. Nobody knew how. Nobody ever knew how.

---

That night, the boot-drying room was dark and warm and smelled like wet wool and cinnamon. Cleo's comic was taped to the wall — with duct tape, which Finnegan felt was in poor taste. Rosa slept standing on one leg by the sign. Tico snored through his canoe-paddle beak like a foghorn with opinions.

Lala lay on a folded blanket with her hands loose on her chest, palms in, and thought about slack.

Not the rope kind. The other kind. The kind where you take a breath and let it out and everything that was pulled tight gets a little bit of room. She had spent her whole life soaking things in — milk, mostly, and sunsets, and other people's sadness. It had never once occurred to her that soaking things in was a skill. That *staying soft* could be the strong thing.

She turned her wrists. There was room.

"**鬆,**" she whispered to the dark. *Loose.* And went to sleep exactly that way.

## Goodnight Blessing

Let your shoulders down, little one. Let your breath out.
There's always a little room, if you stay calm enough to find it.
Nothing is tied tonight — not your hands, not your heart, not your day.
Go loose. Go soft. Go to sleep.

## 🛡️ What We Learned

- **Hands in FRONT.** If someone ties you, ask for hands in front — wrists together, palms facing, elbows close. That's where the slack hides. Behind your back there's nothing.
- **Slack is a secret.** Relax your shoulders, turn your wrists, and the space between your hands grows. Calm makes room; panic pulls everything tighter.
- **Zip ties:** tighten it yourself so the lock sits between your wrists — arms up high — slam down hard into your belly with elbows flaring back like a chicken. Or saw it with a shoelace.
- **Duct tape:** arms up, slam down and *out*. Tape is strong going around, weak going sideways.
- **Rope:** puff your chest and wiggle while they tie; let your breath out after and wiggle for slack.
- **Mouth tape:** breathe through your nose, work your jaw slowly. Tape hates a moving thing.
- **Then run.** Getting loose is step one. The moment you're free — go, and tell a grown-up.
