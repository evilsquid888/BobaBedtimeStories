# Story 18: Don Café and the Cloud Forest Climb

**Main Character:** Don Café (a roasted Costa Rican coffee bean from the highlands)
**Guest Characters:** Gallo, Pinto
**Setting:** The misty, cloud-wrapped slopes of the Monteverde cloud forest
**Theme:** The higher you climb with friends, the clearer the view.
**Spanish Spotlight:** montaña (mohn-TAH-nyah) = mountain

---

It was so early that even the moon was thinking about going to bed.

Two tiny dots came bouncing up the dark trail — well, one bounced, and one rolled politely beside it. The bouncer was Gallo, a plump grain of fried rice in a little woven palm hat, hopping so fast his golden specks blurred. "Rise and pinto!" he sang, hum-hum-humming. The roller was Pinto, a glossy black bean with a pale stripe like a smile, carrying his tiny wooden spoon.

"Don Café! Don Café!" Gallo squeaked. "We're here for the climb! We woke up before the roosters! We woke up before the *idea* of roosters!"

At the top of a mossy step sat the oldest, kindest coffee bean in all the highlands. Don Café wore a straw farmer's hat and leaned on a miniature walking stick. He smelled, as he always did, of warm mornings and good news.

"Buenos días, little ones," he chuckled. "You are early. That is good. Today we climb the **montaña** (mohn-TAH-nyah) = mountain — and the mountain rewards those who begin in the dark."

Gallo gasped. "A mountain! A whole one?!"

"A whole one," said Don Café. "Good things brew slowly. So do good climbs."

---

The trail wound up into the cloud forest, where the trees wore sweaters of moss and the air was so wet you could practically *drink* the breeze. Mist drifted between the branches like soft gray cats nobody could quite pet.

Gallo zoomed ahead immediately.

"This is EASY!" he hollered from up the path. "Why do people complain about mountains? Mountains are just hills that got ambitious! Hum-hum!" He hopped onto a root, then a rock, then a higher rock — *boing boing BOING* — and promptly vanished into a patch of ferns with a muffled *thwump*.

"Gallo?" called Pinto, calm as ever.

A small voice from the ferns: "...I'm okay. The ferns hugged me. Tightly. Possibly forever."

Don Café tapped up the path at his slow, steady pace, *tik-tik-tik* went his little stick. He helped untangle Gallo from the friendly ferns. "Easy now, mae. The montaña is patient. The bean who races her arrives tired and sees nothing. The bean who walks with her arrives happy and sees everything."

"But I want to see the *quetzal*," Gallo whispered, eyes huge. "Tico says the resplendent quetzal is the most beautiful bird in the whole forest! Green like wet leaves and red like a sunset and a tail longer than a kite! He only comes at dawn! If we're too slow we'll MISS him!"

"Ahh," said Don Café, eyes twinkling. "Then we must not be too fast. The quetzal does not visit hurried hearts. He visits the ones who are *paying attention*."

Pinto nodded slowly and gave his little wooden spoon a thoughtful tap. "Better together, mae. We'll all watch out for each other. Slow and side by side."

---

So up they went, the three of them, into the silver mist.

And here is the funny thing about going slowly: you start to *notice* things.

Gallo, who had been a blur, was now a careful little hopper — and that's how he spotted the first wonder. "LOOK!" he whispered, pointing a grainy arm. A hummingbird, no bigger than Pinto, hung in the air beside a flower, its wings going *hmmmmmmm* so fast they had practically given up on being visible. "It's stuck! It's hovering! How is it doing that?!"

"It is drinking breakfast," said Don Café warmly. "Even the smallest creatures climb this montaña in their own way. That one just does it sideways. And upside down. And backwards." He chuckled. "Show-off."

The hummingbird zipped a tiny loop, as if it had heard and was rather proud, then vanished.

They climbed a little further, and Pinto — quiet, watchful Pinto — was the next to notice a wonder. He stopped rolling and pointed his wooden spoon at a leaf the size of a dinner plate. On it sat a frog so green and so glossy he looked like he'd been polished, with bright red eyes and little orange toes like ten tiny carrots.

"Buenos días," said Pinto politely.

The frog blinked his enormous red eyes — *blip, blip* — one at a time, which is a very dramatic way to blink. Then he yawned an enormous froggy yawn.

"He's sleepy too," whispered Gallo, delighted. "Everybody up here is just... cozy. The whole mountain is one big nap waiting to happen, hum."

"That is the cloud forest," said Don Café, smiling under his hat. "It wraps the world in a soft wet blanket and tells it *shhh*. A hurrying bean would have stepped right past that little fellow. But a slow bean? A slow bean gets a good-morning from a frog. That is no small thing, mae. That is a treasure."

The frog gave one more sleepy *blip*, then hopped — *ploop* — into the ferns, off to find a leaf even cozier than the last.

Higher they climbed. The path grew steeper, and the mist grew thicker, until the world was a cozy gray hush. Pinto, who was round and not built for steep slick rocks, began to slide *backward* on a wet patch — *eeeeeeeee* — like a marble on a tilted table.

"Pinto!" Gallo squeaked.

But Don Café was already there, planting his walking stick like a tiny fence post. "Lean on me, mae. A mountain teaches you something: nobody climbs it alone. Even the tallest tree is holding hands with a hundred roots you cannot see."

Pinto bumped gently to a stop against the stick, breathing out. "Gracias, Don Café."

"De nada," said the old bean. "Now — we rest. The slow path has a secret. It gives you time to *rest in good company*." He pulled three tiny dewdrops off a leaf and passed them around like cups of morning water. "Drink. The montaña offers refreshment to those who stop to accept it."

Gallo sipped his dewdrop and sighed the biggest sigh a grain of rice has ever sighed. "Okay. Okay. Slow is... kind of nice, hum."

As they rested, a tiny voice came drifting through the leaves: "Beak the NEWS! Beak the news, comin' through!"

Down swooped Tico the toucan, his huge rainbow beak parting the mist like a colorful little boat. He landed on a branch, wobbled, and tried very hard to look as though he hadn't almost fallen off.

"Don Café!" Tico announced to the entire mountain. "I have FLOWN the whole slope this morning and let me TELL you — the quetzal is in a marvelous mood! Best tail-fluffing I've seen all season! He's perched just above the misty window, oh, three hundred wingbeats up! But—" Tico leaned in, lowering his loud voice to a slightly-less-loud voice, "—he will NOT come down for noise. So if a certain bouncy grain of rice could keep it to a dull roar...?"

"I can be quiet," said Gallo, deeply offended and also extremely loud about it. "I am the *quietest*, hum!"

Don Café laughed his slow warm laugh. "We are nearly there, Tico. We came the slow way."

"The slow way is the *true* way up a montaña," Tico agreed solemnly — and then, because he was Tico, immediately ruined the solemn moment by yelping "BEAK THE NEWS!" and flapping off into the fog to tell everyone everything.

---

They were so cozy, resting on the mossy ledge, that they almost didn't notice the fog beginning to glow.

It happened slowly — like everything good. The gray turned pearl. The pearl turned gold. Somewhere far below, the sun was clearing the lowlands, and its light came up through the cloud forest in long shining ribbons, lighting each hanging dewdrop until the whole montaña sparkled like a tree full of tiny lamps.

"Ooooh," said all three of them at once.

"This," said Don Café softly, "is the gift of the slow climb. The hurried bean would be at the top already, sweaty and grumpy, looking at his own feet. But we — we are right *here*, exactly where the morning is happening."

Then came a sound.

A clear, soft *keow... keow...* — like a tiny silver bell wrapped in velvet.

Don Café went very still. "Listen," he breathed. "He is near. Move slowly. Move kindly. The quetzal trusts patience."

Gallo, who one hour ago could not have held still if you'd glued him, held *perfectly* still. His golden specks didn't even twinkle.

And then — through a window in the mist — he came.

The resplendent quetzal landed on a branch dripping with light. His chest was the deep red of a ripe coffee cherry. His back and head shimmered green-gold, every color the forest had ever dreamed of. And his tail — oh, his tail — streamed down off the branch like two ribbons of emerald river, longer than the bird himself, swaying gently in the dawn breeze.

Nobody breathed.

The quetzal turned his head, looked right at the three small travelers — at the tired round bean, the toasty grain, and the old farmer with the kind eyes — and gave one slow, friendly blink, as if to say: *Welcome. You came the right way.*

Then he lifted off, and his great tail trailed behind him like a comet made of feathers, looping once through the golden mist before he disappeared into the high green hush.

Gallo finally let out his breath. "He blinked at us," he whispered, a little wobbly. "He *blinked* at us, hum."

"He did," said Don Café. "And do you know why he showed himself to us, and not to the hurried ones?"

"Because we went slow?" said Pinto.

"Because we went slow *together*," said Don Café. "The higher you climb with friends, the clearer the view. One bean climbing alone sees only the next rock. But three friends climbing kindly? They see the whole montaña wake up."

---

They sat a while longer, the four of them — Gallo, Pinto, Don Café, and the warm new sun — and they watched the clouds roll across the slopes like a slow gentle sea.

"I take it back," Gallo announced. "About mountains being ambitious hills. They're not. They're patient grandparents. And the best ones let you sit on their shoulders." He yawned a tiny grainy yawn. "I'm sleepy now. The good kind. The earned kind."

"The very best kind," agreed Don Café, tipping his straw hat down over his eyes. "Pura Vida, little ones. Pura Vida."

Pinto tucked his wooden spoon away and leaned, contented, against the warm flank of the old coffee bean. Gallo curled up against Pinto. And the mist came back soft and slow, like a blanket the montaña pulled up over all of them.

"Better together, mae," murmured Pinto.

"Rise and pinto," hummed Gallo, already half-dreaming.

And the old mountain held them gently, the way mountains have held small sleepy travelers since the very first morning of the world.

## Goodnight Blessing
May your climbs always be slow enough to notice the hummingbirds, and your friends always close enough to lean on. May you wake to golden mist and a quetzal's quiet blink, and remember that the best views are the ones you reach side by side. Wherever your montaña is, little one, may you take it one kind step at a time. Snuggle in now, soft as cloud-forest moss. Pura Vida, dear one. Goodnight.
