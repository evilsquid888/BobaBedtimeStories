# Story 52: The Cy Dance Works Recital

**Main Character:** Coco the Granizado and Lala the Tres Leches
**Guest Characters:** Flan the Coconut Flan, Cajeta the Coconut Fudge, Tato the Patacon, Gallo, Pinto, Tamalita, Slowy the Sloth, Tico the Toucan, Doña Cy
**Setting:** Recital night at Cy Dance Works, a little dance studio in Pura Vida Town
**Theme:** Nerves are just excitement that hasn't found its rhythm yet — dancing is about heart, not being perfect.
**Spanish Spotlight:** **baile** (BYE-leh) — a dance

---

Backstage at Cy Dance Works, three minutes before the curtain went up, every single dancer was pretending not to be terrified, and every single one of them was terrible at it.

Cy Dance Works was the warmest little dance studio in all of Pura Vida Town — one cheerful room with a squeaky wooden floor, a wall of slightly wobbly mirrors, and string lights looped along the ceiling like a smile. Tonight the floor was a real stage, the folding chairs out front were packed with proud families, and behind the big red curtain the whole class of dessert friends stood lined up, shivering in their costumes.

"I'm cool," announced Coco the Granizado, who was not cool. He was sweating. A drip of rainbow syrup slid right off his shaved-ice shoulder and went *plip* on the floor. "I'm so cool I'm basically frozen. Tuanis."

"You just melted on my shoe," said Tato the Patacon.

"That's a dance move," said Coco. "I'm warming up."

This, dear reader, is how the bravest-looking dancer at Cy Dance Works very nearly fled out the back door of his own big night.

---

At the center of the backstage flutter stood Doña Cy herself, the dance teacher, tall and graceful in a long flowing skirt the color of sunset. She moved through the nervous crowd like a slow river, patting a shoulder here, straightening a costume there, humming so calmly that just being near her made your wobbles a tiny bit smaller.

"Breathe, mis amores," she said softly. "A **baile** (BYE-leh) — a dance — is not a test. There is no wrong way to feel the music. Those butterflies in your tummies? That is only your excitement, dancing ahead of you, looking for the beat."

"Mine found a DRUM SOLO," whispered Flan the Coconut Flan, wobbling so hard his caramel sloshed in slow shiny circles. "Steady, steady... ooh, wobbly! Doña Cy, I forgot everything. I forgot the steps. I forgot my own name. What's my name?"

"Flan," said Pinto gently.

"That's the one! Thank you. Now I've forgotten the steps AGAIN."

Doña Cy laughed her warm river laugh. "Then you will make up new ones, and they will be perfect, because they will be yours." She kissed the top of his jiggly head and glided on, leaving Flan looking slightly less like a puddle and slightly more like a pudding with a plan.

---

Out front, a loud and very excited voice boomed through the studio, because the emcee tonight was Tico the Toucan, and Tico the Toucan had never in his life said a quiet thing.

"BEAK THE NEWS, PURA VIDA TOWN!" he announced, his rainbow beak swinging toward the crowd. "Welcome to the Cy Dance Works recital, the GREATEST night of dancing this studio has EVER SEEN! Our first dancers are so nervous backstage I can SMELL it, and friends, it smells like caramel and panic!"

"TICO!" hissed everyone behind the curtain.

"THAT WAS A COMPLIMENT," Tico called back cheerfully.

Backstage, the nerves rippled down the line like a wave nobody wanted to ride. Cajeta the Coconut Fudge was bouncing so fast she'd become a small fuzzy blur. "I'm okay I'm okay I'm okay," she squeaked, ricocheting off a music stand. "Sweet and round, watch me — oof! — bound! Am I wearing my hat? I'm bouncing too fast to feel my own hat!"

"You're wearing it," said Gallo, ducking as she whizzed past. "Rise and pinto, Cajeta, you've got the peppiest number in the whole show."

"That's the PROBLEM," Cajeta wailed mid-bounce. "What if I'm peppy at the wrong TIME?"

Tamalita giggled a little puff of warm steam, which was what she did when she was nervous, so right now she was puffing like a tiny cozy kettle. "I keep forgetting to breathe out," she admitted, "and then I steam up the whole curtain." Sure enough, the red curtain had gone foggy in a perfect Tamalita-shaped patch.

And in the very back was Slowy the Sloth, who had a solo — an interpretive movement piece about a leaf. It was four and a half minutes long. So far, in rehearsals, Slowy had completed approximately one arm-raise.

"Are you nervous, Slowy?" asked Tato.

"Iiiii... willll... tellll... youuuu..." said Slowy, "...whennnn... I... fiiiinish... thinkinggg... about... ittt." He blinked, very slowly. "Puuura... viiida."

---

But the two who were the most nervous of all weren't saying so.

Coco the Granizado had the hip hop number — the big flashy one, all sharp freezes and spins and a backslide finish. Everybody expected it to be the coolest thing in the show, because Coco was the coolest one in the class, with his little round sunglasses and his breezy beach-skater swagger.

Only right now, in the wings, Coco's swagger had completely deserted him.

"What if I freeze?" he whispered to Lala the Tres Leches, beside him. "Not the cool kind. The bad kind, where I just — stop. Forget every move. And these stage lights are SO HOT, Lala. Hip hop is all sharp and crisp, and I'm shaved ice. What if I do a spin and just... slosh into a puddle in front of everybody's abuela?"

It was the most un-cool thing Coco had ever said, and saying it out loud made his sunglasses fog right up.

Lala looked at him with her big dreamy eyes — and then, instead of feeling better, she felt worse, because if even COCO was scared, what hope did a soft soggy cake have?

Lala had the lyrical number. The gentle one — drifting arms and soft turns, all feeling, the kind of dance that's supposed to make hearts ache in the nicest way.

"I'm going to ruin it," she whispered back. "Lyrical is supposed to be graceful, and look at me — I'm a square of cake soaked in three milks. I wobble when I stand STILL. And the music is so beautiful I already want to cry, and if I cry in the middle of my dance everyone will think I'm sad, and I'm not sad, I'm just — too FULL of feelings, all the time. It's so embarrassing."

The two of them stood there in the wings, the cool one and the soft one, both shaking, both certain they were the only one falling apart.

And that — that exact moment — was when they accidentally fixed each other.

---

"Wait," said Coco slowly, peering over his foggy sunglasses. "*You're* scared too?"

"You're scared too?" said Lala.

"I'm TERRIFIED," they said at the very same time, and then both of them burst out laughing — the surprised, relieved kind of laugh that pops out when you find out you were never the only one.

"I thought you had it all figured out," said Coco. "You're so... feel-everything. I thought feelings were your whole thing."

"And I thought NOTHING scared you," said Lala. "You're the cool one!"

"The cool one is having a meltdown behind a curtain," said Coco. "Literally. I think that's an oil spill of me on the floor."

Lala giggled and wiped her dreamy eyes, and as she did, something gentle clicked into place inside her. "Coco," she said slowly, "you just told me you might melt. And you said it like it was the worst thing in the world. But... what if it isn't?" Her wobble, for the first time all night, felt less like a flaw and more like an idea. "What if you melt a little, and you just... go with it? Melting is the most Coco thing there is."

Coco blinked behind his sunglasses.

"And you," he said back, the breezy grin sneaking onto his face for the first time all evening, "you keep saying you're too wobbly and too full of feelings for a soft dance. But Lala — lyrical IS feelings. The wobble isn't the mistake. The wobble's the whole dance. Nobody's ever gonna out-feel you out there. That's your superpower."

They stared at each other.

"Oh," whispered Lala.

"Yeah," said Coco.

Down the line, the others had gone quiet, listening — and one by one, the wobbles in the wings started to settle. Because it turns out the cure for being scared all alone is finding out you were never alone at all. Flan stopped sloshing. Cajeta touched down and stayed down. Tamalita breathed all the way out, and the foggy patch on the curtain cleared.

"We just cheer each other on," said Pinto softly, in his calm grounding voice. "From the wings. Every single number. Nobody dances out there by themselves — we're all backstage, pulling for you. Better together, mae."

"BETTER TOGETHER!" everyone whisper-shouted, and even Slowy raised one arm, which took him most of the rest of the show but he meant it from the very start.

---

"AND NOW," boomed Tico from the stage, "PUT YOUR HANDS, WINGS, FLIPPERS, AND SPOONS TOGETHER FOR... COCO THE GRANIZADO!"

Coco took one deep frosty breath, pushed his sunglasses up his nose, and skated out into the hot bright lights.

The music dropped — boom, boom, *boom-bap* — and Coco was OFF. Spins, sharp freezes, a backslide that made a little kid in the front row gasp out loud. He was crisp, he was cool, he was nailing it —

— and then, on the big spin near the end, his syrupy base hit a slick spot on the floor (his own melt, of course), and Coco's foot shot right out from under him.

Time slowed down. Backstage, every friend gasped.

But Coco remembered. *Go with it. Melting is the most Coco thing there is.* So instead of fighting the slip, he leaned into it — turned the wipeout into a long, low, impossibly smooth slide across the entire stage, popped up at the very end into a sudden, perfect, dead-still freeze-frame, one finger pointed at the ceiling, sunglasses gleaming.

The studio went bananas. Families leapt up. A grandmother banged a pot somewhere. It was the coolest move anybody had ever seen, and the very best part was that Coco hadn't planned it at all.

"THAT WAS ON PURPOSE!" Tico screamed. "WAS THAT ON PURPOSE? IT DOESN'T MATTER, IT WAS AMAZING!"

Coco skated off into the wings straight into a pile of his cheering friends, grinning so wide his sunglasses nearly fell off. "Stay frosty, stay sweet," he panted happily. "I melted and it was the best part. LALA. You were right. Now GO. It's you."

---

The lights went soft and golden. The slow, aching music began. And Lala the Tres Leches drifted out onto the stage, trembling like a cloud of whipped cream in a breeze.

She did her first slow turn — and her milk sloshed, just like she'd feared, a soft creamy wobble all down her front.

But she didn't stop. She thought about Coco sliding into his freeze, and she let the wobble become part of her, swaying gentle as a hammock, her arms drifting like soft milk poured slow. The music swelled, and Lala felt every single note of it, all the way down, the way she always did — and this time she didn't fight it. She let one happy tear roll right down her spongey cheek, glittering in the golden light, and then another.

And out in the dark, the whole audience felt it with her. A grown-up sniffled. Then another. Doña Cy, watching from the wings, pressed a hand to her heart. Because Lala wasn't performing the feelings — she was *feeling* them, right out loud, and that is the rarest and most beautiful thing a lyrical dancer can do.

She finished with her arms open wide, milk-streaked and wobbly and radiant, and the studio was completely, perfectly silent for one held breath —

— and then everyone was on their feet, clapping and crying and clapping some more.

Lala floated back into the wings in a happy daze. "I cried," she whispered. "I cried right in the MIDDLE."

"You made everybody cry," said Coco. "That's the whole job. There's not even a word for it."

"Soak it all in," Lala sighed, beaming through her tears, and for once she wasn't dramatic about it at all. She just meant it.

---

The rest of the recital tumbled by in a warm bright blur.

Flan wobbled out and forgot his steps exactly as predicted — so he made up brand-new ones, a jiggly caramel shimmy that had the whole crowd clapping along, which, Doña Cy noted, was somehow better than the routine. Cajeta bounced her peppy number so high she nearly touched the string lights, and her hat stayed on the whole time. Tamalita twirled in a happy puff of steam. Gallo and Pinto did a sweet side-by-side two-step, never more than an inch apart. Tato stomped a sturdy, ground-shaking solo and only fell over once, which for Tato counted as flawless.

And Slowy did his four-and-a-half-minute interpretive leaf. He completed nearly three whole movements. The audience, charmed beyond words, gave him a standing ovation while he was still finishing his second arm-raise. "Puuura... viiida," he murmured, deeply moved, about a minute after they sat back down.

---

When the very last bow was taken and the string lights were dimmed to a cozy glow, the friends peeled off their costumes and collapsed in one big tired heap on the squeaky studio floor — sweaty, sticky, sloshed, and prouder than they'd ever been in their lives.

Doña Cy sat down among them, her sunset skirt pooling around her like a warm blanket, and she didn't give a speech. She just looked around at all of them, her whole nervous, wonderful class, and smiled.

"You were all so scared," she said softly. "And you all danced anyway. That, mis amores, is the only step that ever truly matters."

"I almost ran out the back door," Coco admitted, melting contentedly into the floorboards.

"Me too," said Flan.

"Me three," puffed Tamalita.

"I'm still finishing my dance," murmured Slowy, raising one slow arm, and everybody laughed.

Lala the Tres Leches yawned an enormous, milky yawn and snuggled in between Coco and Cajeta. Outside, Pura Vida Town had gone quiet and dark, and through the studio window the stars were coming out, unhurried, one by one, like a slow applause that would never quite end.

"Best **baile** ever," Coco mumbled sleepily.

And in that little dance studio with the squeaky floor and the string-light smile, full of friends who had been brave together, nobody could think of a single reason to disagree.

## Goodnight Blessing

Little dancer, those flutters in your tummy when something big is coming? They're not fear, not really — they're your excitement, dancing ahead of you, looking for the beat. May you always find that you were never the only one who was scared. May you melt a little, and wobble a little, and discover that the melting and the wobbling were the most beautiful part all along. You don't have to be perfect, sweet one. You only have to dance from your heart, and let yourself feel it all the way down. Be brave tonight, and be proud of yourself — you did so much braver things today than you ever stop to notice. The stars are out, slow and soft, clapping just for you. Pura vida, sleepy one. Goodnight.
