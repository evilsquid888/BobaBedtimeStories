# Story 14: The Tree Frog Lullaby

**Main Character:** Slowy the Sloth
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** The rainforest at dusk, full of red-eyed tree frogs
**Theme:** Everyone has a song worth adding to the night's music.
**Spanish Spotlight:** rana (RAH-nah) = frog

---

Slowy the Sloth was hanging upside down from his favorite branch, doing what he did best — absolutely nothing, very slowly, and feeling marvelous about it.

The sun was sliding behind the mountains like a warm egg yolk slipping off a spoon, and the whole **selva** (SEL-vah) = jungle was beginning to glow gold and pink. Slowy yawned. The yawn took about four minutes. Halfway through, he forgot he was yawning and started again.

"Puuura... viiida...," he sighed, which was Slowy's way of saying *life is good and there is no need to rush, ever, about anything, including this sentence.*

Then — *PLOP.*

A small green something landed right on Slowy's belly. It had two enormous tomato-red eyes, bright orange feet like little sticky mittens, and it was trembling so hard it made Slowy's tummy fur jiggle.

"Well, helloooo," Slowy drawled, blinking down at it. "You... are... a verrry... fast... visitor."

---

The little creature gulped. "I'm s-sorry! I didn't mean to land on you! I jumped to get away and — and — oh no, oh no, oh no."

"A **rana** (RAH-nah) = frog," Slowy said warmly, recognizing the tomato eyes at last. "A red-eyed tree frog. How... lovely."

"My name is Renato," the little frog squeaked. "And I have a terrible, *terrible* problem."

Now, Slowy did not get alarmed about much. Once a coconut had fallen and bonked him on the head, and he'd simply said "ow" about a half-hour later. But he could tell this little **rana** was truly upset, so he gave his very slowest, kindest blink.

"Tell me... everything," Slowy said. "We have... all night."

Renato's red eyes welled up. "That's just it! It IS all night! The Lullaby starts soon!"

"The... Lullaby?"

"You don't know about the Lullaby?" Renato gasped, so shocked he nearly fell off Slowy's belly. "It's only the most important thing in the whole **selva**! Every single night, when the stars come out, all the frogs sing together. Big frogs, tiny frogs, croaky frogs, peepy frogs. We layer our voices and it becomes this... this enormous, cozy blanket of sound, and the whole jungle uses it to fall asleep. The toucans. The turtles. The little baby monkeys. Even the river slows down to listen."

"That sounds... beautiful," Slowy said dreamily.

"It IS beautiful!" Renato wailed. "And tonight is MY night to add my peep. I've waited my whole life for my turn. And I — I —"

He hid his face in his orange mittens.

"I'm too scared. My voice is too small. Everybody else booms and trills and croaks like champions, and I just go *peep.* A teeny, embarrassing little *peep.* What if everyone laughs? What if I ruin the whole Lullaby? What if the babies *don't fall asleep* and it's all my fault?"

And with that, the little red-eyed **rana** burst into the smallest, saddest froggy tears you ever did see — about the size of dewdrops, which is exactly what they looked like.

---

Slowy thought about this for a long, long moment. (For Slowy, a long moment was roughly the length of a nap.) He gazed up through the canopy, where the first stars were poking out like sleepy fireflies.

"Renato," he said at last, "may I... tell you... a secret?"

The frog sniffled. "Okay."

"I... cannot... sing."

Renato peeked out from his mittens. "You can't?"

"Not... at all," Slowy admitted serenely. "When I try, the birds... fly away. The bugs... cover their... ears. Once I sang... and a flower... closed up... to get away... from me." He smiled his slow, mossy smile. "But do you know... what I CAN do?"

"What?"

"I can... listen. And here is... the thing... I have learned... from a lifetime... of listening." Slowy lowered his voice to a gentle, drowsy hum. "Every song... in the **selva**... is made of small sounds. The big croaks... are only loud... because the small peeps... are underneath... holding them up. Take away... the little peeps... and the whole song... falls flat... like a hammock... with no tree."

Renato blinked his big red eyes. "Really?"

"The Lullaby... is not a contest, mae," said Slowy kindly. "Nobody wins it. It is a blanket. And a blanket... needs every... single... thread."

Renato sat up a little straighter on Slowy's belly. But then his shoulders drooped again. "But what if I open my mouth and nothing comes out? What if I freeze?"

"Ahhh," said Slowy. "Then... we practice. Slowly. My favorite... speed."

---

So there, in the gold-and-purple dusk, the slowest creature in the entire rainforest gave singing lessons to the smallest, shyest **rana** in the world.

"First," Slowy instructed, "take... a big... breath." He demonstrated. The breath took so long that two leaves drifted down and a beetle walked all the way across his nose before he finished.

Renato giggled — a tiny, watery, hiccuping giggle. "That's the slowest breath I've ever seen."

"Thank... you," said Slowy proudly. "Now... you."

Renato puffed up his little throat like a shiny green balloon. He squeezed his tomato eyes shut. He opened his mouth and —

"...peep," he whispered. So quietly that a passing moth didn't even notice.

"Beautiful," said Slowy.

"That was *terrible,*" moaned Renato.

"It was... a start," said Slowy. "Again. But this time... do not sing... for the whole jungle. Just... sing for me. One sloth. One friend."

Renato looked at Slowy — at his kind half-moon eyes, at his patient, mossy grin, at the way he hung there so cozy and unbothered, like the whole world was a soft blanket and there was nothing to fear in it at all.

The little frog took a breath. A *Slowy-sized* breath, nice and big.

"*Peep!*"

This one came out clear and round and bright, like a single glass bead dropped into a still pond.

"OH!" said Renato, clapping his orange mittens over his mouth. "That one felt different!"

"That one," said Slowy, "had your heart in it." He gave a slow nod. "Now... do you hear that?"

---

From far across the darkening **selva** came a sound. It started low — a single deep *gronk* from a bullfrog down by the river. Then another joined: *gribbit, gribbit.* Then a high silvery trill. Then twenty more voices, then a hundred, then too many to count, all weaving together into the warmest, most enormous, most velvety blanket of sound you can imagine.

The Lullaby had begun.

Renato froze. His red eyes went wide as guava halves. "It's starting. It's *starting!* And I'm all the way up here on your tummy instead of on the singing branch and oh no oh no —"

"Renato," said Slowy gently.

The frog stopped.

"You do not have to run... to the singing branch," Slowy said. "The branch... is wherever... you are. The Lullaby... goes everywhere. The **selva**... is one big song... and you... are already... inside it."

Renato looked up at the stars, now sprinkled thick as sugar across the sky. He listened to the great cozy blanket of frog voices, rising and falling, rising and falling, like the whole jungle was breathing in its sleep.

And he realized — there *was* a little empty space in the song. A tiny gap, light and high, exactly the shape of one small peep.

His peep.

The space had been waiting for him all along.

Renato closed his beautiful red eyes. He took one more big, slow, Slowy-sized breath. And then, for the very first time in his whole life, the smallest **rana** in the rainforest lifted up his voice and sang.

"*Peep. Peep. Peep-peep-peep.*"

It was small. It was bright. It was perfectly, exactly right.

And the most wonderful thing happened: the great booming Lullaby seemed to *brighten,* like a song that had been missing one note and just found it. The deep croaks rolled warmer. The silvery trills sparkled higher. And tucked right in the middle, holding it all up like a thread in a blanket, was one little peep — Renato's peep — making the whole thing sweeter.

"You hear that?" Renato whispered between peeps, his eyes shining. "I'm in it! I'm part of the song!"

"You always... were," said Slowy.

---

All across the rainforest, the **selva** began to drift off to sleep.

The toucans tucked their big rainbow beaks under their wings. The baby monkeys curled into furry little commas against their mamas. Down at the shore, a sea turtle paused in the moonlit waves to listen, and even the river slowed its rushing to a soft, sleepy murmur, just to hear the Lullaby better.

And nobody — not one single creature in the whole jungle — knew that the sweetest thread in that night's song belonged to a tiny frog who had nearly been too shy to sing it.

Well. Nobody except one very slow, very wise, very happy sloth.

Renato sang and sang, peep after peep after peep, until his little throat grew tired and his red eyes grew heavy. Finally he hopped up onto Slowy's chest, curled into the warm mossy fur, and let the great cozy blanket of song wrap all around him.

"Slowy?" he yawned. "Thank you for helping me find my voice."

"I did not... find it," Slowy murmured, already half-asleep. "It was... always yours. I only... helped you... listen for it."

"Puuura... viiida," whispered Renato, trying out Slowy's favorite words.

"Puuura... viiida," Slowy agreed.

And high in the branches, under a sky stuffed full of stars, the slowest creature and the smallest **rana** in all the **selva** fell fast asleep together, rocked gently by a lullaby that — at long, long last — had every single voice in it.

---

## Goodnight Blessing

Little one, somewhere out there the whole jungle is singing you to sleep, and your voice has a place in it too — soft and small and perfectly, exactly right. You do not have to be the loudest. You do not have to rush. Just breathe a big slow breath, and let the night wrap around you like warm mossy fur. The stars are out, the song is full, and there is nothing at all left to do but rest. Puuura... viiida, sweet dreamer. Goodnight.
