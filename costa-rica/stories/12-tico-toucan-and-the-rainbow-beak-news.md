# Story 12: Tico Toucan and the Rainbow-Beak News

**Main Character:** Tico the Toucan
**Guest Characters:** Slowy the Sloth
**Setting:** The treetops of a noisy, colorful jungle
**Theme:** Sharing good news spreads joy faster than gossip.
**Spanish Spotlight:** **pájaro** (PAH-hah-roh) = bird

---

High in the dripping-green treetops, before the sun had even finished yawning, a glossy black **pájaro** (PAH-hah-roh) = bird burst out of a fig tree like a sneeze with feathers.

"BEAK THE NEWS!" Tico hollered, flapping so hard a beetle fell off his branch in surprise. "GOOOOD MORNING, JUNGLE! It is I, your one and only town-crier, here to tell you EVERYTHING!"

His enormous beak — striped in banana-yellow, leafy-green, and sunset-orange, like a rainbow that had decided to become a nose — caught the first light and practically glowed. Tico loved his beak. He loved it more than mangoes, which is saying a lot, because Tico loved mangoes the way puddles love rain.

"Today's headlines!" he announced to absolutely no one yet. "A frog hiccuped! A flower opened! Somewhere, a leaf is THINKING about falling! Stay tuned!"

A sleepy voice drifted up from below.

"Tiiicooo... it is suuuch an early hour for so much... shouting..."

It was Slowy the Sloth, hanging upside-down from a mossy limb, blinking his half-moon eyes very, very slowly.

"It's NEVER too early for news, Slowy!" Tico chirped, hopping in tiny circles. "The jungle DEPENDS on me! Without Tico, nobody would know ANYTHING!"

Slowy smiled the way sloths do — like a sunrise that's in no hurry. "Puuura... viiida..." he murmured, which is the Costa Rican way of saying *pure life* — relax, all is well, breathe. "But Tico... before the words fly... let them... ripen. Like a good mango. Like a slow... coffee."

"Ripen? Slowy, by the time YOU finish a sentence, the news is YESTERDAY'S news!" Tico laughed, doing a little loop-the-loop. "No no no. News must be FRESH! News must be FAST! News must be —" he paused, struck by his own brilliance — "**chismes** (CHEES-mehs) = gossip, but the GOOD kind! The kind everyone whispers about!"

Slowy's eyebrows rose roughly one whisker per second. "There is no... good kind... of chismes, mi amigo. Only... truth that helps... or talk that... tangles."

But Tico was already gone.

He swooped through the canopy, beak first, gossip second. By the great kapok tree he spotted a flurry of motion and a squeaky little voice going "EEK! EEK! EEK!"

"OOH! NEWS!" gasped Tico, screeching to a flappy halt.

Down among the roots, a tiny brown agouti — a sort of jungle rabbit-mouse with a backside like a furry teakettle — was bouncing up and down, squeaking and pointing at the river.

Tico did not ask what was wrong. Tico did not get closer. Tico did the thing Tico always did: he made up the most exciting story he could imagine and zoomed off to tell the world.

"BEAK THE NEWS!" he bellowed at the top of the canopy. "TERRIBLE TROUBLE BY THE RIVER! The agouti is in a PANIC! Something HUGE and SCARY came out of the water! Possibly a MONSTER! Possibly a CROCODILE WEARING A HAT! EVERYONE PANIC IMMEDIATELY!"

Now, here is the trouble with having the biggest, brightest, loudest beak in the whole jungle.

People believe it.

The howler monkeys heard "MONSTER," and started howling. The parrots heard the howler monkeys and shrieked "MONSTERRRR!" The frogs heard the parrots and dove under leaves. A family of coatis grabbed their snacks and ran the wrong way. Somewhere, a very confused butterfly fainted dramatically into a flower.

In about the time it takes to peel a mango, the whole jungle was in an enormous, feathery, furry, flapping FLAP.

"AHHH, A MONSTER!"
"WHERE?!"
"I DON'T KNOW, TICO SAID SO!"
"WELL THEN IT MUST BE TRUE, HE HAS THE RAINBOW BEAK!"

Tico landed on a branch, puffing out his chest, very pleased. "Now THAT," he said proudly, "is a headline."

He preened a glossy feather and admired the chaos below the way an artist admires a painting. Branches snapped. Leaves rained down. A toucan two trees over started his OWN panic broadcast, except he'd misheard, so now half the jungle thought the monster was a CROCODILE WEARING A *CAT*, which was somehow even more upsetting. A wise old tapir tried to organize an evacuation but kept walking into the same tree.

"Magnificent," sighed Tico. "I am SO good at my job."

But the pleased feeling didn't last.

Because down by the river, a small wobbly cry rose up over the chaos — a familiar, sweet, jiggly little voice.

"Helloooo? Could somebody — oh dear, steady, steady — could somebody PLEASE come help? I'm a teeny bit STUCK!"

Tico's feathers drooped. He knew that voice. It was Flan the Coconut Flan — the politest, wobbliest, most caramel-drizzled friend in all of Costa Rica. And nobody was helping her, because everybody was too busy running from a crocodile in a hat that didn't exist.

Tico flew down, beak suddenly feeling very heavy.

There was no monster. There was no crocodile. There was no hat.

There was only Flan, who had wobbled down to the riverbank for a morning drink and gotten her jiggly bottom wedged between two smooth stones, jiggling helplessly like a golden pudding in a gentle earthquake. The agouti hadn't been panicking about a monster at all — the little fellow had been squeaking "HELP! HELP! HELP!" trying to get someone to come unstick her.

"Oh," said Tico, in a very small voice for such a large beak. "Oh, *cielos*. Oh no."

"Tico!" Flan wobbled hopefully. "Thank goodness, a familiar face! Could you — ooh, wobbly! — could you possibly fetch some help? Everyone seems to have run off in the WRONG direction. They keep yelling about a hat."

Tico hung his head. The whole jungle was sprinting away from the river — away from Flan — because of HIS news. His big, loud, made-up, rainbow-beak news.

A slow, peaceful rustle came through the leaves. It was Slowy, arriving the way Slowy always arrived: eventually.

"Aaah," said Slowy, taking in the scene over roughly the length of a nice nap. "I seee... no monster. Just... a friend... who needs... a hand." He blinked. "Or a... flipper. Or a... beak."

"Slowy, I messed up," Tico whispered. "I told everyone the WRONG story. I didn't even ASK. I just — I just wanted a big headline." His rainbow beak, usually held high and proud, drooped toward his toes.

Slowy smiled, slow as honey off a spoon. "Then... fix it... with the same beak. A beak that... shouts trouble... can also... shout truth. The words you choose... that is the difference, mi amigo."

"But what if everyone's still ANGRY at me?" Tico fretted, scuffing the branch with one toe. "What if they don't believe the truth because I told them a whopper first?"

"Then you... tell it... anyway," said Slowy, settling in for a long, comfortable thought. "Because the truth... does not need everyone... to believe it... right away. It only needs... someone... brave enough... to say it. And then... it grows. Slow... like a tree. But strong... like a tree, too."

Tico looked up. His beak was still big. Still bright. Still the loudest thing in the canopy. But now he understood — a thing that powerful could either start a flap, or stop one.

He took the biggest breath a toucan has ever taken.

"BEAK THE NEWS!" he thundered, and the whole jungle froze. "CORRECTION! REPEAT, CORRECTION! There is NO monster! There is NO crocodile! There is DEFINITELY no hat! Our friend FLAN is stuck by the river and needs a hand — bring your strongest arms and your softest hearts! This is the BEST news of all: somebody needs HELP, and WE GET TO BE THE ONES TO GIVE IT!"

The jungle blinked.

Then something wonderful happened. The same beak that had whipped everyone into a panic now pulled them together like a magnet. The howler monkeys came swinging — arm over arm over arm. The coatis came galloping (the right way, this time). The parrots formed a cheering, fluttering rainbow overhead. Even the fainted butterfly woke up and came to supervise.

"Heave!" called Tico, perched between the two stones, conducting like the leader of a very feathery orchestra. "And HO! And jiggle to the left! And — *un, dos, tres!*"

The howler monkeys gripped the stones. The coatis braced their little paws. A parrot, who was no help at all, mostly just shouted encouragement. And the tiny agouti — the very one who'd been squeaking for help all along — squeezed in beside the biggest stone and shoved with all his teakettle-shaped might.

With a great squelchy *FWOOMP*, Flan popped free and wobbled magnificently into the soft river grass, caramel glistening, completely unharmed and only slightly dizzy.

"Steady, steady... oooh... wobbly!" she giggled, settling at last. "Oh, thank you, EVERYONE! And thank you, Tico! What a kind thing to announce!"

"And I'm SO sorry about the monster," Tico said, hanging his head. "There never was one. I made it up. I should have just... asked."

Flan blinked her caramel-glazed eyes. "A monster? Goodness! No wonder the howler monkeys hugged me three times each on the way out!" She giggled until steam-free little wobbles ran all down her sides. "Don't you fret, dear Tico. You also made the RESCUE. That counts double in my book — and my book is mostly recipes, but still."

Tico's chest puffed up — but it was a different kind of puff this time. Not the look-at-ME kind. The we-did-this-TOGETHER kind, which is a much cozier feeling and fits inside your chest much more comfortably.

Slowy ambled over at last, just in time to do absolutely none of the lifting. "Welll done," he yawned warmly. "Yooou see, Tico... bad news... flies fast... but good news? Kind news? *True* news?" His slow smile spread all the way across his fuzzy face. "It flies... fastest of all... because everyone... wants to catch it."

From that day on, Tico kept being the loudest **pájaro** in the jungle — because some birds simply ARE, and there's no use squishing it. But before he hollered a single headline, he did three small new things. He asked. He looked. He listened.

"BEAK THE NEWS!" he'd call each morning, beak blazing in the sunrise. "A flower bloomed by the falls — go SEE it! Tato learned a new joke — go LAUGH at it! Somewhere a friend could use a hug — go GIVE it! This has been Tico, your trusty *pájaro*, telling you the loveliest truth I could find. *Pura vida!*"

And the funny thing was — the jungle had never been happier. Because it turns out that when the loudest voice in the treetops decides to spread joy instead of jitters, the whole forest catches it, branch by branch by branch, like sunlight tumbling down through the leaves.

Down below, Slowy hung peacefully, eyes half-closed, listening to the good news drift by.

"Puuura... viiida..." he hummed.

It really, really was.
