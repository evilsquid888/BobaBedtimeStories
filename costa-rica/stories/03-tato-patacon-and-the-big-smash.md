# Story 3: Tato Patacon and the Big Smash

**Main Character:** Tato the Patacon
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A roadside soda (little family diner) with a bubbling fryer
**Theme:** Sometimes getting flattened just makes you stronger and crunchier.
**Spanish Spotlight:** **tuanis** (TWAH-nees) = cool / awesome

---

On a sleepy curve of jungle road, where the howler monkeys yawned and the rain smelled like leaves, there sat a tiny **soda** (SOH-dah) = little family diner — the cheerful sort with three plastic tables, a wobbly fan, and a fryer that bubbled like a happy little volcano.

And inside the kitchen, on a wooden cutting board, sat Tato.

Tato was a green plantain. Round. Chunky. Pleasingly potato-shaped, if you squinted. He had a cozy little belly, two stubby feet, and a habit of humming when he was nervous — which, that particular morning, was constantly.

"Okay," Tato whispered to himself, wobbling in a small circle. "Today is the day. The big day. The big... *squish* day."

He gulped.

Across the kitchen, the fryer went *blub-blub-bloop*, and Tato squeaked and hid behind the salt shaker, which is not a very good hiding place when you are slightly larger than the salt shaker.

---

You see, every plantain in the soda knew the legend. When you were ripe and ready, Doña Mari — the round, smiling cook with flour on her elbows — would scoop you up, fry you golden, and then... *flatten you.* Smash you flat with the bottom of a cup. Pancake-flat. Coin-flat. Flatter than a sleeping iguana.

And THEN, only then, did you become a **patacón** (pah-tah-KOHN) = a twice-fried smashed plantain. The crispy, crunchy, salty star of every plate.

But Tato did not want to be flattened. Tato liked being round. Round was comfortable. Round was *him.*

"What if I get smashed and I don't like it?" he fretted, pacing. "What if flat is itchy? What if I forget how to hum? What if — *oh no* — what if I'm not even tasty?"

The biggest worry sat in his belly like a stone: *What if changing means I won't be me anymore?*

He had been practicing being round for his entire life. He was *good* at it. He could roll left. He could roll right. He could roll in a slow, dignified circle and stop with his stubby feet pointing in exactly the right direction. Once, he had rolled all the way across the cutting board, bonked the pepper grinder, and bounced back — a feat the other plantains still talked about with great respect.

"You can't be round if you're flat," he muttered. "It's just a fact. It's *plantain science.*"

From the shelf above, a deep, slow voice rumbled like warm gravel.

"Hmmm. A worried little plantain. I know that hum."

It was Don Café, the old coffee bean, resting in his jar with his tiny straw hat tipped over his eyes. He smelled wonderful, like a cozy morning hug.

"Don Café!" Tato cried, scrambling up the spice rack. "They're going to *smash* me. Flat! I'll be totally different! I don't want to be different. I just got used to being a *me-shaped* me."

Don Café chuckled, a sound like beans rolling down a hill. "Ah, little one. Let me tell you something." He leaned forward on his miniature walking stick. "Once, I was just a soft green berry on a bush. Squishy. Plain. Did nothing all day but dangle."

"That sounds nice," said Tato hopefully.

"It was *boring,*" said Don Café. "Then the sun roasted me. Cracked me. Changed me completely. And *that* is when I became... *delicious.* Good things brew slowly, mae. But they do brew." He winked. "Sometimes a little squish is just the start of the good part."

Tato chewed on this. It did not make the fryer less bubbly. But it made the stone in his belly a tiny bit lighter.

"But Don Café," he said, "what if I'm in the middle of being squished and I change my mind? What if I want to be round again? You can't *un-smash* a thing. There's no... no *re-round* button."

"No," Don Café agreed kindly. "There is not. But tell me, little one — when was the last time you were truly happy being round?"

Tato opened his mouth. Then he closed it. Because, if he was being honest, rolling in a dignified circle and bonking the pepper grinder had been fun for about, oh, *one afternoon.* After that it had mostly been... waiting. Waiting in the basket. Waiting for something to happen. Waiting to *become.*

"Hmm," said Tato.

"Hmm," agreed Don Café, and tipped his hat back over his eyes for a little snooze, because wise old beans give exactly one piece of advice at a time and then they nap.

---

Just then — *FWOOMP* — the kitchen doors swung open, and in marched the breakfast rush.

The little soda filled up fast. A bus had stopped outside, and now there were hungry travelers everywhere, tummies rumbling, calling out for **gallo pinto** and eggs and — oh dear — *patacones.*

"¡Patacones, por favor!" sang a lady in a sunhat.

"Make mine extra crispy!" said a man with a fishing rod.

"Crispy, crispy, CRISPY!" chanted three small children, banging their forks like tiny drummers.

The whole kitchen burst into a flurry. Eggs hissed. Coffee gurgled. The wobbly fan spun so fast it lifted one corner of a napkin and waved it like a tiny flag. And the basket of plantains, where Tato lived, began to *empty.*

One by one, his neighbors were scooped up.

"Adiós, Tato!" called a plump plantain named Pepa, as Doña Mari lifted her toward the board. "See you on the other side of the smash! I hear it's *tuanis* over there!"

"Wait — what's it like?" Tato squeaked. "Does it hurt? Is it itchy? PEPA—"

But Pepa was already golden and giggling, and Tato could only watch as the cup came down — *SMASH* — and his friend spread out into the most beautiful crispy disc, ruffled at the edges, glowing like a little sun. Pepa did not look hurt. Pepa looked *delighted.* Pepa looked, frankly, like she was having the best day of her entire plantain life.

"Huh," whispered Tato, very quietly.

Doña Mari laughed her big round laugh and reached again into the basket of plantains.

And her warm hand closed gently around... Tato.

"Oh no oh no oh no," Tato hummed, very fast, his little feet kicking. "It's happening it's happening it's HAPPENING—"

"Pura vida, plantain," said Doña Mari softly, the way she said it to all her plantains, like a lullaby. And she sliced Tato into thick, friendly rounds — which, surprisingly, did not hurt one bit, and only tickled — and *plop, plop, plop,* into the bubbly fryer they went.

---

Now, here is the part Tato had dreaded most. But here is the part Tato had not expected.

The fryer was *warm.* Not scary-warm. *Bubble-bath* warm. The oil hugged him on every side, and tiny golden bubbles giggled up around his edges like ticklish fizz.

"Oh," said Tato, surprised. "Oh, this is — heh — this is actually kind of *tuanis.*"

He started to turn golden. He felt himself getting firmer, stronger, his soft middle settling into something cozy and warm. The travelers cheered when they smelled it. The children sniffed the air and went *mmmmmm.*

But Tato was still round. Round and golden and toasty.

And then Doña Mari lifted him out, set him on the board, picked up the bottom of a wide tin cup, and —

"Here it comes," Tato whispered. He squeezed his eyes shut. He remembered Don Café. He took a breath. "Smash now... worry never."

*SMASH.*

---

For one whole second, the world went flat and quiet.

And then Tato opened his eyes.

He was wide. He was thin. He was *enormous* now, spread out like a little golden moon, his edges crinkled into a hundred crunchy ruffles.

But the most surprising thing of all?

He felt *amazing.*

"Wait," said Tato. He gave himself a little wiggle. *Crunch.* He gasped. "Was that — did I just — *CRUNCH?*" He wiggled again. *Crackle-crunch.* "I'm *crunchy!* I'm CRISPY! I have — I have RUFFLY EDGES!"

Back into the fryer he went for a second golden swim, and *this* time he wasn't scared at all. This time he splashed.

"Whee!" Tato cheered, bubbles fizzing all around his crispy new ruffles. "Look at me! I'm not less me — I'm MORE me! I'm me with EXTRA CRUNCH!"

When Doña Mari fished him out, sprinkled him with a kiss of salt, and stood him up on the plate, Tato stretched his wide golden self as tall as a flat thing can stretch.

"I am," he announced grandly, "the most *tuanis* patacón this soda has ever seen."

From the spice shelf, Don Café tipped his little straw hat and beamed. "What did I tell you, mae? The good part."

---

But the very best moment was still to come.

Doña Mari carried Tato out to the table where the three small children were waiting, forks at the ready. She set the plate down right in the middle. And all three children's eyes went huge and round and shining, the way eyes only go for something truly wonderful.

"WHOA," said the smallest one. "That's the crispiest one EVER."

And Tato — flat, golden, ruffly, *brand-new* Tato — felt his whole crunchy self glow with pride. Because he understood it now, all the way down to his salt.

Getting smashed hadn't made him less.

It had made him the best, brightest, crispiest version of himself — the version that made children's eyes go wide. Like a quiet little firework that had finally, *finally* gotten to light up the sky.

"Go on," he wanted to tell them, and somehow they heard. "I worked very hard to get this crunchy. Enjoy every bite."

And oh, how he wished he could tell his round little this-morning self about it. *Stop fretting,* he'd say. *Stop hiding behind the salt shaker. The squish isn't the end of the story, you silly green dumpling — it's the very best chapter.*

*Crunch.* Cheers around the table.
*Crunch crunch.* Happy little kicking feet.
*Cruuunch.* Pure, golden joy.

And Tato had never, in all his round green days, felt so completely, deliciously, *patacón-ly* himself.

---

That night, when the soda was quiet and the fryer had hushed to a sleepy *blip... blip... blip,* the leftover plantains snuggled in their basket and listened as Don Café told the story of the round little plantain who became the crispiest star of all.

"Don't be afraid of the squish, little ones," he murmured, his straw hat low. "The squish is just the door. And behind it..." He smiled. "...the good part."

And somewhere out in the warm jungle night, a brave golden patacón — proud, crunchy, and entirely at peace — drifted off to the happiest sleep of his life.
