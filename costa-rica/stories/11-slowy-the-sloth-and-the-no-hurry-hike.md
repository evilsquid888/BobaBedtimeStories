# Story 11: Slowy the Sloth and the No-Hurry Hike

**Main Character:** Slowy the Sloth
**Guest Characters:** Cajeta the Coconut Fudge
**Setting:** A lush green rainforest canopy dripping with vines
**Theme:** Slowing down lets you notice the most beautiful things.
**Spanish Spotlight:** **selva** (SEL-vah) = jungle

---

High in the green roof of the rainforest, where the vines hung down like wet noodles and the air smelled of rain and warm leaves, lived the slowest, smiliest fellow in all of Costa Rica.

His name was Slowy.

Slowy was a fuzzy, mossy-furred sloth with sleepy half-moon eyes and a gentle smile that never, ever went away. He moved at the speed of dripping honey. Right now, for example, he was reaching for a leaf. He had started reaching for that leaf about an hour ago. He was nearly there. Almost. Just a little more.

"Puuura... viiida...," Slowy hummed, which was sloth for *life is good and there is no hurry whatsoever.*

And that is exactly when a small golden ball came rocketing through the trees like a sneeze with legs.

"HELLO-HELLO-HELLO!" it squealed, bouncing off three vines and a mushroom. "I'm Cajeta! Sweet and round, watch me bound!"

---

Cajeta was a tiny ball of coconut fudge, chewy and golden, rolled in toasted coconut shreds so she looked deliciously fuzzy. She bounced when she was excited, and Cajeta was *always* excited. She bounced when she was happy. She bounced when she was nervous. She bounced when she was thinking, which made her thoughts come out in little hops: *what's-that — and-that — and-OOH-that!* Once she bounced so hard during a hiccup that she landed in a toucan's breakfast. The toucan had been *very* surprised. Cajeta had simply waved and bounced right back out, leaving a trail of coconut crumbs and a confused, hungry bird.

"Are you the famous Slowy?" Cajeta zipped in a circle around his head. "Everyone says you know the **selva** (SEL-vah) = jungle better than anybody! I want to see EVERYTHING. The waterfall! The flowers! The secret heartbeat of the jungle! Let's GO! Right now! This second! Hurry-hurry-hurry!"

Slowy turned his head to look at her. This took a while.

"Hellooo, little friend," he said, each word as slow and soft as a yawn. "Yes... I know the selva. Would you... like to take... a hike?"

"YES!" Cajeta bounced so high she vanished into the leaves and came back down. "Let's race! On your marks, get set—"

"There is... no race," Slowy said kindly. "Only... a no-hurry hike."

Cajeta blinked. "A *no-hurry* hike? But how will we see everything if we go slow?"

Slowy's smile somehow got even gentler. "That, dear Cajeta... is the secret."

---

And so the strangest hike in the history of the rainforest began.

Slowy lifted one long arm. Then he set it down. Then he lifted the other. The whole jungle could have grown a new banana in the time it took him to move one branch over. A leaf drifted past. A cloud went by. Somewhere, a tree got a little bit taller.

Cajeta zoomed ahead, looped back, zoomed ahead again, and rolled in eleven impatient circles. She zipped up a vine, did a loop, and came down so fast she made her own little breeze.

"Are we there yet? Are we there yet? What are we even looking AT? It's just green! Green-green-green everywhere!" She squinted very hard at the wall of leaves. "Okay, I looked. There's nothing here. Can we go faster now? I bet I could see the whole selva in TWO MINUTES if you'd just let me bounce!"

"Look closer," Slowy hummed.

"I AM looking!"

"Closer than that."

Huffing, Cajeta stopped. She stopped *all the way*, which was very hard for her. She held very still. She looked at the leaf right in front of her nose.

And on that leaf — so tiny she'd zoomed right past it a hundred times — sat a frog the size of a raindrop. He was bright red with bright blue legs, and he was fast asleep, his little chest going up and down, up and down.

"Ooooh," Cajeta whispered. "A teeny sleepy frog. I almost squished him with my bouncing!"

"You did," Slowy agreed peacefully. "Three times. He forgives you. He is... a very forgiving frog."

The frog let out a snore the size of a soap bubble. It floated up, wobbled, and popped on Cajeta's nose with a tiny *pip!*

She giggled. For the first time all day, she did not bounce.

"He's so *small,*" she whispered. "How does someone so small make such a big snore?"

"The smallest things," Slowy hummed, "often hold... the biggest surprises. But only... if you stop... to meet them."

Cajeta nodded slowly. She was beginning to understand. Just a little. Just enough.

---

They kept going — slowly, slowly — and the selva began to show its secrets like a friend opening a treasure box one tiny gift at a time.

Because Cajeta was finally slow enough to *see*, she noticed a flower she'd never have spotted at full speed: a fat pink bloom with petals like tiny cupped hands, holding a perfect drop of last night's rain. A hummingbird, no bigger than a thumb, drank from it and zipped away in a shimmer of green.

"That flower," Slowy said, "only opens... once a year. We are... very lucky. Pura vida."

"Pura vida," Cajeta repeated softly, tasting the words like candy. "What does it mean again?"

"It means," said Slowy, slow and warm as melting butter, "*pure life.* It means... slow down... breathe easy... and notice... how good it all is. The Ticos say it... when they are happy. The selva says it... all day long. You just have to be... quiet enough to hear."

Next, they passed a curtain of vines, and Slowy stopped — well, he was already stopped, but he stopped *meaningfully.* "Listen," he whispered.

Cajeta held her breath.

At first she heard nothing. Then she heard everything. The plink of dripping water. The hum of a thousand insects. The whisper of leaves rubbing leaves. A faraway toucan announcing the news to absolutely no one. And underneath it all, a deep, steady, gentle thrum — like the whole forest was breathing in its sleep.

"That," said Slowy, "is the heartbeat... of the selva. You cannot hear it... when you hurry."

Cajeta's little fudgy eyes went wide and shiny. "It's the most beautiful sound I've ever heard," she breathed. "And I almost rolled right past it."

---

But the no-hurry hike was about to get a *teensy* bit hurried — because the sky did the thing the rainforest sky loves to do most.

It rained.

Not a gentle drizzle. A great big warm rainforest *whoosh* of rain, drumming the leaves like a hundred happy chefs.

"EEK!" Cajeta squealed. Now, here is the thing about coconut fudge: it does not love getting soggy. A soggy Cajeta is a sad, melty puddle of a Cajeta. "I'm gonna melt! I'm gonna melt! HURRY, Slowy, we have to RUN!"

Slowy did not run. Slowy could not run if a thousand jaguars asked him to politely. Instead, he lifted one arm — slowly, calmly, certainly — and reached for an enormous leaf hanging just above them.

"Slowy, there's no TIME!" Cajeta wailed, bouncing in worried little hops.

"There is... always time," Slowy hummed, "for the right slow thing."

And just as Cajeta felt the first fat raindrop wobble toward her toasty-coconut head, Slowy tipped the giant leaf.

It folded over them like an umbrella.

The rain drummed harmlessly above. Underneath, Slowy and Cajeta stayed cozy and dry, watching the silver rain stitch the whole green selva together. Cajeta wasn't soggy at all. Not one bit.

"You... knew where the big leaf was," Cajeta said slowly. "Because you go slow enough... to remember things."

"I have looked at that leaf," Slowy said proudly, "for six years. I know its every wrinkle. I know which way... it folds. It is a very good leaf. We are... good friends, the leaf and I."

Cajeta laughed so hard a puff of toasted coconut fell off her shoulder. "You're FRIENDS with a *leaf!*"

"When you go slowly," Slowy said with great dignity, "you have time... to be friends... with everything." A raindrop slid off the leaf's edge and plopped onto his nose. He did not flinch. He simply smiled at it. "Hello, raindrop," he said. "Goodbye, raindrop."

Cajeta couldn't help it — she snorted another little coconut puff, and the two of them sat snug and dry while the warm rain played its drumming song all around their cozy green tent.

---

When the rain stopped, the whole selva sparkled. Every leaf wore a diamond. Every vine dripped silver. The air smelled green and clean and brand new, the way the world always does right after rain. A frog plinked. A bird whistled a single bright note. And then, in the fresh wet light, a wide ribbon of butterflies rose up all at once — blue ones, the famous shimmery-blue **morpho** butterflies, their wings flashing like little pieces of fallen sky — turning the air into a flying river above their heads.

Cajeta stood very, very still. She did not bounce. She did not zoom. She just watched, her little chewy heart full to the top.

"Slowy," she whispered. "If I'd gone fast... I would have missed all of this. The sleepy frog. The once-a-year flower. The heartbeat. The big good leaf. The blue river in the sky."

"Yes," said Slowy, and his half-moon eyes crinkled with kindness. "The selva does not hide her treasures from us. She only gives them... to those who slow down... enough to be given them."

Cajeta thought about this for a long, slow moment — which, for her, was a brand new and rather wonderful thing to be able to do. The old Cajeta would have bounced away by now, off to the next thing and the next and the next, missing the morphos, missing the heartbeat, missing the whole soft glittering miracle of it. But the new Cajeta — the slowing-down Cajeta — stayed exactly where she was, and felt her busy little heart go quiet and full.

"Can I take a no-hurry hike with you tomorrow?" she asked. "And the day after? I want to learn how to be slow. Just a little. Just enough to see the good stuff."

"Of course, dear one." Slowy began, very slowly, to smile even more. "But first... we must finish... *this* hike. We are nearly... to the next branch."

Cajeta looked. The next branch was about one footstep away.

"How long will that take us?" she asked.

"Oh," said Slowy happily, gazing up at the sparkling, breathing, beautiful selva all around them. "A lovely... long... while."

And Cajeta — the bounciest ball of fudge in all of Costa Rica — settled down beside her new slow friend, tucked her feet under her, and decided that a lovely long while sounded just about perfect.

So they sat. And they listened to the heartbeat of the jungle. And they were, both of them, in absolutely no hurry at all.

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, the world is full of treasures that only show themselves to slow and gentle eyes. Tonight, let your busy thoughts settle down like rain finding the leaves. There is no race to sleep, and no hurry to dream — only the soft, steady heartbeat of a quiet night holding you close. Breathe slow. Smile soft. Pura vida, sweet dreamer. Goodnight.
