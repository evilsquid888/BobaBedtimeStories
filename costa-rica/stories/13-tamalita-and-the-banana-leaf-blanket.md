# Story 13: Tamalita and the Banana-Leaf Blanket

**Main Character:** Tamalita
**Guest Characters:** Tico the Toucan, Slowy the Sloth
**Setting:** A rainforest clearing during a warm afternoon shower
**Theme:** A good wrapping keeps your warmth and love safe inside.
**Spanish Spotlight:** **lluvia** (YOO-vyah) = rain

---

In the warmest, greenest corner of the rainforest, where ferns grew taller than hats and the air smelled like wet leaves and faraway cinnamon, a little tamale named Tamalita was having the loveliest afternoon of her whole steamy life.

She bounced through the clearing in her glossy green banana-leaf wrapping, tied snug with a string bow at the top, just like a present that had decided to go for a walk. Steam puffed out of her in tiny happy clouds.

"Wrapped with love!" she giggled, and *poof* — a curl of warm steam wandered up into the leaves.

She had come to the clearing to do absolutely nothing, which, she had decided, was the very best plan a tamale could have. The sun was buttery and bright. A beetle the size of a thumbnail was conducting an orchestra of crickets. And somewhere up high, a very loud bird was about to make her afternoon a great deal more interesting.

"BEAK THE NEWS! BEAK THE NEWS!" came a squawk from the canopy.

Tamalita looked up, and *poof*, out came another giggle-puff.

A glossy black toucan came swooping down like a feathery comet, his enormous rainbow beak flashing every color a crayon box ever dreamed of. He landed on a low branch with a dramatic flap and pointed his banana-shaped beak straight at her.

"Big news, very big, enormous news!" Tico announced, hopping from foot to foot. "The clouds upstairs are getting fat and gray and grumbly, which means — and I have this on excellent authority, which is to say I looked up — that we are about to get rained on! **Lluvia** (YOO-vyah) = rain! Buckets of it! Possibly bathtubs!"

"Ooh," said Tamalita, peering at the sky. Sure enough, the buttery sun had tucked itself behind a big gray pillow of cloud. "Are you sure?"

"Am I sure? Am I *sure*?" Tico puffed out his bright yellow chest. "I am the town crier of this entire jungle, and I have never once been wrong about weather, except for the seven times I was, which don't count because I'd had too many berries. Trust the beak!"

"The beak does look very official," Tamalita admitted, tilting her head. "All those colors. Like a little rainbow that learned to talk."

"It is the finest beak in the canopy," Tico agreed proudly, polishing it with one wing. "Now, if you'll excuse me, I have to go warn the lizards, and the frogs, and that grumpy old beetle who never says thank you — "

And right then, as if the sky had been waiting for its cue, the first fat drop of *lluvia* went *PLOP* on the very tip of Tico's beak.

"AH-HA! See? The beak is never — " *PLOP* *PLOP* *PLOP-PLOP-PLOP* — "oh. Oh, that's a lot of beak news all at once."

The warm afternoon shower came down soft and steady, pattering on the leaves like a thousand tiny drummers practicing for a tiny parade. The clearing turned silvery and sparkling. The crickets stopped their orchestra and the little beetle conductor scurried under a mushroom, waving his arms as if to say *concert postponed!* It was, Tamalita thought, rather beautiful.

It was also, she realized, rather wet.

Tico fluffed his feathers and hunched under a fern, but the fern had holes in it the way ferns do, and soon the poor toucan was dripping from beak to tail. "This is *terrible* news," he grumbled, water sliding off his great big beak in a little waterfall. "A soggy crier can't crier. My announcements come out all *blubbery*."

"Don't worry!" said Tamalita brightly. But just as she said it, she heard a sound she hadn't expected — a slow, sleepy, slightly worried voice drifting down from a tree at the edge of the clearing.

"Ohhh... noooo..."

Tamalita and Tico both looked up.

There, hanging from a branch by his long curved arms, was Slowy the sloth. His mossy-green fur was already darkening with rain, and his sleepy half-moon eyes blinked very, very slowly, the way a candle flickers when it's thinking about going to bed.

"I waaas... going... to climb... dowwwn," Slowy explained, in the unhurried way he explained everything. "But cliiimbing dowwwn... taaakes... me about... an hour. And the raaain... will be... finished... by theeen. Prooobably." He gave a slow, soggy sigh. "I aaam... a bit... coooold, friends."

Now, here is a thing you should know about sloths: they really do move that slowly, and they really do feel the cold, because their fuzzy fur soaks up rain like a sponge soaks up a spill. Slowy wasn't in any danger — sloths are wonderfully tough — but he was shivery and damp, and his gentle smile had gone a little droopy at the corners. And nobody, not in the whole rainforest, liked to see Slowy's smile droop. It was like watching a sunbeam decide to take a nap.

Tamalita's warm little masa heart squeezed. Up in the steaming pot where she'd grown up, all bundled together with her cousins, she had learned that the worst feeling in the world was being cold and alone. And she was *not* about to let her friends feel that, not on a beautiful glittery afternoon like this one.

"Right!" she said, hopping into the middle of the clearing where everyone could see her. Steam was still puffing off her in the rain, which made her look like a tiny green teakettle. "Nobody stays cold and soggy on my watch. I have a plan, and it is a *cozy* one."

"Does the plan involve being dry?" Tico called hopefully. "Because I'd vote for that. I'd vote for it *twice*."

Tamalita looked down at herself — at her snug, glossy, water-beading banana leaf, tied so neat with its little string bow. Banana leaves, you see, are nature's own raincoat. The *lluvia* didn't soak into her at all. It just rolled right off, *plip plip plip*, leaving her warm and toasty inside.

And that gave her an idea.

"My wrapping," she said slowly, "keeps the rain *out*. And it keeps my warm *in*." She reached up and gave her string bow a gentle tug. "So maybe... it could do that for all of us."

"Wait, wait," said Tico, hopping over with a *squelch*. "You'd unwrap yourself? In the rain? Isn't that — won't you get cold, little tamale?"

It was a fair question. Tamalita paused, one masa-hand on her bow, and felt a flutter of worry. She *did* love her wrapping. It was cozy and safe and smelled of holidays and home. Giving it up felt like giving away a hug she hadn't finished having.

But then she looked at Slowy, shivering slow up in his tree. And she looked at Tico, dripping like a sad rainbow. And she thought about something her abuela tamales always said back in the steaming pot, all bundled together: *the warm doesn't run out just because you share it.*

"I won't get cold," Tamalita decided, and she found, to her surprise, that she meant it. "Because I've got so much warm packed inside me, there's plenty to spare. **Pura vida**, right? That means we look after each other."

She unrolled the top of her big green banana leaf — careful, careful — keeping her own cozy middle wrapped, but letting the wide leafy edges spread out like the world's snuggest blanket. Steam billowed out warm and sweet, smelling of corn and comfort.

"Slowy!" she called. "Can you reach a *little* way down? Just a little. I'll bring the cozy up to you."

Slowly — because it was the only speed he had — Slowy stretched one long, slow arm toward the ground. Tamalita hopped onto a mossy log, then a fern frond, then a low loop of vine, climbing up bit by bit until she could throw the edge of her warm banana leaf right over the sloth's damp green shoulders.

"Ohhhh," sighed Slowy, and his droopy smile lifted at last. The leaf shed the rain right off him, *plip plip plip*, and the steam wrapped him up warm as a freshly toasted hug. "That is... verrry... *tuanis*."

"Make room for the press!" squawked Tico, scrambling up the vine after her. Tamalita tucked the other wide edge of her leaf right over the toucan, big beak and all, so that he peeked out the front looking exactly like a present that had grown a rainbow nose.

And there they all were: a tamale, a sloth, and a toucan, bundled together under one glossy green banana-leaf blanket, warm and dry and giggling, while the soft *lluvia* drummed its tiny parade all around them. The leaf was just big enough, if everyone breathed in at the same time, which they tried, and then all breathed out at once, which made Tico's beak pop out the side like a periscope.

"Scooch," said Tamalita.

"I am scooching," said Tico. "Beaks do not scooch easily. It's a known fact."

Slowy let out the longest, happiest sigh anyone had ever heard, a sigh so slow it had three separate parts to it. "Thiiis... is the niiicest... rainstorm... I have ever... been in," he murmured. "And I haaave... been in... maaany. Hundreds. Possibly thousands. I loooose count... around... twoooo."

"You know," said Tico, his voice all snug and cozy now, "this is the best news I've delivered all day, and I didn't even have to announce it."

Tamalita laughed her steamy little laugh, and *poof*, a warm cloud curled up and tickled Slowy's nose, which made him do the slowest sneeze in the history of the rainforest — *aaaaaa-chooooo* — that took so long they all started giggling before it even finished, and were still giggling when it did.

"Beak the news," whispered Tico, snug as could be. "Local toucan declares this the coziest spot in all of Costa Rica." He paused. "I'll save the loud version for when we're dry."

She had been a little bit worried, at the start, that sharing her wrapping might leave her cold and empty. But it turned out to be exactly the opposite. Wrapped up tight with her friends, sharing all that cozy steam, Tamalita felt warmer than she ever had alone. The warm hadn't run out at all. It had only grown bigger, the way a song gets bigger when more voices join in.

When the *lluvia* finally drifted away and the buttery sun came peeking back through the dripping leaves, the whole clearing glittered like it had been dusted with diamonds. Slowy was dry and grinning his slow grin. Tico's feathers fluffed back up bright and proud. And Tamalita, retying her string bow snug at the top, discovered she still had every bit of warmth she'd started with — and a great deal of love besides.

"Wrapped with love," she said softly, and three happy steam-puffs floated up to meet the sun. "All of us."

Sleep deep, dream soft, and let the steam of good dreams curl all around you. **Pura vida**, little one. Goodnight.
