# Story 43: The Rainy-Season Board Game Night

**Main Character:** Lala the Tres Leches
**Guest Characters:** Flan the Coconut Flan, Tamalita
**Setting:** A cozy living room during the green-season afternoon rains
**Theme:** A rainy day indoors with family is its own kind of treasure.
**Spanish Spotlight:** jugar (hoo-GAR) = to play

---

The sky had been blue all morning, and Lala the Tres Leches had Big Plans. Soft, spongey plans. Dripping-with-sweet-milk plans. She had pressed her squishy cheek to the window of the cozy little living room, leaving a small milky smudge on the glass, and announced to absolutely everyone, "Today we picnic! Today we sparkle in the sun!"

And then — *plip*.

One fat raindrop landed on the windowsill.

*Plip. Plip. PLIP-PLIP-PLIP.*

The green-season rains came down like the whole sky had decided to take a bath. Lala's wobbly cloud of whipped cream sagged in disappointment. Her single cinnamon dot looked positively gloomy.

"Oh, *no*," she swooned, sliding down the window in a slow, dramatic squish. "Our picnic. Our *sparkle*. Drowned. Drizzled. Doomed!"

A small voice piped up from the couch.

"Steady, steady... ooh, *wobbly!*"

---

It was Flan the Coconut Flan, jiggling so hard with worry that his shiny caramel drizzle nearly slid right off his head. He had been watching the rain too, and the longer he watched, the more he wobbled.

"What do we *do?*" Flan fussed, flecks of toasted coconut trembling. "We cannot go outside. We are trapped! Trapped in the living room with nothing but a roof and snacks and blankets and each other and — oh dear, when I say it out loud it doesn't sound so terrible, does it?"

From the kitchen doorway came a warm little puff of steam.

*Pfff-giggle.*

Tamalita waddled in, snug in her glossy green banana-leaf wrapping with its tidy string bow. She smelled wonderfully of the holidays, even though it was the middle of green season, because Tamalita always smelled a little bit like Christmas no matter the month.

"Trapped?" she giggled, releasing another puff of steam. "Oh, you two. We are not *trapped*. We are *wrapped*. Wrapped up cozy together, like a present nobody has opened yet!"

Lala lifted her drippy head. "But the *sun*," she swooned. "The picnic. The sparkle."

Tamalita waddled over and patted Lala with one leafy corner. "Sunny days come back, *mae*. They always do. But a rainy afternoon with familia? *That* is a gift. You just have to unwrap it." She paused, and her steam puffed extra big. "Besides. I know what we should do."

Flan stopped wobbling. "You do?"

Tamalita's eyes twinkled like tinsel.

"We **jugar** (hoo-GAR) = to play," she said. "*Board game night!*"

---

Now, here is a thing you should know about Lala the Tres Leches: once a sad idea leaves her head, a *delicious* idea moves right in. And the idea of jugar was very delicious indeed.

"Board games!" Lala gasped, springing up so fast she splashed a little sweet milk on the rug. (She left tiny milky footprints wherever she went. She always did. It was, she felt, part of her charm.) "Oh, *yes*. Soak it all in, friends — this rainy day is about to become the *coziest* day!"

The trouble was, when they pulled out the old board game box from under the couch, they discovered a small disaster.

The game pieces were *gone*.

"No little markers," Flan moaned. "No tokens! How can we jugar with no pieces? The rain has ruined us after all!"

But Lala only smiled a soft, wobbly smile.

"We don't need *little* pieces," she said. "We have *us*."

And that is how the greatest board game night in the history of green season began — with Lala herself plopping right onto the START square, leaving a happy milky splotch, and announcing, "*I'll* be a piece! Roll the dice!"

---

They could not find dice either, so Tamalita invented something better. She tucked herself into a snug ball, and when you gave her a gentle nudge, she would roll across the floor and stop with a giggle and a puff of steam, and however many steam puffs came out — *that* was your number.

"One puff!" she announced, rolling to a stop. *Pfff.* "Lala moves one space!"

Lala squelched forward one square. "Soak it all *in!*"

"My turn, my turn," fussed Flan, who was secretly very excited and pretending not to be. He nudged Tamalita, and she rolled — *pfff-pfff-pfff* — three puffs!

"Three!" Flan cheered, then immediately worried. "Oh — is three good? Is three too many? What if I land on a bad square? What if the bad square is a *trap?*"

"Flan," said Lala gently. "It's a board game. The worst square just says 'Go back two spaces.'"

Flan thought about this. He wobbled. He un-wobbled. "...That *is* a fairly gentle worst," he admitted.

He hopped three squares forward, his caramel drizzle bouncing, and landed on a square that read **TELL A STORY**.

---

This, it turned out, was the best square of all.

Because when Flan landed there, he had to tell a story — and Flan, for all his fussing, had a *big* heart and a bigger imagination. He told them about the time he had been a brand-new flan, fresh from the kitchen, wobbling so much that he thought he might wobble himself clean apart.

"And then," Flan said, lowering his voice the way you do at the good part, "an old coffee bean — Don Café, you know him — he sat beside me and said, *'Good things brew slowly, little one. So does courage.'* And do you know, I wobbled a *little* less after that."

Lala had gone all soft and dreamy. "Oh," she swooned, a happy tear of sweet milk rolling down her sponge. "That's *beautiful*. Soak it ALL in."

"Pfff," went Tamalita warmly. "See? *That* is what jugar is for. Not just to win. To be *together*."

Outside, the rain drummed on the roof — *tat-tat-tatta-tat* — like the whole jungle was applauding.

---

They played round after round. Tamalita rolled and giggled and puffed. Lala squelched from square to square, leaving a trail of milky footprints that made the whole board look like a tiny dessert had gone on an adventure (which, of course, it had). Flan stopped checking every square for traps and started laughing instead, and the more he laughed the less he wobbled, until he was practically *steady*, which for a flan is a small miracle.

When someone landed on **TELL A STORY**, they told one.

Tamalita told about her very first Christmas, all snug in her banana leaf, surrounded by a whole steaming family of tamales.

Lala told about the time she tried to be "dry and dignified" for one entire afternoon and lasted exactly four minutes before dripping on everyone, which made even the rain seem to giggle.

And when Flan landed on **TELL A STORY** for the *third* time and groaned, "But I've used up all my stories!" — Lala bonked him gently with a corner of whipped cream and said, "Then make one *up!*" — and so he did, a wild tale about a flan who became a pirate captain, and it was so silly that Tamalita laughed out so much steam the windows fogged completely over.

When the windows fogged, Lala drew a little sun in the mist with her drippy fingertip.

"There," she said softly. "We made our *own* sunshine."

---

By the time the rain began to ease — gentle now, just a sleepy *plip... plip...* — nobody had even noticed who was winning. Somewhere along the way, *winning* had stopped mattering at all. They had pulled the big blanket off the couch and draped it over all three of them like a tent, and the game board sat in the middle, soft and milky and lived-in, like a little world they had built together.

"You know," Lala murmured, her cinnamon dot drooping sleepily, "I had Big Plans this morning. Sunshine plans. Sparkle plans."

"And?" puffed Tamalita, snuggling close.

Lala gave a slow, happy, dripping sigh.

"This was better," she said. "Sunny days are loud and bright and busy. But *this* —" she wiggled her toes, leaving one last milky footprint on the blanket "— this was *soft*. This was *us*. To jugar with familia beats any sunny day ever could." She yawned, a sweet little milky yawn. "Pura vida."

Flan, no longer wobbling even a tiny bit, smiled the contentest smile a flan has ever smiled.

"You were right, Tamalita," he said. "We weren't trapped. We were just... wrapped."

Tamalita giggled one last sleepy puff of steam.

"Wrapped with love," she whispered.

And outside, the green-season rain pattered them all the way to dreamland, soft as a lullaby, while three little Costa Rican treats dozed off beneath one warm blanket — proof, if anyone needed it, that the very best treasures are the ones you can't picnic with, can't sparkle in, and can't put back in a box. The ones who roll the dice, and tell the stories, and stay.

---

## Goodnight Blessing
Tuck yourself in tight, little one, the way three friends tucked beneath one soft blanket. May your rainy days be cozy, your stories be silly, and the people you love be always close enough to share the dice. There is no weather that can dampen a warm heart, and no sun as bright as people who love you. So close your sleepy eyes, listen to the gentle rain, and remember — to jugar with familia is the sweetest treasure of all. Pura vida, dear one. Goodnight.
