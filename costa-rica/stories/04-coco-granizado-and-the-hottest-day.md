# Story 4: Coco Granizado and the Hottest Day

**Main Character:** Coco the Granizado
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A bustling town square on the hottest afternoon of the year
**Theme:** Keeping cool is easier when you share the chill with friends.
**Spanish Spotlight:** **hola** (OH-lah) = hello

---

On the hottest afternoon of the entire year, the town square of Pura Vida Town was so warm that the cobblestones were practically toasting marshmallows all by themselves. The fountain had given up and started steaming. A chicken fanned itself with a banana leaf. And somewhere near the bakery, a very confused snowman puddle that had been there since last December finally evaporated with a sad little *poof*, leaving only a carrot and the faint memory of winter.

Everybody in town was *wilting*.

That is — everybody except a tall, fluffy mountain of rainbow shaved ice riding into the square on a squeaky wooden cart, dusted with powdered milk like fresh snow and wearing tiny round sunglasses.

"HOLA, everybody!" he hollered, flinging up two icy arms. **Hola** (OH-lah) = hello. "It's Coco the Granizado, and woooo, it is *spicy* out here today! Stay frosty, stay sweet!"

Nobody answered. They were too busy melting.

---

Coco lowered his sunglasses and peeked over the top. The whole square looked like a wax museum left out in the sun.

There was Tato the Patacon, the brave little smashed plantain, flopped flat on a bench. "Too... hot... to smash," he mumbled. "Smash later. Maybe."

There was Flan the Coconut Flan, jiggling so hard from the heat that his caramel was sliding clean off the top. "Steady, steady... ooh, I'm *melting*, this is not at all dignified—!"

There was even Don Café the wise old coffee bean, fanning himself with his tiny straw hat. "My, my," he sighed. "Good things brew slowly... but today, *everything* is brewing."

Coco gasped, and a little puff of cold air shot out of his mouth like a tiny snowstorm. "Oh no. Oh no no no. This is a granizado *emergency*."

You see, a granizado is the coolest thing in all of Costa Rica. He is shaved ice piled tall in a paper cone, drizzled with rainbow syrups — strawberry red, lime green, blueberry blue — and snowed over with sweet powdered milk. On a day like today, Coco wasn't just a treat. He was a *hero*.

"Okay, square!" Coco shouted, hopping off his cart. "I'm gonna cool every last one of you down, *tuanis*? One scoop at a time! Who's first?"

---

He skated over to Tato first, because Tato looked the floppiest.

"Open up, big guy!" Coco scooped a curl of frosty rainbow ice right off the top of his own fluffy head and plopped it onto Tato's warm, crispy edge. *Tssssss!* went the steam.

Tato's eyes flew wide open. "Oh," he said. "*Oh.* That's — that's the best thing I've ever felt." He sat up. He wiggled his crinkly edges. He did a little victory hop. "SMASH NOW, WORRY NEVER!"

"That's the spirit!" Coco laughed.

But here's the thing nobody mentions about being made of ice on the hottest day of the year. Every time Coco gave away a scoop, he got just a teeny bit... smaller.

He didn't notice. He was having too much fun.

He whooshed over to Flan, who was now more puddle than custard. "Hola, wobbly friend! Hold still — well, try!" He patted a cool dollop of powdered-milk snow right onto Flan's quivering dome.

Flan froze mid-jiggle. His caramel slid happily back into place. "Oh my goodness," he whispered, dabbing his forehead. "I feel positively *refined* again. Steady, steady... and not even wobbly. Thank you, dear Coco. You are a gentleman of ice."

"I'm a gentle*MOUNTAIN* of ice," Coco said, striking a pose. Then he noticed his sunglasses had slipped down. They used to sit on his nose. Now they sat closer to his middle.

"Huh," he said. "That's weird."

He shrugged it off and zoomed to the next wilting townsfolk.

---

And oh, did Coco zoom.

He cooled the baker, who'd been sweating into the bread dough (the loaves came out extra salty that day, but nobody complained). He cooled three street cats who were lying belly-up like furry pancakes. He cooled a mail snail who had been trying to deliver one letter since *Tuesday*. He even cooled the steaming fountain, leaning over the edge and shaking powdered-milk snow into it until the water sighed with relief and started gurgling its happy fountain song again.

"HOLA! Stay frosty! Stay sweet!" Coco kept calling, scoop after scoop after scoop. Strawberry red for the baker. Lime green for the cats. Blueberry blue for the mail snail, who turned a lovely shade of cool and finally, *finally* finished delivering his letter (it said, *"Happy Birthday — sorry I'm late."*).

The whole square was perking up. People stood. People stretched. People remembered how to *smile*. The toasty cobblestones cooled into a comfy warm. The chicken put down her banana-leaf fan and started clucking a little tune.

It was working! Coco was saving the day, scoop by rainbow scoop!

There was just one problem.

By the time Coco had cooled the last cat's last whisker, he reached up to fix his sunglasses again — and his arm went straight up over his head and *kept going*, because there was hardly any Coco left to stop it.

He looked down.

The tall, fluffy mountain of rainbow ice was now... a small, wobbly hill. About the size of a teacup. With sunglasses sliding off the top.

"Oh," said Coco, in a much tinier voice than usual. "Oh, *coco*."

---

He sat down — well, he was already mostly sitting, on account of being so short now — right in the middle of the cooled-off, happy square.

"Hola," he said to a passing ant. The ant waved a leg and kept walking.

For the first time all day, Coco the Granizado felt small. He'd cooled the whole town. But there hadn't been anyone to cool *him*. And the sun, even a little kinder now, was still warm on his melting back.

A drop of strawberry-red syrup trickled down. *Drip.*

That's when a shadow fell over him. A cool, soft, milky shadow.

It was Tato. And Flan. And Don Café. And the baker, and the three cats, and the mail snail, and the chicken, and what looked like the *entire town square*, all gathered in a big circle around the tiny puddle that used to be a mountain.

"Coco," said Tato, kneeling down on his crispy knees. "You cooled all of us. Now it's our turn."

"It's the *least* we can do," Flan agreed, "and I do mean that as the highest compliment."

Don Café tipped his little straw hat. "Young friend, you have learned the oldest secret in the world. Cool given away does not vanish — it *spreads*. And what spreads, comes back. Good things brew slowly, but kindness? Kindness brews *fast*."

---

Then the whole town got to work.

The baker fetched a fresh paper cone. The three cats — who turned out to be surprisingly good at this — gently scooped up every last drop of Coco, every rainbow trickle and powdered-milk flake, and piled him carefully back inside. The mail snail brought a special letter that said *"You matter"* (he'd been saving it for someone important). And Flan, wobbling with excitement, carried over a fresh block of ice the baker kept in his icebox for emergencies.

*Scrape, scrape, scrape* went the ice into fluffy snow. Up and up Coco grew. Tato squeezed on fresh ribbons of syrup — strawberry, lime, blueberry, all his favorites. The chicken sprinkled powdered milk like she was decorating the world's most important cake.

And little by little, the small wobbly teacup-sized Coco rose up, and up, and *up*, until he was a tall, fluffy, sparkling mountain of rainbow shaved ice once more — only now, somehow, even brighter than before.

Coco pushed his sunglasses back up to the very tippy-top of his head, right where they belonged.

"Woooo!" he cheered, throwing up two icy arms. "Now THAT is tuanis! You guys built me back, scoop by scoop!"

"Better together," said Tato, beaming.

"Indeed," said Don Café, with a slow and knowing smile. "Cool shared is cool doubled."

Coco looked around the square — at every happy, cooled-off, smiling face, all gathered close in the gentle golden evening light. And he realized the very best thing about being a granizado wasn't being the coolest one in town.

It was getting to share the chill, scoop by rainbow scoop, with everybody he loved.

"HOLA, friends!" he shouted one last time, as the first cool stars began to twinkle. "Last call! Who wants a scoop before bed?"

Everybody raised a hand.

And on the hottest afternoon of the entire year, the town of Pura Vida cooled down together — not because of one mountain of ice, but because the whole square learned that the chill always tastes sweeter when you share it.
