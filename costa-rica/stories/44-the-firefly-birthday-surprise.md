# Story 44: The Firefly Birthday Surprise

**Main Character:** Flan the Coconut Flan
**Guest Characters:** Gallo, Pinto, Don Café, Cajeta the Coconut Fudge
**Setting:** A backyard birthday at twilight strung with paper lanterns
**Theme:** The best surprises are the ones made with everyone's help.
**Spanish Spotlight:** cumpleaños (koom-pleh-AH-nyos) = birthday

---

Flan the Coconut Flan had exactly one job, and he had already wobbled about it eleven times before breakfast.

"Keep the secret," he whispered to himself, jiggling so hard that his shiny caramel drizzle sloshed like a tiny golden wave. "Just keep one little secret. How hard could that be?"

Very hard, it turned out, when the secret was a **cumpleaños** (koom-pleh-AH-nyos) = birthday party for the most beloved bean in the whole rainforest village — Don Café — and Don Café happened to be strolling toward Flan at that exact moment, tapping his miniature wooden walking stick and sniffing the warm twilight air.

"Buenas, young Flan," said Don Café kindly. "You look unusually... shimmery today. Is something wonderful happening?"

"NOTHING IS HAPPENING," Flan announced at the top of his custardy voice, then clapped a coconut-flecked hand over his own mouth. A toasted coconut flake popped right off the top of his head.

This, dear reader, is how the wobbliest secret in Costa Rica very nearly fell apart before the sun even set.

---

The plan was simple — at least, that was what Gallo had said that morning, hopping in little excited circles in the backyard. "Rise and pinto!" he hummed, his golden fried bits glinting. "Tonight, the moment Don Café finishes his evening coffee walk, he comes back HERE, and surprise! The whole village throws him the best **cumpleaños** ever! Lanterns! Cake! Everyone he loves!"

"Better together, mae," agreed Pinto softly, twirling his tiny wooden spoon. The little pale stripe on his glossy black side curved up like a smile. "Don Café has given everyone good advice for years. Tonight we give something back."

The backyard was already half-magic. Paper lanterns hung in loops between the banana trees, glowing soft orange, soft pink, soft buttery yellow. Cajeta the Coconut Fudge bounced from lantern to lantern, fluffing the paper edges.

"Sweet and round, watch me bound!" she squeaked, springing so high she bonked a lantern with her fuzzy coconut head. "Oops! Still pretty! Everything's still pretty!"

Flan wobbled nervously at the center of it all, holding a clipboard nearly as big as himself. (He liked clipboards. Clipboards made worrying feel organized.)

"All right," Flan said, reading from his list. "Lanterns — check. Music — check. The cake is hidden behind the big fern. Don Café knows nothing. NOTHING." He paused. "Except... I sort of shouted 'NOTHING IS HAPPENING' directly into his face this morning."

Everyone froze.

"You did WHAT?" gasped Gallo.

"I panicked!" Flan wailed, jiggling so dramatically his whipped-cream cloud nearly slid off. "He smiled at me and my caramel went all swirly and the words just fell out! Steady, steady... ooh, wobbly!"

Pinto patted him gently with his spoon. "It's all right. Don Café is wise, but he's also very polite. He'll pretend he heard nothing. Pura vida."

"Pura vida," everyone repeated, breathing out together. Even Flan's wobble slowed to a nervous shimmy.

But there was a bigger problem, and it arrived right on schedule — with the dark.

---

As the sun melted behind the mountains, the backyard sank into shadow. The lanterns glowed, yes, but only a little. Between them, everything went dim and grayish-blue.

"We can't see the cake," Gallo said.

"We can't see each other," said Pinto.

"I can't see my own bounce!" cried Cajeta, bouncing anyway and landing in a flowerpot. "Hello! I'm in a plant now!"

Flan looked at the dark yard and felt his entire body turn to one giant nervous jiggle. "It's too dim," he whispered. "Don Café will come back, and it'll just be... shadows. A surprise nobody can SEE. The most beautiful **cumpleaños** in the world, and it'll look like a backyard full of confused vegetables."

"I AM a confused vegetable!" called Cajeta from the flowerpot.

This was the moment Flan wanted to run. Or melt. Or possibly both at once. His list said nothing about darkness. His clipboard had no answer. And he was in charge — sweet, jiggly, worried little Flan was in charge — and he had absolutely no idea what to do.

So he did the only thing he could think of.

He sat down right there in the grass, wobbled until his caramel pooled into a sad shiny puddle, and said, very quietly, "I think I've ruined it."

And that, friends, is exactly when the grass began to glow.

---

At first it was just one tiny green-gold light, blinking near Flan's wobbly elbow. Then another. Then ten. Then a hundred. Then the whole backyard filled with floating, twinkling sparks, drifting up from the grass like the stars had gotten sleepy and decided to come down for a nap.

**Luciérnagas** (loo-see-AIR-nah-gahs) = fireflies.

"Ooooh," breathed Flan, forgetting to worry for a whole entire second.

One firefly landed right on the tip of his caramel-drizzled nose. It blinked at him — *blink, blink* — patiently, the way you'd wait for someone to understand a very good idea.

"You want to... help?" Flan whispered.

*Blink.*

"You want to be our lights?"

*Blinkblinkblink* — which is firefly for *obviously, you wobbly little custard, that's why we came.*

Flan's whole face lit up — partly with hope, and partly because there was now a firefly sitting on it.

"GALLO!" he called. "PINTO! CAJETA! The fireflies — they want to help! But there are so many of them, and we have to arrange them, and Don Café will be back any minute, and I can't do it alone!"

"You don't have to," said Pinto, stepping into the soft glow, his little stripe-smile beaming. "Nobody makes a surprise alone, mae. That's not how surprises work. That's not how anything good works."

"Better together!" hummed Gallo, already bouncing toward the lanterns. "Come on, everyone — let's make this the brightest backyard in all of Costa Rica!"

---

And oh, what a wonderful, ridiculous, glowing scramble it was.

Gallo hummed a happy work-song, and the fireflies loved it — they swirled around his palm hat like a tiny golden tornado. He guided whole clouds of them up into the banana trees, where they nestled between the leaves and twinkled like a sky come down to visit.

Pinto, calm and steady, gathered the gentlest fireflies and arranged them in a soft glowing path from the garden gate all the way to the center of the yard, so that when Don Café walked back, the light would lead him home like a trail of fallen stars.

Cajeta — well, Cajeta bounced. She bounced SO high and SO fast that the fireflies thought it was a game, and they chased her all over the yard, lighting up everything she touched. "Tag! You're glowing! Now YOU'RE glowing!" She bonked three more lanterns and apologized to all three.

And Flan?

Flan stopped wobbling with worry and started wobbling with *work*. He climbed onto an upside-down flowerpot — the same one Cajeta had landed in — and held his clipboard high, and for the first time all day, he didn't read from it. He just looked at his friends and called out, "Fireflies over the cake, please! A big glowing crown above it! And — oh! Oh! Could some of you spell something?"

The fireflies could.

They could, and they did, and it took every single one of them and all five friends pointing and laughing and guiding, but slowly — gloriously, wobbily — a hundred tiny lights drifted into place in the air above the fern, and spelled out, in soft golden letters that floated and shimmered:

***¡FELIZ CUMPLEAÑOS, DON CAFÉ!*** (Happy Birthday, Don Café!)

Flan looked up at it, and his caramel went all swirly again — but this time it wasn't from panic.

"We did it," he whispered. "*We* did it."

"Of course we did," said Pinto. "Now — quiet. I hear a walking stick."

---

*Tap. Tap. Tap.*

Don Café came through the garden gate, following the glowing path of fireflies, sniffing the warm night air with a small puzzled smile. He looked up.

He saw the trees full of stars. He saw the lanterns glowing soft and pink and gold. He saw a hundred fireflies floating his name across the sky. He saw a cake, and a tornado of light around a palm hat, and a coconut-fudge ball stuck — once again — in a flowerpot.

And he saw little Flan, perched up high, wobbling with the biggest, proudest, jiggliest smile a flan has ever smiled.

"¡SORPRESA!" everyone shouted. "¡FELIZ CUMPLEAÑOS!"

Don Café was quiet for a long, warm moment. Then his old eyes went all twinkly, brighter than the fireflies, and he chuckled the slow rich chuckle of someone who has been loved for a very long time.

"You know," he said gently, tapping his stick, "this morning a certain young flan shouted 'NOTHING IS HAPPENING' so loudly that I knew at once something wonderful was being planned." He smiled at Flan. "And good things, mijo... good things brew slowly. But the very best things — they brew *together*."

Flan wobbled so hard with happiness that he toppled right off the flowerpot and landed in a soft custardy splat in the grass — and the fireflies, every one of them, drifted down to glow around him like a warm golden blanket.

"Best **cumpleaños** ever," Flan sighed.

And it was. It really, truly was.

They ate cake under the floating stars. Gallo hummed and Pinto kept time with his spoon. Cajeta bounced (and apologized). And Don Café sat in the middle of all his friends, glowing inside and out, in a backyard that no longer had a single dark corner left in it.
