# Story 44: The Firefly Birthday Surprise

**Main Character:** Flan the Coconut Flan
**Guest Characters:** Gallo, Pinto, Don Café, Cajeta the Coconut Fudge
**Setting:** A backyard birthday at twilight strung with paper lanterns
**Theme:** A small kindness, given even in a panic, has a way of coming back to find you.
**Spanish Spotlight:** **cumpleaños** (koom-pleh-AH-nyos) — birthday; **por favor** (por fah-VOR) — please

---

Flan the Coconut Flan had exactly one job, and he had already wobbled about it eleven times before breakfast.

"Keep the secret," he whispered to himself, jiggling so hard that his shiny caramel drizzle sloshed like a tiny golden wave. "Just keep one little secret. How hard could that be?"

Very hard, it turned out, when the secret was a **cumpleaños** (koom-pleh-AH-nyos) — a birthday — party for the most beloved bean in the whole rainforest village, Don Café. And Don Café happened to be strolling toward Flan at that exact moment, tapping his miniature wooden walking stick and sniffing the warm afternoon air.

"Buenas, young Flan," said Don Café kindly. "You look unusually... shimmery today. Is something wonderful happening?"

"NOTHING IS HAPPENING," Flan announced at the top of his custardy voice, then clapped a coconut-flecked hand over his own mouth. A toasted coconut flake popped right off the top of his head and landed on Don Café's hat.

"Ah," said Don Café, plucking it off and eating it politely. "How delicious that nothing is."

This, dear reader, is how the wobbliest secret in Costa Rica very nearly fell apart before the sun even set.

---

The plan was simple — at least, that was what Gallo had said that morning, hopping in excited circles in the backyard. "Rise and pinto! Tonight, when Don Café finishes his evening walk, he comes back HERE, and surprise! The whole village throws him the best **cumpleaños** ever!"

"Better together, mae," agreed Pinto softly, twirling his tiny wooden spoon. "Don Café has given everyone good advice for years. Tonight we give something back."

The backyard was already half-magic. Paper lanterns hung in loops between the banana trees, each one waiting for its little candle. Cajeta the Coconut Fudge bounced from lantern to lantern, fluffing the paper edges.

"Sweet and round, watch me bound!" she squeaked, springing so high she bonked a lantern with her fuzzy coconut head. "Oops! Still pretty! Everything's still pretty!"

Flan wobbled at the center of it all, holding a clipboard nearly as big as himself. (He liked clipboards. Clipboards made worrying feel organized.) "Lanterns — check. Candles — in the box by the fence. Cake — hidden behind the big fern. Don Café knows nothing." He paused. "Except I sort of shouted 'NOTHING IS HAPPENING' directly into his face."

"You did WHAT?" gasped Gallo.

"I panicked! Steady, steady... ooh, wobbly!"

He was hurrying the big jug of **agua dulce** (AH-gwah DOOL-seh) — sweet sugarcane drink — to the long table, muttering his list, when he saw it: a tiny green-gold firefly, paddling tired circles in the jug.

Flan had exactly zero minutes to spare. He stopped anyway.

"Oh no, no, no. Excuse me, señorita." He fished her out with the punch spoon, set her on his caramel-sticky palm, and dabbed her wings dry with the corner of a party napkin. "There. Nobody does the backstroke in the birthday punch at MY party. It's on the clipboard. Rule one." (It was not on the clipboard. He wrote it down so it would be.)

The firefly blinked at him once, slow and wobbly, and Flan blinked back without thinking, because Flan was polite even to bugs.

Just then a sneaky little green-season drizzle swept across the yard — two minutes of rain, no more. Everyone dove for cover. Flan dove too, but on his way he spotted three more fireflies huddled on a guava leaf, getting rained on and looking like very small, very soggy lost socks.

"Honestly," Flan huffed, tipping a banana leaf over them like an umbrella while the rain pattered on his own caramel. "I am EXTREMELY busy, you know. I have a clipboard." The rescued firefly snuggled into his coconut flakes. "Yes, yes, fine, you can stay."

Then the rain stopped, and Flan rushed off, and he forgot all about it. He had a party to worry about, and worrying was the one thing he never forgot to do.

---

The trouble arrived right on schedule — with the dark.

As the sun melted behind the mountains, the friends ran to light the lanterns. Gallo opened the candle box by the fence and went quiet in a way that Gallo never, ever went.

"Um," he said. "The candles got rained on."

Every single wick was soggy. The matches sputtered. One candle caught for a glorious half-second, and then the evening breeze leaned over the fence and blew it out, almost politely, like an uninvited guest helping himself to the cake.

"We can't see the cake," said Pinto.

"We can't see each other," said Gallo.

"I can't see my own bounce!" cried Cajeta, bouncing anyway and landing in a flowerpot. "Hello! I live here now!"

Flan stared at the dim gray-blue yard, and his whole body became one enormous nervous jiggle. Don Café's tapping stick would come up the lane any minute. The most beautiful **cumpleaños** in the world, and it would look like a backyard full of confused shadows.

He sank toward the grass, caramel pooling into a sad shiny puddle, and got exactly halfway into giving up — which, for the record, is the farthest Flan ever got that night.

Because right then, deep in his coconut flakes, something went *blink*.

Flan went very still. He remembered a tired swimmer in the agua dulce. He remembered a banana-leaf umbrella, and three soggy socks with wings, and the guava bush at the edge of the yard where the little lights had scooted off to dry.

"Oh," he whispered. "OH." He scooped his caramel back into place and stood up as straight as a flan can stand, which is not very, but he meant it. "Nobody panic! I have to go ask somebody something!"

"Ask WHO?" called Gallo.

"Some very small neighbors!" Flan called back, already wobbling across the yard at top wobble. "We didn't lose our lights! We just haven't said por favor yet!"

---

The guava bush at twilight was full of faint green-gold blinks, like the bush was thinking things over.

Flan stopped in front of it, smoothed his caramel, and bowed the way you bow to somebody important — which, he had decided, fireflies were.

"Good evening," he said. "You may remember me. I'm the dessert who fished your friend out of the punch. I also held the leaf during the rain. I'm not saying that so you'll owe me!" he added quickly, wobbling. "I'm saying it so you'll know I'm the polite kind of custard and not the kind that puts bugs in jars."

The bush blinked. Flan took a breath.

"**Por favor** (por fah-VOR) — please — my friend Don Café is having a birthday, and our candles drowned, and the breeze is a bully, and I cannot light the yard by myself because I am, frankly, a pudding." He held up his clipboard like a tiny shield. "Would you come and shine for him? Just for the evening. There will be cake crumbs. I'll write you into the plan. Official party guests, right at the top."

For one terrible second, nothing.

Then the firefly from the punch jug rose out of the bush, hovered in front of Flan's nose, and blinked twice — *blink, blink* — which is firefly for *we were hoping you'd ask, you wobbly little custard.*

And the whole guava bush lifted into the air.

A hundred green-gold lights spiraled up around Flan like warm confetti, and Flan laughed out loud — a surprised, jiggly, delighted laugh — and led them back across the yard like the world's most nervous parade marshal.

"GALLO! PINTO! CAJETA!" he hollered. "The guest list just got LONGER!"

---

What followed was a wonderful, ridiculous, glowing scramble.

Flan climbed onto the upside-down flowerpot (Cajeta had just moved out) and, for the first time all day, he didn't read from his clipboard. He conducted.

"Glowing path from the gate, please — Pinto will show you! Gallo, take a swirl of them up into the banana trees! Cajeta — " Cajeta was already bouncing, and the fireflies had decided she was a game. "Fine! Cajeta, you're the fun! Keep being the fun!"

"BEST JOB AT THE PARTY!" Cajeta shrieked from somewhere in midair.

"And you," Flan said softly to the punch-jug firefly hovering by his nose, "I have a special favor. Above the cake. Can your friends spell?"

They could.

It took every light and all four friends pointing and giggling, but slowly — gloriously, wobbily — the fireflies drifted into place over the fern, in soft golden letters that floated and shimmered.

Cajeta read them out loud at full bounce-volume, because Cajeta read everything at full bounce-volume: "It says ¡FELIZ CUMPLEAÑOS, DON CAFÉ! — happy birthday, Don Café! IN FIREFLIES! WE HAVE FIREFLY HANDWRITING, PEOPLE!"

"Quiet, quiet!" Pinto whispered, stripe-smile beaming. "I hear a walking stick."

*Tap. Tap. Tap.*

Don Café came through the garden gate, following a glowing path of fireflies, and looked up at the trees full of drifting lights, at his own name written warm across the dark, at a coconut-fudge ball frozen mid-bounce, and at one small flan standing on a flowerpot wearing the proudest jiggle in Costa Rica.

"¡SORPRESA!" everyone shouted. "¡FELIZ CUMPLEAÑOS!"

Don Café was quiet a long, warm moment. Then his old eyes went twinkly, and he chuckled the slow, rich chuckle of someone who has been loved for a very long time.

"This morning," he said, "a certain young flan shouted 'NOTHING IS HAPPENING' so loudly that I knew at once something wonderful was being planned." His gaze drifted up to the glowing letters. "But this — hum. Even an old bean could not have guessed this."

---

They ate cake under the floating lights. Gallo hummed and Pinto kept time with his spoon. Cajeta bounced, bonked one final lantern, and apologized to it personally. The fireflies took turns sitting on the frosting, which everyone agreed counted as their slice.

And slowly, the way good evenings do, the party grew soft around the edges. Neighbors drifted home down the lane, calling sleepy goodnights. The fireflies dimmed, one gentle blink at a time, settling back into the guava bush like coals banking down for the night. The punch-jug firefly gave Flan's nose one last *blink* and zipped away.

That was when Don Café came and sat beside Flan in the cool grass.

"I heard a little story tonight," the old bean said quietly. "About a worried flan, on his busiest day, who stopped everything to dry a stranger's wings." He tipped his straw hat. "**Gracias** (GRAH-see-ahs) — thank you, mijo. The party was beautiful. But that — that was the part that made it shine."

"I almost gave up," Flan admitted, wobbling.

"Almost is not a thing you did," said Don Café. "Remember this, young one: the kindness you plant in a hurry still grows. And when you needed light, you did not wait for luck. You went and asked, politely, like a friend." He smiled. "Good things brew slowly. Good deeds brew quietly. Both of them get there."

Above them the stars came out, unhurried, and somewhere in the guava bush, one tiny light blinked goodnight.

"Best **cumpleaños** ever," Flan sighed.

And it was. It really, truly was.

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, you never know which small kindness will come back to find you — the spider you carried outside, the friend you waited for, the please and thank-you you remembered even on a flustered day. May your worries melt like caramel into something sweet, and may you always be brave enough to ask for help, gently and politely, when the evening turns dim. Tonight, somewhere, a tiny light is blinking just for you, slow and kind. You are loved, you are safe, and your smallest kindnesses matter more than you know. Pura vida, sleepy one. Goodnight.
