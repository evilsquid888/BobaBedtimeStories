# Story 50: The Grand Pura Vida Feast

**Main Character:** Gallo and Pinto
**Guest Characters:** The entire Costa Rican cast — Lala, Coco, Tato, Don Café, Tamalita, Flan, Cajeta, Slowy, Tico, Estrella — plus Boba (Taiwan crossover guest)
**Setting:** A giant table set up at the corner soda, big enough for the whole world of friends
**Theme:** Pura Vida means a life full of family, food, and gratitude.
**Spanish Spotlight:** corazón (koh-rah-SOHN) — heart

---

It started, as the very best stories end, with Gallo standing on a salt shaker at dawn, counting chairs.

"Eleven... twelve... thirteen," he announced, his little woven palm hat slipping over one eye. "Pinto! We have thirteen chairs and only twelve friends! Either I have invented a chair, or a chair has invented itself, and frankly both possibilities frighten me."

Pinto, the small glossy black bean beside him, gave his tiny wooden spoon a calm twirl and pointed it at the very end of the long, long table — at one empty seat with a name-card in careful handwriting: *Boba — our friend across the sea.*

"That chair is for Boba, mae," Pinto said softly. "The tapioca pearl from Taiwan. The mail-boat is bringing her across the whole wide ocean, just like she promised."

Gallo squinted down the dusty road toward the harbor. No boat. No bounce. Nothing but one very unimpressed chicken.

"Pinto," Gallo whispered. "The sea is extremely large. What if she gets lost in it?"

"Then we will keep her chair warm," said Pinto, "until she finds her way."

And that — friend — is how the most ridiculous, most wonderful, most *waited-for* feast ever cooked began.

---

The corner **soda** (SOH-dah) — the little family restaurant that was everyone's home base — had been scrubbed until it gleamed. Every table had been pushed together into one enormous table, so long it bent around the mango tree out front and nearly tripped the chicken, who filed a formal complaint by clucking at it.

Because Gallo and Pinto were the hosts of the Grand **Pura Vida** (POO-rah VEE-dah) — pure life, the happiest hello there is — Feast, they were in charge of the most important dish of all: a great steaming batch of gallo pinto, cooked from their very own rice-and-bean cousins, who had traveled in from every corner of the country just for the honor.

"Crispier, cousin Gallo!" hollered a rice cousin from the skillet, wiggling with delight. "I want EDGES! I want to be the crunchiest thing anyone has ever loved!"

"You shall have edges beyond your wildest dreams," Gallo promised solemnly, stirring with both arms.

Nearby, Flan the coconut flan jiggled in worried circles. "I have been awake since four in the morning," he confessed, his pale-gold dome trembling under its caramel. "What if the caramel slides? Steady, steady... ooh, *wobbly*!" He gave a nervous giggle and nearly slid off his own plate.

"Flan, my friend," came a slow, warm rumble. Don Café the coffee bean shuffled in, leaning on his tiny walking stick, smelling like the most beautiful morning. "Good things brew slowly. The caramel will hold. The boat will come. A feast is not made by hurrying — it is made by gathering."

"...And if the boat is late?" Gallo asked, glancing at the empty road again.

"Then we will gather *patience* first," said Don Café, "and eat it as an appetizer."

---

By mid-morning the friends arrived in a glorious, clattering parade.

Coco the granizado came skating in on a melting trail of rainbow syrup, sunglasses gleaming. "TUANIS!" he bellowed, spraying a fine sweet snow over everyone. "This table is HUGE! It has its own weather!"

Tico the toucan swooped down next, rainbow beak first, fresh from his morning loop over the harbor. "Harbor report!" he announced. "No mail-boat yet. Also, there is a big gray cloud sitting out on the sea like a grumpy cat, and the fishermen say the tide is running late. *Also* also — somebody already ate three tortillas before we even sat down!"

Everyone turned. Tato the patacon, the flat golden plantain disc, froze mid-chew, crumbs on his crispy edges. "...I was testing them," he said earnestly. "For safety. Smash now, worry never!"

But Gallo barely laughed. He was up on the salt shaker again, watching the road. A gray cloud. A late tide. A small pearl, alone on a big sea.

"She'll come," said Pinto quietly, from right beside him. He had climbed up without a word, the way best friends do.

"Pura vida," sighed Slowy the sloth, arriving the way Slowy always arrived — which is to say, he had actually left home four days ago. He draped one long arm over a chair. "I... made... gooood... tiiiime..."

"You're early for *next* week's feast," Tico reported.

"Theeen... I... shall... naaap... until... it... staaarts..." said Slowy, and did.

---

Tamalita the tamale bustled in with her banana-leaf bow tied just so, steam puffing out as she giggled, and Cajeta the little coconut-fudge ball bounced in beside her — so high on the last bounce that she landed in Lala's whipped-cream cloud with a soft *poomf*.

"Sweet and round, watch me BOUND!" Cajeta announced, slightly muffled, from inside the cream.

"Oh!" gasped Lala the tres leches, the soft milky cake square glistening as she swooned. "A guest in my cream! How dramatic! How *delicious*! My cream is honored." She lifted Cajeta out with a gentle scoop and set her on a napkin, where she immediately bounced again out of sheer joy.

Last of all came Estrella the sea turtle, gliding slowly up from the beach, her mossy star-patterned shell still wet with morning sea. Gallo hopped down from the salt shaker so fast he lost his hat.

"Estrella! You came from the water — did you see the mail-boat? Did you see Boba?"

Estrella's ancient eyes were calm as old tide pools. "I saw the boat, little one, far out where the gray cloud grumbles. The captain has slowed down to let the squall pass — wise captains always do. The sea is wide, and the tide is late, but neither of them is unkind." She touched the empty chair gently with one flipper. "Follow the moon home, I told her once. The moon pulls the tide, and the tide carries the boat. Your friend is exactly where the sea wants her."

"The sea," Gallo muttered, "could want her a little *faster*."

---

Noon came. The gallo pinto stayed warm in its big clay pot. The friends played a waiting game where Tico described foods and everyone guessed them, which Tato lost immediately by answering "tortilla" eleven times in a row, ten of which were wrong and one of which was extremely correct.

The afternoon light turned gold. Flan's caramel held, but Gallo's smile was starting to slide.

"We could begin," offered Coco gently. "Save her a plate?"

"No," said Gallo, and his voice was small but very sure. "A feast for everyone isn't a feast for everyone if it's missing *someone*. We wait."

The whole table went quiet and warm at that, the way a kitchen does when the bread is almost done.

And then — far off, down at the harbor — *TOOOOT.*

Slowy opened one eye. "Eeeither... that... is... the... maaail-boat..." he said, "or... the... chicken... has... learned... truuumpet..."

"BEAK THE NEWS!" Tico exploded, rocketing straight up out of his chair. "THE MAIL-BOAT IS IN! THE FRIEND FROM ACROSS THE SEA HAS ARRIVED! THIS IS THE BIGGEST NEWS THE CORNER SODA HAS EVER HEARD, AND I ONCE REPORTED ON A GOAT!"

Up the dusty road came something small and round and shiny-dark, bouncing — *boing, boing, boing* — past the astonished chicken, calling out in a bright, breathless voice:

"AM I LATE? Please-please-please tell me I'm not too late!"

It was **Boba** — the perfectly round, glossy tapioca pearl from a night market in Taiwan, squishy and bouncy and grinning from one chewy edge to the other. She came to a wobbling stop at the empty chair, panting.

"The mail-boat hit a squall and the captain said we had to wait, and I rode the whole way in the parcel bin between a birthday package and a box of socks, and the socks SNORED," she gasped. "But I made it! Did I make it?"

"You made it just right, mae," said Pinto warmly, pulling out her chair with his little spoon.

Gallo didn't say anything at all. He just hugged her, which for a grain of rice hugging a tapioca pearl looked like a polka dot hugging a period, and felt like the end of a very long wait.

---

And oh, *what* a feast it became.

"Rise and PINTO!" Gallo crowed at last, and he and Pinto proudly carried out the great clay pot of gallo pinto they had cooked — the batch made from their rice-and-bean cousins, who cheered from inside the pot like a tiny stadium.

"We get to be the very first bite!" sang the crispiest rice cousin, and the bean cousins all cheered, because in their world, becoming the feast was the happiest, most honorable thing of all — like being the first song at a party, the one everybody remembers. It wasn't sad one bit. The cousins giggled all the way onto the plates.

There was **casado** (kah-SAH-doh) — the "married" plate, where rice and beans and plantains all live happily together — and tortillas, and Tato's beautiful crispy patacones. Tamalita popped a tiny banana-leaf bow onto the top of Boba's round head. "Wrapped with love!" she beamed. "*Everyone* at this table gets a bow."

"A bow!" Boba whispered, delighted. "**Xièxie** (SHYEH-shyeh) — that's thank you, where I come from. Xièxie, xièxie, a hundred times!"

"Stick around," said Coco, sliding her a fizzing rainbow granizado. "Stay frosty, stay sweet, new friend."

Boba looked down the impossibly long table — every friend, every plate, every crumb of it. "We have night markets in Taiwan," she said softly, "with a thousand foods and a thousand lights. But I have never, *ever* seen a table where every single friend brought their whole heart."

"That," said Don Café gently, raising his tiny cup, "is pura vida. Not just pure life. A life full of family. Full of food. Full of gratitude. A full belly — and a fuller **corazón** (koh-rah-SOHN) — that's the heart, the warm drum in the middle of you."

"Corazón," Boba repeated, pressing a squishy hand to her round middle. "Mine is the fullest it has ever been. And I ate almost nothing on that boat, so I know it isn't dinner."

---

They ate until the sun grew sleepy and gold and the first stars came out to see what all the laughing was about. Slowy woke precisely in time for dessert, which everyone agreed was his greatest achievement. Cajeta bounced one last bounce and rolled to a gentle, happy stop against Tamalita. Flan glowed with quiet caramel pride. Tico ran out of news for the first time in his life and simply sat, full and content, his rainbow beak resting on the table.

"Soak it all in," Lala murmured dreamily to nobody and everybody, "soak it *aaall* in."

Gallo and Pinto climbed up onto the salt shaker one final time and looked down the long table — at every friend, from every story, with milky footprints and crispy crumbs and syrup smiles, all of them gathered, all of them home, and not one chair empty anymore.

"Pinto," Gallo whispered, his toasty edges glowing. "Every adventure we ever had. Every friend we ever made. All at one table."

"Better together, mae," said Pinto, leaning his small black-bean shoulder against his best friend's. "Always better together."

Boba raised her fizzy granizado high. "To pura vida!" she called.

And the whole table — turtle and toucan and sloth, fudge and flan and tamale, cake and coffee and crispy plantain, one tapioca pearl who crossed an ocean in a parcel bin, and two little grains of gallo pinto at the very top of the salt shaker — lifted their voices together until the mango tree shivered with happiness:

"**PURA VIDA!**"

Full bellies. Fuller corazones. And a whole collection of love, gathered at last around one enormous, ridiculous, perfect table, under a sky slowly filling up with stars.
