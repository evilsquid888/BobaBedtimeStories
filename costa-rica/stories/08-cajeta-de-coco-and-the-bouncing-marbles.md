# Story 8: Cajeta de Coco and the Bouncing Marbles

**Main Character:** Cajeta the Coconut Fudge
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A village sweet shop window full of candy jars
**Theme:** Being small and sweet is a superpower.
**Spanish Spotlight:** dulce (DOOL-seh) = sweet / candy

---

In the cheeriest little sweet shop in all of Pura Vida Town, there lived a coconut-fudge ball no bigger than a thumbprint, and her name was Cajeta. She was round — gloriously, perfectly round — and rolled in toasted coconut shreds so she looked fuzzy, like a tiny golden tennis ball who had hugged a fluffy towel. When she got excited (which was often), she bounced. *Boing, boing, boing.*

"Sweet and round, watch me bound!" she sang, springing across the shop windowsill at sunrise.

But this particular morning, Cajeta wasn't bouncing so high. She'd just rolled past the tall glass jars on the display shelf — the towering peppermint sticks, the grand frosted cookies, the proud wedge of cake the size of a house — and felt, for the very first time, terribly, achingly *small.*

"They're so big," she whispered. "And I'm... a crumb."

The morning sun winked through the window, but Cajeta didn't wink back.

---

The sweet shop window was the most magnificent place in town. Every morning the candy came alive the moment the shopkeeper flipped the little sign to *Abierto* — open — and went off to brew her coffee. Then the jars rattled, the wrappers rustled, and the whole window stretched and yawned like a sleepy rainbow.

There was Don Bombón, a round chocolate truffle who was very tall and very dignified and gave speeches about *posture.* There was Señora Galleta, a frosted sugar cookie wide as a dinner plate, who fanned herself with a sprinkle. And up on the highest shelf — way, way up — sat the Marble Jar.

Oh, the Marble Jar. It was the grandest thing in the whole window: a fat glass jar crammed with hundreds of swirled candy marbles, every color of a tropical sunset, glittering like a treasure chest left open by a pirate parrot.

"Now *those* are important," said Cajeta with a sigh, gazing up. "They sit on the highest shelf. They sparkle. People press their noses to the glass just to see them." She looked down at her own fuzzy little self. "Nobody presses their nose to see a crumb."

"You're not a crumb, mija," boomed Don Bombón from his velvet cushion. He puffed out his glossy chest. "You are simply... *compact.* Like me, but, ehm. Less."

"Less," repeated Cajeta sadly. "That's the whole problem."

Señora Galleta fanned herself. "Don't fret, dear. Being small is *very* fashionable this season. Why, I'd give my best sprinkle to be smaller. Do you know how hard it is to turn around in this window?" She tried to demonstrate, knocked over a lollipop, and pretended she hadn't.

Cajeta giggled — just a little. But the achy feeling stayed.

"Watch this," she said, hoping to feel grand for a moment. She bounced as high as she possibly could — *BOING!* — straight up past the chocolate box, past the jelly beans, past the candy giraffe's knobby knees. For one glorious second she was the highest thing in the whole window!

And then gravity remembered her. Down she came — *boing, boing, plop* — landing in a soft fuzzy heap beside Señora Galleta's elbow.

"Marvelous!" said the cookie kindly. "Such *spring!*"

"It's just bouncing," sighed Cajeta. "Anyone can bounce. The big candies do *important* things. Don Bombón gives speeches. You hold the whole middle shelf together. The marbles are admired by the whole town. And me? I just... go *boing.*" She tucked her chin into her coconut fluff. "Boing doesn't save anybody."

She wanted to *matter.* She wanted to be the kind of **dulce** (DOOL-seh) = sweet that people remembered. And how could anyone remember the smallest treat in the window? Big things were what people noticed. Big cakes, big jars, big speeches. Not a thumbprint-sized ball of fudge who only knew one trick.

So she did what small sad things often do: she rolled off to a quiet corner behind a peppermint stick, curled up round and tight, and watched the big candies have their big important morning. The sun climbed higher. The town outside began to wake — bicycle bells, a barking dog, the warm smell of someone's breakfast. And Cajeta, the smallest **dulce** in the window, felt about as small as a treat could possibly feel.

---

That's exactly when it happened.

A great gust of wind came barreling down the street — the kind that flings hats off heads and turns umbrellas inside out. *WHOOSH!* It rattled the shop door. It rattled the window. And way up high on the tallest shelf, the grand Marble Jar gave a little wobble.

Then a bigger wobble.

"Oh dear," said Don Bombón.

"Oh *DEAR,*" said Señora Galleta.

The jar tipped. The lid popped. And down rained the marbles — hundreds of swirled candy marbles, every color of sunset, bouncing and scattering and rolling in every direction at once. *Plink! Plonk! Pling-pling-pling!* They skittered toward the open shelf edge, toward the long, long drop to the hard wooden floor below — where, if they fell, they'd roll into the dusty dark under the counter and be lost forever, swept up and thrown away before the shopkeeper ever saw.

"They'll be lost!" cried Señora Galleta. "They're too far! We're too big to chase them — we'd squash them flat!"

Don Bombón tried to roll forward and immediately got stuck between two jars. "I am — hupf — too dignified for this!"

The marbles tumbled toward the edge. Closer. Closer.

And in her quiet corner, Cajeta the smallest fudge ball uncurled.

She looked at the marbles. She looked at the gap between the shelves. She looked at her own round, springy, perfectly *round* self.

And she grinned.

"Sweet and round," she whispered, "watch me **bound!**"

---

*BOING!*

Cajeta launched herself off the windowsill like a tiny golden cannonball. She bounced off a peppermint stick — *boing!* — sailed clean across the gap, and landed *plop* right in the path of the first runaway marble. *Tink!* She bumped it gently — just enough — and sent it rolling safely back into a teacup the shopkeeper had left out.

"One!" she cheered.

A whole stream of marbles raced toward the edge. Too many for any big candy to catch. But Cajeta was small. Cajeta was round. Cajeta could go *anywhere.*

She bounced off the rim of a gumball jar — *boing!* — flew through the air, and rolled herself flat across the shelf like a bowling ball, scooping three marbles into the curl of a cinnamon-roll wrapper. *Tink-tink-tink!*

"Three! Four! FIVE!"

"Look at her GO!" gasped Señora Galleta, fanning herself so fast a sprinkle flew off.

A marble dropped clean off the high shelf, plummeting toward the floor. Cajeta dove after it — *whee!* — bounced off the side of a chocolate box halfway down, met the falling marble mid-air, and *bopped* it gently back up onto a lower shelf where it wobbled to a safe, happy stop.

"How is she *doing* that?!" cried Don Bombón, still wedged between his jars.

"Because she's small enough to fit and round enough to bounce!" called Señora Galleta. "She's the only one who *can!*"

Cajeta heard that. And something warm bloomed right in the middle of her chewy little heart.

*The only one who can.*

She bounced higher. She rolled faster. *Boing, plink, boing, tink!* She zigged through the legs of a candy giraffe and zagged under a row of jelly beans, herding marbles like a tiny sheepdog made of fudge. Where a big candy would have crushed them, Cajeta nudged them. Where a tall candy couldn't reach, Cajeta *fit.* She was a marble herself, in a way — round and rolling and sweet to the core — so she knew exactly how to speak their bouncing language.

Two marbles broke away, racing toward a crack between the floorboards beyond the shelf. Don Bombón gasped. Señora Galleta shrieked. But Cajeta was already moving. She pinged off a striped lollipop, ricocheted off the gumball jar, and *whammed* into the pair just before the crack — *clack-clack!* — knocking them both into the safe curl of the cinnamon wrapper. A perfect double-bounce. She didn't even slow down.

"This way, little ones! *¡Vamos!*" — let's go! — "Into the teacup! ¡Pura Vida!"

One by one, *plink* by *plonk,* she gathered every last marble into the teacup, the cinnamon wrapper, and a cozy little pile in the corner — safe, sound, and accounted for. Not one was lost. Not one rolled into the dusty dark.

When the very last marble settled with a contented *clink,* the whole window went quiet.

Then it erupted.

"¡BRAVO!" boomed Don Bombón, finally popping free from his jars and toppling over with joy.

"Our hero! Our tiny, fuzzy, magnificent hero!" sang Señora Galleta, raining sprinkles like confetti.

The marbles themselves — all hundreds of them — rolled together and spelled out, in wobbly swirly candy letters: *G-R-A-C-I-A-S.* Thank you.

Cajeta sat in the middle of it all, a little dizzy, a lot proud, and grinning so wide her toasted coconut shreds quivered.

"You saved us," squeaked the smallest marble, rolling up beside her. "Because you understood us. The big candies meant well — but you're the one who *fit.*"

Cajeta looked down at her round little self. The self she'd called a crumb that very morning.

"I'm not too small," she said softly, like she was tasting the words for the first time. "I'm exactly the right size for the job that needed doing." She gave a little hop, and this time the *boing* felt different — not silly, but proud. "All this time I thought being small meant being less. But it just meant I could go where nobody else could go."

"That, mija," said Don Bombón, dabbing his glossy eye with a corner of velvet cushion, "is the wisest thing anyone has said in this window all year. And I give a *lot* of speeches."

"Far too many," whispered Señora Galleta, and they all laughed until sprinkles flew.

"You're the sweetest **dulce** in the whole window," said Don Bombón warmly. "And the bounciest."

---

When the shopkeeper came back with her coffee, she found her marbles tidied into the teacup as if by magic, and a tiny fuzzy fudge ball resting right in the center of the sunny windowsill.

"Well now," she chuckled, picking up Cajeta and setting her gently on a little golden pedestal — front and center, right where every passing child could see her. "*This* one belongs up where she'll be admired. The sweetest things come in the smallest packages, don't they?"

And from that day on, the smallest treat in the window had the very best seat in the house. Noses pressed to the glass to see *her.* Children pointed and grinned. And every morning when the shop sign flipped to *Abierto,* Cajeta would bounce a happy little hello to the marbles, the cookie, and the truffle — her friends, her family, her whole sweet world.

*Boing, boing, boing.*

"Sweet and round," she'd sing, "watch me bound!"

And the whole window would cheer.

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, you are exactly the right size for the wonderful things only you can do. The world doesn't need you to be bigger or taller or grander — it needs *you,* round and warm and sweet to the core, just as you are. So snuggle down small and cozy, like a fuzzy fudge ball in the warmest corner of the window, and know that your sweetness is a superpower all its own. Pura Vida, dear heart. Bounce gently into your dreams. Goodnight.
