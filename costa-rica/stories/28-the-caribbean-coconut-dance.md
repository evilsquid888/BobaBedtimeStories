# Story 28: The Caribbean Coconut Dance

**Main Character:** Cajeta the Coconut Fudge
**Guest Characters:** Coco the Granizado
**Setting:** A breezy Caribbean-side beach town with calypso music
**Theme:** Be proud of where you come from and dance your own way.
**Spanish Spotlight:** baile (BY-leh) = dance

---

Cajeta the coconut-fudge ball bounced off the bus and landed face-first in a pile of warm sand, which is exactly how every great adventure should begin.

"PWAH!" she sputtered, popping up like a tiny round cannonball. Sand stuck all over her fuzzy toasted-coconut shreds, so now she looked less like a fudge ball and more like a confused little hedgehog. "Sweet and round, watch me bound!"

She had bounced clear across the country, from the cool green mountains all the way to the **playa** (PLAH-yah) = beach on the Caribbean side, where the air smelled of salt and ginger and something deliciously sweet. Palm trees leaned over like they were trying to hear a secret. And somewhere, drifting on the breeze, was the bounciest, jiggliest, happiest music she had ever heard.

*Plinkity-plink, boom-boom, plinkity-plink!*

Cajeta's whole golden body wiggled. She couldn't help it. Her toes — well, she didn't have toes, but if she'd had them, they would have wiggled too.

---

"TUANIS! You made it, mae!"

A tall, frosty cone of rainbow shaved ice came skating down the boardwalk on a wave of his own melted drips, tiny round sunglasses perched on his snowy peak. It was Coco the Granizado, dusted in powdered milk like fresh snow, somehow not melting even in the Caribbean heat. (Coco refused to melt out of pure coolness. That is a real skill.)

"Coco!" Cajeta bounced three times, very fast, because that is what she did when she was excited, and she was almost always excited. "Where ARE we? What's that MUSIC? Why does it make my insides feel like jelly? Are we late? Are we early? Is there food? I'm food, but is there OTHER food?"

"Whoa, whoa, slow your sprinkles." Coco laughed, lowering his sunglasses. "This is the Caribbean coast, the sweetest stretch of sand in all of Costa Rica. And THAT—" he pointed his frosty arm toward the music "—is **calipso** (cah-LEEP-soh) = calypso, the song of this whole town. Tonight's the big beach **baile** (BY-leh) = dance. Everybody's coming. Stay frosty, stay sweet!"

"A baile!" Cajeta's eyes went enormous. "I LOVE bailes! I dance ALL the time! Watch—"

And she launched into her dance, which was the same dance she did everywhere: a fast, frantic, mountain-style bounce-bounce-spin, the kind that went *boing-boing-boing* and kicked up little clouds of fudge dust.

The calypso music slid and swayed, smooth and slow as warm honey.

Cajeta went *boing-boing-boing.*

The two did not match. At all. It was a bit like a hummingbird trying to dance with a hammock.

Coco watched with his sunglasses slowly sliding down his frosty nose. "Huh," he said. "That is... a lot of bounce."

"Thank you!" Cajeta beamed, still going *boing-boing-boing.* "I've been practicing my whole life! Up in the mountains, everybody bounces! It's the only way to keep warm!"

"Mae, we are at the beach," Coco said gently. "It is approximately one thousand degrees."

"Then I'll bounce to keep COOL!" Cajeta declared, and bounced so high she nearly knocked a sleeping pelican off a fence post. The pelican gave her a very grumpy look and tucked its head back under its wing. "Sorry! My bad! Lovely beak, by the way!"

Coco laughed so hard a little snow flurried off his peak. "Okay, okay. There's plenty of time before the baile starts. Let's get you a snack — er, I mean, let's go meet everybody."

So Cajeta bounced along the boardwalk after her frosty friend, past little wooden stalls selling spiced patties and coconut bread, past a goat playing dominoes with a parrot, past a row of bicycles all leaning together like they were telling each other secrets. Everywhere she looked, the Caribbean coast was bright and loose and easygoing — and everywhere she went, Cajeta bounced. It was the only speed she had.

---

By the time the sun began melting into the sea — turning it all orange-sherbet and pink — the whole beach had filled up with the most wonderful crowd.

There were crabs clacking their claws like little castanets. There were fireflies blinking in time with the beat. There were coconuts — actual whole coconuts — swaying on their palm trees with their husky brown faces grinning, going *swish, swish, swish* to the rhythm like they'd been practicing since the world began.

And in the middle of it all, a band played on a driftwood stage: a smiling steel drum, a guitar with a sunburn, and a singing pineapple who could not carry a tune but carried it anyway, with great enthusiasm.

Everyone was doing the calypso baile. Smooth. Loose. Rolling like the waves. *Sway one way, sway the other, dip, and spin.*

Two starfish were doing it with all five of their arms at once, which looked extremely complicated and a little showy. A family of hermit crabs swayed in a polite little line, smallest to biggest. Even a passing sea breeze seemed to dip and swirl in time, ruffling everyone's hair and hats and husks.

"Just feel the rhythm, mae!" Coco called, gliding across the sand in a long, smooth, frosty figure-eight that left a sparkling trail behind him. He dipped. He swirled. He spun so coolly that three crabs fainted from sheer admiration. "Easy as melting!"

Cajeta rolled out onto the sand and gave it her very best.

*Boing-boing-boing!*

A nearby crab got bounced clean off his feet. *"¡Ay!"* he yelped, landing in a tide pool.

*Boing-boing-SPIN!*

Cajeta's fast spinning flung sand all over a very dignified sea cucumber who had spent twenty minutes getting comfortable.

*Boing—*

She bonked right into a swaying coconut, bounced off it like a pinball, *boinged* off a beach umbrella, *ponged* off the singing pineapple (who hit a note so high a dolphin answered from out at sea), and rolled to a stop, dizzy and dusty and very out of breath. A small crowd had gathered, and they were all watching her with the careful, kindly expression you make when someone has tried very hard at something they are very bad at.

The music kept going smooth and slow all around her, and Cajeta suddenly felt very, very small, and very, very *bouncy*, and not at all like everybody else.

"I can't do the baile," she said quietly. Her bounce had gone all flat. "Everyone here is so smooooth. And I'm just... boingy."

---

Coco skated over and sat down beside her in the cooling sand. For once, he wasn't loud.

"Hey, mae," he said gently. "Why are you trying to dance like the waves?"

"Because that's the dance!" Cajeta sniffled, which for a fudge ball sounds like a tiny squeak. "The smooth, swishy, swirly one. Everybody here is so swishy. You're swishy. The crabs are swishy. Even the WIND is swishy. And I'm not smooth. I'm a little round ball who goes boing. I bounce off pelicans. I bounce off pineapples. I bounced off a pineapple, Coco."

"You did," Coco agreed solemnly. "It was a memorable pineapple moment."

Just then, the breeze carried a familiar voice — slow, deep, and kind as morning.

"Good things brew slowly," said Don Café, the old roasted coffee bean, hobbling up the beach with his tiny straw hat and his little walking stick. He had traveled all the way from the highlands for the baile. "May I ask you something, little one? What are you made of?"

Cajeta blinked. "Coconut. Coconut fudge. Rolled in toasted coconut."

"Mmm." Don Café smiled, a slow crinkly smile. "And look around you. The palm trees? Coconut. The husky friends swaying on every branch? Coconut. The sweet milk in the granizado, the candy in the market, the song in the air—" he tapped his walking stick to the beat "—this whole coast was built on coconut, my dear. You did not travel away from home tonight."

He leaned in close, and he smelled like warm mornings.

"You came home."

---

Cajeta looked up.

She looked at the coconuts on the palm trees, swaying *swish, swish, swish.* She looked at the toasted coconut shreds all over her own fuzzy little body. She listened to the calypso music — and this time, she didn't try to chase it.

She just felt it.

And here is the secret she discovered, the one nobody had told her: the coconuts weren't dancing smooth like the waves at all. They were doing their OWN thing. *Swish-swish-BOUNCE. Swish-swish-BOUNCE.* They swayed AND they bobbed. They were bouncy too! They had just learned to bounce *in time.*

Cajeta gasped so hard she nearly inhaled a firefly.

"Coco," she whispered. "I don't have to dance like the waves. I have to dance like a *coconut.*"

"Now you're cooking with frost," said Coco, grinning wide.

"They bounce!" Cajeta said, bouncing once, just a little, just to test it. "They bounce IN TIME! I've been bouncing my whole life — I just never bounced to the *music* before!" She turned to Don Café, eyes shining. "Don Café, you're a genius. You smell like genius. You smell like Saturday mornings."

"I smell like coffee," said the old bean, chuckling. "But I will take the compliment, little coconut. Now—" he tipped his straw hat toward the stage "—go on. The baile is waiting. And so is everyone you bounced into. I suspect they would very much like to see what you can do."

---

Cajeta rolled to the very center of the beach, right under the driftwood stage, where the singing pineapple was wailing happily and the steel drum went *plinkity-plink.*

She closed her eyes. She felt the beat in her chewy little middle. And then, slowly, she began.

She swayed one way. *Swish.* She swayed the other. *Swish.* And then — *BOUNCE!*

*Swish, swish, BOUNCE!*

She wasn't fighting the calypso anymore. She was riding it, like a coconut on a swaying branch. Her bounce wasn't a mistake. It was her *flavor*. It was the springy, joyful, can't-sit-still heart of a coconut-fudge ball who had finally found her own kind of baile.

*Swish, swish, BOUNCE!*

The crab she'd knocked into the tide pool clambered out, watched for a moment, then started doing it too. *Clack, clack, HOP!* The dignified sea cucumber, sand and all, gave a little wobble. *Wobble, wobble, BOOP!* Even the coconuts up in the trees seemed to lean in closer, swaying along with their newest, smallest, sweetest cousin.

"¡PURA VIDA!" shouted the singing pineapple, completely off-key, and the whole beach cheered.

Coco skated in beside her. Don Café tapped his walking stick. The fireflies blinked along. And the entire Caribbean baile became one big, glorious, bouncing, swishing, hopping, wobbling celebration — every creature dancing in their very own way, all together, under a sky full of stars and the smell of salt and sweet coconut.

Cajeta bounced so high she could see the silver moon over the sea, and she laughed out loud, dusty and sandy and happier than she had ever been.

"This is MY baile!" she sang. "Sweet and round, watch me bound!"

---

When the music finally grew soft and slow, and the steel drum hummed its last sleepy *plink,* Cajeta curled up in a warm hollow of sand between Coco and Don Café, with the waves whispering hush-hush-hush and the coconuts nodding sleepily above.

She was still a little round ball who went boing. She would always be a little round ball who went boing. And now she knew — that was the most wonderful thing she could possibly be.

"Better than I dreamed," Cajeta yawned, her fuzzy coconut shreds glowing gold in the moonlight. "I found a whole town made of me."

"Pura vida, little coconut," Don Café murmured. "Pura vida."

And the warm Caribbean wind tucked them all in.

## Goodnight Blessing
May you dance your own dance, little one, and never once worry that your feet don't match the music. May you find, like Cajeta, that the things that make you different are the very things that make you home. The waves are whispering hush now, and the coconuts are nodding off, and somewhere a steel drum is humming you a lullaby. Wherever you come from, be proud — for there is no one in the whole wide world who bounces quite like you. Sleep sweet and round, dear heart, and dream of warm sand and starlit shores. Goodnight.
