# Story 19: Arenal the Gentle Volcano

**Main Character:** Lala the Tres Leches
**Guest Characters:** Flan the Coconut Flan, Coco the Granizado
**Setting:** The grassy base of warm, glowing Arenal volcano with hot springs
**Theme:** A warm heart can soothe even the grumpiest of giants.
**Spanish Spotlight:** volcán (vohl-KAHN) = volcano

---

Now, you should know something about Lala the Tres Leches before we begin. Lala was the squishiest, drippiest, most dramatic little cake square in all of Costa Rica, and she had exactly one problem: she loved warm things SO much that she could not stay away from them. Warm bread. Warm hugs. Warm puddles of afternoon sunlight on a windowsill.

So when Lala heard a faraway *RUMBLE-GRUMBLE-HARRUMPH* drifting across the green hills one evening, she gasped a soft, milky gasp and pressed her whipped-cream cloud flat against her cheek.

"Did you HEAR that?" she swooned, leaving a little milky footprint as she spun in place. "Something big. Something warm. Something *grumpy.* Oh, friends — we simply MUST go and soak it all in!"

And that, dear reader, is how three desserts ended up tiptoeing toward a volcano in the dark. Which, I'll admit, sounds like a terrible idea. But this volcano was no ordinary volcano. This was Arenal.

---

The path wound up through misty grass that tickled their bottoms. Coco the Granizado bounced along in his paper cone, his tiny round sunglasses glinting even in the moonlight, leaving a frosty sparkle-trail behind him.

"Tuanis," Coco said, which is the coolest way a Costa Rican can say *awesome.* "A midnight adventure. I am SO here for this. Stay frosty, stay sweet!"

Beside him wobbled Flan the Coconut Flan, jiggling at every single bump in the path. Flan, you see, was a worrier of the highest order, a polished golden dome of nerves under a shiny caramel drizzle.

"A — a *volcán?*" Flan wibbled. "We're walking *toward* the **volcán** (vohl-KAHN) = volcano? On PURPOSE? Steady, steady... ooh, *wobbly!*" He wobbled so hard a fleck of toasted coconut popped right off his top.

"Don't worry, Flan-Flan," Lala cooed, scooping his coconut fleck back on with a gentle dab. "I have a feeling about this one. A *warm* feeling. Right here in my spongey middle."

"Your spongey middle is also full of milk," Coco pointed out.

"It is BOTH," Lala said grandly. "Feelings AND milk. That is the magic of being me."

The closer they crept, the warmer the air grew, until the breeze felt like a cozy blanket fresh from the dryer. And then they reached the top of the rise — and there he was.

Arenal.

He was *enormous.* A great rounded mountain of a fellow, glowing a soft, sleepy orange from somewhere deep inside, like a night-light the size of the sky. Little curls of steam puffed from his peak and drifted up to tickle the stars. And all around his big grassy feet bubbled the most beautiful pools of steaming water — the famous hot springs, swirling and shimmering and warm.

But Arenal was *frowning.* You could tell, because the two craggy boulders above his glowing belly were scrunched down low like big stone eyebrows.

*HARRUMPH,* he rumbled, and the ground gave a tiny shiver. *HMPH. Go away. Nobody comes to see ME anymore.*

Flan let out a tiny squeak and ducked behind a tuft of grass. Coco's sunglasses fell clean off.

But Lala? Lala put her little squishy hands on her hips.

---

"Why," she said, marching right up to the giant, "are you so GRUMPY, you big warm mountain?"

Arenal blinked his glowing eyes, surprised that anyone had stayed. *Because,* he grumbled, steam huffing from his peak, *I used to be the most popular **volcán** in all the land. Everyone came to soak in my springs. They laughed. They splashed. They told me their dreams.* He sniffled, which for a volcano sounds like a far-off thunderclap. *But now? Now everyone is busy. Nobody visits. My springs sit empty and warm with nobody to warm. A fire with nobody to gather round it is just... a lonely fire.*

"Oh," Lala breathed, and her cinnamon dot quivered. "Oh, you poor, big, glowy thing. That is the *saddest* thing I ever heard, and I once watched a churro roll off a table."

"That WAS sad," Coco agreed solemnly, popping his sunglasses back on.

Arenal harrumphed again and turned his glowing face away. *It's no use. Desserts don't soak. You'll melt. You'll crumble. You'll get all soggy.*

And here — *here* — is where most desserts would have agreed and gone home. Soggy is a real and serious danger for cake.

But Lala the Tres Leches lifted her chin, and her whipped cream wobbled bravely.

"Mister Arenal," she announced, "I am *already* soggy. I am the soggiest cake who ever lived. I am made of milk and dreams and three kinds of soak. And I think — I *think* — a warm pool is exactly where a cake like me belongs."

---

What happened next, dear reader, was the bravest cannonball in the history of dessert.

Lala took three squishy steps back. She wiggled her bottom. And then — "SOAK IT ALL IN!" — she leapt right into the nearest steaming spring with a *sploosh!*

"LALA!" Flan shrieked, wobbling so violently his caramel sloshed.

But Lala did not crumble. Lala did not melt away. Lala bobbed up to the surface like a happy little dumpling, her whipped cream now a soft, warm halo, her whole spongey self glowing pink with bliss.

"Oh," she sighed, eyes fluttering shut. "Oh, *friends.* This is what I was BORN for. Get in here this instant."

Coco was next, naturally. "A hot tub volcano? This is the most *tuanis* thing I have ever — CANNONBALL!" He flung himself in, and the warm water swirled around him as he half-melted into the most relaxed slushie in all the land. "Okay. Okay. I take back everything cold I've ever said. This is incredible."

That left Flan, trembling at the edge.

"I — I can't," Flan whimpered. "What if I jiggle apart? What if I dissolve? What if my caramel runs off and I'm just a sad naked custard?"

Lala paddled over to the edge and looked up at her wobbly friend with the kindest, milkiest eyes.

"Flan," she said softly. "You've been wobbling your whole life because you were scared. But you know what a wobble *really* is?" She smiled. "It's a little dance. And volcanoes LOVE a good dance. Isn't that right, Mister Arenal?"

There was a long pause.

Then, from deep inside the great glowing giant, came a sound nobody expected.

*...Heh.*

A little one. Then a bigger one. *HEH-HEH-HARRUMPH-HO-HO!* Arenal was *chuckling,* and his chuckle made the warm water ripple and dance, and the ripples tickled Flan's toes, and Flan — well. Flan giggled. He couldn't help it. And before he knew it he had wibbled, wobbled, and *plopped* into the spring all on his own.

He didn't dissolve. He didn't fall apart. He floated like a happy golden bath toy, his caramel swirling into pretty ribbons in the warm water.

"I'm... I'm doing it," Flan whispered, amazed. "I'm SOAKING. Steady, steady... ooh — *lovely!*"

---

And oh, what a soak it was.

The three friends floated in Arenal's warm springs while the giant beamed brighter and brighter, no longer grumpy in the slightest. They told him their dreams, just like the old visitors used to. Lala dreamed of a bathtub made entirely of milk. Coco dreamed of a frozen wave he could surf forever. Flan dreamed — shyly — of one single day without a worry, and Arenal promised that tonight could be that day.

The big **volcán** glowed like a sleepy fireplace, warming them all the way through. Steam drifted up into the starry sky. Tico the Toucan, flying home far above, looked down and squawked, "Beak the news! Arenal's smiling again!" — and the whole jungle smiled back.

"You know," Arenal rumbled gently, his eyebrow-boulders soft now and content, "I thought I needed a *crowd* to feel warm. A hundred visitors. A thousand splashes." He sighed a happy, steamy sigh. "But it turns out... three good friends and one brave little cake are more than enough. *Pura vida,* my squishy ones."

"Pura vida," they sighed back together, melting into the warmth.

Lala floated on her back, her cinnamon dot pointing at the moon, the warmest and happiest she had ever been. She had come looking for something warm to soak in. But the truth — the cozy, milky truth — was that the warmest thing of all hadn't been the water at all.

It had been one grumpy giant's heart, finally, finally feeling loved again.

And that, she decided as her eyes grew sleepy, was the very best soak there ever was.
