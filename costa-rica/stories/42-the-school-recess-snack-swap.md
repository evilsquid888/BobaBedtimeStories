# Story 42: The School Recess Snack Swap

**Main Character:** Tato the Patacon
**Guest Characters:** Cajeta the Coconut Fudge
**Setting:** A sunny schoolyard at recess (recreo)
**Theme:** Trying a new friend's snack opens a new friendship.
**Spanish Spotlight:** amigo (ah-MEE-goh) = friend

---

The bell at Snackdrop Elementary did not ring. It *crunched*. That is what happens when the school bell is made of a giant toasted plantain chip, which it is, because every single thing at Snackdrop Elementary is made of food, including the principal, who is a very dignified mango.

*CRUNCH-CRUNCH-CRRRRUNCH!*

"**¡Recreo!**" cheered all the little snacks at once, which is the Spanish word for **recreo** (reh-KREH-oh) = recess, and it is everyone's favorite part of the day.

Tato the Patacon burst out the door first, because Tato always burst out doors first. He was a flat, round, golden-crispy smashed plantain disc, with crinkled crunchy edges and a soft chewy middle, dusted all over with a little sprinkle of salt. He was built sturdy, a little flat-footed, and — if we are being honest, and we always are — a tiny bit clumsy.

"Smash now, worry never!" Tato hollered, and immediately tripped over his own crunchy edge into a somersault.

The whole schoolyard giggled. Tato took a bow.

---

The schoolyard was bright and warm, with a big shady almond tree and a wobbly seesaw made of a churro. The snack-kids spread out their lunch napkins on the grass to begin the most important business of recreo: comparing snacks.

"I've got toasted corn nuts," said one.

"I've got a guava roll," said another.

"I," announced Tato proudly, lifting his lunch napkin, "have... me. I packed myself. With extra salt." He paused. "Is that weird? That feels weird now that I've said it out loud."

But across the yard, near the wobbly churro seesaw, sat a little snack that nobody recognized.

She was small and round, a ball of golden coconut fudge, chewy and dense, rolled all over in toasted coconut shreds so that she looked soft and fuzzy, like a tiny fluffy marble. She was sitting all by herself, holding her lunch napkin tight, and she had not opened it.

This was Cajeta. She was brand-new at Snackdrop Elementary. It was her very first recreo, and her tummy felt as wobbly as the churro seesaw.

---

Tato noticed her right away, because Tato noticed everybody. That was one of the very best things about him.

He flat-footed his way over and plopped down in the grass with a soft *crinkle*.

"Hi! I'm Tato! I'm a patacon, which means I'm a plantain that got smashed twice and fried twice and now I'm crunchy and very brave. Mostly brave. Brave-ish." He grinned. "What's your snack?"

Cajeta pulled her napkin even tighter. "I'm... Cajeta," she whispered. "I'm a **cajeta de coco**. Coconut fudge."

"A WHAT de WHO now?" Tato leaned in, fascinated.

"See, this is the problem." Cajeta's voice went small and sad. "Nobody here has ever heard of me. At my old school, everybody knew what a cajeta was. But here..." She peeked at all the corn nuts and guava rolls. "Here I'm the only one. What if I'm too strange? What if I taste *wrong*?" She squeezed her eyes shut. "What if nobody wants to be my **amigo** (ah-MEE-goh) = friend?"

Now, Tato did not fully understand being shy. Tato had never met a stranger in his life. But he understood feeling like the odd one out — he was, after all, the only kid in school who packed *himself* for lunch, which even he agreed was a little odd.

So he did what he always did when he didn't know what to do.

He thought very hard. His crispy forehead crinkled with effort. You could practically hear it: *crinkle, crinkle, crinkle.*

"AH-HA!" he shouted, leaping up so fast he tipped over backward. From the grass, upside down, he announced: "I have an IDEA!"

"Are you okay?"

"I'm GREAT. Help me up, I'm too flat to roll over."

Cajeta gave him a gentle push and he popped upright with a *boing-clatter*, like a coin landing flat.

"Okay," she said, brushing a coconut shred out of her eyes. "But before you tell me your idea — what if it's a *bad* idea? You looked very confident just now and it made me nervous."

"Most of my ideas are bad," Tato admitted cheerfully. "But this one is medium-good, which for me is practically a miracle. Here's the secret: I am the most clumsy snack in this whole school. I have fallen down nine times today. NINE. And do you know what happened every single time I fell?"

"...You got hurt?"

"Somebody laughed, then somebody helped me up, then we were friends." Tato beamed. "That's the trick nobody tells you about being new or strange or a little wobbly. The wobble is what makes everybody come closer. You just have to wobble where they can see you."

---

Here was Tato's idea, and I think it was rather a good one.

"A snack swap!" he declared, once he was right-side-up. "Everybody brings their snack to the middle of the yard. Everybody breaks off one little bite. And everybody trades, so we all taste *everything*. That way nobody's snack is strange — because by the end, *everybody* will have tried it!"

Cajeta's coconut eyes went wide. "But what if they try mine and make a face?"

"Then they make a face," said Tato simply. "I make a face fourteen times a day. Watch." He made a face. It was a very silly face. Cajeta giggled — and a tiny, surprised laugh is a wonderful first crack in a wall of worry.

"Besides," Tato added, "I have a secret weapon. I'm so curious about your snack I might explode. And I'm crunchy. If I explode, it'll be *loud*."

Cajeta laughed for real this time.

---

Tato stomped to the middle of the yard — well, he didn't *stomp*, exactly. Patacones are too flat to stomp dramatically. He sort of *flapped* importantly.

"GATHER ROUND!" he boomed. "SNACK SWAP! One bite each! Everybody trades! New rule of recreo, effective immediately, signed me, Tato!"

The snack-kids buzzed with excitement and tumbled into a big circle on the grass. The corn-nut kid put down a corn nut. The guava-roll kid put down a guava curl. Somebody added a slice of star fruit. Somebody added a fried cassava chip. A shy little tamale cousin unwrapped one corner of herself and offered a warm crumb of masa. Tato proudly snapped off one of his own crunchy edges and set it down with a *tk*.

"OW," he said. "Worth it."

It was, in fact, the very first time anyone in Snackdrop history had volunteered to share a piece of *themselves*, and the whole circle looked at Tato with enormous respect.

"That's the bravest thing I've ever seen," whispered the guava-roll kid.

"I'm crunchy," Tato said modestly. "It grows back. Probably. We'll find out."

"And our newest amiga," Tato announced grandly, "brings something *nobody* has ever tasted. Which makes it the most exciting snack in the entire circle. Cajeta?"

Every face turned to her.

Cajeta gulped. Her fuzzy coconut shreds trembled. She *so* wanted to run and hide behind the almond tree. But Tato gave her a little nod, the kind of nod that says *I'm right here, you can do this.*

So she took the tiniest, bravest breath, rolled forward, and pinched off one little piece of golden fudge. She set it down in the circle, soft and shy.

"It's... cajeta de coco," she squeaked. "Be gentle."

---

The snack-kids all reached in and grabbed a bite of everything. They popped them in, all at once, *munch munch crunch chew*.

There was a long, chewing silence.

This, dear reader, is the scariest moment of any snack swap. Tato held his breath. Cajeta held *all* of her breath, plus some breath she had been saving since Tuesday.

Then the corn-nut kid froze mid-chew. His eyes went huge.

"Whose," he said slowly, "is the *golden fuzzy one*?"

Cajeta winced. "...Mine."

"It is the BEST THING I HAVE EVER EATEN IN MY ENTIRE LIFE."

"WHAT?" said Cajeta.

"WHAT?" said Tato, delighted.

"It's chewy AND sweet AND coconutty AND it sticks to your teeth in the *good* way!" The corn-nut kid spun in a circle. "MORE. I need to be its friend so I can have more."

And then the whole circle erupted.

"Cajeta, sit by me at lunch!"

"No, sit by ME!"

"Can you bring more tomorrow?"

"Teach me to say it — ca-HE-ta?"

"What's your favorite color, new amiga?!"

Cajeta sat in the middle of it all, completely surrounded, completely overwhelmed, and completely, gloriously happy. She bounced once — *boing!* — because that is what coconut fudge balls do when they are excited. "Sweet and round, watch me bound!" she cried, and the whole yard cheered.

Tato flapped his crispy edges with joy. He had been right. Cajeta wasn't too strange at all. She was just *new* — and new, it turns out, is one of the most wonderful things a snack can be, because new means there's a whole delicious friend nobody has discovered yet.

---

For the rest of recreo, Cajeta was never alone for one single second. She taught everyone how to say *cajeta de coco* and explained that *coco* means coconut, and the snack-kids practiced it so much that "**¡Pura vida!**" and "*coco!*" rang all across the sunny grass.

She got swept up onto the churro seesaw, where she discovered that coconut fudge balls are the perfect weight for seesawing and bounced up and down squealing with laughter. She organized a game of tag and won every round, because — as everyone now learned — a round fuzzy ball can simply *roll* away, which the corn-nut kid declared "extremely unfair and also the best thing ever." She let three different kids practice their pronunciation on her until "cajeta" rolled off their tongues as easily as their own names.

And the wonderful part, the part that made Tato's crispy chest feel warm and toasty all over, was watching Cajeta forget — completely forget — that she had ever been afraid at all.

She and Tato sat together under the almond tree, sharing the last crumbs of the snack swap.

"Thank you," Cajeta said softly. "I was so scared nobody would want a snack like me."

Tato smiled his crinkly smile. "That's the funny thing about being the new flavor," he said. "Everybody's curious. You just have to let them have a bite." He nudged her. "And a bite of you is *tuanis*."

"What does that mean?"

"It means *awesome*. Coco taught me." Tato puffed up proudly. "I learn things sometimes. Don't tell anyone."

Cajeta laughed her bouncy laugh, and somewhere across the yard, the plantain-chip bell began to crunch the end of recreo.

But this time, when it crunched, nobody groaned.

Because tomorrow there would be another recreo. And another snack swap. And a new amiga who, this morning, had been too shy to open her napkin — and who now had a whole schoolyard of friends waiting to say her name.

*CRUNCH-CRUNCH-CRRRRUNCH!*

"Best recreo ever," sighed Cajeta.

"Smash now, worry never," agreed Tato. And the two amigos walked back inside together, leaving little salty, coconutty footprints all the way to the door.

## Goodnight Blessing
May you always be brave enough to open your napkin, little one, and offer the world a little bite of who you are. May you find a Tato who flaps over to say hello, and may you be a Tato for someone who is sitting all alone. There is no flavor too strange, no snack too new — only friends you haven't met yet. So snuggle down, soft and warm as coconut fudge, and dream of sunny grass and golden crumbs and a whole circle of amigos calling your name. *Pura vida*, sweet dreamer. Goodnight.
