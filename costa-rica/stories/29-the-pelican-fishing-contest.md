# Story 29: The Pelican Fishing Contest

**Main Character:** Tato the Patacon
**Guest Characters:** Coco the Granizado
**Setting:** A wooden fishing pier on the warm Pacific
**Theme:** Trying your best matters more than winning.
**Spanish Spotlight:** **pez** (pess) — fish

---

The morning the sun came up over the Pacific, it found Tato the Patacon already standing at the very end of the wooden pier, holding a fishing pole nearly twice as tall as he was.

He was a flat, round, golden-crispy smashed plantain — sturdy, salty, and just the tiniest bit flat-footed. The pole wobbled. So did Tato. He planted his crunchy little feet wider and squinted out at the sparkling water.

"Smash now, worry never!" he announced to nobody, because nobody else was awake yet.

A wave slapped the pier. Tato slapped right back, lost his balance, and sat down hard on a coil of rope with a crispy *crunch*.

"Okay," he said, dusting salt off himself. "First try doesn't count."

It was the day of the Great Pier Fishing Contest, and Tato had decided — very bravely, very loudly, and possibly very foolishly — that this was the year he would win.

---

By the time the sun was fully up, the whole pier had filled with friends and folding chairs and the smell of warm sea breeze.

Coco the Granizado came skating down the boards in his paper cone, a tall fluffy mound of rainbow-syruped shaved ice with tiny round sunglasses and a dusting of powdered milk like fresh snow. He was already half-melting in the heat and didn't seem to mind one bit.

"Taaato!" Coco called, gliding to a stop and spraying a little frosty mist everywhere. "My guy! You really signed up for this? Tuanis. Stay frosty, stay sweet!"

"I'm going to catch the biggest **pez** (pess) — that means *fish* — anybody's ever seen," Tato said, puffing out his crispy chest. "Maybe two."

"Bold," said Coco, nodding slowly behind his sunglasses. "I respect it. I will be cheering so loud the *pez* will hear me underwater."

That was when the show-off arrived.

He came swooping in low over the water with a *WHOOSH*, then landed on the pier railing so smoothly that not one feather wiggled out of place. He was an enormous pelican with a beak like a giant scoop and a grin like he'd already won.

"Greetings, snacks," said the pelican, which was a rude way to say hello, but he said it so cheerfully that it was hard to be mad. "Pelican Pancho, three-time fishing champion. You may applaud."

Nobody applauded.

Pancho applauded for himself.

"Watch and learn," he said. He tipped forward, dove off the railing, plunged into the sea with barely a splash, and popped back up a second later with a fat silver *pez* flapping in his beak. He gulped it down, smacked his lips, and bowed.

"That's one," Pancho said. "Anybody else? No? I'll wait."

Tato's crispy edges crinkled with determination. "Game on," he said.

---

Tato reeled back his pole. He aimed at the water. He took a deep breath that smelled like salt and sunshine.

"Smash now—" he began, and hurled his line with all his might.

The hook flew up.

The hook flew sideways.

The hook came right back around and caught the brim of Tato's own little cap, yanking it off his head and dropping it into the sea, where it floated away looking very relaxed about the whole thing.

"...worry never," Tato finished weakly.

Coco laughed so hard he nearly tipped over. "Bro, you fished your *own hat!* That's a new record!"

"It was a warm-up cast," said Tato with great dignity.

Pancho dove again. Came up with another *pez*. "That's two," he sang.

Tato tried again. This time the hook landed in the water — a real, honest-to-goodness splash! He held his breath. The line twitched. He yanked!

Up came a long, slimy ribbon of green seaweed, which slapped him directly in the face with a wet *FLOP* and hung there like a soggy mustache.

"I caught a vegetable," Tato said through the seaweed.

"Looks good on you," said Coco. "Very mature. Distinguished."

Pancho dove. Came up. "Three," he announced, not even bothering to look.

---

And so it went, all morning long, under the bright warm sun.

Tato cast, and caught an old rubber boot.

Tato cast, and hooked a crab who climbed up the line, looked Tato right in the eye, said something rude in crab, and climbed back down.

Tato cast, and somehow reeled in a starfish that was *extremely* annoyed about being woken up.

Tato cast, and pulled up nothing at all, which was honestly the best result so far.

Every single time, Pancho swooped, splashed, and surfaced with another wriggling silver *pez*, until the pile beside him grew taller than Coco's cone.

By lunchtime, Tato's arms ached, his line was tangled, and his sturdy crispy shoulders had begun to droop.

"Maybe I'm just no good at this," he said quietly, sitting down on the warm planks. "I trained all week. I practiced my casting in a bucket. I even named my fishing pole. Her name is Brenda." He patted the pole. "And I haven't caught one single *pez*."

Coco skated over and sat down beside him, dripping a small puddle of rainbow.

"Hey," Coco said gently, lifting his sunglasses for once. "Can I tell you something kinda real?"

"Okay."

"This whole morning?" Coco said. "Pancho's been the best fisher on the pier. No question. But you've been the best *day*."

Tato blinked. "...What?"

"You fished your own hat!" Coco said, grinning. "You wore a seaweed mustache! You had a whole conversation with a crab! Everybody on this pier has been laughing and clapping and having the best morning of their lives — and it's because of *you*, man. Not Pancho. Pancho's just catching fish. You're catching *fun*."

Tato looked back at the pier. And — oh. Coco was right. The whole crowd was smiling. A little girl waved at him. Two old fishermen were chuckling and pointing at the rubber boot, retelling the story to each other like it was the funniest thing they'd seen in years.

Tato felt something warm bloom in his crispy middle. It felt better than winning. It felt a little like sunshine.

"You know what," Tato said, standing up and brushing the salt off his knees. "Pura vida. I'm gonna stop trying to win... and just try my best, and have fun doing it."

"NOW you're talking," said Coco. "Let's gooo!"

---

So Tato fished his best for the pure joy of it.

He cast big silly loops just to make the little girl giggle. He pretended his rubber boot was a fancy trophy and gave it a bow. When the grumpy starfish came up again, Tato gently set it back in the water and said, "Sorry, friend. Sleep tight."

And every time Pancho surfaced with another *pez*, instead of feeling small about it, Tato cheered.

"WHOA! Nice one, Pancho! That's a beauty! Look at the size of that thing!"

Pancho paused, a fish halfway to his beak. Nobody had ever cheered for him before. Mostly they just rolled their eyes.

"You're... clapping for me?" Pancho said.

"Of course!" said Tato. "You're amazing at this! That dive? Gorgeous. Ten out of ten. Brenda and I could never."

Pancho landed on the railing, looking at his enormous pile of fish, and then at Tato, who had caught one boot, one seaweed mustache, and the respect of an entire pier.

The big pelican got very quiet.

"You've been having more fun than me," Pancho said slowly, "and you haven't caught a single thing."

"That's the secret," said Tato, beaming. "Trying your best and having fun *is* the prize. The fish were never the point. The point was the morning."

Pancho looked down at his fish. Then he did something a three-time champion had never, ever done.

He scooped up half his catch in his great big beak, glided over, and gently laid them in a bucket at Tato's feet.

"Then let's share the morning," Pancho said, a little shyly. "Winner and best-day-haver. Together."

"Better together!" said Coco, who had heard Pinto say that once and thought it was just about the nicest thing ever.

The whole pier burst into cheers — and this time, they were cheering for *everybody*.

When the contest judge counted the catches at last, Pelican Pancho was declared the Great Pier Fishing Champion, four times running. But instead of his usual bow-for-himself, Pancho just tipped his beak toward Tato and said quietly, "Couldn't have won a better morning without you, amigo."

---

That evening, the three of them sat at the end of the wooden pier, watching the sun melt into the warm Pacific like a scoop of orange syrup over Coco's head. They shared the fish with all the hungry pelican families nearby, and Tato finally fished his soggy hat back out of the sea and wore it proudly, salt-stains and all.

"Best fishing contest ever," sighed Tato.

"And you didn't even catch a *pez*," laughed Coco.

"Nope," said Tato, grinning his crispy grin as the stars came out one by one over the water. "I caught something way better."
