# Story 7: Flan Coconut and the Wobbly Stage

**Main Character:** Flan the Coconut Flan
**Guest Characters:** Lala the Tres Leches
**Setting:** A family birthday party on a breezy patio
**Theme:** Even the wobbliest among us can stand tall when friends cheer.
**Spanish Spotlight:** **mae** (MY-eh) — buddy, or dude

---

Flan the Coconut Flan had exactly one job that afternoon, and it was the scariest job in the whole world.

He had to sit. On a plate. In the middle of the table.

That was it.

But Flan was a jiggly pale-gold dome of coconut custard, glistening under a shiny river of caramel, and the trouble with being made of jiggle is that jiggle has opinions. The moment somebody set his plate down on the long birthday table, Flan felt his caramel slide one nervous inch sideways.

"Steady, steady," he whispered to himself. "Ooh — wobbly!"

The whole patio was decorated for a birthday. Paper streamers swooped between two mango trees. A warm breeze carried the smell of grilled corn and somebody's grandmother's perfume. And right in the middle of it all sat Flan, wobbling like a custard caught in a tiny earthquake of his own making.

He had never felt so much like dessert in his life.

---

"Ay, mae!" called a soft, swoony voice. (**Mae** — that's MY-eh — means *buddy* or *dude*, and Costa Ricans say it to everyone they love.) "You look like a little golden sunrise!"

It was Lala the Tres Leches, a square of milk-soaked sponge cake topped with a wobbly cloud of whipped cream and a single cinnamon dot. She scooted across the table on her own little trail of milky footprints and plopped down beside him with a happy *squish*.

"Lala," Flan whispered, "I cannot do this."

"Do what, my love? You are just sitting."

"That's the problem! Everyone is going to *look* at me." Flan's caramel rippled. "I'm the centerpiece. The star of the table. And stars are supposed to be smooth and calm and elegant, and I —" he gulped — "I am made entirely of *wibble*."

Right on cue, a gust of patio breeze rolled through. Flan jiggled so hard his caramel did a full loop-the-loop around his middle.

"See?!" he squeaked. "WIBBLE!"

Lala gasped the way she gasped at beautiful things. "Oh, *soak it all in*, mae — that was gorgeous. You moved like the ocean."

"The ocean is supposed to move. *I* am supposed to sit still and look polished." Flan wobbled so dramatically that a toasted coconut fleck nearly slid off his dome. He caught it just in time, breathing hard. "If I keep this up, I am going to jiggle right off the plate before the birthday song even starts."

---

Now, the birthday party belonged to a little girl named Sofía, who was turning nine. And in Pura Vida Town, when a child turns nine, the family puts the most special dessert in the very center of the table so that the birthday kid can make a wish over it before the first slice. That dessert was Flan. Which meant Flan was not just sitting on a plate.

He was sitting on a *throne of expectations*.

"I can't be the wish dessert," Flan moaned. "What if Sofía wishes for a calm, smooth, fancy flan, and instead she gets — *me?*" He demonstrated by wobbling so hard his whole plate went *clink-clink-clink* against the table.

Lala patted him gently with a fluffy whipped-cream arm. "Mae. Listen. Have you ever watched what happens when someone wobbles?"

"They get embarrassed?"

"No! People *smile*. Wobbling is the happiest thing a dessert can do." Lala did a little shimmy of her own, and her cinnamon dot bounced. "When I jiggle, I make people giggle. *Better together*, mae — your wobble and a kid's laugh."

Flan was not convinced. He'd spent his whole short custardy life trying very hard to *not* wobble. He'd practiced sitting still. He'd practiced holding his caramel in place. He had even, once, tried to balance a single grain of rice on his dome for three whole minutes to prove he could be *steady*. (The rice grain, a cheerful fellow named Gallo, had hummed the entire time and shouted "Rise and pinto!" before bouncing off. It had not gone well.)

"You don't understand," Flan said, lowering his voice as if telling a great secret. "Yesterday I practiced in front of the bathroom mirror. I told myself: *Flan, you are a smooth and serious dessert.* And do you know what happened?"

"What happened, mae?"

"I wobbled so hard I scared myself. I jumped. And then I wobbled *more* because I'd jumped." He sighed a tiny custardy sigh. "I am not built for stillness, Lala. I am built for jiggling at the worst possible moments."

Lala tilted her cinnamon-dotted head. "And what if the moments aren't the worst? What if they're the *best*?"

Flan blinked. He had genuinely never considered this. The idea sat in his caramel like a warm spoonful of sunshine.

But then the patio breeze gusted, his dome did a frightened little hop, and the warm thought slid right back out. "No," he decided. "No, I'm sure it's the worst moments."

"Steady, steady," Flan whispered again, squeezing his eyes shut. "I will NOT wobble. I will be the calmest flan that ever flanned."

---

The patio door swung open.

Out came the whole family — aunts and uncles, two grandmothers, a baby being carried upside down by a giggling cousin, and right in the middle, wearing a paper crown, the birthday girl herself. Sofía.

She had a gap in her front teeth and a smile that took up most of her face.

"Where's the flan?" she shouted. "I want to make my wish!"

Everyone gathered around the table. And Flan, sitting in the very center under all those eyes, felt his nervous wobble start up so badly that his caramel sloshed clear up to his coconut flecks.

*No no no,* he thought. *Be smooth. Be elegant. Be a fancy dessert that does NOT —*

The breeze gusted.

A streamer fluttered.

The baby shrieked happily.

And Flan, trying with all his might to hold perfectly still, did the exact opposite of perfectly still. He went off like a wobble-volcano. He jiggled north. He jiggled south. He bounced. He boinged. His caramel did three loops and a curtsey. His whole dome shimmied like jelly in a hurricane, and a single toasted coconut fleck launched off the top of his head and landed — *plip* — right on the tip of Sofía's nose.

Flan was mortified. He froze mid-jiggle, certain he had ruined the entire birthday.

Then Sofía laughed.

Not a polite laugh. A *real* one — the snorty, helpless, can't-stop kind that bends a kid in half. "He JIGGLED!" she howled, pointing. "He bounced a coconut on my NOSE! Do it again! Wobble again!"

And the whole family laughed with her. The grandmothers clapped. The upside-down baby squealed. An uncle wobbled his own belly in solidarity.

---

Flan blinked. "They're... laughing?"

"They're laughing *with* you, mae," Lala said warmly. "Look at their faces. You did that."

Flan looked. And it was true — every single person around the table was beaming. Not at a smooth dessert. Not at a fancy dessert. At *him*, the wibbliest dessert in all of Pura Vida Town, the one who couldn't hold still to save his caramel.

A little distance away, a fuzzy mossy-green sloth had been hanging from the lowest branch of the mango tree, watching the whole party in his sleepy, slow-motion way. This was Slowy, who lived in the trees around the patio and never missed a celebration (mostly because it took him so long to get anywhere that he simply stayed). He raised one long, dreamy arm and gave Flan the slowest, kindest thumbs-up in the history of thumbs.

"Puuura... viiida..." Slowy drawled, smiling his permanent gentle smile. "Theee... wobble... is... beauuutiful, mae."

Even a passing toucan got in on it. Tico, a glossy black bird with an enormous rainbow beak, swooped down onto a streamer and announced to the entire patio: "BEAK THE NEWS! Local flan discovered to be the WIGGLIEST, WONDERFULLEST dessert in Pura Vida Town! Eyewitnesses report uncontrollable giggling! More at bedtime!"

Flan was so surprised he forgot to be embarrassed. "Wait — you all *like* it?"

"Like it?" Sofía was still wiping happy tears. "It's the best thing that's ever happened at a birthday! Wobble again!"

Flan glanced nervously at Lala.

"You heard the birthday girl," Lala whispered, giving him a gentle whipped-cream nudge. "Soak it all in."

So Flan did the bravest thing he had ever done.

He stopped holding still.

He let himself go — really, truly *wobble*. He bounced left and right. He did a big dramatic dome-shimmy. He spun his caramel in a glistening swirl and finished with a wibble so grand it sent a tiny coconut snowfall sprinkling across the whole table.

The patio exploded with cheers.

"Best flan EVER!" shouted Sofía.

"He's *dancing*!" cried a grandmother.

And Flan — who had spent his whole life thinking his wobble was a flaw — suddenly understood. His wobble wasn't the broken part of being a flan. His wobble was the *gift*. It was the thing that turned a quiet birthday into a giggling, clapping, coconut-flying party.

"I think," Flan said breathlessly, doing one last happy jiggle, "I think I was born to be wobbly."

"That's the whole point, mae," Lala said, beaming. "Better together — you and every smile in this yard."

And here is the funny thing about confidence: once Flan stopped being afraid of his wobble, it stopped feeling scary at all. The breeze still gusted. The streamers still fluttered. The baby still shrieked. And Flan still jiggled with every single one — but now, instead of clenching up and panicking, he just rolled with it, like a little boat bobbing happily on friendly waves.

"Watch this one," he told Lala, and he did a wobble so smooth and silly and grand that it looked almost like a bow. The grandmothers gasped with delight.

"Encore!" cried the upside-down baby's cousin.

"He's taking *requests* now!" Tico announced from his streamer. "BEAK THE NEWS — flan accepts dance requests, line forms behind the birthday girl!"

Slowy, still hanging in the mango tree, had managed to lift his other arm too. Now he had two slow thumbs up. For Slowy, this was practically a standing ovation.

Flan looked around at all of it — the laughing family, the cheering animals, his dear mae Lala beaming beside him — and felt something he had never felt in his whole nervous little life. He felt *proud* of his wobble. Not embarrassed. Not sorry. *Proud*. As if the jiggle had been a present hidden inside him all along, and he had finally worked up the courage to unwrap it.

---

Then came the part Flan had been most afraid of, and it turned out to be the most wonderful part of all.

Sofía leaned over her birthday flan, closed her eyes, and made her wish. Flan didn't know what she wished for — wishes are secret — but he hoped, with all his custardy heart, that it was a good one.

When she opened her eyes, she picked up her spoon. "Ready, flan?"

And Flan — proud, glowing, the happiest dessert on the whole breezy patio — wobbled one last cheerful *yes*.

Because here is a secret about desserts in Pura Vida Town: being eaten on someone's birthday is the greatest honor there is. It means you were chosen. It means you were the most special thing on the table. It means a kid will remember you, giggling, for the rest of her life.

So when Sofía took that first sweet, cool, caramel-soaked bite, Flan didn't feel scared at all. He felt like a firework finally getting to light the sky. He felt like the best wibble in the world, going out in a burst of joy.

"Mmmm," Sofía sighed. "Wobbliest. Best. Birthday. Ever."

And Lala the Tres Leches, soaking quietly in her own little puddle of milk, wiped a happy tear with her whipped-cream arm and whispered, "*Pura vida*, mae. Pura vida."

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, you do not have to be smooth or perfect or still to be the star of the table. The very thing that makes you *you* — your giggle, your wibble, your wonderful one-of-a-kind self — is exactly the thing the people who love you cheer for the loudest. So tonight, let your worries jiggle right off the plate. You are special, you are chosen, and you are loved just as you are, mae. Wobble proud and rest easy. Goodnight.
