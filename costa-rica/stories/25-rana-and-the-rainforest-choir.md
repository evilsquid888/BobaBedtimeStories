# Story 25: Rana and the Rainforest Choir

**Main Character:** Rana — a tiny bright red-eyed tree frog with blue-striped legs, shy but with a surprising croak
**Guest Characters:** Don Café
**Setting:** The Monteverde rainforest at nightfall, full of dripping leaves
**Theme:** Every small voice helps complete the song
**Spanish Spotlight:** canción (kahn-SYOHN) = song

---

High up in the cloud forest of Monteverde, on a leaf no bigger than a teaspoon, lived the shyest frog in all of Costa Rica.

Her name was Rana, and she was about the size of a grape that had decided to grow legs. Her back was a green so bright it looked freshly painted, her belly was creamy white, and her skinny legs were striped in blue and orange like somebody had drawn on her with crayons. But the most marvelous things about Rana were her eyes — two enormous, round, ruby-red eyes that took up nearly half her face. When she blinked, it sounded like two tiny umbrellas opening. *Foomp. Foomp.*

The trouble was this: Rana had never once croaked in front of anybody. Not once. Not ever. She was certain — absolutely, positively certain — that her croak was far too small and silly to matter.

And tonight, of all nights, that was a very big problem.

---

You see, every year when the first cool rains rolled down the mountain, the whole rainforest gathered for the Grand Nightfall Concert. And tonight the mist was sliding down between the trees, fat raindrops were plopping off every leaf, and the entire jungle was tuning up.

"PLINK," went the raindrops on the broad banana leaves.
"Chirr-chirr-chirr," went the crickets in the ferns.
"Whoooo," went a sleepy owl who was, frankly, still mostly asleep.

Rana crouched on her teaspoon leaf and pulled her legs in tight. The concert was beautiful. The concert was enormous. And the concert, she was sure, did not need a grape-sized frog squeaking along.

"They have raindrops," she whispered to herself. "They have crickets. They have an entire *owl*. Who needs me?"

A drop of mist landed right between her ruby eyes. *Foomp. Foomp.* She blinked it away.

She had been practicing her croak, of course. Just never where anyone could hear. In the deepest hours of the night, when even the owl was finally and truly asleep, Rana would creep to the very tip of her leaf, take a great big breath, and — just barely, just for herself — let out the tiniest *brk*. Then she would look around in a panic to make absolutely sure no toucan, no katydid, no curious tree-snail had been watching. And then she would pull her legs in tight and feel both relieved and, somehow, a little bit lonely.

Because here is a secret about shy things: they are not quiet because they have nothing to say. They are quiet because they are saving it all up, hoping that someday it will be wanted.

That was when she smelled something warm and wonderful drifting up through the cold, wet air — something roasty and toasty and sweet. It smelled, Rana thought, like the coziest morning in the world had gotten lost in the middle of the night.

Down a winding root staircase came a round, jolly fellow in a little woven hat, balancing a steaming tray on his belly. He was a coffee bean — a great big polished one, dark and glossy as a chestnut, with a cheerful crease running right down his middle like a permanent smile.

"¡Buenas noches!" he boomed. "Don Café, at your service! Finest cup in the cloud forest, and the only waiter who is also the beverage. Convenient, no?"

"Oh!" squeaked Rana, and immediately clapped both front feet over her mouth, mortified that any sound had escaped her at all.

Don Café set down his tray and squinted his shiny coffee eyes at the little leaf. "Now then. Why is the smallest frog in Monteverde sitting all alone, missing the best concert of the year?"

Rana peeked between her toes. "Because," she mumbled, "I don't have anything to add. Everyone else is loud and important. My croak is too small. It's *silly*."

Don Café gasped so dramatically that he nearly tipped off the root. "*Silly?* My dear, *silly* is the secret spice of the universe! Do you know what they call music here?" He swept off his little hat. "**Canción** (kahn-SYOHN) = song. And a true *canción* is never built from big voices alone."

---

He hopped — well, *rolled*, mostly — onto the leaf beside her, and the leaf bent so low that Rana nearly slid into his coffee tray.

"Listen, little one," said Don Café, cupping a hand to where his ear would be, if coffee beans had ears. "Tell me what you hear."

Rana listened. "Raindrops," she said. "And crickets. And a snoring owl."

"Good. Now — listen for the spaces *between* the raindrops."

Rana scrunched her ruby eyes and listened very, very hard. And she heard it — a tiny gap, a little pocket of silence, a hole in the music shaped *exactly* like one small croak.

"You see?" Don Café whispered, his coffee-crease smile stretching wide. "The forest left you a seat at the table. There is a part of the *canción* that no raindrop and no cricket and no enormous half-asleep owl can fill. There is a part that only fits *you*."

"But what if I croak wrong?" said Rana, her voice trembling like a dewdrop about to fall.

"Then you will croak *gloriously* wrong, and we will all enjoy it tremendously," said Don Café, and he took a noisy sip of himself for courage. "Do you know, when I was a young green bean, no bigger than a pea, I was certain I was too plain to ever amount to anything? A little brown nothing on a hillside. And then somebody roasted me, and ground me, and stirred me into hot water — and *suddenly*..." He spread his arms wide, sloshing happily. "I was the most beloved cup in all of Costa Rica! The same little bean. I simply had to be *brewed*." He leaned in close. "You, *ranita*, are about to be brewed."

Rana giggled in spite of herself — a small, surprised, hiccupy sound. It was the closest she had ever come to making a noise on purpose in front of somebody.

"There!" cried Don Café, pointing a triumphant arm. "A laugh! A laugh is just a croak that escaped before you could stop it. The hard part is already done. Come. The conductor is about to begin."

---

The conductor, it turned out, was a firefly named Lucecita, who hovered at the center of the clearing glowing soft and gold. She raised her tiny glowing tail like a baton, and the whole rainforest leaned in.

Down came the baton.

"PLINK-plink-PLINK!" sang the raindrops, drumming the banana leaves.
"Chirr-chirr-CHIRR!" sang the crickets, sawing away on their little leg-fiddles.
"Whoooo... whoo... *zzz*," sang the owl, who managed two notes before drifting back to sleep, which everyone agreed was very on-brand for the owl.

And it was lovely. It truly was. But Don Café leaned close and murmured, "Wait for it... wait... *there!*"

And Rana heard it again — that little gap, that pocket of quiet, that hole in the music exactly her size.

Her heart hammered. Her ruby eyes went round as full moons. Every shy bone in her grape-sized body told her to pull her legs in tight and stay silent. *They will laugh,* whispered the shy little voice inside her. *You are too small. You will ruin it.*

But across the clearing she caught the glint of Don Café's glossy smile, and he gave her the gentlest nod, as if to say: *the seat is yours, little one. Nobody else can sit in it.*

But then she thought about the empty space in the song. A *canción* with a hole in it. A song that wasn't finished. A song waiting — just for her.

So Rana filled her tiny lungs, pointed her chin at the misty sky, opened her mouth...

...and croaked.

Now. Everyone expected a squeak. A peep. A noise like a wet hiccup.

What came out instead was a **"GA-DOONK!"** so deep and round and *enormous* that three sleeping geckos fell out of a fern, a moth dropped its sandwich, and the owl woke up shouting, "I'M AWAKE! I HEARD NOTHING! WHO SAID FROG?"

Rana clapped her feet over her mouth in horror.

But the clearing had gone perfectly, magically still.

And then —

"¡Bravo!" boomed Don Café, sloshing happily. "Did you *hear* that? The little frog with the big voice!"

Lucecita the firefly was blinking so fast with delight that the whole clearing flickered like a disco. "Again!" she chimed. "The hole in our *canción* — you filled it! Croak again, little one, right on the beat!"

So Rana did.

"PLINK-plink-PLINK!" went the raindrops.
"Chirr-chirr-CHIRR!" went the crickets.
"GA-DOONK!" went Rana, right in the pocket, right where the song had been waiting all along.

And — oh! — it was *complete*. The raindrops needed the crickets. The crickets needed the rain. And both of them, it turned out, had been needing one deep, round, glorious **GA-DOONK** to tie the whole thing together, like the last bead on a necklace.

The whole forest sang as one. The mist swirled in time. Don Café did a little shimmy that nearly cost him his hat. Even the owl joined in, mostly on the wrong notes, which somehow made it perfect.

And the most wonderful thing of all was this: now that Rana was listening so carefully, she could hear *everyone*. The shy little tree-snail tapping his shell, *tik-tik-tik*. A nervous beetle who only dared to hum. A line of leaf-cutter ants marching in tiny, papery footsteps. Voices she had never noticed before — because she had been too busy believing that only the loud ones counted. Every single one had a pocket of the *canción* shaped just for them. And the song was bigger and richer and cozier for every small voice in it.

"You hear them now, don't you?" said Don Café softly, settling beside her. "Once you find your own voice, you start hearing everybody else's. Funny how that works."

---

When the last raindrop plinked and the last cricket chirred and Rana's final GA-DOONK rolled away into the soft, glowing dark, the whole clearing burst into the patter of applause — which, in a rainforest, sounds an awful lot like more rain, and is just as cozy.

Rana sat on her teaspoon leaf, blinking her enormous ruby eyes, feeling about ten sizes bigger than a grape.

"I had it the whole time," she whispered, amazed. "A voice that fit."

"Of course you did, *ranita*," said Don Café, tucking himself back behind his tray. "Everybody does. The trick is only to *use* it." He winked his glossy coffee eye. "A song is just a lot of small voices, holding hands."

And from that night on, no Grand Nightfall Concert in Monteverde was ever, ever held without one small, shy, blue-striped frog — who was no longer quite so shy — sitting right in the middle, filling the pocket of quiet with the biggest little voice in the cloud forest.

## Goodnight Blessing

Now snuggle down, little dreamer, into your warm and rumpled nest.

Listen — can you hear it? The soft *plink* of rain, the cozy *chirr* of crickets, and somewhere out in the misty dark, one tiny frog adding her one perfect note. Your voice is part of the song too, you know. Small and silly and yours, and the world is not quite finished without it.

But that is a song for tomorrow. Tonight, let the rainforest hum you to sleep, slow and gentle and warm.

Close your great big sleepy eyes. *Foomp. Foomp.*

Goodnight.
