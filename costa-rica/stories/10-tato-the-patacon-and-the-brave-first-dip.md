# Story 10: Tato the Patacón and the Brave First Dip

**Main Character:** Tato the Patacón — a twice-fried green plantain round, crispy-golden and chunky, cautious and proud of staying crunchy
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A sunny Pacific-coast soda (little eatery) by the beach
**Theme:** Trying something new can be the best crunch of all
**Spanish Spotlight:** **valiente** (bah-lee-EN-teh) = brave

---

On the warm Pacific edge of Costa Rica, where the waves went *shhh* like the sea was telling everybody a secret, there sat a little blue-and-yellow **soda** (SOH-dah) = a little family eatery — the kind with sandy floors, a parrot-shaped clock, and a window that looked straight out at the bobbing fishing boats.

And on a wide white plate, sunning himself like a tiny crispy king, sat Tato.

Tato was a patacón — a green plantain that had been fried, smashed flat, and fried all over again until he was golden as a doubloon and chunky as a cookie. He had ruffly crunchy edges, a soft warm middle, and two stubby feet he liked to tap when he was pleased.

"Listen to that," Tato said proudly, wiggling. *Cruncha-cruncha.* "Crunch like that doesn't grow on trees, you know. Well." He paused. "It sort of *did.* But you understand."

The trouble started the moment the dips arrived.

---

A breakfast plate in a Pacific soda is a busy, friendly place, and Tato's plate was no exception. Beside him sat his pals: a wedge of fried cheese, a scoop of soft white rice, and a little crowd of plantain cousins fresh from the fryer, still giggling about how *tuanis* the bubbles had felt.

But right in the very center of the plate, brand-new and gleaming, sat a small bowl of something dark and smooth.

"And *what,*" said Tato, narrowing his eyes, "is THAT?"

"That," said the fried cheese in a sizzly little voice, "is the new dip. Doña Lucía made it this morning. **Frijoles** — black beans! Mashed up all velvety with a squeeze of lime."

Tato leaned away so fast he nearly tipped off the plate. "Velvety," he repeated, horrified. "Smooth. *Wet.*"

"It's delicious!" said a plantain cousin, who promptly dove right in, swam through the dark glossy dip, and came up wearing a beanie of beans, grinning ear to (where ears would be). "Mmm! Try it, Tato! It's the best thing I've ever — *mmf* — tasted!"

But Tato had gone very still and very pale (well, as pale as golden gets).

You see, more than anything in the whole wide world, Tato was proud of being crunchy. Crunchy was his *thing.* And every patacón knew the terrible legend, whispered in the basket late at night: *dip yourself in something wet, and you'll go SOGGY.* Floppy. Bendy. A patacón with no crunch left at all — the saddest thing a snack could be.

"No, thank you," Tato said firmly, scooting to the dry edge of the plate. "I am a *crunchy* patacón. I have worked very hard on this crunch. I am not about to go diving into a bowl of mush and come out a wet noodle. I'll just stay right here. Crunchy. Safe. Dry as a beach towel."

And he folded his stubby feet and refused to budge.

---

All morning long, the plate emptied around him.

His plantain cousins dunked themselves boldly, one after another, each coming up shinier and happier than the last. "Whee!" cried one, doing a little cannonball — *ploop!* The fried cheese took a delicate dip and sighed like she'd just had a spa day.

"Come on, Tato!" they called, beans glistening. "Be **valiente** (bah-lee-EN-teh) = brave! Just one little dip!"

"I am PLENTY brave," Tato huffed, though he stayed exactly where he was. "I'm brave at being crunchy. That's my brave. A patacón knows his crunch and *guards* it."

But here is the thing nobody tells you about guarding your crunch on the dry edge of the plate while everybody else is splashing and laughing in the middle: it gets awfully lonely. And awfully quiet. And after a while, even the most beautiful crunch in the world starts to feel a little bit like just sitting still.

By mid-morning, Tato was the only one left.

The other cousins were gone — scooped up, dunked, and carried off on happy forks to delighted children at the tables. The plate was empty except for Tato and that small dark bowl, sitting together in the warm sun.

And the bowl, to Tato's enormous surprise, spoke.

"You've been staring at me all morning, little crunchy one," it said, in a soft, slow, velvety voice — exactly the voice you'd expect from mashed black beans. "Are you afraid of me?"

"I am NOT afraid," said Tato, who was a little bit afraid. "I'm... *cautious.* There's a difference. You're wet. I'm crunchy. We are simply not compatible. It's plantain science."

The dip rippled gently, which is how black beans chuckle. "I'm not going to drown your crunch, you know. I'm just here to dress it up a little. To make it *more.* Crunch and creamy, salty and smooth — that's not the end of crunch, niño. That's crunch's best friend."

Tato hummed nervously. "But what if I go soggy?"

"What if you don't?" said the dip. "What if you've been guarding a treasure so hard you forgot to *enjoy* it? The bravest thing a crunch can do," the beans murmured, "is trust that it'll still be there after the dip."

Tato thought about that for a long, quiet moment, his stubby feet tapping.

He thought about his cousins, splashing and laughing.
He thought about how *tuanis* their grins had looked.
He thought about how *very, very* alone the dry edge of the plate had become.

And way down in his warm soft middle, underneath all that careful crunch, Tato felt something brand-new and a little bit fizzy. Something that felt an awful lot like *curiosity.*

---

Just then the kitchen doors swung open with a cheerful *bang,* and out came Doña Lucía — the round, beaming cook with sand on her sandals and a flower behind her ear. Behind her trailed a small girl in a sun hat, clutching a fork and gazing at Tato's plate with enormous shining eyes.

"Oh!" the little girl gasped. "There's one patacón left! The crispiest one!" She turned to Doña Lucía. "Can I dip it in the frijoles? Pleeease?"

Doña Lucía laughed her big warm laugh. "Pura vida, mija. Go on. That's the best part."

The fork came down.

And Tato — for one wild, wobbly, glorious second — made a choice.

He did NOT scoot to the dry edge. He did NOT hide. He took the deepest breath a flat crispy thing can take, squeezed his eyes shut, remembered the velvety voice, and whispered the bravest words he knew:

"Be **valiente.** Dip now... worry never. *Allá vamos!*"

And down he went.

*PLOOSH.*

---

For one whole second, the world went dark and smooth and surprising.

The dip wrapped around him — not scary, not drowning, but *cozy,* like the softest velvet blanket folding over a warm cookie. It tickled into his ruffly edges. It hugged his golden self. And Tato, eyes squeezed tight, braced for the dreaded sog...

...which did not come.

He came up out of the bowl gleaming, wearing a glossy dark coat of creamy black beans, and the little girl lifted him high into the sunshine.

"Wait," Tato whispered, astonished. He gave himself a tiny wiggle.

*CRUNCH.*

He gasped so hard a fleck of bean flew off his edge. "I'm — I'm still crunchy! I dipped AND I'm crunchy! I'm crunchy *and* creamy! I'm crunchy and creamy and salty and *smooth* and — oh my goodness, I'm DELICIOUS!"

Because here is what no one had told Tato, hiding out there on his lonely dry edge: the dip hadn't taken his crunch at all. It had simply added a whole new wonderful layer *on top* of it. He was everything he'd been before — and now he was more.

"I was MORE the whole time I was scared of being LESS!" Tato cried, doing a happy little spin on the fork. "Why did nobody — oh, everybody DID tell me. I just wasn't listening!"

The little girl's eyes went round as sand dollars. "This is the BEST one," she breathed. "The crunchy creamy one."

And then — *crunch* — she took a great big joyful bite.

Now, you might think that's a frightening way for a story to end. But for a patacón, being eaten by a child whose eyes have gone wide with wonder is the proudest, happiest, most glorious thing in the whole world. It is the patacón's quiet little firework, finally lighting up the sky. And Tato — crispy, creamy, *valiente* Tato — glowed with pure golden joy all the way down.

*Crunch.* A happy munch.
*Crunch crunch.* A delighted giggle.
*Cruuunch.* The best bite she'd had all summer.

From the little bowl, the last of the black beans rippled warmly. "What did I tell you, niño? Crunch's best friend."

And Tato, in his last shining moment on the fork, understood it all the way down to his salt. Staying safe on the dry edge had kept his crunch — but it had kept him *small.* One brave dip had cracked the whole world open, and behind it had been something better than just crunch.

Behind it had been *everything.*

---

That evening, when the soda had gone quiet and the sea was singing its sleepy *shhh,* the fresh plantains snuggled into their basket and listened as the little bowl of black beans told them the tale of the crispiest patacón who almost missed the best bite of his life — until he was brave.

"Don't guard your crunch so hard you forget to dip, little ones," the beans murmured, soft as a tide going out. "The new thing isn't the end of you. Sometimes it's the very best part."

And out beyond the window, where the moon laid a silver path across the gentle Pacific, a brave golden patacón had drifted off into the happiest, crunchiest, creamiest sleep a snack ever knew.
