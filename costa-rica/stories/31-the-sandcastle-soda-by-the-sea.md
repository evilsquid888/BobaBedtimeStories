# Story 31: The Sandcastle Soda by the Sea

**Main Character:** Gallo and Pinto
**Guest Characters:** Coco the Granizado, Tato the Patacon, Lala the Tres Leches
**Setting:** A pop-up beach soda made of driftwood and shells
**Theme:** You can build a little bit of home anywhere with friends.
**Spanish Spotlight:** arena (ah-REH-nah) = sand

---

The very best mornings, Gallo always said, smelled like the sea — and also, just a little, like a skillet. So when the sun came peeking over the **playa** (PLAH-yah) = beach, painting the waves gold, Gallo bounced up so fast his little woven palm hat nearly flew off.

"Rise and pinto!" he hummed, shaking salt off his speckled white body. "Pinto, wake up, mae — today we open the soda!"

Pinto, the small glossy black bean snoozing beside him, opened one eye. His tiny pale stripe curled into a sleepy smile. "A soda? On the beach?" He patted his little wooden spoon. "All the way out here?"

"Especially out here!" Gallo spun in a happy circle. "Hungry people come to the beach! Tired surfers! Sandy turtles! And nobody — *nobody* — has fed them a single warm spoonful of gallo pinto. We will build the coziest little kitchen in all of Costa Rica."

Pinto looked at the wide, empty, windy stretch of shore. He looked at the gulls. He looked at one crab walking sideways like it owed nobody an explanation.

"Better together, mae," Pinto said warmly. "Let's build."

---

Now, a **soda** (SOH-dah) = small Costa Rican eatery is usually a tidy little kitchen with a few stools and the smell of something delicious. Gallo and Pinto didn't have a building. They had a beach. So they did what clever friends do — they used what the sea gave them.

They gathered driftwood, smooth and silver, and leaned it together into a snug little stand. They found shells for bowls — big curly ones for soup, small flat ones for spoons. Pinto hung a banana-leaf roof so the sun wouldn't burn anybody's breakfast. Gallo painted a sign on a flat piece of bark: **SODA LA ARENA** — *Sand Soda* — because the whole place sat right in the soft golden **arena** (ah-REH-nah) = sand.

"It's beautiful," Pinto breathed.

"It's HOME," Gallo hummed. "A little bit of home, anywhere."

They lit the tiny driftwood stove. They warmed the rice and beans in a shell the size of a hat. Steam curled up, smelling so good a pelican fainted gracefully into the surf.

And then the wind blew.

*Whoooosh.*

A puff of **arena** swirled up off the dunes and pattered — *tippity-tap* — right down into the gallo pinto.

"Hm," said Gallo.

Pinto peered into the shell-bowl. The rice and beans now had a faint, sparkly, sandy crunch. He took the smallest, bravest taste.

"...It's crunchy," Pinto reported gently.

"It's *seasoned*," Gallo said hopefully.

The wind blew again. *Whoooosh.* More **arena**.

"Okay," said Gallo, less hopefully, "now it's *very* seasoned."

Pinto tried covering the pot with a banana leaf. The wind flipped it off and sent it sailing down the beach like a tiny green kite. A gull caught it, looked deeply confused, and let it go.

Pinto tried digging the stove into a little hollow to hide from the breeze. The hollow promptly filled with sand, then with a small wave, then with a startled crab who had clearly been having a much better morning thirty seconds ago.

"Sorry, mae," Pinto said to the crab.

The crab said nothing. Crabs rarely do. But it gave Pinto a look that said, quite clearly, *this beach is mine and your breakfast is suspicious.*

---

This was, the narrator must admit, a wobbly start. (Don't worry — wobbly starts are how the best stories warm up.)

Every time Gallo stirred the pot, the sea breeze tossed another sprinkle of sand into it. Every bowl came out crunchier than the last. A passing crab tried a spoonful, made a face like a tiny grumpy general, and marched sideways back into a hole.

Gallo's sunny hum got a little quieter. "Pinto," he whispered, "what if I can't build home here after all? What if the beach is just... too beachy?"

Pinto put his little spoon on Gallo's shoulder. "We don't have to fix it alone, mae. Homes aren't built by two. They're built by friends."

And right on cue — because friends in this collection have *excellent* timing — three shapes came strolling down the **playa**.

First came Coco the Granizado, a tall fluffy mountain of rainbow shaved ice in a paper cone, tiny round sunglasses on, sparkling in the sun. "GALLO!" Coco boomed, breezy and loud. "A beach soda?! That's *tuanis*! Stay frosty, stay sweet, my friend!"

Behind him bounced — well, Tato the Patacon didn't bounce so much as *thud* cheerfully — a flat golden disc of twice-fried smashed plantain, crispy at the edges, dusted with salt, looking strong and dependable and just a tiny bit flat-footed. "Heard you needed help!" Tato announced. "Smash now, worry never!"

And gliding last, leaving little milky footprints in the sand, came Lala the Tres Leches — a soft pale-gold cake square dripping sweet milk, crowned with a wobbly cloud of cream and one perfect cinnamon dot. She gasped at the sea. She gasped at the soda. She gasped at a particularly nice seashell.

"Oh, *friends*," Lala swooned. "A kitchen made of driftwood and shells, right by the singing waves? Soak it all in!"

Gallo's eyes went shiny. "You all came?"

"Pura Vida," said Tato simply. "Friends come."

"We didn't even send a message," Gallo marveled.

Coco shrugged a cool, sparkly shrug. "Tico flew over yelling 'BEAK THE NEWS! GALLO IS OPENING A SODA ON THE PLAYA!' to the entire jungle. So." He adjusted his sunglasses. "We came."

Lala dabbed at a happy tear of milk. "It was either help our friends or sit at home being beautiful and lonely. Easy choice."

---

So Gallo explained the problem, and everyone leaned in over the gritty, sparkly, sandy pot.

"The **arena** keeps blowing into the gallo pinto," he sighed. "Every gust. We can't keep the beach *out* of the food."

Coco lowered his sunglasses thoughtfully. Tato scratched his crispy chin. Lala dripped a little, dramatically.

Then Tato stood up tall. "A wall," he said. "Beaches blow sideways. We build sideways back!" And before anyone could answer, he *thudded* himself flat into the sand, then another patacon-pal flopped beside him, and another — a whole row of crispy golden discs standing on edge like a little crunchy fence. They made a sturdy windbreak right where the breeze came whistling off the dunes.

"Smash now, worry never!" Tato cheered, slightly out of breath.

The next gust hit the patacon wall — *fwump!* — and the sand spilled harmlessly down its crispy side. Not a single grain reached the pot.

"It worked!" Gallo hummed, twirling.

"Of course it worked," said Tato, then quietly tipped over sideways into the sand because he had stood up too fast. "I'm okay. I meant to do that. Smash now, worry never!"

The crab, watching from its hole, gave a single approving claw-clack. Even the grumpiest general respects a good wall.

"Wait, wait," said Coco, breezy and grinning. "The food's safe — but it's *hot* out here. Hot beach, hot soup, sweaty surfers. You need cold, mae. You need *me*." He gave a cool little shimmy and his shaved-ice top sparkled. Coco set up beside the stove, scooping rainbow ice into shell-cups, dusting each one with powdered milk like fresh snow. "Granizado on one side, gallo pinto on the other. Hot and cold, salty and sweet. *That's* a soda."

"Just don't melt into the rice," Pinto said gently.

"Mae," said Coco, deeply offended, "I am a *professional*. I have melted in the finest places in Costa Rica." He paused. "And also a few sidewalks. But mostly fine places."

Then Lala glided forward, leaving her milky little footprints. "And every cozy home," she said dreamily, "needs something sweet at the end. May I?" She dripped three soft drops of her three-milk sweetness onto a plate, set out little cake squares, and topped each with a cloud of cream. "For after the rice and beans. To soak it all in."

But there was one more touch, and it was Pinto who thought of it.

He gathered the prettiest shells — the pearly pink ones, the swirly white ones, a blue one shaped like a tiny sleepy moon — and pressed them into the soft **arena** all around the soda, making a little path that twinkled when the sun hit it.

"So friends know the way," he said softly. "Every home should have a path that says *come in, you're welcome here.*"

Gallo's eyes went the shiniest of all. "Pinto. That's the best part."

"Better together, mae," Pinto said, and pressed one last shell into place.

Pinto looked around at the windbreak, the ice, the cake, the warm pot of finally-sand-free gallo pinto, and the friends laughing in the salty sun. His little stripe-smile went very wide.

"Gallo," he said softly. "Look. It's a kitchen. It's a *real* one."

"It's home," Gallo whispered. "We built a little bit of home... right here in the **arena**."

---

And oh, what a morning it became!

Word travels fast on a beach (especially when a certain loud toucan is involved, but that's another story). Soon the whole sunny **playa** lined up at Soda La Arena. Surfers dripping seawater. A family of crabs, including the grumpy general, who came back for seconds and pretended he hadn't. Sleepy iguanas who ordered, then forgot they had ordered, then ordered again. A pelican who paid in fish nobody had asked for. Even Estrella the gentle sea turtle drifted in on a wave, kind ancient eyes crinkling.

"This is a lovely little home you've built," Estrella said in her slow, kind tides. "Follow the moon home, my dears — but feed your friends first." And she had three bowls, because turtles are bigger than they look and twice as hungry.

Slowy the Sloth arrived too, lifting one long arm to wave hello — though, the narrator must report, he wouldn't actually finish that wave until sometime around lunch tomorrow. The friends propped a bowl beside him so it would be warm and waiting whenever he got there. That, too, is what home does.

Tato's wall held strong. Coco's granizado sparkled. Lala's cake disappeared in joyful spoonfuls. And Gallo and Pinto stood side by side at the warm shell-pot, serving bowl after bowl of perfect, crunch-free, golden gallo pinto, humming and spooning, spooning and humming.

"Better together, mae," Pinto said, scooping.

"Rise and PINTO!" Gallo sang to the whole beach, and the whole beach laughed.

When the lunch rush slowed and the sun hung high and golden, the five friends flopped down together in the soft warm **arena**, bellies happy, paper cones empty, the tide going *hush... hush... hush.*

"You know what's funny?" Gallo said, watching a gull steal one last grain of rice. "I thought home was a place. A kitchen with walls. But it isn't, is it?"

Lala sighed happily. "Home is wherever you soak it all in *together*."

"Home is a windbreak made of friends," said Tato.

"Home is cold treats and warm hugs," said Coco.

Pinto rested his little spoon on his round black tummy and looked at the sea. "Home," he said gently, "is whoever's sitting beside you in the sand."

And the waves said *hush*, and agreed.

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, wherever you lay your head tonight, know that home isn't only walls and roofs — it's the warm faces who build their breakfast beside you. You can carry a little bit of home into any new place, as long as you bring kindness, and friends, and room enough to share a bowl. May your dreams be soft as fresh shaved ice, warm as rice from a seaside pot, and gentle as a tide that says *hush, hush, you are safe.* Snuggle in close, brave builder of cozy places. Pura Vida, and goodnight. Goodnight.
