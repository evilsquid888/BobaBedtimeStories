# Story 41: The Mango-Season Fiesta

**Main Character:** Cajeta the Coconut Fudge
**Guest Characters:** Coco the Granizado, Lala the Tres Leches
**Setting:** A backyard mango tree heavy with ripe golden fruit
**Theme:** Abundance is best when you share it with the whole street.
**Spanish Spotlight:** mango (MAHN-goh) = mango

---

It was the kind of warm afternoon when the whole street smelled like sunshine and syrup, and Cajeta the Coconut Fudge was bouncing — because Cajeta was always bouncing — beneath the biggest, leafiest, most ridiculous tree on the block.

"Sweet and round, watch me bound!" she sang, springing up to bonk her fuzzy coconut head on a low branch.

*Thunk.*

A **mango** (MAHN-goh) = mango fell on her.

Then another. Then six more. Then so many golden mangoes rained down that Cajeta disappeared into a fragrant, glorious pile of them, with only her two little arms sticking out and waving like a swimmer caught in a delicious wave.

"Help!" she giggled. "I'm being attacked by breakfast!"

She popped her head free, blinking sticky sweet juice out of her eyes, and looked up. The tree was *groaning*. Every branch sagged. There were more mangoes up there than there were stars at night — fat, blushing-gold mangoes, each one as plump as a sleeping kitten.

And not one single soul to eat them all.

---

Cajeta climbed out of the mango mountain and put her tiny hands on her tiny hips.

"This," she announced to the empty backyard, "is a problem of *too much wonderful*."

She tried to eat one. It was the best thing she had ever tasted — sweet and tangy and dribbly, like the sun had learned to be a snack. She tried to eat a second. Lovely. She managed half of a third and then had to lie down on the grass and breathe slowly, the way you do after the fourth slice of birthday cake.

"Okay," she wheezed. "I cannot eat a whole tree. That was a foolish plan."

The mangoes kept falling. *Thunk. Thunk-thunk.* Soon there would be a hill of them, and then a mountain, and by Tuesday the whole backyard would be a sticky golden lake, and that seemed like an enormous waste of something so delicious.

Cajeta scrunched up her fuzzy little face and *thought*, which she did very fast, because Cajeta did everything very fast.

Eat them alone? No.

Pile them up and guard them? *No.* That sounded lonely, and also slightly sweaty.

But then her eyes went wide and shiny, and a grin spread across her toasted-coconut cheeks, because she had just had the kind of idea that is so good it makes your whole body fizz.

"A FIESTA!" she shouted, leaping so high she nearly landed back in the mango pile. "If I can't eat all the **mangos**, I'll invite the *whole street* to eat them with me! Sweet and round — share it all around!"

And off she bounced to spread the word, leaving a trail of little fuzzy footprints across the grass.

---

Her first stop was the coolest corner of the neighborhood, where Coco the Granizado was leaning against a fence post, slowly melting and refreezing in his paper cone, tiny round sunglasses perched on his rainbow-syruped head.

"Coco!" Cajeta bounced up. "Mango emergency!"

"Whoa, *tranquilo*, little fuzzball," Coco said, raising one frosty eyebrow. "There are no emergencies in summer. Only opportunities. What's *tuanis*?"

"My backyard mango tree dropped about ten thousand mangoes and I can't eat them and I thought — a FIESTA — and you have ICE — and—"

Coco lifted his sunglasses. "Did you say... ice... plus mango?"

"YES."

A slow, cool grin spread across his frosty face. "Mango granizado," he breathed, as though saying the name of a long-lost friend. "Shaved ice, rainbow syrup, fresh mango piled on top like a little golden hat. That, my bouncy amiga, is the most *tuanis* idea I have heard all season." He pushed off the fence. "Stay frosty, stay sweet — let's GO."

---

Their second stop was the squishiest, dreamiest house on the lane, where Lala the Tres Leches sat on a porch swing, glistening with three milks and wobbling her whipped-cream cloud at a passing butterfly.

"Lala!" the two of them called. "We're throwing a mango fiesta!"

Lala gasped. She gasped so dramatically that her cinnamon dot nearly fell off. "A *fiesta*?" she swooned, pressing one soft milky hand to her spongey chest. "Oh, I adore a fiesta. The streamers! The music! The way the evening light goes all golden and—" She stopped. Her cloud drooped. "Oh, but... what would I bring? I am only a humble little cake. I am soft. I leak. I cannot carry heavy things; I would simply... *squish*."

"Lala," said Cajeta gently, bouncing close. "You're made of *milk and sweetness*. Do you know what a mango *loves* most in the whole world?"

Lala blinked her sugary eyes. "...A nap?"

"Cream," said Cajeta. "Mango with cream is like a hug for your tongue. We *need* you."

Lala's whipped-cream cloud puffed right back up, fluffier than ever. "Then I shall come!" she declared, sliding off the swing and leaving a trail of little milky footprints. "Soak it all in, my darlings — Lala is on the case!"

---

By the time the three of them got back to the backyard, word had already spread, because word in this neighborhood traveled faster than a toucan with gossip.

Tato the Patacon arrived first, crunching up the path. "Heard there's heavy lifting!" he announced, flexing his crispy golden edges. "Smash now, worry never! Where do you want the mangoes? I'll stack 'em! I'll carry 'em! I'll—" He tripped over a mango and rolled gently into a bush. "...I'll do that, too."

Gallo and Pinto came humming down the lane with a giant pot between them.

"Rise and pinto!" chirped Gallo, her golden flecks gleaming. "We brought a pot for fresh mango juice, mm-hmm!"

"Better together, mae," said Pinto warmly, patting his little wooden spoon. "Plenty is heavy to carry alone. Lighter when shared."

Even Don Café shuffled in beneath his straw hat, leaning on his tiny walking stick, smelling of warm mornings. He looked up at the groaning, overstuffed tree and gave a slow, knowing nod.

"Ahh," he rumbled. "A tree this full has been *waiting* for this day, child. Good things brew slowly... but a generous heart? That ripens all at once."

Cajeta wasn't entirely sure what that meant, but it sounded very wise, so she nodded back as seriously as a bouncing fudge ball can possibly nod.

"Now then," Don Café went on, tapping his walking stick thoughtfully. "Before a fiesta, there is always one small worry. Where will all the people *sit*?"

And right on cue, a polite cough came from the gate.

Flan the Coconut Flan had arrived. He stood very straight and very nervous, his pale-gold custard dome glistening, his caramel drizzle catching the afternoon light. "I — I heard there was a gathering," he said carefully, "and I thought perhaps I could be of some use, although I do worry that with so many guests there will be a great deal of jostling, and I am, as you all know, *terribly* jiggly, and—" A mango rolled past his foot and he flinched. "Steady, steady... ooh, *wobbly*!"

"Flan!" Cajeta bounced over and gave his wobbly side a gentle pat. "You're perfect. You're the softest seat-cushion in the whole street. Everybody loves sitting near you because you're so squishy and cool."

Flan went a delighted shade of pink under his caramel. "Well," he sniffed proudly, "I *am* quite a comfortable fellow." And he settled himself in the shade like a fancy little ottoman, wobbling happily whenever a guest leaned against him.

---

And so the great Mango-Season Fiesta began.

Tato stacked mangoes into golden pyramids (and knocked over exactly two of them, which everyone agreed only added charm). Coco built a towering mango granizado, shaving ice into a fluffy snowy mound, drizzling rainbow syrup, and crowning it with fresh-cut mango that glowed like little suns. "Behold," he said, sliding on his sunglasses, "the most *tuanis* dessert in the history of the street."

Lala wobbled from neighbor to neighbor, dolloping soft milky cream onto mango slices, gasping with delight every single time, because Lala simply could not get *over* how beautiful it all was. "Look how the juice catches the light!" she swooned. "Look at the *colors*! I may faint. I shall faint deliciously."

Gallo and Pinto squeezed buckets of golden juice, humming a little working song that went *squeeze-and-pour, squeeze-and-more*. Don Café told slow, twinkly stories under the tree about the mango seasons of long ago, when he was just a young bean and the tree was just a sapling. And more neighbors kept arriving — the whole street, it seemed, drawn by the smell of ripe **mango** and the sound of laughing.

There was a contest, too, which Cajeta declared on the spot because she loved a good contest. The first event was Highest Mango Toss, which Tato won easily by smashing one so hard it flew over the fence and landed — *splat* — in the neighbor's birdbath. The birds did not mind one bit. The second event was Most Dramatic Mango Reaction, which Lala won without even trying, simply by tasting a slice, gasping, and announcing, "I have seen the *face of summer itself*," before fainting backward onto Flan, who caught her with a soft, polite *boing*.

"That's a fair point," Flan admitted, dabbing a little juice from his caramel. "It *is* a very good mango."

Cajeta bounced through all of it, peppy as a sugar-rush, handing mangoes to everyone she could reach. "Take two!" she chirped. "Take a *handful*! There's plenty — there's *so much* plenty!"

And the strangest, sweetest thing happened.

The more mangoes she gave away, the *more* there seemed to be. Not because the tree grew faster — but because every mango she handed over came back to her as something bigger: a laugh, a thank-you, a neighbor she'd never met sitting down beside her in the grass. The pile of fruit got smaller and smaller, but the fiesta got *bigger and bigger*.

"You know," Cajeta said, plopping down next to Pinto with a mango-cream-granizado swirl bigger than her head, "this morning I was so worried. All these mangoes, and only one tiny me. I thought too much wonderful was a *problem*."

Pinto smiled his calm little pale-stripe smile. "And now?"

Cajeta looked around — at Coco doing skateboard tricks off a mango pile, at Lala happily fainting onto the grass cushion, at Tato proudly guarding a pyramid, at Don Café snoozing under his hat, at the whole golden, giggling, milk-and-syrup-sticky street.

"Now," she said softly, "I think too much wonderful is just *enough wonderful for everybody*."

"Better together, mae," said Pinto, clinking his wooden spoon against her granizado. "Plenty kept to yourself only gets heavy. Plenty given away gets *light* — light enough to fill a whole street."

Cajeta thought about that, slowly, for once. She thought about how she'd nearly spent the whole day worrying over a pile of fruit, when all along the answer had been so simple it was practically silly: when you have more than you need, you don't have *too much*. You have *enough to be a friend with*.

"You know what, Pinto?" she said, bouncing just a little where she sat. "I think mangoes were *invented* for sharing. I don't think a single mango in the whole history of mangoes was ever meant to be eaten alone."

"Pura Vida," agreed Pinto, and the whole street, somehow hearing them, raised their sticky golden treats and called it back across the grass: "*Pura Vida!*"

---

As the sun went down, the backyard glowed amber and pink. Bellies were full. Cheeks were sticky. And the mango tree, for the first time all season, sighed and relaxed its branches, light and happy and finally, finally shared.

Cajeta looked up at it and grinned her fuzzy little grin.

"Same time next year?" she whispered.

And she could have sworn the tree rustled back: *Yes, please.*
