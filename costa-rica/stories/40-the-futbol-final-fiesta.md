# Story 40: The Futbol Final Fiesta

**Main Character:** Tato the Patacon
**Guest Characters:** Gallo, Pinto, Coco the Granizado
**Setting:** A packed neighborhood watching the big national futbol final
**Theme:** Win or lose, the joy is in cheering together.
**Spanish Spotlight:** gol (gohl) = goal

---

The whole barrio had gone bananas — which, if you thought about it, was a little awkward for Tato, on account of him being a smashed banana himself.

Tato the Patacon stood in the middle of Doña Marisol's courtyard, his crinkly golden edges quivering with nerves. He was a flat, round disc of twice-fried smashed green plantain, crispy on the rim and soft and chewy in the middle, with a friendly dusting of salt across his face. Tonight that face was sweating. Possibly fried plantains do not sweat. Tato was doing it anyway.

"It's the final," he whispered. "The biiig national futbol final. And I... I am very flat. What if I can't take the pressure?"

A tiny television flickered on a plastic table. Folding chairs. Forty snacks. One enormous nation's worth of hope.

"Smash now," Tato told himself, puffing out his crispy chest. "Worry never!"

It did not work. He worried completely.

The barrio had been buzzing since lunchtime. Streamers in red, white, and blue zigzagged across the courtyard. Someone had hung a flag the size of a bedsheet, which was awkward, because it WAS a bedsheet, with a flag drawn on in marker. Tiny snacks crowded onto every chair, every windowsill, every upturned bucket, all squinting at the little television.

And on that screen: the national team, in their bright shirts, jogging onto the field for the most important match of the whole entire year.

"Okay, okay, okay," came a sunny, slightly-toasty voice. "Everybody breathe!"

It was **Gallo** the grain of fried rice, a plump glossy white grain freckled with golden toasty bits and little flecks of red pepper and cilantro, wearing his small woven palm hat at a confident angle. He hopped up onto the table beside the television.

"Rise and pinto!" he chirped, then hummed a little happy hum, *mm-hm*. "Friends, tonight we cheer with our WHOLE hearts. Win or lose — together!"

"Together," agreed **Pinto**, the small deep-glossy black bean at Gallo's side, smooth and round with a pale stripe across him like a calm little smile. He carried his tiny wooden spoon and tapped it gently for attention. "Better together, *mae*," he said softly. *Mae* (mah-eh) means *buddy* or *pal* — the word friends use for one another all across the country.

Tato wobbled over to them. "But what if we LOSE?" he squeaked. "What if the other team scores a — a —" He couldn't even say the word. It was too big and too scary.

"A **gol** (gohl)?" said Pinto kindly. A *gol* is a *goal* — the wonderful moment a ball whooshes into the net and the whole world shouts at once.

"THAT," Tato whimpered. "What if THEY get one and WE don't and everyone is sad and the fiesta is ruined and I let down the entire barrio because I am only a flat fried plantain who—"

"Tato." Pinto patted him with the little spoon. "Breathe."

Tato breathed. He felt slightly less flat. Only slightly.

Just then a cone of glorious noise came sliding through the gate.

"¡PURA VIDA, MI GENTE!" hollered **Coco** the granizado — a tall fluffy mound of rainbow-syruped shaved ice piled in a paper cone, dusted with powdered milk like fresh snow, sporting tiny round sunglasses even though the sun had gone down hours ago. Coco was, as always, slightly half-melting and refreezing, which made his colors sparkle and drip in the most magnificent way. "Who is READY for the biggest night of the YEAR? This is gonna be TUANIS!"

"Tato is having a small meltdown," reported Gallo.

"Hey, no no no, leave the melting to the professionals," said Coco, gesturing at himself with a frosty flourish. "Listen, panita. You know what makes a great fan?"

"A... calm one?" guessed Tato.

"NO! A LOUD one!" Coco threw his slushy arms wide and a few rainbow sprinkles of ice went flying. "Stay frosty, stay sweet, and SCREAM your little heart out! C'mon, gimme a cheer!"

"...goooo team?" said Tato, in approximately the quietest voice ever produced.

The entire courtyard went silent. A cricket coughed.

"We'll workshop it," said Coco.

---

The match began.

Oh, it was a nail-biter — and not just because Flan the coconut flan was so nervous he kept nibbling the rim of a nearby paper plate. The two teams ran up and down the green field, the little white ball zipping back and forth like a moth that had had far too much café.

Every time the national team got close to the net, the whole barrio sucked in one enormous breath — *hwoooop* — and held it. And every time the ball missed, forty snacks let it out again in one giant disappointed *awwwww* that nearly blew Gallo's palm hat clean off.

"This," wheezed Tato, gripping the edge of the table, "is the most stressful thing that has ever happened to a plantain."

"Half-time!" announced Tico the toucan from the doorway, his huge rainbow beak pointed at the score. "Beak the news — it's ZERO to ZERO! Nobody's scored! The suspense is DELICIOUS!"

"It's the opposite of delicious," moaned Tato. "It's the worst flavor ever invented. It tastes like a tummy full of butterflies who are ALSO nervous."

Coco slurped half of himself in distress. "I have melted three sizes since kickoff," he announced. "If we go to penalties, I will simply become a puddle. A very cool, rainbow puddle, but a puddle."

"Nobody is becoming a puddle," said Pinto firmly, though he was clutching his little spoon so hard it had nearly snapped.

Pinto rolled over beside him, calm as a still pond. "Tato, mae. Let me ask you something. Why did you come tonight?"

Tato blinked his salty face. "To... watch the game?"

"You could watch it alone, at home."

"Well — no, I came because everybody's HERE. Doña Marisol and the little tamale twins and Coco and you and Gallo and—" Tato stopped. "Oh."

"Mm-hm!" hummed Gallo, who'd been listening. "You came for the cheering-TOGETHER part. Not the winning part."

"Winning is nice," said Pinto. "But it is not the reason we are all squished onto one tiny porch eating snacks off a bedsheet flag. The reason is each other."

Tato looked around the courtyard — at all the bright nervous faces, all leaning toward the same little glowing screen, all hoping the same hope at the same time. And something warm bubbled up in his chewy middle, right under the crispy.

"Huh," he said. "That's actually really nice. I— wait. WAIT. THE GAME IS BACK ON."

---

The second half was even worse. Or better. Tato genuinely could not tell anymore.

Back and forth, back and forth went the ball. The minutes ticked down. Eighty. Eighty-five. Eighty-eight. Coco had stopped narrating and was just making a low continuous frosty whine. Flan had eaten through two entire paper plates. Gallo was clutching Pinto, and Pinto was clutching his little spoon, and Tato had gripped the table edge so hard he'd left a perfect Tato-shaped salt print on it.

Ninety minutes. Almost over. Still zero to zero.

And then —

The national team's striker got the ball near the corner. She dodged one defender. Spun past another. Looked up. And from way, way out, with the very last breath of the match, she kicked.

The ball sailed.

The whole barrio rose as one.

Time slowed down like Slowy the sloth had reached out and gently pressed pause on the universe.

The ball curled through the air, past the goalie's fingertips, and —

*SWISH.*

Into the net.

"**¡GOOOOOOOOOL!**"

The courtyard EXPLODED.

It was the loudest sound Tato had ever heard in his crispy little life. Snacks leapt off their chairs. The bedsheet flag came down in a happy avalanche. Coco launched a fountain of rainbow shaved ice straight into the air like edible fireworks. Gallo and Pinto spun in a dizzy hug, Gallo humming so hard his hat fell off. Flan wobbled so violently he nearly turned inside out. Tico did three loop-the-loops and announced "GOL! GOL! G-O-L! WE DID IT! BEAK THE NEWS, WE WON THE FINAL!"

And Tato — flat, nervous, salt-dusted little Tato —

Tato JUMPED.

He jumped higher than a smashed plantain has any business jumping. And as he came down, the most marvelous idea landed with him.

"FIESTA!" he bellowed. "EVERYBODY — IT'S TIME FOR A CHIFRIJO-AND-PATACON CELEBRATION!"

Now, **chifrijo** (chee-FREE-hoh) = a beloved party snack of rice, beans, crispy pork, and fresh salsa, all piled up and scooped onto crunchy patacones — and Tato, being a patacon, knew EXACTLY what to do. He was the scoop! He was the crunch! This was, quite literally, his moment to shine.

"Gallo! Pinto! I need rice and beans, NOW!" he commanded, suddenly the bravest snack in the whole barrio.

"YES CHEF!" laughed Gallo, scooping up a happy heap of himself and his bean partner.

"Better together, mae!" beamed Pinto.

"Coco — you're on shaved-ice refreshments for the whole street!"

"On it, capitán! Stay frosty!" Coco saluted with a frosty drip.

And just like that, the courtyard became the best fiesta the barrio had ever seen. Tato stood proud and crispy at the center of the table while friends piled rice, beans, and fresh salsa onto crunchy patacones, laughing and cheering and singing the team's song completely off-key. Music spilled out the windows. The whole street joined in. Even Doña Marisol danced, and so did the two little tamale twins, leaving tiny steamy footprints across the porch.

Flan the coconut flan wobbled past with a plate stacked impossibly high. "I was SO nervous I ate three plates," he confessed, "but now I am so HAPPY I could eat three more! Steady, steady... ooh, wobbly!" And he wibbled right into a hug with Coco, who caught him with a frosty *splat* and declared it the best hug of the entire night.

Tico swooped in low over the table, scattering streamers with his big rainbow beak. "FRONT-PAGE NEWS!" he crowed. "Local plantain throws greatest party in barrio history! Witnesses describe it as — and I quote — 'extremely tuanis!'"

"That was ME, I said that," called Coco, beaming.

Tato beamed his salty beam. He wasn't nervous anymore. He realized he hadn't been afraid of losing, not really. He'd been afraid of letting everyone down. But you couldn't let anyone down at a party like this. There was nothing to do but be together, and be loud, and be happy.

"You know," he said to Pinto, as confetti made of torn streamers drifted down, "I think I finally get it. The gol was great. Wonderful. Top three plantain moments of my life."

"Top three?" said Pinto.

"But THIS—" Tato spread his crispy arms at the whole singing, dancing, scooping, hugging barrio. "*This* is the part I came for. We would've had the fiesta either way, huh?"

Pinto smiled his calm pale-striped smile. "Win or lose, mae. The joy was always going to be the cheering together."

Tato nodded. Then he grinned. "Now somebody scoop me up some chifrijo before I eat my own crispy edges out of happiness."

And the whole barrio laughed, and the night went sweet and golden and warm, and somewhere far away the team lifted their trophy — but right here, on one little porch under a marker-drawn flag, the real victory was forty friends together, full of snacks and song and joy.

Pura vida, indeed.

## Goodnight Blessing
May your heart be a courtyard full of friends, and your hopes be cheered all together. May your wins feel sweet and your losses feel small, because the ones beside you make everything golden. Snuggle down now, little one, soft and warm as a chewy patacon middle. The game is over, the fiesta is done, and every friend is fast asleep and smiling. Rest easy, and dream of confetti drifting down like quiet stars. Goodnight.
