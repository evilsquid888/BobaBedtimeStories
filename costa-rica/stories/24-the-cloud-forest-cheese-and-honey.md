# Story 24: The Cloud Forest Cheese and Honey

**Main Character:** Lala the Tres Leches
**Guest Characters:** Don Café
**Setting:** A Monteverde dairy-and-honey farm in the cool mist
**Theme:** Sweet and savory friends balance each other beautifully.
**Spanish Spotlight:** miel (mee-EL) = honey

---

High up where the clouds come down to take a nap, there is a farm so misty that the cows wear their fog like cozy gray sweaters. This is Monteverde, the green mountain, and on the morning our story begins, a small square of cake was bouncing up the muddy path leaving tiny milky footprints behind her.

"Ooh! Ohhh!" gasped Lala the Tres Leches, her wobbly cloud of whipped cream trembling with delight. She was soaked golden and dripping sweet, with a single cinnamon dot on top like a little brown freckle. "It's so *misty*! Soak it all in!"

Behind her shuffled a wise old coffee bean in a straw hat, tapping his tiny walking stick. "Slow down, mija," chuckled Don Café, smelling of warm mornings. "Good things brew slowly. And this farm — ho ho — this farm is brewing a very silly argument indeed."

Lala blinked her sugary eyes. "An argument? On a farm this *dreamy*?"

"Listen," said Don Café. "You'll hear it soon."

So up they climbed, deeper into the cloud forest, where everything dripped and sparkled and smelled like rain and green leaves. Ferns the size of umbrellas waved hello. A little hummingbird zipped past so fast it left a tiny whistling streak in the fog. And the higher they went, the cooler and creamier the mist became, until Lala felt like she was floating inside a giant bowl of whipped cream.

"Ooooh, Don Café, *look* at it all," she swooned, twirling so hard that a dollop of her own cream nearly flew off. "Whoops! Caught it!" She patted it back into place. "Sorry. I get carried away when things are beautiful. It's a problem. Everyone says so."

"It is the very best kind of problem," chuckled Don Café, planting his little walking stick in the soft mud. "Pura vida, mija. Now — careful on this slope. It's slippery."

"I'm a cake," Lala giggled. "Slippery is my whole personality." And down she slid — *whoooosh* — straight into a soft pillow of moss, leaving a perfect milky cake-print behind. A passing beetle stopped to admire it.

---

When at last they reached the little wooden barn, two voices were squabbling so loudly that the cows had stopped chewing to watch.

"I am the TASTIEST!" boomed a big round wheel of fresh white cheese sitting on a shelf. He was pale and bouncy and squeaky, with little holes like surprised mouths, and his name was **Queso** (KEH-so) = cheese. "I am salty! I am savory! I squeak when you bite me — *squeak squeak* — and everybody loves a squeak!"

"Ha! As IF!" buzzed a golden voice. From a glass jar dripped a glistening ribbon of pure gold — la **miel** (mee-EL) = honey, gathered by the cloud-forest bees. She glittered and oozed and posed dramatically on her spoon. "I am SWEET! I am sunshine you can *eat*! The flowers themselves give me their best perfume. Nobody chooses squeaky and salty over sunshine, darling."

"SALTY IS THE FLAVOR OF CHAMPIONS!"

"SWEET IS THE FLAVOR OF DREAMS!"

"SQUEAK!"

"SPARKLE!"

The two of them glared. The cows looked nervous. One little calf hid behind her mother's leg.

Lala the Tres Leches pressed her squishy hands to her cheeks. "Oh my goodness," she whispered. "They're both so *lovely*, and they're being so *grumpy* about it. My heart can't take it. It's wobbling."

"Mm," hummed Don Café, sipping the misty air. "They have argued like this for three whole days. Nobody on the farm has had a snack the entire time. The bread is bored. The crackers are lonely. Something must be done."

---

Lala squelched bravely forward, leaving a trail of milky little footprints. "Hello!" she sang. "I'm Lala! And I think you're *both* the tastiest!"

Queso and Miel turned to her at exactly the same time.

"WRONG," they said together. Then they glared at *each other* for agreeing.

"Tell us, soggy cake," said Queso, bouncing importantly. "Salty or sweet? Choose! Whose side are you on?"

"Yes, choose," dripped Miel, gleaming. "Cast your vote, you adorable little puddle."

Lala gulped. Her whipped-cream cloud wobbled left, then right. "Oh dear," she breathed. "But I — I like the salty squeak AND the sweet sparkle. Choosing is like asking the moon to pick between two stars. I simply *can't*."

"Then you're no help at all," huffed Queso, and he turned his squeaky back.

"None whatsoever," sniffed Miel, and she oozed the other way.

Poor Lala's cinnamon dot drooped. A little milky tear plopped onto the barn floor — *plip* — and a thirsty chicken hurried over to drink it.

"Don't mind me, just a snack," said the chicken, and waddled off.

"I only wanted everyone to be *happy*," Lala sniffled, watching the cheese and the honey sulk on opposite ends of the shelf. Queso was muttering "salty, salty, salty" to himself. Miel was sighing "sweet, sweet, sweet" to the ceiling. The whole barn felt as gray and droopy as the fog outside.

Don Café shuffled over and patted her with his walking stick. "There, there, mija. Don't soak the floor too much — you'll flood the chickens." He tilted his straw hat thoughtfully. "You know, when I was a young bean on the highland slopes, I learned something. The strongest coffee isn't *only* bitter. A little sweetness, a little cream — and suddenly, *ahhh*. Two flavors holding hands. **Pura vida.**" He smiled. "Perhaps the answer isn't choosing. Perhaps the answer is *combining*."

Lala's eyes went wide as saucers. Her whipped cream stood straight up. "Don Café," she gasped, "you brilliant little bean. SOAK IT ALL IN! I have an idea!"

---

Now, here is the thing about Lala the Tres Leches. She may be soft and squishy and a teeny bit dramatic — but when she gets an idea, she gets *busy*. She squelched into action so fast that she sloshed sweet milk in three directions at once, and Don Café had to hop sideways to keep his straw hat dry.

"Pardon my dripping!" she sang. "Big plans always come with big puddles!"

"Señor Queso!" she called sweetly. "Could you do me the smallest favor and lie down flat? Just for one tiny moment?"

Queso frowned suspiciously. "Why?"

"Because," said Lala, batting her sugary lashes, "I have heard — and I could be wrong — that you are too cowardly to try anything new."

"*COWARDLY?*" Queso bounced so hard he nearly rolled off the shelf. "I am the BRAVEST cheese in Monteverde! Watch me lie flat! I lie flatter than ANYONE!" And he flopped down with a proud little *squeak*.

"Marvelous!" beamed Lala. She turned. "And Señorita Miel, my golden darling — I heard *you* are far too proud and stiff to ever flow somewhere new."

"STIFF? *PROUD?*" Miel sparkled furiously. "I am the FLOWINGEST honey ever gathered! I flow where I *please*! Watch me flow, you doubting little dessert!" And she poured herself in a long glistening ribbon — *driiiizzle* — right across the flat white cheese.

The whole barn went quiet.

The golden miel slid slowly over the cool white queso. It pooled in the little squeaky holes. It glittered like a sunset melting onto fresh snow. The smell that rose up — salty-creamy and sweet-flowery all at once — made every cow lean in, made the calf peek out, made Don Café close his eyes and sigh, "*Ahhhh.*"

"Now," whispered Lala, trembling with excitement, "everyone — take one teeny bite. Together. On three. Uno... dos... *tres*!"

---

The cows took a bite. The chickens took a bite. The bored bread and the lonely crackers crowded in for a bite. Don Café took the tiniest, wisest nibble.

And Queso and Miel, very carefully, each took a bite of *themselves together*.

There was a long, long pause.

Then Queso's surprised-mouth holes opened even wider. "It's... it's *salty*," he murmured, "AND it's sweet. The squeak is dancing with the sparkle. I taste... I taste sunshine and snowflakes at the *same time*." He gave a wobbly little squeak that sounded suspiciously like a happy hiccup.

"Más, más!" mooed a cow, which means "more, more," and stuck out her tongue for another lick. "Best snack in the whole cloud forest!"

"Oh," whispered Miel, and a single drop of her wobbled like a happy tear. "Oh, I'm not *better* than you, am I? I'm... I'm better *with* you."

"Better together," hummed a soft voice from the doorway — and would you believe it, that was just the mountain wind, but it sounded so much like an old friend that Lala giggled milk bubbles.

Queso and Miel looked at each other. The big proud cheese and the glittering golden honey. And then — *squeak* meeting *sparkle* — they hugged. A salty-sweet, sticky, squeaky, delicious hug, right there on the misty shelf.

"I'm sorry I called you stiff," squeaked Queso.

"I'm sorry I called you a puddle," dripped Miel to Lala. "You're a very *wise* puddle."

Lala the Tres Leches glowed so warmly that her whipped cream practically fluffed. "You see?" she swooned, spinning in a happy little squelch. "Salty needs sweet. Sweet needs salty. The moon doesn't have to pick between two stars — it just lets them *both* shine. Soak it ALL in!"

---

That evening, the whole cloud-forest farm had a feast — the biggest, happiest, most delicious feast the green mountain had ever seen. The cows lined up politely for warm white queso drizzled in golden miel, mooing little thank-yous. The chickens did a clucky little dance that mostly looked like falling over on purpose. The bored bread and the lonely crackers finally got their snack and wept happy crumbs everywhere. Even the hummingbird zoomed back for a sip of miel, said "*delicioso!*" in a tiny squeak, and zoomed off again before anyone could blink.

"To Lala!" boomed Queso, raising himself in a little squeaky bow. "The wisest puddle in all of Monteverde!"

"To friends who are *different*!" sparkled Miel, drizzling a happy heart-shape onto the shelf.

Lala blushed pink as her cinnamon dot. "Oh, stop, you'll make my cream fizz," she swooned, though secretly she loved every bit of it.

As the mist rolled thick and cool and the lanterns glowed amber in the barn, Lala curled up between Don Café and a very contented Queso, with Miel sparkling softly nearby like a tiny lamp. The cows breathed slow and warm. Somewhere far below the mountain, a sleepy frog sang one last song.

"Don Café," Lala yawned, milk-sweet and sleepy, "I think savory and sweet are the best kind of friends. The kind that aren't the *same* — but fit together *perfectly*."

The old coffee bean tipped his straw hat low and smiled in the gathering fog. "Good things brew slowly, mija," he said softly. "And the sweetest things... brew best together. Pura vida."

Outside, the clouds tucked the green mountain in for the night. And somewhere on a quiet shelf, a wheel of cheese and a jar of honey leaned gently against each other, salty and sweet, dreaming the very same dream.
