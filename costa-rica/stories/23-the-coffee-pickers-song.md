# Story 23: The Coffee Pickers' Song

**Main Character:** Don Café
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** Red coffee-cherry rows on a highland farm at harvest time
**Theme:** Hard work feels like play when you sing it together.
**Spanish Spotlight:** trabajo (trah-BAH-hoh) = work

---

High up where the mountains wear their hats of morning fog, there grew a farm so green and so steep that the rows of coffee bushes looked like ribbons draped over a giant's knees. And on every bush, bright red coffee cherries clung to their branches like a thousand tiny lanterns — round, glossy, and, on this particular morning, absolutely shaking with nerves.

"Don't make me go!" wailed a little cherry named Rubí, gripping her branch so tightly her stem squeaked. "If they pick me, I'll have to become a bean. And if I become a bean, I'll have to become — oh, I can't even say it — a *cup of coffee!*"

A whole row of cherries gasped. Stems trembled. One fainted into a leaf.

And that was when a small dark figure with a tiny straw hat and a miniature wooden walking stick came strolling up the hill, humming a tune older than the mountain itself.

"Good morning, my little red lanterns," said Don Café. "Why all the long faces on such a bright day?"

---

Don Café was a roasted coffee bean — deep brown and glossy, with a crease right down his middle like a wise little smile. He smelled of warm mornings and far-off kitchens. He had been a cherry once, long ago, which meant he knew exactly what these trembling little fruits were feeling.

"We're scared, Don Café," squeaked Rubí. "Today is harvest. The pickers are coming with their baskets, and they'll *take us away.*"

"Away from our branch," sniffled a cherry named Pepe.
"Away from our friends," whimpered another.
"And then we have to do *trabajo,*" moaned Rubí. **"Trabajo** (trah-BAH-hoh) = work. And work sounds *terrible.*"

Don Café leaned on his little walking stick and chuckled, a low warm sound like coffee bubbling in a pot. "Ahh. Trabajo. Now there is a word people get all wrong." He tipped his straw hat back. "Good things brew slowly, little ones. So let an old bean tell you a secret about work. Will you listen?"

The cherries, who would do almost anything to delay being picked, nodded so hard the whole branch bounced.

"When I was young and red, just like you," said Don Café, "I thought work was a heavy thing. A frown you carry. A weight on the back." He smiled. "But then I learned the secret the pickers know. The thing that turns trabajo from a chore into a *party.*"

"What is it?" breathed Rubí.

Don Café's eyes twinkled. "A song."

---

"A *song?*" said Pepe doubtfully. "Singing doesn't fill a basket."

"Doesn't it?" said Don Café. "Watch this." He cleared his little throat, planted his walking stick, and began to sing in a voice that wobbled but glowed:

*"Pick a cherry, plink-plink-plink,
red as a sunset, sweet as you think —
into the basket, off we go,
rise and shine and ¡pura vida-O!"*

It was, frankly, not the world's most magnificent song. Don Café could not hold a note any better than he could hold a thundercloud. In fact, when he reached the high part, his voice cracked so badly that a sleepy beetle two bushes over rolled right off its leaf in surprise. But something funny happened. Pepe's leaf started to bounce. Rubí's stem began to sway. And before she even noticed, Rubí was humming along — *plink-plink-plink* — and *grinning.*

"Wait," she said, stopping herself. "That's a trick! You can't sing the scary out of harvest!"

"Can't I?" said Don Café, and he winked at — well, at *you*, dear reader, because he could tell you were listening too. "Then let us find out properly. Let us bring the whole hillside into it."

---

So Don Café began his great trabajo. Not the picking — the *gathering.* He marched (slowly, because he was old and small and very dignified) all the way down the first row, tapping each branch with his walking stick and teaching the song.

To the worried cherries he sang the bouncy *plink-plink* line.
To the giggly cherries he taught a part where you go *"¡PURA VIDA-O!"* as loud as you possibly can — and *pura vida*, he explained, means the good sweet life, the easy happy kind.
To the shyest cherries, who could barely whisper, he gave the gentlest job of all: the soft little *"ohh-ohh"* that floats underneath everything like fog over the mountain.

"Everyone gets a part," said Don Café. "That is the second secret of trabajo. Big work feels small when everybody carries a piece."

There was, of course, one tiny disaster. A round cherry named Gordo got so excited about the *"¡PURA VIDA-O!"* part that he flung both little arms wide and very nearly bounced clean off his stem before lunch even started.

"Steady, steady!" laughed Don Café, catching him with the crook of his walking stick. "Save your big jump for the basket, my friend. A song is a *journey*, not a leap. We arrive together, or we do not arrive at all."

"Sorry, Don Café," said Gordo, settling back with a sheepish wobble. "I just got *carried away.*"

"That," said Don Café warmly, "is the whole idea." And he gave Gordo a part too — the very last *plink* at the end of each verse, the one that lands like a period at the end of a sentence. Gordo took the job so seriously that he practiced his single *plink* over and over, which made everyone giggle, which made the giggling spread, which made the song grow even bigger.

By the time the sun had climbed a hand's width up the sky, the entire first row was singing. Then the second row caught it. Then the third. The song rolled across the hillside the way a wave rolls across the sea — *plink-plink-plink, pura vida-O,* with the shy little *ohh-ohh* humming underneath and Gordo's proud lonesome *plink!* punctuating the end — until the whole green mountain was singing, and even the fog seemed to sway.

A pair of pickers with baskets on their backs came up the path, blinking. They had expected a quiet, tired morning of trabajo. Instead they had walked into a *concert.*

"¿Qué es esto?" laughed one. "What is this?"

The other shrugged, smiled, and — because songs are the most catching thing there is, more catching than a yawn, more catching than a giggle — began to hum along.

---

And here is the truly marvelous part, the part Don Café had known all along.

When the pickers reached out their gentle fingers, the cherries were no longer scared. How could you be scared when you were in the middle of the best song of your whole short red life? Rubí was so busy belting her *"¡PURA VIDA-O!"* that she barely noticed the soft warm hand close around her — *plink!* — and tuck her into the basket with all her singing friends.

"Wait — did I just get picked?" she said, peeking over the basket's edge.

"You did, dear," said a cherry beside her, who was Pepe, who was *also* grinning.

"And I wasn't even scared!"

"None of us were," said Pepe. "We were too busy with the *ohh-ohh* part."

The basket, it turned out, was not a scary place at all. It was warm and round and full of friends, and the song kept right on going inside it, bouncing off the woven walls until it sounded twice as big. Down the rows they traveled, gathering more singers with every step, until the basket was a brimming red bowl of music.

"This is the comfiest place I have ever been," announced Gordo, who had landed (with one enormous, finally-permitted leap) right in the middle of the heap. "It's like a hug made of all my friends!"

"It *is* a hug made of all your friends," said Rubí, and the whole basket laughed, which made the woven walls hum, which made the song swell up again, brighter than before. A passing hummingbird stopped to listen, hovered upside down for a moment in pure confusion, and zipped off to tell the flowers that the coffee bushes had, apparently, formed a choir.

Don Café rode along on the rim like a tiny conductor, tapping time with his walking stick, his straw hat bobbing. He had picked thousands of mornings in his long life, but he never grew tired of this one moment — the moment a whole hillside forgot to be afraid. "You see?" he called over the chorus. "Trabajo with a song is not work at all. It is *play that gets something done.*"

"It went so fast!" said Rubí, amazed. "We sang the whole hillside empty before lunch!"

"That is the last secret," said Don Café, and his voice went soft and proud, like a grandfather at the end of a long good day. "When the heart is light, the hands fly. Frown at your trabajo and a morning lasts a year. Sing at your trabajo and a mountain melts before noon."

---

That evening, as the basket cherries were laid out to begin their slow, cozy journey — drying in the sun, resting, becoming, in time, beans like Don Café himself — Rubí asked the question she had been holding all day.

"Don Café... what happens *next?* After we're beans?"

Don Café settled beside her, took off his little straw hat, and held it over his heart.

"Next," he said warmly, "you become the most wonderful thing. Somewhere, far down the mountain, a person will wake up tired and gray. The morning will feel too heavy. And then they will lift a steaming cup — *your* cup — and breathe in, and the warmth will reach all the way to their toes." He smiled. "And do you know what they will feel?"

"What?" whispered Rubí.

"They will feel the way you felt today. Light. Ready. Like the whole day just might be a song after all." His eyes shone. "You will not just *be* the morning, little one. You will *make* it."

Rubí thought about that — about being someone's bright beginning, their first warm sip of courage. And a feeling bloomed in her that was the exact opposite of the fear she'd felt at dawn.

It was pride. The proud, glowing kind. Like a firework that has waited all year and finally, *finally* gets to light up the sky. Like a lantern that has been saving its light for exactly the right dark morning.

"Then I can't wait," she said. "I want to be the best morning anyone ever had."

"You will be, mae," said Don Café gently. "You will be."

And as the highland stars came out one by one over the sleeping rows, the whole hillside hummed its soft little *ohh-ohh* into the dark — a lullaby now instead of a work song, but somehow exactly the same. Because the best songs, like the best trabajo, are the ones you do together.

## Goodnight Blessing
May your tomorrow be a song you sing with people you love. May your work feel like play, and your play feel like a hug. When something seems big and a little bit scary, remember the cherries on the mountain, and hum a tiny *ohh-ohh* until your heart feels light again. You are someone's bright morning, sweet one — warm and brave and good all the way to your toes. Now snuggle down, close your sleepy eyes, and let the soft mountain fog tuck you in. Goodnight.
