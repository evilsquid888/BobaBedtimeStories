# Story 6: The Casado That Couldn't Agree

**Main Character:** Gallo and Pinto
**Guest Characters:** Tato the Patacon
**Setting:** A big lunch plate at the soda — the casado ('married' plate)
**Theme:** Different friends taste better together than alone.
**Spanish Spotlight:** **casado** (kah-SAH-doh) — the 'married' plate of rice, beans, plantain, salad and more

---

In the cozy corner of a little **soda** (SOH-dah) — a small family lunch restaurant — called Doña Ana's, a fresh white plate clinked onto the counter, and everyone on it landed with a soft *fwump*. Sunlight slid through the window, the ceiling fan went *whirr-whirr-whirr*, and somewhere a radio played a song about the sea.

"Rise and pinto!" hummed Gallo, a plump glossy grain of fried rice in a tiny woven palm hat, brushing a fleck of cilantro off his shoulder. "What a beautiful spot we landed in. Hmm-hmm!"

Beside him rolled Pinto, a small shiny black bean with a pale stripe like a smile, carrying his little wooden spoon. "Better together, mae," he said warmly, looking around.

But the rest of the plate was NOT feeling warm. Not even a little. In fact, the casado that morning was having the silliest argument in the entire history of lunch.

---

It had started with the lettuce.

"Ahem," announced a curly green leaf of Salad, standing very tall and crunchy at the edge of the plate. "I think we can all agree the BEST part of any casado is *me*. I am fresh. I am crisp. I am, frankly, the only thing here that's good for you." She crunched proudly. "The rest of you are just... heavy."

A round golden plantain disc flipped himself upright with a determined little *bounce*. This was Tato the Patacon, twice-fried, crispy at the crinkled edges, soft and chewy in the middle, dusted with salt. He was sturdy, brave, and just a teeny bit flat-footed.

"Smash now, worry never!" Tato declared. "But also — excuse me, Señorita Salad. The best part is obviously ME. I'm crunchy AND soft. I'm sweet AND salty. Children eat me FIRST. That makes me number one." He thumped his chest and nearly tipped over. "Whoop— okay, that makes me number one *and* a little dizzy."

"First does not mean best," sniffed Salad.

"It kind of does," said Tato.

"It absolutely does not!"

A tomato slice rolled over to watch, like it was tennis. Then a slice of avocado flopped over too, slow and green and buttery, just to have something to lean on.

"Don't look at me," said the avocado smoothly. "I'm only here to be smooth. Smoothest thing on the plate. That practically makes me the best by default."

"You're not even *trying* to argue," huffed Salad. "You just float around being soft and green."

"That's my whole thing," said the avocado, completely relaxed. "Soft. Green. Best."

Gallo blinked. "Oh dear. Hmm-hmm. They seem a touch... cranky."

"Hungry-cranky," whispered Pinto wisely. "It's the worst kind. Everyone gets it before lunch."

---

Just then a soft, dreamy voice drifted up from the far side of the plate. It was the fried egg, wobbling gently on his round white throne of a sunny middle.

"Friends," sighed the Egg, "must we argue? The sun is shining. Soak it all in." (The Egg was a bit of a dreamer. He'd clearly been spending time with Lala the Tres Leches.)

"YES we must argue!" boomed Salad and Tato together — then glared at each other for agreeing.

The Egg wobbled nervously. "Steady, steady... ooh, wobbly."

Pinto leaned toward Gallo. "We should help. A grumpy plate is a sad plate."

"Hmm-hmm, agreed," said Gallo. He cleared his tiny throat and rolled into the middle of the plate, his golden fried bits sparkling. "Friends! Amigos! Could I say one little thing?"

Everybody paused. Even the tomato slice.

"I just want to understand," said Gallo sweetly. "Why does there have to be a *best* part at all?"

The whole plate gasped, as if he'd asked why water was wet.

"Because," said Salad slowly, like she was explaining to a baby pea, "there's always a best. In everything. The best song. The best beach. The best part of the plate. And it's me."

"It's me," said Tato.

"Me," squeaked a small voice — and from under a lettuce leaf popped a little cube of fried cheese, glistening and squeaky. "I'm cheese. People LOVE cheese. I should at least be in the top one."

"There's only ONE top one!" everyone shouted.

"Exactly!" shouted the cheese, who had not thought this through.

The whole plate erupted. Salad crunched. Tato thumped. The cheese squeaked so hard a little squeak-note went *eep!* into the air. The tomato slice tried to say something fair and got shushed by everybody. Even the avocado raised one buttery eyebrow and murmured, "This is getting silly," which, coming from the avocado, was practically yelling.

Doña Ana, somewhere above the plate, wiped a glass and chuckled, "My, what a *lively* lunch today." She had no idea.

---

Gallo looked at Pinto. Pinto looked at Gallo. And in that quiet way that best friends have, they both knew exactly what the other was thinking — which was: *oh boy.*

"Let's try something," said Pinto, calm and grounding as ever. He tapped his little wooden spoon. "Everybody stand alone for a moment. All by yourself. Spread out. Go on."

Confused, they shuffled apart. Salad to one side. Tato to another. The Egg wobbled to a lonely corner. The cheese cube sat by itself looking suddenly very small. Gallo and Pinto stayed in the middle.

"Now," said Pinto. "Imagine a hungry kid sits down. And the plate is ONLY salad."

Everyone imagined it. Tato made a face. "That's... a lot of leaves."

"Only plantain," said Pinto.

"Mmm," said Tato dreamily. Then he frowned. "Wait. Just plantain and nothing else? By the third bite I'd be... kind of much. Even *I* know that, and I love me."

"Only egg," said the Egg, and even *he* wobbled. "Oh. That IS rather a lot of egg."

"Only cheese," squeaked the cheese, turning pale. "I'd squeak too much. I'd be too squeaky."

"Only avocado," said the avocado, and for the first time all morning he sat up a little. "Just... a whole plate of soft green me. No crunch to wake me up. No warm bite to keep me company. That sounds, honestly, a bit lonely." He sank back down. "And I'm too smooth to be lonely. It doesn't suit me."

"Only tomato," sighed the tomato slice, who finally got to speak. "Just me, rolling around all by myself. Nobody to be juicy *next to*." He looked a little misty. "I don't want to be juicy alone."

Salad opened her mouth to argue, then slowly closed it. "Only salad," she admitted, "would make somebody very sad and still very hungry. And also they would still be looking for me to come with something nice. Like... rice." She glanced at Gallo. "And maybe a friend."

A soft, warm quiet settled over the plate.

---

Gallo rolled forward, his golden flecks catching the sun. "You see, friends," he hummed, "do you know what kind of plate we are?"

"A lunch plate?" guessed Tato.

"We're a **casado** (kah-SAH-doh) — the married plate," said Gallo. "Rice, beans, plantain, salad, egg, cheese — all of us, together. Casado means *married*. Do you know why they call it that?"

The plate shook their heads — well, the parts that had heads.

Pinto smiled his pale-stripe smile. "Because it's a marriage of flavors. Everybody brings their own taste. And alone, sure, we're each pretty good. But together?" He scooped a tiny bit of himself onto Gallo. "Together we're *Pura Vida.*"

"Together," said Gallo, "you taste the cool crunch of Salad *right after* the warm crispy chew of Tato. Hmm-hmm! You taste the soft egg *with* a bite of rice and bean. Every part makes the next part more delicious. We don't take turns being best. We make each *other* best."

The Egg's lip wobbled, but this time in a happy way. "Soak it all in," he whispered.

Tato's crunchy edges turned a little pink. "So... I'm not number one?"

"You're number one," said Pinto kindly, "at being Tato. Nobody crunches like you. Nobody is sweet AND salty like you. We *need* the Tato part. But we need the Salad part too. And the cheese squeak. And the rice. And the bean."

"Better together, mae," added Pinto softly.

---

Well. That did it.

Tato bounced clear across the plate — "Whoop!" — and landed right beside Salad. "Señorita Salad," he said, "I am very sorry I said you were just leaves. You are extremely good leaves. You are the freshest, crunchiest leaves on the whole plate."

Salad blushed a deep leafy green. "And I'm sorry I called you heavy. You're not heavy. You're... cozy."

The cheese cube squeaked happily and rolled into the middle. The Egg wibbled over and tucked himself against the rice. The tomato slice, who had been watching this whole time, finally said, "Can I be in the marriage too?"

"Everybody's in the marriage!" laughed Gallo. "That's the whole point! Hmm-hmm!"

The avocado slid over and draped himself gently across one corner, soft and cool. "I'll be the smooth part," he said. "Every good thing needs a smooth part." And nobody argued, because it was true.

"You know what I just realized?" said Tato, his crunchy edges glowing. "When I sit next to Salad, her cool fresh crunch makes my warm crunch taste even crunchier. Two crunches! Different crunches! A whole crunch family!"

"And when the tomato sits by me," squeaked the cheese, "his juicy makes my squeaky less squeaky and more... yummy."

"That's the magic," said Pinto warmly. "Alone, a flavor is just one note. Together, you make a *song*."

The little radio behind the counter, as if it had been listening, swelled into a gentle tune about the sea, and the whole plate swayed.

And just like that, the casado snuggled itself into the prettiest, coziest plate Doña Ana's had ever served. Rice beside bean. Plantain beside salad. Egg beside cheese. All of them touching just a little, the way friends lean on each other at the end of a long, sunny day.

"You know," said Tato, sniffing the warm air, "I think I taste better next to all of you than I ever did alone."

"That," said Pinto gently, "is the secret of every casado."

---

A little while later, a girl with two braids sat down at the counter, swinging her feet on the tall stool. She picked up her fork, and her whole face lit up like the morning sun.

"My favorite!" she said. "I love how everything tastes together."

Did the casado feel scared? Oh, not even a little. They felt *proud* — proud as the very first star of the evening, switching on right when somebody wished for it. Because being eaten, for a casado, is the happiest ending there is. It means you did your job. It means you made somebody glad.

"Ready, mae?" hummed Gallo, hugging Pinto close.

"Better together," said Pinto.

"PURA VIDA!" cheered the whole plate at once — Salad and Tato and Egg and cheese and tomato and rice and bean, all of them laughing, all of them friends, all of them married up into one perfect, happy lunch.

And the girl took the very first bite — a little of everything, all at once — and smiled the way you smile when something is *exactly* right.

---

## Goodnight Blessing
May you remember, little one, that you don't have to be the best at everything to be exactly right. You only have to be your own true self, leaning gently against the friends you love. The world is a casado, and you are one delicious, important part of it. Snuggle in now, soft and warm and married up with your blankets, and let the sleepy night soak all the way through. Pura Vida, sweet dreamer. Goodnight.
