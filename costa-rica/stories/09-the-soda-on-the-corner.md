# Story 9: The Little Soda on the Corner

**Main Character:** Gallo and Pinto
**Guest Characters:** Lala the Tres Leches, Tato the Patacon, Coco the Granizado, Flan the Coconut Flan
**Setting:** The corner soda — the beloved family diner that is everyone's home base in Pura Vida Town
**Theme:** Home is wherever your familia gathers to share a meal.
**Spanish Spotlight:** **soda** (SOH-dah) = a small Costa Rican family-run diner

---

Now, you should know that the busiest, brightest, butter-warmest place in all of Pura Vida Town was a tiny little **soda** (SOH-dah) = a small family-run diner on the corner of Mango Street and Everybody-Knows-Your-Name Lane. It had six wobbly tables, one ceiling fan that spun like it was telling a long story, and a window so steamed-up you could draw smiley faces on it. Every single morning, Gallo the fried-rice grain skipped through the door and shouted his favorite words to the whole world.

"Rise and pinto!" he sang, his golden-toasty edges glinting, his little woven palm hat tipped just so.

"Better together, mae," murmured Pinto, the small black bean beside him, polishing his tiny wooden spoon.

But on THIS evening — a drippy, dribbly, rain-tapping evening — something was wrong. The soda was quiet. Quiet as a cookie that nobody had crunched.

The fan creaked. The rain whispered. And not one single laugh bounced off the walls.

Gallo froze mid-hum. "Pinto," he said. "It's... it's *too* quiet in here-hmmm."

Pinto's pale stripe-smile drooped just a smidge. He set down his spoon. He looked around the empty soda — the empty tables, the dark window, the lonely little salt shaker shaped like a volcano.

"The rain kept everyone home," Pinto said softly. "And a soda with no familia in it..." He patted the nearest table. "...is just a room with chairs, mae."

Gallo's golden flecks went pale. A room with chairs! That was the saddest thing he had ever heard, and Gallo had once heard a sad thing about a banana who lost his peel.

"Then we'll FIX it," Gallo declared, hopping onto a salt shaker so he could be tall and dramatic about it. "We'll bring the familia back! Tonight! With a surprise!" He wobbled. The salt shaker was not built for speeches. "We'll throw the cozUMblest, gigglUMblest pot-luck this corner has ever — whoa —" He toppled into Pinto, who caught him without even looking, because that is what best friends do.

"A pot-luck," Pinto repeated, and his calm warm smile came back. "Everyone brings a little something. Together it becomes a feast." He nodded slowly. "Now *that* is pura vida."

So out into the rain the two of them went, one rice grain and one bean, sharing one extremely small leaf as an umbrella, which protected approximately none of them.

"This leaf is doing a *wonderful* job," said Gallo, who was completely soaked.

"It is doing *no* job at all, mae," said Pinto, who was also completely soaked, but smiling.

"Best leaf I've ever owned-hmmm," Gallo agreed, and they splashed onward, because when you're with your best friend, even a useless leaf feels like the finest umbrella in all of Costa Rica.

Their first stop was Lala the Tres Leches, who lived in the bakery two doors down, glistening and dripping sweet milk onto a doily and gazing dreamily at the rain.

"A pot-luck?" Lala gasped, her wobbly cloud of whipped cream trembling with joy. "At the *soda*? Oh, soak it ALL in! The steam! The spoons! The way the light goes golden when the rain stops!" She clasped her squishy little hands. "I'll bring DESSERT. I'll bring MYSELF, technically, since I *am* dessert, but also — extra cream! I shall be the cozy!"

She left three milky footprints out the door before anyone could answer, and then a fourth footprint came back through the door, attached to Lala, who poked her wobbly cream-cloud head in to add, "Oh, and tell everyone to bring a CHAIR, because I have a feeling there won't be enough — I can *feel* it, the way I feel a good cinnamon morning!" Then she swooned at the rain on the window and floated off again, leaving Gallo and Pinto blinking through a fine mist of whipped cream.

"I like her," said Gallo, wiping cream off his hat.

"Everybody likes her," said Pinto. "That's her superpower."

Next they splashed to Tato the Patacon, the flat golden smashed-plantain disc who was busy doing push-ups in a puddle because Tato believed every puddle was a chance to get stronger.

"Pot-luck. Soda. Friends. Say no more." Tato cracked his crunchy crinkled knuckles. "I'll bring the salty crunch. I'll bring the MUSCLE. Need a table carried? Tato carries it. Need a heavy pot of beans hauled across town in the rain?" He flexed. A little dusting of salt sprinkled off his shoulder. "*Smash now, worry never!*"

Then Tato tried to lift a bench to demonstrate, tipped slowly sideways, and sat down in the puddle with a soft, brave little *flop*.

"I'm okay," said Tato, from inside the puddle. "I meant to do that."

Gallo laughed so hard his hat fell off.

"Tato," said Pinto kindly, helping him up with a steady little tug, "you don't have to lift the whole world. Just bring your crunch and your big warm heart. That's the heaviest, best thing you've got."

Tato thought about this for a long, earnest moment, water dripping off his crinkled edges.

"...My heart," he said slowly, "is *very* strong." Then he beamed, gave a soggy salute, and marched off toward the soda — walking straight into the doorframe of his own house first, because Tato was brave, not graceful.

Up the hill they bounced to find Coco the Granizado, the tall fluffy mound of rainbow shaved ice, leaning cool and breezy against a lamppost in his tiny round sunglasses, even though it was night, even though it was raining.

"A *fiesta* at the soda?" Coco grinned. "Tuanis! That's the most tuanis news I've heard all week, mae! I'll bring the MUSIC. Coco's got beats. Coco's got rhythm. Coco's got a maraca made entirely of —" he shook himself and rattled — "myself!" His powdered-milk snow sparkled. "Stay frosty, stay sweet — but tonight, stay *dancing*!"

Then Coco did a little spin, slipped on a raindrop, and slid clear down the wet hill on his cone-bottom like a rainbow rocket, hollering "TUAAANIS!" the whole way down. He landed, miraculously, right-side-up, struck a cool pose, and pretended that had absolutely been the plan.

"Did you see that?" Gallo whispered, eyes shining.

"I saw it, mae," said Pinto. "I will never forget it."

Last of all, behind a fern, they found Flan the Coconut Flan, the jiggly pale-gold custard dome, wobbling nervously under his shiny caramel.

"Oh — oh dear — a party?" Flan quivered, his toasted coconut flecks all aflutter. "But what if nobody comes? What if the rain doesn't stop? What if I bring my famous caramel and I — I *spill* it, and everyone laughs, and — steady, steady... ooh, *wobbly*!" He wobbled clean in a circle. "I'm not sure I'm a party kind of flan."

Pinto stepped close and put a gentle bean-hand on Flan's trembly side.

"Flan," he said softly. "Nobody throws a party because they're brave. They throw a party so nobody has to be alone in the rain. Bring your caramel. Spill it if you spill it. We'll lick it off the table together." He smiled his striped smile. "Better together, mae."

Flan went very still. A single happy wobble rolled through him, gentle as a yes.

"...I make a very good caramel," he admitted.

"The BEST!" cried Gallo. "Now RUN — well, *wobble* — there's a soda to fill!"

Back at the little soda, the rain still drummed on the tin roof, but inside, magic was brewing — slow and warm, the way Don Café always said good things should.

Tato hauled the heavy pot of bubbling rice and beans, grunting and proud, and only dropped it twice (both times onto his own foot, both times announcing "I'm okay!").

Coco shook himself into a rattly rhythm and a song bounced off the steamy walls — a bouncy, beachy tune that made even the salt shaker want to wiggle. Lala swooned over the way the ceiling fan spun like a slow waltz and dripped extra cream on absolutely everything, including Pinto, who did not mind one bit, and including the salt shaker, who now had a tiny cream hat and looked very pleased about it.

Gallo darted from table to table, lighting little candle-stubs, fluffing napkins, and humming so happily that his golden flecks practically glowed. "Almost ready! Almost cozy! Almost — Tato, that's the THIRD time you've dropped the pot on your foot-hmmm!"

"Building character!" called Tato cheerfully, hopping on one crispy foot.

Flan set out his caramel with shaking hands — and yes, he spilled a little — and Gallo immediately dipped his hat in it and wore it as a delicious caramel helmet, and the whole soda burst out laughing, and Flan laughed hardest of all, wobbling with the kind of giggle you only get when you find out a mistake can be the best part.

Then Gallo climbed onto the counter (much sturdier than a salt shaker) and called out into the warm bright room.

"Familia! Tonight the rain tried to make our soda quiet." He swept off his caramel helmet. "But a soda isn't quiet because of weather. A soda is quiet because it's *empty of us*. And look — *look* —"

He didn't have to finish. Because the door kept swinging open. Neighbors who'd seen the golden light glowing through the steamy window came shaking off their rain hats. Tico the Toucan swooped in shouting "BEAK THE NEWS — THE SODA'S HOPPING!" Slowy the Sloth arrived three sentences later, mid-yawn, murmuring "puuura... viiida..." Cajeta bounced through the door so fast she bonked the doorframe and squealed, "Sweet and round, watch me bound!"

The six wobbly tables filled up. The ceiling fan spun its long story. The window fogged so thick with happy steam that someone drew a smiley face on it — and then everyone drew smiley faces on it, until the whole window grinned back at the rain.

Pinto stood quietly in the middle of it all, his little spoon in hand, watching the warm golden glow bounce off every laughing face.

"Mae," Gallo whispered, hopping down beside him. "We did it-hmmm. We filled it back up."

"No," Pinto said softly, and his smile was the warmest thing in the whole warm room. "We didn't fill it. *They* did. We just opened the door." He looked around at the music, the caramel, the steam, the laughter, the rain that didn't matter anymore. "This was never just a room with chairs, Gallo. It was always home. Home is just... wherever the familia comes to share a meal."

Gallo's golden flecks glowed brighter than the lamp.

And outside, the rain finally hushed — as if even the sky had wandered close to the steamy window, peeked at all that warmth and laughter inside the little soda on the corner, and decided to stop and listen for a while.

It was the fullest the soda had ever been. And it stayed that way long into the soft, sleepy night.

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, wherever you are tonight, may your home be warm as a soda full of laughing friends. May the rain stay outside your window and the love stay snug within. Know that home was never the walls or the tables or the roof — it is the people who gather, the meal you share, the door you open wide. Rest now, soaked in sweetness like Lala's cream, steady and safe like Pinto's gentle voice. You are never a room with chairs; you are always, always loved. Goodnight.
