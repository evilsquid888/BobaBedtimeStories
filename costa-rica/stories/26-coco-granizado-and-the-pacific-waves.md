# Story 26: Coco Granizado and the Pacific Waves

**Main Character:** Coco the Granizado
**Guest Characters:** Tato the Patacon
**Setting:** A golden Pacific surf beach in Guanacaste
**Theme:** Riding the wave is more fun than fighting it.
**Spanish Spotlight:** **playa** (PLAH-yah) = beach

---

Now, between you and me, there has never been a single dessert in all of Costa Rica as confident as Coco the Granizado on the morning he decided to learn how to surf.

Coco was a tall, fluffy mound of rainbow-syruped shaved ice, tucked into a paper cone, dusted with powdered milk like fresh snow, and wearing his tiny round sunglasses even though the sun had barely yawned awake. He stood at the edge of the **playa** (PLAH-yah) = beach, where the golden sand of Guanacaste met the big blue Pacific, and he struck what he believed was an extremely cool pose.

"Behold," Coco announced to a passing crab, "the future greatest surfer this coast has ever seen. Stay frosty, stay sweet!"

The crab, who had seen a great many confident creatures wipe out spectacularly, simply waved a claw and scuttled off to find a better view.

You should know that Coco had never actually surfed before. Not even once. He had, however, watched a pelican glide over a wave that very morning, and he had thought to himself, *Well, how hard can that be?* This is a thought that has gotten more creatures into trouble than any other thought in the whole history of the world, and Coco was about to discover exactly why.

---

Coco had borrowed a surfboard about three sizes too big for a cone of shaved ice. It was bright yellow with a painted pineapple on the nose, and Coco loved it immediately.

"Okay, board," he said, hugging it. "You and me. We're going to be *tuanis* together." (That, dear reader, is the Costa Rican way of saying *super cool*, and Coco said it about roughly everything.)

He waded into the warm shallows, hopped onto the board with a *fwump*, paddled out toward a gentle little wave — and promptly tumbled off sideways into the sea with a *SPLOOSH*.

When he popped back up, half his rainbow syrup had run pink and his powdered-milk snow had gone soggy.

"That," he sputtered, "was a practice fall. Professionals do practice falls."

He climbed back on. He fell off again. *SPLOOSH.* He climbed back on. He fell off the *other* way. *SPLOOSH.* He climbed back on so fast and fell off so quickly that, honestly, it looked less like surfing and more like a cone playing peekaboo with the ocean.

A small wave nudged him gently, as if to say *hello*, and Coco shouted, "NOT TODAY, WAVE!" and flopped off the board face-first.

This went on for quite a while.

A pelican landed nearby to watch, the way you might pull up a beach chair for a good show. A sandpiper jogged over. Two seagulls argued about whether Coco's next fall would be a sideways fall or a backwards fall. (One bird bet a clam on it. The other bet two clams. The clams, who had not agreed to any of this, said nothing.)

Coco fell sideways.

"Pay up," said the first seagull.

---

Up on the warm sand, somebody was watching, and that somebody was Tato the Patacon.

Tato was a flat, round, golden-crispy smashed plantain with crinkled crunchy edges, a soft chewy middle, and a light dusting of salt. He was built sturdy and just a tiny bit clumsy, which is exactly why he had decided to *watch* the surfing rather than do any. He had been building a sandcastle, though it had collapsed twice because he kept patting it too hard. (Tato did most things too hard. It was simply his way.)

"Coco!" Tato called, jogging down to the water's edge with his flat-footed *pat-pat-pat*. "Are you okay? You've fallen off, like, a hundred times!"

"A hundred and *six*," Coco corrected proudly, draining seawater out of his cone. "But who's counting?"

"You are, apparently," said Tato.

Coco pushed his sunglasses up his slushy face. "Tato, my crispy friend, I have a foolproof plan. The waves keep knocking me down. So I am going to *fight* them. I am going to surf so *hard* and stand so *stiff* that no wave will ever knock Coco Granizado off his board again. Smash now, worry never, right? That's your thing!"

"That *is* my thing," Tato agreed, puffing up a little. "Smash now, worry never!" Then he frowned, which for a patacon is a very serious crinkling of the edges. "But Coco... I don't think you can smash a *wave*. Waves are very squishy. And also very large. And also made of the entire ocean."

"Watch me," said Coco.

"I'm watching," said Tato. "I'm watching very nervously. I have my nervous-watching face on. This is it. This is the face."

It was, in fact, the exact same face as his regular face, because patacones only have one face. But it was the thought that counted.

---

Coco paddled out again, gritted his slushy teeth, and waited for the next wave with every fluffy crystal of his body clenched tight as a fist. He locked his little legs. He stiffened his cone. He scrunched his face into the fiercest expression a dessert can make, which is roughly the expression of someone smelling a very strong cheese.

The wave came.

Coco braced.

The wave lifted him up, up, up — and because Coco was so stiff and so tense and so absolutely determined to *win*, the board shot out from under him like a watermelon seed pinched between two fingers. Coco went up. The board went forward. They had, as they say, irreconcilable differences.

*KER-SPLOOSH-PLOP.*

Coco landed in the foam upside down with his cone stuck in the sand and his two slushy feet waving in the air.

Tato came hurrying. "Are you—"

"Practice fall," came Coco's muffled voice from inside the sand.

"That is *definitely* not a practice fall."

Tato gently un-stuck him. Coco's sunglasses were now on sideways, and a small crab had taken up residence on top of his head, enjoying the view.

For the very first time all morning, Coco the Granizado looked just a little bit melty — and not the fun, sparkly kind of melty. The quiet kind.

"Maybe," he said softly, "I'm just not a *tuanis* surfer after all."

---

And that, dear reader, is precisely when the wave spoke up.

Not a big scary wave. Oh no. This was a round, smiling, sparkly little wave — soft and curling and silvery-blue, with a froth of white foam on top like whipped cream. It rolled up close to Coco's feet and *giggled*, the way water giggles when it tickles your toes.

"*Hola*, frosty friend," said the Wave, in a voice like a warm bath and a lullaby mixed together. "I've been trying to play with you all morning."

Coco blinked. "Play? You've been knocking me OVER all morning!"

"Only because you keep pushing *back*," said the Wave kindly. "You stiffen up. You clench. You try to win. But waves aren't for winning, little cone. Waves are for *riding*." She did a gentle, swooshy loop, foam sparkling. "Stop fighting me. Lean into me. Go soft and let me carry you. You'll see."

"But I'll fall," said Coco.

"You're *already* falling," the Wave pointed out gently, "and you're as stiff as a frozen popsicle. Try it the soft way. What's the worst that happens — you get wet?" She winked a little foam-wink. "You're shaved ice, my dear. You were *born* wet."

Coco, who was indeed dripping all over the sand, had to admit she had a point.

"But what if I look silly?" Coco whispered. He glanced back at the beach, where a whole row of crabs and birds had gathered to watch. "Everyone's looking."

"Oh, *little cone*," laughed the Wave, and her laugh sounded like a hundred tiny bells made of water. "Do you know what looks silly? Fighting the entire ocean and losing. Do you know what looks *tuanis*? Letting go." She curled close and whispered, so only Coco could hear, "Besides — the ones who are laughing have never even tried. And the ones who've tried are the ones cheering for you."

Coco peeked at the beach again. Tato was not laughing. Tato was holding both his crispy edges together, hoping so hard for his friend that little flecks of salt were trembling off him. And that, somehow, made Coco feel brave.

---

So Coco tried one more time. But this time was different.

He paddled out, and instead of locking his legs and scrunching his face like an angry cheese, he took a long, slow breath. He let his shoulders go loose. He let his cone go wobbly. He even let the little crab stay on his head, because honestly the crab was having a wonderful day and it seemed rude to evict him.

"Just ride," Coco whispered. "Don't fight. Ride."

The Wave rose up beneath him, smooth and shining and gold in the morning sun.

And this time, Coco did not brace.

He *bent*. He swayed. He let his knees go bouncy and his arms go floaty, and when the board tilted, he tilted *with* it instead of *against* it. The wave lifted him high — and instead of shooting off like a watermelon seed, Coco *glided*. He glided along the curling silver-blue wall of water, sunglasses gleaming, powdered-milk snow streaming behind him like a comet tail, rainbow syrup catching the light in every color all at once.

"I'M DOING IT!" Coco shrieked. "TATO! TATO, LOOK! I'M SURFING! THIS IS SO TUANIS!"

On the beach, Tato leapt up so high he knocked over his sandcastle for the third time, and didn't even care. "THAT'S MY FRIEND!" he hollered at a passing pelican. "THE FLOATY ONE! THE FROSTY ONE! THAT'S COCO!"

The wave carried Coco all the way to the shore in one long, perfect, sparkling ride, and set him down on the warm sand as soft as a kiss.

"You see?" said the Wave, foaming happily around his feet. "Riding is *always* more fun than fighting."

"It's a hundred times more fun," Coco breathed. Then he grinned. "A hundred and *six*, even."

---

By afternoon, every creature on the **playa** had heard the news (mostly because Tato told all of them, very loudly, at least twice each). Crabs gathered. Pelicans circled. A sleepy iguana cracked one eye open.

And they watched Coco the Granizado ride wave after wave after wave — soft and loose and laughing, never once fighting, letting the warm Pacific carry him wherever it pleased. His syrup shone in stripes of pink and orange and blue. His snow sparkled. His sunglasses never slipped again.

He had become, without a single doubt, the coolest cone on the entire coast.

"*Pura vida*," sighed Tato happily, flopping down beside him as the sun melted gold into the sea. "You're a real surfer now, Coco."

"Nah," said Coco, leaning back, letting the foam tickle his toes. He smiled up at his friend the Wave, who curled close and tucked him in with a blanket of soft white froth. "I'm just a cone who finally learned to go with the flow." He yawned a frosty, happy yawn. "Stay frosty... stay sweet."

And the warm tide rocked him, slow and gentle, all the way to sleep.

---

## Goodnight Blessing
May you be soft as foam and gentle as the evening tide, little one. When the day feels too big to fight, may you remember Coco, and simply lean back, and let the good things carry you home. You don't have to win every wave — you only have to ride. Close your eyes and feel the sea rock you, slow and warm and safe. Goodnight.
