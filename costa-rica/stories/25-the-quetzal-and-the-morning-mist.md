# Story 25: The Quetzal and the Morning Mist

**Main Character:** Don Café (a roasted Costa Rican coffee bean from the highlands)
**Guest Characters:** Slowy the Sloth
**Setting:** A hushed cloud-forest dawn high in the volcano highlands, where the shy emerald quetzal lives
**Theme:** Patience rewards you with the rarest kind of beauty.
**Spanish Spotlight:** **pluma** (PLOO-mah) = feather

---

Long before the sun bothered to wake up, while the mountain was still wearing its softest blanket of fog, Don Café was already awake — and already losing an argument with a leaf.

"Move along, little one," he said politely to the leaf, which had landed squarely on top of his tiny straw hat. He poked it gently with his miniature walking stick. The leaf did not move along. It just sat there being a leaf, the way leaves do.

"Good things brew slowly," sighed the old coffee bean, "but this leaf is testing me."

A drop of dew rolled off the leaf and went *plink* on his nose.

"Ha! There," said Don Café, wiping it. "You see? Even a leaf can drip a little wisdom, if you wait long enough."

And waiting, dear reader, is exactly what this whole story is about.

---

Don Café lived high, high up in the cloud forest, where the trees grew so tall they tickled the bellies of the clouds, and the air was so cool and damp that everything wore little beads of water like jewelry. This was the morning he had promised his friends something very special.

"Today," he had told them, "if we are patient — and I do mean *patient* — we may glimpse the **quetzal**."

The quetzal! Even saying the name made the forest hush. The quetzal was the shyest, rarest, most beautiful bird in all the highlands: a streak of living emerald with a long, ribboning tail and a chest as red as a ripe coffee cherry. Nobody saw the quetzal. People waited whole lifetimes and never caught a peek. The quetzal was a rumor with wings.

"I would loooove to see it," came a voice so slow it seemed to arrive in pieces.

It was Slowy the Sloth, hanging from a mossy branch, blinking his sleepy half-moon eyes. Slowy had been climbing toward this branch since *yesterday*. He had just now arrived.

"Puuura... viiida..." he yawned, which took a remarkably long time.

"Perfect timing, my friend," said Don Café warmly. "We are about to do the hardest thing in the whole forest."

Slowy's eyes brightened. "Run?"

"No."

"Climb fast?"

"Goodness, no."

Slowy looked deeply relieved. "Oh good. I am not built for either."

"We are going to be *still*," said Don Café. "Completely, perfectly still. And quiet. And patient. The quetzal is shy. It will not come to noise or hurry or wiggling. It will only come where it feels respected."

Slowy considered this for a long, long moment.

"Don Café," he said at last, "I have been training for this my entire life."

And honestly? He had a point.

---

So the three friends — Don Café, Slowy, and a leaf that had absolutely no intention of leaving the hat — settled into the misty hush to wait.

But first, of course, Slowy had to get comfortable, and *that* was its own small adventure. He chose a branch. He decided it was the wrong branch. He chose another branch, which turned out to be the first branch again, because Slowy had circled the trunk so slowly he hadn't noticed. He hung upside down, then right-side up, then in a way that was, frankly, neither, and finally settled into a cozy curl with his chin tucked into his chest.

"There," he said, very pleased. "I am now officially still."

"You took half an hour to stop wiggling," said Don Café, amused.

"I like to wiggle thoroughly," said Slowy, "and then stop completely. It is the sloth way." He yawned. "Pura vida."

At first the waiting was easy. The fog drifted between the trees like slow gray ghosts who were very polite. Somewhere far off, water dripped. *Plink. Plink-plink.* A beetle the size of a button trundled past, tipped its tiny beetle face up at them, paused as if to ask whether they had seen anything interesting, decided they had not, and trundled on with the busy air of a beetle who had places to be.

"This is nice," whispered Slowy after what may have been an hour. "I have not moved in ages. It feels like a holiday."

"Shhh," said Don Café gently, smiling under his hat. "Listen. Don't *do*. Just listen."

So they listened.

They heard the mountain breathing. They heard a leaf let go of a branch and spend a long lazy time tumbling down. They heard the soft creak of the forest stretching as the first thin light came sliding in, turning the fog from gray to silver to the palest peach.

It was, Don Café thought, the most beautiful sound in the world — the sound of *almost nothing*. The sound of a place that trusted you enough to relax.

But waiting is sneaky. It starts out cozy. Then it starts to *itch*.

"Don Café," whispered Slowy, "is it here yet?"

"Not yet."

A while passed.

"Is it here *now*?"

"Patience, my friend."

Another while passed.

"How about — "

"Still no, Slowy."

"I was going to ask if you wanted to play I-Spy," said Slowy, a little hurt.

Don Café sighed the soft sigh of someone who loved his friend very much. "Go on, then. One round. Quietly."

"I spy," whispered Slowy, "with my little eye... something... grayyy."

Don Café looked around at the fog, which was everywhere, in every direction, being extremely gray. "Is it... the mist?"

"It's the mist!" Slowy beamed. "Your turn."

"I spy something gray," said Don Café.

"The mist!"

"Correct."

"This," declared Slowy happily, "is the best game I have ever played." And because everything in the cloud forest was either gray or green or quietly dripping, they played eleven more rounds, and Slowy won them all, even the ones where Don Café was clearly thinking of the mushroom.

A longer while passed after that. A much longer while. The leaf on the hat got bored and grew a tiny mushroom. (That is how long it was.) A snail set out to cross the branch in front of them and was still only halfway by the time the light began to change, which made even Slowy feel speedy by comparison.

"Don Café," Slowy whispered, and there was a tremble in his slow voice, "what if it never comes? What if we wait and wait and wait, and the quetzal is somewhere else having breakfast and laughing at us?"

Don Café was quiet for a moment. Then he chuckled, a deep, warm, brown sound, like the gurgle of a coffee pot.

"Ah," he said. "Now *that* is the real test. Not the waiting. The wondering."

"The wondering is the worst part," Slowy agreed mournfully.

"It is," said Don Café. "When I was a young bean, still growing red and round on the mountainside, I wanted everything to hurry. Hurry and ripen, hurry and roast, hurry and be done. But the farmers, they would walk past me and say, *despacio* — slowly. Good coffee cannot be rushed. The longer it grows in the cool and the cloud, the sweeter it becomes. And the things worth waiting for the most? They come *quietest of all*."

Slowy was quiet. Then, very slowly, he smiled.

"So the wondering," he said, "is just... waiting wearing a worried hat."

Don Café laughed so hard the mushroom on his real hat wobbled.

"Slowy," he said, "that is the wisest thing anyone has said all morning. *Bien hecho.* Well done."

"I have a lot of time to think," said Slowy modestly. "Mostly while reaching for leaves."

So they settled deeper into the hush. Don Café showed Slowy a little trick the old farmers had taught him: when the waiting itched the worst, you breathed *in* for the count of a falling raindrop, and *out* for the count of the next one. *In... plink. Out... plink.* Slowly, the worried hat slid off the wondering. The forest grew enormous and soft around them, and the friends grew small and calm inside it, like two seeds tucked safe in the same warm pod.

"Don Café," Slowy murmured, almost asleep, "even if the quetzal never comes... this was a nice way to spend a morning."

"That," said Don Café gently, "is exactly when the rare things choose to arrive. The moment you stop *needing* them — and simply enjoy the wait."

And the forest, as if it had been listening all along, drew a slow breath and held it.

---

And then — it happened.

It did not happen with a trumpet or a splash or a *ta-daaa*. The rarest things never do. It happened the way dawn happens: so softly you almost miss it.

A branch high above them dipped. Just a little. Just enough.

The fog parted like a curtain drawn by gentle hands. And there, lit by the first true gold of morning, sat the quetzal.

Oh, dear reader. *Oh.*

It was greener than green — a green that seemed to glow from the inside, like the heart of a leaf when the sun shines straight through it. Its chest blazed soft red, the exact color of a ripe coffee cherry, which made Don Café's tiny heart swell with pride. And its tail — its impossible, ribboning tail — streamed down from the branch like a waterfall made of emerald silk, swaying ever so slightly in the breeze.

Nobody breathed. Nobody even thought about breathing.

The quetzal turned its head and looked at them. Right at them. Three small, still, patient creatures who had not run, had not grabbed, had not made a single greedy sound. Who had simply *waited*, and *respected*, and *been gentle* in its quiet green home.

And the quetzal did something it had never done for anyone in a hurry.

It tipped its head, the way you nod to a friend. Then it lifted one wing — slow, graceful, deliberate — and let go of a single, shimmering **pluma**.

The feather spiraled down through the gold-and-silver light, turning over and over, flashing green-then-blue-then-green again, like a tiny dancer who could not decide which color she loved best. Down it drifted, and down, until it landed — *exactly* — on the very tip of Don Café's straw hat, right beside the leaf and the mushroom, who scooted over politely to make room.

When they looked up again, the branch was empty. The quetzal was gone, melted back into the mist as if it had only ever been a beautiful idea.

But the **pluma** was real. It glimmered there, soft and impossible, a thank-you from the shyest heart in the forest.

"It... gave us a gift," whispered Slowy, and a slow, happy tear rolled down his fuzzy cheek. By the time it reached his chin, the sun was fully up.

"No, my friend," said Don Café softly, gazing at the shimmering feather. "It gave us a *reward*. Not for being clever. Not for being fast. For being *still* enough to deserve it." He smiled. "The rarest beauty does not come to the loudest hand. It comes to the patient one."

---

They carried the feather home together — which, with Slowy setting the pace, took most of the day, and that was perfectly fine. The whole forest seemed to lean in as they passed, the way friends do when they want to hear the good part of a story again.

That night, Don Café tucked the shimmering **pluma** into the brim of his hat, right where the morning had placed it, so that wherever he wandered in the highlands, he carried a little secret green light with him — proof that the gentlest patience earns the most wonderful surprises.

And the leaf? The leaf decided to stay forever. The mushroom, too. Don Café didn't mind one bit.

"Good things brew slowly," he hummed, settling into the cool, sweet dark. "And the best things... arrive on quiet wings."

## Goodnight Blessing
May your evening be soft as cloud-forest mist, and your waiting always gentle. May the rarest, loveliest things find you not when you chase them, but when you grow still and kind and ready. Carry a little green feather of patience in your heart, dear one, and let it shimmer in your dreams. The mountain is breathing slow now, the fog is tucking the trees in tight, and somewhere an emerald wing is folding softly to rest. *Pura vida.* Sleep deep, sleep warm, sleep wrapped in quiet wonder. Goodnight.
