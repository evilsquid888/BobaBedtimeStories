# Story 15: The Hummingbird and the Hidden Flower

**Main Character:** Cajeta the Coconut Fudge
**Guest Characters:** Tico the Toucan
**Setting:** A sun-dappled rainforest garden bursting with blossoms
**Theme:** Kindness given freely always comes buzzing back.
**Spanish Spotlight:** **flor** (flohr) — flower

---

Now, if you have ever tried to hold a bouncy ball underwater, you already know exactly what it was like to be friends with Cajeta the Coconut Fudge. She was a small round ball of golden coconut fudge, chewy and dense, rolled in toasted coconut shreds until she looked deliciously fuzzy — and she simply could *not* stop bouncing.

*Boing. Boing. BOING.*

"Sweet and round, watch me bound!" she squealed, ricocheting off a dewy leaf, a mushroom cap, and a very surprised snail, all before breakfast.

This particular morning, the rainforest garden was showing off. Blossoms popped open everywhere like tiny pink fireworks. Sunbeams poured through the leaves in long golden ribbons. Somewhere a frog was practicing its burp-song, and not improving.

It was, in Cajeta's expert opinion, the most *tuanis* morning ever.

She had no idea it was about to get a whole lot more interesting.

Because high above her, something tiny and tired was beginning to fall out of the sky.

---

*Plip.*

A tiny something landed in the soft grass right beside Cajeta. She bounced backward in surprise — *boing!* — then crept close to look.

It was a hummingbird. The teeniest, most jewel-colored hummingbird you ever saw, with feathers like spilled emeralds and a throat that flickered ruby-red. But her wings, which should have been humming, were drooping. Her little eyes were sleepy and sad.

"Oh no, oh no, oh *no,*" Cajeta said, all in a rush, because Cajeta said most things in a rush. "Are you okay? You don't look okay. You look like a tired feather pillow!"

"I'm hungry," the hummingbird whispered. Her name, she said, was **Colibrí** — which is just the lovely Spanish word for hummingbird, *colibrí* (koh-lee-BREE). "I've flown the whole garden. Every **flor** (flohr) — every flower — has already been sipped dry. There's no nectar left anywhere, and I'm so, so tired I can barely buzz."

To prove it, she tried to lift off — *bzzt* — and flopped right back into the grass like a dropped sock.

Cajeta's chewy little heart squeezed. She didn't even think about it. Helping somebody who needed help was, to her, as automatic as bouncing.

"Don't you worry one bit," she said. "We are going to find you a flower if I have to bounce off every petal in this whole jungle. Sweet and round, watch me — well, you know the rest."

---

"BEAK THE NEWS!"

The shout came from above, loud enough to knock three beetles off a twig. Down swooped Tico the Toucan, his enormous rainbow-striped beak slicing the air like a banana that had learned to fly. He landed on a branch, twinkling and bouncy and bursting with gossip.

"Big news, BIG news!" Tico announced to absolutely everyone, even the snail. "Word around the canopy is — a tired little hummingbird has crash-landed in the garden! Eyewitnesses say she's about *yay* big, very shiny, last seen looking sad in the grass! If you have any information—"

"Tico," said Cajeta. "She's right here. You're looking at her."

Tico blinked his twinkly eyes. He looked at Colibrí. He looked at Cajeta. He looked back at Colibrí.

"...That was the fastest investigation of my entire career," he said proudly. "I am *very* good at this."

Cajeta giggled, a little fizzy giggle like soda. "Tico, we need your help. Colibrí can't find any nectar. Every flower is empty. But *you* fly all over the jungle — you see everything! Is there a flower left? Any flower at all?"

Tico puffed up his bright yellow throat, which is what he did when he was thinking very hard. He thought so hard you could practically hear it — a sort of *hmmmmmm* like a tiny lawnmower.

"Every **flor** in the meadow... sipped dry," he murmured. "The orchids by the river... empty since Tuesday. The big trumpet flowers... a sloth fell asleep in one and now nobody goes there." He gasped. His beak shot straight up. "But WAIT. There is a *legend.*"

"A legend?" Cajeta bounced twice from sheer excitement. *Boing-boing!*

"They say," Tico whispered, leaning in so close his beak bonked a leaf, "that deep, *deep* in the greenest part of the garden, where the sun barely peeks and the moss grows fuzzy as a kitten, there blooms one last secret flower. The hidden **flor.** Bigger than your head, full of sweet nectar, and *nobody* has found it in ages." He paused dramatically. "Mostly because nobody can be bothered to look. It's quite far."

Cajeta did not hesitate. Not for one chewy second.

"Then that's where we're going," she said. "Climb on, Colibrí. You're too tired to fly, so you'll ride. And I bounce *very* comfortably. Probably."

Colibrí, with the last of her strength, hopped onto Cajeta's fuzzy coconut shoulder and held on tight.

"Pura vida!" cheered Tico. "I'll fly ahead and scout! Adventure! Drama! Possible nectar! This is the best news *all week!*"

---

And so they set off into the deep green.

Cajeta bounced. Tico flapped. Colibrí clung on, tiny and trusting.

But the rainforest, it turned out, did not want to give up its hidden flower easily.

First came a wide, glassy stream, too far across to leap. Cajeta peered at it, chewing her lip. "Smashing won't help here," she muttered, "and I'm *terrible* at swimming. I just sort of... dissolve."

Tico flapped down. "Allow me — your local airborne ferry service! Hop on my beak. *Carefully.* It's a precision instrument."

Cajeta and Colibrí balanced on the big banana beak, and Tico flew them across in a wibbly-wobbly swoop, hollering "PASSENGERS, PLEASE KEEP YOUR ARMS AND COCONUTS INSIDE THE BEAK AT ALL TIMES." They landed in a giggling heap on the far bank.

Next came a steep, slippery hill of mossy stones. Cajeta — and let's be honest, this is exactly what coconut fudge is *for* — simply bounced. *Boing, boing, boing,* up and up, with Colibrí squeaking "wheee!" on her shoulder, until they reached the top.

And then came the Tickle Vines.

These were long, dangly green vines with soft feathery tips, and the moment Cajeta brushed one — *fwip!* — it tickled her right under what would be her chin, if fudge had chins.

"Hee — hee — STOP — heehee — I can't — HEEHEE—!" Cajeta wheezed, bouncing in a hundred helpless directions, giggling so hard she nearly rolled back down the hill.

"They only tickle the ticklish," said Colibrí weakly, but with a tiny smile, which was the first smile she'd smiled all day.

"Then I am *doomed,*" gasped Cajeta, "because I am the most ticklish fudge in all of Costa Rica."

It was Tico who saved her. He hovered above the vines and announced, in his most official town-crier voice, "ATTENTION, VINES. THIS IS A SERIOUS RESCUE MISSION. NO TICKLING ON DUTY." And whether the vines understood or were simply startled by the volume, they curled politely out of the way.

(They did sneak in one last tickle behind Cajeta's back, though. Vines can never quite help themselves.)

---

At last, they pushed through a curtain of soft fuzzy moss into a small hidden clearing.

And there it was.

The hidden **flor.**

It was enormous — bigger than Cajeta's whole head — with petals the deep velvet purple of evening, glowing faintly in the dim green light. And cupped inside, sparkling like a little pool of melted starlight, was nectar. Sweet, golden, *untouched* nectar, just waiting.

Colibrí's tiny eyes went wide.

"Go on," whispered Cajeta, gently lifting her from her shoulder toward the flower. "It's all yours. We found it for you. *Better together,* as my friends always say."

Colibrí dipped her long beak into the flower.

And oh — you could *see* it work. With every sip, color rushed back into her feathers. The emeralds gleamed. The ruby throat blazed. Her drooping wings lifted, twitched, and then — *BZZZZZZ!* — burst into a hum so bright and happy it sounded like the whole forest sighing with relief.

She zoomed up into the air, looped a joyful loop, and spun so fast she became a glittering blur.

"I CAN FLY! I CAN FLY AGAIN!" she sang.

---

Now, here is the part Cajeta never expected. Because Cajeta hadn't helped to get *anything* back. She'd helped because helping was simply what her chewy little heart did.

But kindness, you see, has a funny habit of buzzing back around.

"You found me when I was falling," Colibrí said, hovering before them, her voice strong and clear now. "You carried me when I couldn't fly. You looked when nobody else would bother to look. So now... let me give you the only thing a hummingbird can give. Watch."

And then Colibrí *danced.*

She zigged and she zagged. She drew shimmering figure-eights in the air, faster than thought, trailing glints of green and red and gold. She dipped through sunbeams so the light scattered off her wings into a thousand tiny rainbows that rained gently down over the clearing. She spun a spiral so dazzling that even the Tickle Vines stopped to watch, and the burp-song frog finally hit a beautiful note entirely by accident.

Cajeta bounced in delighted circles. Tico forgot to narrate, which had genuinely never happened before in his life.

"Now *that,*" he finally breathed, "is the best news I have ever, *ever* reported."

When the dance was done, Colibrí settled softly on Cajeta's fuzzy shoulder one last time, warm and humming and full.

"Thank you, friends," she said. "Pura vida."

"Pura vida," Cajeta and Tico said together.

And the whole hidden garden glowed.
