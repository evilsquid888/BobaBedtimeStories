# Story 48: The Cloud Forest Lantern Walk

**Main Character:** Don Café
**Guest Characters:** Slowy the Sloth, Tico the Toucan, Cajeta the Coconut Fudge
**Setting:** A nighttime lantern walk through the misty cloud forest
**Theme:** A little light shared makes the whole forest glow.
**Spanish Spotlight:** **farol** (fah-ROHL) = lantern

---

Now here is something you do not see every evening: a roasted coffee bean climbing a mountain at bedtime, carrying a tiny glowing lantern almost as big as himself.

Don Café paused on the misty trail, leaned on his miniature walking stick, and breathed in the cool night air. He was a dark, glossy bean with a deep crease down his middle, and he wore his little straw farmer's hat tipped just so. Around him the cloud forest sighed and dripped, and a soft gray mist curled between the trees like sleepy cotton.

"Good things brew slowly," he murmured to himself, swinging his **farol** (fah-ROHL) = lantern. Its warm orange flame wobbled, lighting up exactly one mossy step ahead.

"AND HERE COMES THE COFFEE!" shrieked a voice from above. A glossy black bird with an enormous rainbow beak dropped onto a branch, scattering dewdrops. "Beak the news, everybody! The Lantern Walk has officially begun! This reporter is THRILLED!"

"Hello, Tico," said Don Café warmly. "You are loud for a mountain at midnight, hum."

---

It was the night of the great **caminata** (kah-mee-NAH-tah) = walk up the **montaña** (mohn-TAH-nyah) = mountain. Every year, the friends climbed the foggy slope together, each carrying a farol, all the way to a secret clearing at the very top. Don Café had walked it more times than anyone could count, which is why the others had asked him to lead.

There was just one teeny problem.

The mist tonight was thick. Wonderfully, marvelously, ridiculously thick. The kind of mist that makes you wave your hand in front of your face and lose track of where your hand went.

"I cannot even see my own beak," Tico announced, deeply offended. "And my beak is the size of a banana!"

A small round ball of golden coconut fudge came bouncing up the trail, fuzzy with toasted coconut shreds, holding a farol that swung wildly with every hop. "Sweet and round, watch me bound!" she cried. "I'm here! I'm here! Did somebody say WALK? I love walks! Where are we going? How fast can we get there? Can I lead? Ooh, I'll lead!"

"Easy, Cajeta," chuckled Don Café. "Slow steps tonight."

"Slow?" Cajeta froze mid-bounce, scandalized. "I don't think I've ever done a *slow*."

From somewhere far behind them, a long, dreamy voice floated through the fog.

"Puuura... viiida... friends... I am... commming..."

It was Slowy the Sloth, hanging from a low branch, reaching one fuzzy mossy arm toward the next branch at the speed of growing grass. His farol dangled from his toes, glowing gently. "Don't... waaait... for me... I'll catch... uuup... eventually..."

Don Café smiled. He knew "eventually," in Slowy's world, could mean Tuesday.

"Slowy, my friend," Don Café called gently up the trail, "would you like a ride in my pocket? It would be faster, hum."

Slowy considered this for a very, very long time. A leaf drifted down. A snail passed by. Somewhere, a tree grew slightly taller.

"Faster..." Slowy said at last, "...soundssss... stressful. I will... savorrr... the journey."

"Savor the journey," Tico repeated, scribbling on an invisible notepad with his wing. "Ooh, that's good. Beak the news: local sloth invents the slowest hiking advice in history!"

---

They set off up the montaña together, four little lights bobbing in a long misty dark.

But the fog had its own opinions. It pressed in close and damp, swallowing the trail, swallowing the trees, very nearly swallowing Cajeta entirely.

"I can't see the path!" Cajeta wailed, bouncing in a worried little circle. "Don Café, which way? Left? Right? Up? Sideways? Backwards?? I'm getting dizzy! Bouncing while dizzy is NOT recommended!"

Tico flapped up to scout ahead and immediately flew beak-first into a tree.

"BONK," said the tree.

"I MEANT to do that," Tico declared from the ground. "Beak the news: trees are very rude at night."

He tried again, flew with great confidence in completely the wrong direction, and bonked a *second* tree.

"BONK," said that tree, too.

"Two trees," Tico announced, wobbling. "Both rude. The forest is having a very rude evening, and I am reporting it."

Cajeta giggled so hard she rolled into a fern, and the fern dumped a whole night's worth of dew right on top of her.

"SPLOOSH," said the fern.

"Now I'm a *wet* fudge ball," Cajeta sputtered, shaking herself like a tiny fuzzy dog. "Soggy and round, watch me — achoo!"

Don Café held up his single farol and frowned at the wall of gray. Even his light, faithful as it was, could only reach one step ahead. The path forked here — he remembered that much — but which fork led up, and which one wandered off into the soggy nowhere?

"Hmm," he said. "One little light is not enough to see the whole mountain. Not tonight."

Cajeta drooped. "Then how will we ever get to the top?"

---

Don Café did not answer right away. He was an old bean, and old beans know that the best answers come slowly, like coffee through a cloth filter — drip... drip... drip.

He sat down on a velvety stone, took off his straw hat, and listened to the forest. *Drip.* A leaf. *Drip.* Another. And then, faint and far away, he heard it: a soft chorus of little snores.

The sleepy animals of the cloud forest. Hidden all around them in the mist. A nest of dozing hummingbirds. A bundle of yawning frogs. A tree-ful of tucked-in bats hanging like cozy umbrellas. Even a shy little **danta** (DAHN-tah) = tapir, snoozing in the ferns with its funny rubbery nose twitching.

They were everywhere — and all of them, Don Café realized, were a little bit lost in the fog too. Waiting. Sleepy. Hoping for somewhere warm and bright to gather.

He thought of all the long-ago walks, all the mornings he had spent brewing slow and patient on the highland slopes, and of a thing his own grandfather bean had told him once: *the smallest spark, my boy, is never as small as it feels.*

That was when the old coffee bean had his slow, wonderful idea.

"My friends," he said, putting his hat back on, "we have been doing this wrong. We have each been hiding behind our own little farol, trying to light the whole mountain alone." He chuckled. "But a forest this big? It does not need one big light. It needs all of our little ones, *together.*"

He held his farol high.

"Bring your lights close. Right here. Beside mine."

Cajeta bounced over. Tico hopped up, rubbing his bonked beak. And — wonder of wonders — Slowy had *finally* arrived, lowering himself from a branch with the grace of a melting candle.

"I... maaade... it..." he breathed proudly. "Sorry... I'm... earrrly..."

(He was three hours late. Nobody had the heart to tell him.)

Four farols drew together — orange, gold, and warm — and where their small lights touched, something magical happened. The glow doubled. Then it tripled. It pushed back the fog in a great soft golden bubble, and suddenly the friends could see the path, the ferns, the dripping leaves, the whole shining trail winding up the mountain.

"Tuanis," whispered Cajeta, even though she was a fudge ball and that was Coco's word.

"It's like..." Tico said, and for once in his loud life he said it quietly, "...it's like one little lantern was a candle. But four little lanterns? Four little lanterns are a *sunrise.*"

Don Café's eyes crinkled. "Now you are learning, my friend. Beak *that* news, hum."

"BEAK THE NEWS," Tico whispered, which is the strangest and most wonderful thing a toucan can do.

---

But Don Café was not finished, hum.

"Now," he said gently, "we share it."

He looked out at the misty forest where all the sleepy animals dozed. "A little light is a lovely thing to keep. But a little light *shared*..." His eyes twinkled. "...that is how you wake a whole mountain with love."

So they began to walk — slowly, of course, at Slowy's pace, which gave everyone time to enjoy it.

As their joined glow drifted up the trail, it brushed the sleeping animals one by one. And here is the loveliest part: not one of them was frightened. The light was too warm for that. The hummingbirds blinked awake and floated up like tiny living sparks. The frogs hopped out singing a bubbly bedtime tune. The bats unfolded into the air like little waving handkerchiefs. The shy danta lifted her rubbery nose, snuffled happily, and padded along behind.

"BEAK THE NEWS!" Tico crowed, bursting with joy. "The whole forest is following us! This is the biggest story of my LIFE! Possibly the biggest beak of my life too, but that's a different headline!"

Cajeta bounced in delighted loops, her farol swinging. "We made a parade! A sleepy, glowy, snuggly PARADE!"

Up and up they climbed, the line of glowing friends growing longer and brighter with every step, until the whole montaña wore a ribbon of soft light from bottom to top.

Cajeta bounced from animal to animal like a tiny golden welcoming committee. "Hello! Come along! Yes, you with the rubbery nose, you too! Don't be shy, we've got snacks at the top — wait, no we don't, I just like saying that — come ANYWAY!"

The danta gave a sleepy honk that sounded a great deal like a happy yes.

Even Slowy, bringing up the very back of the parade, had collected a small audience. Two baby sloths and a curious moss-green frog were riding gently on his back, perfectly content to travel at the speed of a yawn.

"Plenty... of... rooom..." Slowy told them dreamily. "We will... arrive... when we... arrive..."

---

At last they reached the secret clearing at the very peak.

The mist thinned away to nothing. The clouds had settled below them like a lake of moonlit fluff, and above — oh, above! — the whole sky was *stuffed* with stars, more than anyone could ever count, twinkling so close you felt you could pick one like a berry.

The animals padded and fluttered and hopped into the clearing and curled up together in one big cozy heap. The friends set their four farols in the center, and in that warm shared light, everyone settled down beneath the stars.

"Look at it," sighed Cajeta, her bouncing finally, gently, slowing. "We did that. *All* of us."

Don Café leaned on his walking stick and smiled his slow, kind smile. "Good things brew slowly," he said softly, "and the best light is the one you give away."

Slowy, who had just managed to lie all the way down, gave one enormous, contented sloth-sized yawn.

"Puuura... viiida," he murmured.

And up on that starlit mountaintop, wrapped in mist and friendship and the gentle gold of four little lanterns shining as one, the whole sleepy cloud forest agreed.

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, your light does not need to be big to be beautiful. Hold it gently, the way Don Café holds his farol, and remember: every kind word, every warm hug, every small glow you share makes the whole world a little brighter. Tonight, let your eyes grow soft and your breathing grow slow, like mist settling over the montaña. The stars are watching over you, and somewhere a sloth is *still* yawning. Pura vida, sweet dreamer. Goodnight.
