# Story 34: The Crab Parade on the Shore

**Main Character:** Tato the Patacon
**Guest Characters:** Estrella the Sea Turtle
**Setting:** A sunset beach where bright red crabs march to the sea
**Theme:** Marching to your own beat is wonderful, and so is marching together.
**Spanish Spotlight:** cangrejo (kahn-GREH-hoh) = crab

---

Tato the Patacon went rolling down the beach exactly the way he always rolled — flat, golden, and far too fast to stop politely. He was a twice-fried, smashed green plantain disc with crinkled crispy edges and a soft chewy middle, and right now those edges were collecting a delightful sprinkle of warm sand.

"Smash now, worry never!" he announced, bouncing off a coconut and landing upside down in a tide pool. He sat up, dripping and grinning. "Okay. Maybe worry a *little*."

The sun was melting into the sea like syrup down a granizado, painting the whole shore a sleepy orange. And that's when Tato heard it.

*Click-click-clack. Click-click-clack.*

Hundreds of bright red **cangrejos** (kahn-GREH-hohs) = crabs came marching out of the dunes, every one of them clicking their claws in perfect sideways rhythm, all heading toward the waves.

Tato's crispy little heart did a happy flip. A parade! And he hadn't even had to plan it.

The crabs marched like tiny red soldiers — left, sideways, left, sideways — claws snapping out a beat so cheerful that Tato's flat feet started tapping all on their own. *Clack-clack, snap. Clack-clack, snap.* It was the kind of music that lived in your toes before it reached your ears.

"Beautiful!" Tato cried, flopping along beside them. "What a parade! Where's everybody going?"

A wide green shape lifted slowly out of the foam. It was Estrella the Sea Turtle, her mossy-green star-patterned shell glittering with seawater, her ancient eyes kind and crinkly.

"To the sea, little crisp," Estrella said in her slow, soothing tide-voice. "Every sunset the cangrejos parade down to the water to wish the moon good evening. It is the oldest, happiest tradition on this whole shore." She waved a flipper gently. "Follow the moon home."

"It's the best thing I've ever seen," Tato said, and he meant it with his entire crunchy soul. "Look at them go! All together! All marching! All — "

He stopped.

Because there, half-tucked behind a curl of driftwood, was one very small cangrejo who was not marching at all.

The little crab was no bigger than a button, red as a chili pepper, with two enormous worried eyes and two tiny claws he kept hiding behind his back. Every time the parade clattered past, he peeked out, lifted one little leg as if to step in — and then scuttled right back behind the driftwood, hugging the wood like it was the only safe thing in the world.

"Uh oh," Tato whispered. "A shy one."

He rolled over, very gently, and flopped down beside the driftwood so he wouldn't seem too big or too crispy. "Hola, little cangrejo. Why aren't you marching with everybody?"

The little crab's eyes went even wider. "Be-because," he squeaked, "I can't do it *right*."

"Do what right?"

"The march!" The little crab waved a claw at the parade. "Everybody goes the *same* way. Step, sideways, step, sideways, all together, all matching." He drooped. "But when *I* try, my legs go all wobbly-bobbly and I trip over my own claws and I go the *wrong* way and bump people and — " He hid his face. "So I just stay here. Where I can't ruin it."

Tato felt his soft middle go all warm and squishy with sympathy. He knew exactly what it was like to wobble. He bumped into things every single day! He'd once rolled into a fruit cart, three chickens, and a sleeping Slowy the Sloth, all in one morning.

"Well," said Tato kindly, "what's your name, friend?"

"Cangrejito," the little crab whispered. "It means *little crab*. Because I'm the littlest."

"Cangrejito," Tato repeated. "That is a *tuanis* name. Now — show me your wobble. Go on. March for me. Just me. Nobody else is watching."

Cangrejito gulped. He peeked left, peeked right, and then took a brave little step.

His left legs went one way. His right legs went the *other* way. He spun in a complete circle, tripped over his own claw, and landed *plop* on his round red back, all eight legs wiggling in the air.

"I'm sorry!" he squeaked from upside down. "See? I told you. All wrong."

But Tato was not frowning. Tato was *delighted*.

"Wrong?" he gasped. "Cangrejito, that wasn't wrong — that was the best spin I've ever seen! Do it again!"

"What?"

"The twirl! Right before you flipped! You went *whirl-whirl-PLOP*. It was wonderful!" Tato wobbled to his own flat feet. "Watch — I'll try it!"

And because Tato was a flat round disc and very bad at spinning, he tried to twirl and immediately rolled sideways into a sandcastle, knocking the top off so it landed neatly on his head like a crispy little crown.

Cangrejito blinked. Then a tiny giggle escaped him — *peep-peep-peep* — and once it started it would not stop.

"See?" Tato grinned, sand crown wobbling. "We don't have to march like everyone else. We just have to march like *us*. You don't have a wrong march, Cangrejito. You have your *own* march. And your own march has a twirl in it!"

"My own... march?" Cangrejito flipped himself right-side up. "But everyone else goes step-sideways-step. I go whirl-whirl-plop."

"Then teach your feet the whirl-whirl-plop on *purpose*," said Tato. "If you're going to spin, spin like you *meant* it. Smash now, worry never!"

Estrella drifted closer in the shallows, watching with her gentle, knowing smile. "The wisest creatures in the sea, little ones, are the seahorses," she said softly. "They cannot swim fast or straight like the other fish. They bob and they sway and they go their own slow way. And do you know what? Everyone in the ocean stops to watch them, because nobody is more lovely. *Pura vida.* There is no single right way to move through the world."

Cangrejito looked at the parade. He looked at his own small legs. He took a deep, brave, salty breath.

"Okay," he whispered. "I'll try my own march."

He lifted one claw. He twirled — *whirl* — on purpose this time. He twirled the other way — *whirl* — and instead of flopping over, he popped up on his tiptoes — *plop, but a bouncy plop!* — and did a little hop.

*Whirl-whirl-hop! Whirl-whirl-hop!*

"That's IT!" Tato cheered, bouncing so hard his sand crown flew off. "Look at you go!"

Cangrejito whirled and hopped his way out from behind the driftwood, giggling his *peep-peep-peep* giggle the whole way. He was so busy enjoying his own bouncy beat that he forgot to be afraid — and that was when something marvelous happened.

The big crab parade slowed down.

The marching cangrejos turned their stalk-eyes toward the littlest crab doing his happy whirl-whirl-hop, and one by one, they began to copy him.

A big crab near the front gave a careful twirl. *Whirl.* Then a hop. *Hop!* The crab behind it tried too, and tumbled, and laughed, and tried again. Soon the whole shore was full of bright red cangrejos twirling and hopping and bonking gently into one another and not minding even a little bit, because it was the silliest and most joyful march any beach had ever known.

"They're following ME!" Cangrejito gasped, twirling at the very front now. "They're doing my march!"

"You're leading the parade!" Tato laughed, rolling along beside him, picking up his own happy rhythm — *flop-flop-roll, flop-flop-roll.* Estrella swept her flippers through the foam, splashing a sparkly beat. Even a sleepy little hermit crab came out to clack along.

And here is the truly wonderful part, the part Tato would remember on every sunset for the rest of his crispy life:

It didn't matter anymore who marched which way. Some crabs went sideways. Some crabs twirled. Some hopped. One especially confused crab marched entirely backward and bumped into a starfish, and the starfish didn't mind a bit. They were all going to the same place — down to the sea, to wish the moon good evening — and they were all going there *together*, each in their own bouncy, twirly, sideways, beautiful way.

When they reached the edge of the waves, the whole parade stopped. The moon was rising now, round and silver and soft, laying a shining path across the dark water.

"Follow the moon home," Estrella murmured, and the cangrejos lifted their claws and waved at the moon all at once, hundreds of tiny red claws sparkling in the moonlight.

Cangrejito stood at the very front, no longer hiding, no longer the littlest-and-most-afraid, just the littlest-and-happiest. He looked back at Tato, his enormous eyes shining.

"Thank you," he whispered. "I thought I had to march like everyone. But I just had to march like me. And then... everyone wanted to march like me too."

"Better together, *mae*," Tato said warmly — borrowing the saying from his friends far away. "You marched to your own beat. And your own beat was so happy, it became *everybody's* beat."

The moon climbed higher. The tide whispered in. And one by one, the cangrejos scuttled softly into the cool dark water, twirling and hopping all the way, until the beach was quiet and silver and still.

Tato lay back in the warm sand, his crispy edges full of stars and salt, listening to the last faraway *click-click-clack* of the happiest parade on the shore.

"What a day," he sighed contentedly. "What a *tuanis* day."

And somewhere out in the gentle sea, a tiny red cangrejo was telling the moon all about it — in his own bouncy, twirly, perfectly-himself way.

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, the world will sometimes ask you to march in a straight tidy line, just like everybody else. But your wobbles and your twirls and your own funny beat are some of the most wonderful things about you, and the moon loves to watch you dance. March your own happy march, and you may just find the whole world joining in beside you. Snuggle down now, soft as sand and warm as the last orange light of sunset. The tide is whispering you a lullaby, and the gentle moon is laying a silver path just for your dreams. Follow it home. Goodnight.
