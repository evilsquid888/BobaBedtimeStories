# Story 27: Estrella and the Moonlit Nesting Beach

**Main Character:** Estrella the Sea Turtle
**Guest Characters:** Coco the Granizado
**Setting:** Tortuguero's dark, gentle nesting beach under a full moon
**Theme:** We all find our way home by following a little light.
**Spanish Spotlight:** **tortuga** (tor-TOO-gah) — turtle

---

On the soft black sand of Tortuguero, where the waves whispered like somebody telling a very long, very cozy secret, a hundred tiny eggs began to wiggle all at once.

*Tap-tap. Crick-crick. Pop!*

"Oh my goodness, oh my goodness," gasped a voice from a paper cone bobbing along the dunes. "They're HATCHING, they're hatching, EVERYBODY look —" The voice belonged to Coco the Granizado, a tall fluffy mound of rainbow-syruped shaved ice wearing tiny round sunglasses at night (because Coco believed sunglasses were always a good idea). Powdered milk dusted his rainbow swirls like snow, and he sparkled so brightly you could read a book by him.

"*Pura vida*, little ones," rumbled a deep, gentle, ancient voice. "Take your time. The sea has waited a thousand years for you. It can wait one more minute."

This was Estrella, a great green **tortuga** (tor-TOO-gah) — a turtle — with a mossy shell patterned like a sky full of stars.

---

Estrella was the oldest, kindest turtle on the whole beach. Her shell was the deep green of jungle leaves, and across it spread a scatter of pale starry marks, which is exactly how she got her name. Her eyes were ancient and warm, like two smooth river stones that had watched the moon rise ten thousand times. When she swam, she moved like slow ocean tides. When she spoke, you felt calmer just from listening.

Tonight was the most important night of the year. Tonight, the baby turtles — the *tortuguitas* — were hatching, and they had to cross the wide, wide beach to reach the sea before sunrise.

"It is a long journey for such small flippers," Estrella said softly, watching the sand bubble and burst. "But the babies always know the way. They follow the brightest light on the water — the moon shining on the waves. The moonlight is their map home."

"A MAP made of MOONLIGHT?" Coco zoomed in a slushy circle, leaving a little melty trail. "That is the most *tuanis* thing I have ever heard! That's so cool it gave ME a brain freeze, and I'm literally made of ice!"

The first hatchling poked out of the sand. It was no bigger than Coco's pinky toe (if shaved ice had toes), with sleepy half-closed eyes and damp, floppy little flippers. It blinked up at the enormous world.

"Hello, sweet one," Estrella whispered. "Welcome. Follow the light, and you'll find your way home."

The tiny turtle gazed toward the sea, where the full moon laid a shimmering silver path across the water — a glowing road of light leading straight to the waves.

And then —

The little turtle turned the *wrong way*.

It began crawling up the beach, away from the sea, toward Coco — who was, at that exact moment, sparkling like a disco ball at a firefly party.

"Oh no," said Estrella.

"Oh YES, come to Coco!" Coco beamed, not understanding at all. "Look at you, little buddy, you've got GREAT taste in bright glowy friends —"

"Coco," Estrella said gently. "That is the problem. *You* are very bright."

Coco froze mid-sparkle. A bead of melt dripped off his cone. "...Me?"

More eggs popped open. More baby tortugas wobbled out. And one by one, instead of following the moonlight to the sea, they all turned and began crawling toward the brightest thing on the whole beach.

Toward Coco.

"They think *I'm* the moon," Coco whispered, horrified. He looked at the wave of tiny turtles flopping up the sand toward him, away from the water, away from home. "I'm leading them the wrong way! Estrella, I'm a beautiful, sparkly, terrible MISTAKE!"

---

Now, this is the part where you might worry. But you mustn't, not even a little. Because the baby turtles were not in danger, and Coco was not a villain — he was just a very shiny friend with the *opposite* of a problem. And Estrella, who had seen ten thousand moons, knew exactly what to do.

"Coco," she said, calm as the tide. "You shine so brightly that the babies cannot see the real moon. So we must do something brave."

"Anything!" said Coco.

"You must dim your sparkle."

Coco gulped. Now, you have to understand — sparkling was the thing Coco loved *most* about being Coco. His whole life he'd been the brightest, frostiest, most dazzling treat on any beach. Dimming down felt like asking the sun to whisper.

"But sparkling is my whole *thing*," Coco said quietly. "If I'm not bright, what even am I?"

Estrella looked at him with her warm, ancient eyes. "You are a friend who knows when to shine — and when to let someone smaller shine instead. That is the brightest thing of all, *mae*."

Coco took a deep, slushy breath. He scooped a little powdered milk over his rainbow swirls, dimming his glow. He scooted behind a driftwood log so his sparkle wouldn't leak out. He even tilted his sunglasses down extra low.

The beach went dark and soft and silver.

And there — across the water — the moonlight road blazed brighter than ever, a shimmering silver ribbon stretching all the way to the sea.

The baby turtles stopped. They blinked. And slowly, every single one of them turned around.

Toward the moon. Toward the waves. Toward home.

"They're doing it!" Coco whispered from behind his log. "Estrella, they SEE it now! Look at them GO! Well — look at them *slowly* go, they're very small, but they've got HUSTLE —"

"*Pura vida*," Estrella murmured, her old heart glowing. "Follow the moon home, little ones."

---

But the beach was wide, and baby flippers are slow, and the journey is hard when you are smaller than a cookie. Halfway across the sand, the tiniest tortuga of all — the very last one to hatch — got stuck in a deep footprint a crab had left behind. It scrabbled and flopped, but the walls were too steep.

"*Peep!*" went the tiny turtle. (Which is turtle for "uh-oh.")

Estrella was too big to reach into such a small hole. But Coco — Coco was made of slippery, melty, sloshy shaved ice.

"Hang on, little buddy!" Coco zoomed over and let just a *tiny* bit of his ice melt into the footprint. The cool water filled it up like a little pond, and the baby turtle floated gently to the top and paddled right out.

"*Peep!*" said the turtle. (Which, this time, meant "thank you.")

"You're welcome, you magnificent speck," Coco sniffled, dabbing his sunglasses. "Now go on. Follow the moon. I'm too sparkly to come with you, but my heart is paddling right beside you."

One by one, the tortuguitas reached the edge of the sea. A wave rolled in — soft and warm and frothy — and scooped them up, and carried them out into the big silver dark. Estrella slid in beside them, her great starry shell guiding the little ones through the swells.

"Stay together," she sang to them in her slow tide-voice. "The moon will always show you the way. And no matter how far you swim, no matter how many years pass — *siempre*, always — you will know the way back to this beach. Because home isn't a place you remember. It's a little light you follow."

The last tiny turtle paused at the edge of the foam. It looked back — back at the dark beach, back at the dim sparkly friend hiding behind the log.

"*Peep,*" it said. (Which meant "goodbye, and thank you, glowy one.")

And then the sea took it home.

---

When the beach was empty and the moon was high, Coco crept out from behind his driftwood log. He let himself sparkle again — just a little, then a lot, then his full dazzling rainbow self, brighter than ever.

"I did it," he whispered. "I turned OFF the coolest thing about me so a hundred babies could find the ocean. That's — Estrella, is it weird that turning *down* my shine made me feel like I shined the MOST?"

Estrella floated in the shallows, smiling her ancient smile. "Not weird at all, *mae*. That is the whole secret of the moon. It does not shine for itself. It shines so that lost little ones can find their way home."

Coco looked up at the great round moon, glowing softly over the waves, asking nothing in return.

"Huh," he said. "The moon's pretty *tuanis*."

"Stay frosty," said Estrella, with a twinkle.

"Stay sweet," Coco finished, and the two friends watched the silver sea until the first whisper of morning.
