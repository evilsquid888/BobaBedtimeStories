# Story 39: The Marimba Music Night

**Main Character:** Coco the Granizado
**Guest Characters:** Lala the Tres Leches, Cajeta the Coconut Fudge
**Setting:** A town square dance under string lights with a marimba band
**Theme:** Everyone has a rhythm worth dancing to.
**Spanish Spotlight:** **música** (MOO-see-kah) = music

---

The town square of Pueblo Dulce had gone twinkly. Somebody — probably a firefly committee — had strung a thousand little lights between the mango trees, and now the whole plaza glowed like the inside of a paper lantern. In the middle of it all stood a marimba, that big wooden Costa Rican instrument with rows of polished bars and gourds hanging underneath like sleepy bells. A grasshopper band tuned up. The mallets went *pling-plong-pa-ploom*.

And in walked Coco the Granizado, a tall fluffy mound of rainbow-syruped shaved ice, dusted in powdered milk like fresh snow, wearing his tiny round sunglasses even though it was nighttime.

"Whoa," Coco breathed. "Tuanis. Tonight is gonna be the coolest night ever."

Then the first song started, everybody began to dance, and Coco froze solid as an ice cube.

Because Coco — cool, breezy, frosty Coco — had a teeny tiny secret. He could *not* dance.

---

"What's wrong?" asked Cajeta, bouncing up beside him. She was a little round ball of golden coconut fudge, rolled in toasted shreds so she looked fuzzy, and she could not hold still to save her life. "It's marimba night! It's **música** (MOO-see-kah) = music! It's the best!"

"Yeah, yeah, totally, I love it," said Coco, scooting backward into the shadow of a mango tree. "I'm just gonna, uh, watch. From over here. Where it's nice and dark and nobody can see me."

Cajeta blinked. "But you LOVE music. You hum syrup songs all day."

"That's different," Coco whispered. "Humming I can do. But dancing? In front of *everyone*? Cajeta, look at me." He gestured at his fluffy self with both icy hands. "I'm shaved ice. The second I get embarrassed, I get warm. And the second I get warm —" He gulped. "I *melt*. I'll turn into a puddle right here in the square. A puddle in sunglasses. They'll have to mop me up."

Cajeta gasped a little fudge gasp. "You won't melt."

"I might," said Coco. "I'm very meltable. It's a known fact about me."

Just then there was a soft, dreamy *swoosh*, and Lala the Tres Leches arrived — a squishy pale-gold cake square wobbling with sweet milk, a cloud of whipped cream on top, dripping little milky footprints behind her. She took one look at the marimba and the lights and pressed a hand to her spongey chest.

"Ohhh," she swooned. "The *música*. The lights. It's so beautiful I could cry three milks." She dabbed at her eye with a corner of cream. "Soak it all in!"

"Coco won't dance," reported Cajeta. "He thinks he'll melt of embarrassment."

"I *will* melt of embarrassment," said Coco.

Lala gasped even harder than Cajeta. For Lala, gasping was practically a hobby.

---

"But Coco, dear," Lala said gently, "everyone is dancing. Look."

Coco looked. And, well — she had a point. The whole square was a glorious mess of dancing.

Tato the Patacon, the flat crispy plantain disc, was stomping around so hard that little flakes of salt flew off him with every step. He had absolutely no rhythm. He just stomped. *STOMP. STOMP. STOMP-STOMP-STOMP.* "SMASH NOW, DANCE NEVER WRONG!" he hollered, knocking over a chair.

Flan the coconut custard was wobbling. Just wobbling. That was his whole dance. He jiggled in one spot, his caramel drizzle swaying, going "Steady, steady... ooh, wobbly!" and somehow it looked wonderful.

Don Café, the old roasted coffee bean, was doing a slow, dignified shuffle with his little walking stick, tapping it on the cobblestones in time. *Tap. Tap. Tap.* "Good things groove slowly," he said to nobody.

And way at the back, moving at the speed of a melting glacier, was Slowy the Sloth. He had lifted one fuzzy arm. He was, very slowly, going to lift the other. By next Tuesday, probably. "Puuura... viiida..." he murmured. "I'm... almost... dancing..."

"See?" said Cajeta, hopping in delighted circles. "There's no wrong way to dance! Tato STOMPS. Flan WOBBLES. Don Café does a grandpa-shuffle. Slowy is basically a statue having a lovely time!"

"Everyone has a rhythm worth dancing to," said Lala softly. "Even you, frosty friend. *Especially* you."

Coco peeked over the top of his sunglasses. "But what if mine is bad?"

"Honey," said Lala, "look at Tato. He just danced into a fruit cart."

There was a crash. "I'M OKAY!" yelled Tato, upside down in a pile of guavas. "THE GUAVAS BROKE MY FALL! THE GUAVAS ARE HEROES!"

Coco laughed. He couldn't help it. A tiny laugh leaked out of him, cold and crackly like ice in lemonade.

---

Up bounced Tico the Toucan, his huge rainbow beak clacking, swooping low over the dancers like a flying newspaper. "BEAK THE NEWS! BEAK THE NEWS! Word on the vine is the famous Coco the Granizado — the coolest treat in all of Pueblo Dulce — is hiding behind a *tree*!" He landed right in front of Coco and announced, at the top of his lungs, "IS IT TRUE?!"

"Tico, *shh*," Coco hissed, melting a tiny bit at the edges. "You're making it worse!"

"WHY ARE YOU WHISPERING," Tico whispered, which for a toucan meant only slightly less loud than a foghorn.

This was it. This was the embarrassing moment. Coco felt a warm drip slide down his fluffy side. *Oh no. Oh no, here it comes, I'm gonna be a puddle.*

But then Cajeta did something.

She started to dance right next to him. Not a fancy dance. A goofy one. She bounced and rolled and spun like a fuzzy little marble, going "Sweet and round, watch me bound!" and bonking gently into Coco's icy ankle.

*Bonk.* "Sweet and round —" *bonk* "— watch me bound!" *bonk bonk.*

"Cajeta, what are you doing," Coco said.

"Dancing the only way I know how," she puffed, still bouncing. "Badly! And on purpose! Because nobody cares! Watch — everybody, look at me, I'm a coconut meatball!"

And the whole square cheered for her terrible, wonderful bouncing.

---

That was when Coco understood something.

Nobody in the plaza was dancing *well*. Tato was stomping fruit carts into oblivion. Flan was a nervous pudding earthquake. Slowy had now lifted his second arm and the crowd had genuinely applauded. They weren't laughing *at* anyone. They were laughing *with* everyone, in the big warm way that families laugh, where the laugh is just another word for *I love you, keep going.*

And the marimba kept playing. *Pling-plong-pa-ploom-ploom-PLING.* The música rolled through Coco like a cool river, and his frosty toes started, very quietly, to tap.

"There it is," murmured Don Café, shuffling past with a knowing twinkle. "The ice has found its beat. Good things brew slowly, niño."

Coco took off his sunglasses. (For Coco, this was a *very* big deal. It was basically a declaration.)

He slid out from behind the tree.

And he started to dance.

But here's the thing — Coco didn't stomp like Tato or wobble like Flan. Coco discovered he had his *own* rhythm. He swayed. Smooth and breezy, like a palm tree in a sea wind. He glided across the cobblestones leaving a cool sparkly mist behind him, his powdered-milk snow drifting down like glitter under the string lights. He spun, and rainbow syrup swirled around him in ribbons of red and blue and orange.

"OHHH," gasped the whole crowd at once.

"He's like a snow cone in a *breeze*," whispered Lala, milky eyes shining. "It's so beautiful. Soak it all in!"

Coco swooshed and slid and shimmered. The warmer he danced, the more a little frosty fog rolled off him, and instead of melting away into a puddle, the mist caught the lantern light and made tiny rainbows everywhere. Kids reached up to touch the cold sparkles. The whole square felt like a cool ocean wave on a hot day.

"Stay frosty," Coco called out, gliding past on a wave of his own chill, "stay SWEET!"

"BEAK THE NEWS!" shrieked Tico, doing loops overhead. "COCO IS THE COOLEST DANCER IN COSTA RICA! IT'S OFFICIAL! I'M PUTTING IT ON THE VINE!"

---

By the end of the song, everyone was dancing in their own ridiculous, perfect way. Tato stomped. Flan wobbled. Cajeta bounced like a happy bean. Slowy had begun, with enormous effort, a third arm-lift. Lala swooned in slow milky circles, leaving a ring of cream footprints. And Coco the Granizado swayed in the middle of it all, breezy and glowing and not one bit melted — just a little softer, a little sparklier, exactly as cool as he'd always hoped to be.

"I thought I'd melt," he told Cajeta and Lala when the band paused for water. "But I didn't. I just... found my groove. My own breezy granizado groove."

"That's because you stopped worrying about doing it right," said Cajeta, hopping happily.

"And started doing it *you*," said Lala.

Coco grinned and slid his sunglasses back on, even though it was still nighttime. "Tuanis," he said. "Best night ever. Same time next week?"

And the marimba started up again — *pling-plong-pa-ploom* — and out under a thousand twinkly lights, the coolest little granizado in the whole world danced like a sea breeze, while his friends laughed and stomped and wobbled and bounced beside him, each to their very own beat.

---

## Goodnight Blessing
Sweet dreamer, somewhere out there a marimba is still playing, soft and far away, just for you. May you remember that there is no wrong way to dance, no wrong way to be, and no part of you that needs to hide behind a tree. Sway like a sea breeze. Bounce like a little round bean. Wobble, stomp, or shuffle slow — every rhythm is welcome here. Snuggle in now, soft and frosty-cozy, and let the música carry you gently down to sleep. Goodnight.
