# Story 16: Dan and the Grid of Gold

**Main Character:** Dan (egg waffle)
**Guest Characters:** Mango
**Setting:** A night market stall
**Theme:** Mistakes are how you find the gold
**Word Spotlight:** **gai daan jai** (guy-dahn-jai) — Hong Kong bubble waffle

---

In the buzziest, bounciest corner of the night market — right between the lantern-seller and a noodle stall that sneezed steam — there was a little stall shaped like sunshine. And standing on the counter, pacing in tight worried circles, was **Dan**.

Dan was a **gai daan jai** (guy-dahn-jai) — a Hong Kong bubble waffle, which is the cheeriest thing a cake can be. He was a whole grid of golden bubbles, each one a plump puffed dome the color of toasted butter, all joined together like a sheet of round little hills. When he giggled, his bubbles jiggled. When he paced, he went *pomf-pomf-pomf* like bubble wrap with a deadline.

And tonight, Dan had a deadline.

"I promised," he muttered, pacing faster. "I PROMISED Granny Deng. Her granddaughter Mei-Mei is coming to the night market for the very first time tonight, and I said — out loud, with witnesses — that the moment the lanterns light up, there would be a warm golden waffle cone waiting in her hands. The most golden. The crispiest. The crunch-then-fluffiest."

He stopped pacing and looked up at the sky, where the sun was sliding down behind the rooftops like it had somewhere better to be.

"And the lanterns light when the sun goes down," Dan whispered. "Which is SOON."

---

"You look like a cake having an emergency," said a voice.

The voice belonged to **Mango**, who had just slid up to the stall like a slice of pure summer — golden-orange and glossy, plump and a little drippy at the edges, smelling so sweet that two nearby bees filed a formal complaint about being kept up past their bedtime.

"I AM a cake having an emergency," said Dan. "But a confident one. A professional one. I've made a thousand waffles. I could make one in my sleep. I have, actually. It's how I lost my old apron."

"I came to learn," said Mango, swaying happily. "I heard you make the bubbly grid ones. Teach me!"

"Perfect! You can be my assistant. First — the BATTER!" Dan whirled into action.

He pulled out a big bowl, and together they got to work. In went the flour, and a crack of egg — *plip* — and a spoonful of sugar that Mango definitely did not sneak a taste of (she absolutely did), and a splash of milk, and a whisper of baking powder that Dan called "the bubble magic."

"Now whisk!" said Dan. "Smooth as a golden river — pourable, swirly, no lumps. Lumps are lovely fellows but they ruin a waffle."

Mango whisked. *Swish-swish-swoosh.* The batter swirled from clumpy to creamy, from grumpy to glossy, until it ribboned off the whisk in a shiny stream.

"Now," said Mango, "what's next?"

Dan glanced at the sinking sun. Somewhere deep in his bubbles, a small careful voice said: *the batter should rest now.* And a much louder, much sillier voice said: *NO TIME!*

"Next," Dan announced, "we cook it IMMEDIATELY. Batter naps are for batters with nowhere to be!"

---

Auntie Yu — a kind grown-up with a polka-dot apron, twinkly eyes, and a laugh like a wind chime — was already at the flame, because hot heavy irons are absolutely, positively a grown-up's job. She set the big egg-waffle iron over the fire: two heavy plates covered in little round dents, like an egg carton made of metal.

"Give it a few minutes to get properly hot," said Auntie Yu, "and I'll oil it up, and we'll do the sizzle test—"

"No time for tests!" cried Dan. "Tests are for school! Pour, Auntie Yu! Pour for Mei-Mei!"

Auntie Yu raised one eyebrow the way grown-ups do when they know something is about to become a Learning Experience. But she gave the dents the quickest little wipe of oil — barely a whisper of it — and poured the brand-new, un-napped batter across the barely-warm plates. *Glug-glug-glug.*

"Clamp! Flip! Cook!" commanded Dan, marching back and forth like a tiny golden general.

The iron closed — *clunk* — and flipped, and they waited. Dan vibrated. Mango leaned in. The snail who lived by the lantern post slid over to watch, because nothing this exciting had happened to him since Tuesday.

Then Auntie Yu opened the iron.

*Schlorrrrp.*

Half the waffle stayed on the top plate. The other half stayed on the bottom plate. The middle stretched between them like sad cheese, then tore with a tiny heartbroken *fwip*. What flopped onto the counter was pale as the moon, floppy as a wet sock, and steaming in a way that felt more like a sigh than a celebration.

Nobody said anything. Even the noodle stall stopped sneezing.

"That," said the snail at last, "is the saddest thing I have ever seen, and I once watched it rain on my birthday."

---

Dan stared at the pale, torn, floppy not-gold. His bubbles drooped, one by one, like little lights going out.

"I don't understand," he said quietly. "I've made a thousand waffles. And the one time it matters — the ONE time a little girl is coming — I make... a ghost." He sat down on the counter with a soft *pomf*. "Maybe I'm not a grid of gold. Maybe I'm just... a grid."

Mango looked at her friend. Then she looked at the floppy waffle. Then she picked up the whisk, held it like a magnifying glass, and squinted through the wires.

"Nope," she said. "We are not doing sad. We are doing DETECTIVE. Something went wrong, which means something can go RIGHT. Detective Mango is on the case, and the case smells delicious."

She marched to the iron. "Suspect number one: the IRON. Auntie Yu, was it properly hot?"

"We never checked," said Auntie Yu gently. "There's a trick — you flick one tiny drop of batter on, and if the iron sings *tssss!*, it's ready. We skipped the sizzle test. A too-cool iron can't grab the batter and crisp it. It just... warms it up and lets it sulk. That's where pale-and-floppy comes from."

"AHA," said Mango, scribbling on an imaginary notepad. "Suspect number two: the BATTER. Dan. Look me in the bubbles. Did this batter get its nap?"

Dan's bubbles went pink around the edges. "...No," he admitted. "I skipped the rest. But it's just sitting there! What does sitting DO?"

"Everything," said Auntie Yu. "The batter just had a big whisking workout. Resting lets the flour relax and drink up the milk, and lets the big rowdy bubbles calm down. A rested batter pours smoother, puffs prouder, and crisps better. A rushed batter is like a kid sprinting straight from a trampoline into a school photo."

"Blurry and confused," whispered Dan. "I KNOW that feeling."

"And suspect number three," said Mango, tapping the iron dramatically, "the OIL. That little wipe was not a proper brushing. The dents were thirsty. So when the waffle cooked, it held hands with the metal and would NOT let go."

She snapped the whisk closed like a detective closing a case file. "Three suspects. Three confessions. Cold iron, tired-but-not-rested batter, thirsty plates." She pointed the whisk at Dan. "And one waffle who knew all the steps and skipped them because he was scared of a clock."

Dan blinked. Then, slowly, his bubbles began to re-puff, one by one, like little lights coming back on.

"Detective Mango," he said, "you are juicy AND wise."

"It's basically my whole personality," said Mango.

---

"Then we do it again," said Dan, standing up straight. "Every step. The REAL way. Auntie Yu — heat the iron, properly, both sides. Mango — the batter rests. Starting now."

"And what do we do while it rests?" asked Mango.

"The only sensible thing," said Dan. "The official Egg Waffle Wiggle."

So while the batter napped and the iron warmed, Dan taught Mango the official Egg Waffle Wiggle, which is mostly jiggling in place while humming, and is — experts agree — the silliest dance in the entire night market. They counted the lanterns going up, one by one. The snail attempted the wiggle and pulled a muscle he didn't know he had.

When the batter had rested — smoother now, calmer, glossy as a sleepy river — Auntie Yu brushed every little round dent with a proper slick of oil, shining and generous, so the waffle would slip out smiling.

"Now," she said, "the sizzle test." She flicked one tiny drop of batter onto the iron.

*TSSSSS!* It sang.

"THAT," said Dan, vibrating with delight, "is the sound of GO."

Auntie Yu poured the rested batter across the bottom plate — *glug-glug-glug* — filling every dent to the edges. She pressed the top plate down — *clunk* — squeezing the batter up into the dents to make the bubbles, and FLIPPED the whole iron over so it would cook golden on both sides.

"And now we wait," said Dan, "because rushing is how ghosts are made."

The iron hissed and crackled. The smell rose up — warm sugar, toasted egg, that cozy bakery-morning smell that taps your tummy on the shoulder. The first lantern down the row flickered on. Then another. Then another.

"Mei-Mei's coming!" called Granny Deng from her stall. "She's at the gate!"

Auntie Yu peeked under the iron — *just a peek* — and her whole face went sunny.

"Golden," she said.

"GOLDEN!" everyone shouted, and the lanterns glowed a little brighter just to join in.

---

The iron opened — and the waffle let go with a happy little *pop*, whole and unbroken: a warm golden sheet of bubbles, every dome crisp and puffed and proud, steaming like it had something wonderful to say.

"It's me!" cried Dan, hopping. "It's me, but it WORKED!"

While it was still warm and bendy, Auntie Yu rolled it into a cone, all the golden bubbles curled together like a bouquet of toasted moons — and at that exact moment, a small girl with two pigtails and enormous eyes came running up the lane, lanterns lighting above her head one by one like the night was applauding.

"For you, Mei-Mei," said Dan grandly, as Auntie Yu placed the warm cone in her hands. "One grid of gold. Slightly delayed by a ghost."

Mei-Mei bit the top bubble. *Crunch... then fluff.* Crispy outside. Soft and steamy inside. Her whole face went *mmmf*, exactly the way a face is supposed to.

They shared the rest — a bubble for Mango, a bubble for Granny Deng, a bubble for the snail. And the pale floppy first waffle? They ate that too, torn into cheerful pieces, because even a mistake tastes pretty good when you've learned its secrets. "Floppy," declared the snail, "but full of wisdom."

"You know what I learned tonight?" said Dan softly, as the market hushed and the lanterns swayed. "The steps aren't there to slow you down. They're there to hold the gold IN. The rest, the sizzle, the oil — skip them, and you get a ghost. Keep them, and you get a grid of gold."

"And if you DO get a ghost," said Mango, mango-juice on her chin and stars in her eyes, "you just call a detective."

"Bubble by bubble," said Dan, "case closed."

## How to Make egg waffle 🍽️
*Ask a grown-up to help with anything hot or sharp!*
1. Whisk the batter — flour, egg, sugar, milk, and a little baking powder — until it's smooth, glossy, and pourable, with no lumps.
2. Let the batter REST a little while — don't skip it! Resting lets the flour relax and the bubbles settle, so the waffle puffs prouder and crisps better.
3. Have a grown-up heat the egg-waffle iron on both sides and brush every little round dent with a proper slick of oil so the waffle won't stick.
4. Do the sizzle test: the grown-up flicks one tiny drop of batter on the iron — if it sings *tssss!*, the iron is hot enough. No sizzle, no pour!
5. Have the grown-up pour the rested batter across the iron, filling all the little round dents to the edges.
6. Have the grown-up close the iron and flip it so the waffle cooks golden on both sides — then wait patiently. Rushing makes pale, floppy ghosts.
7. Once it's golden and crisp, have the grown-up open the iron and roll the warm waffle into a cone.
8. Let it cool just a moment, then bite the top — crunch, then fluff — and share your grid of gold!
