# Story 16: Dan and the Grid of Gold

**Main Character:** Dan (egg waffle)
**Guest Characters:** Mango
**Setting:** A night market stall
**Theme:** Bring joy
**Word Spotlight:** 雞蛋糕 (jidangao) = egg waffle

---

In the buzziest, bounciest corner of the night market — right between the lantern-seller and a noodle stall that sneezed steam — there was a little stall shaped like sunshine. And standing on the counter, wobbling with excitement, was **Dan**.

Dan was a **雞蛋糕** (jidangao) = egg waffle, which is the cheeriest thing a cake can be. He was a whole grid of golden bubbles, each one a plump puffed dome the color of toasted butter, all joined together like a sheet of round little hills. When he giggled, his bubbles jiggled. When he hopped, he went *pomf-pomf-pomf* like bubble wrap that learned to dance.

"Today," Dan announced, doing a tiny twirl that rattled every bubble, "we are making JOY. The kind you can eat. The kind that goes crunch-then-fluff."

"You mean cake," said a voice.

"I mean *joy-shaped* cake," said Dan. "Same thing. Mostly."

---

The voice belonged to **Mango**, who had just slid up to the stall like a slice of pure summer. She was golden-orange and glossy, plump and a little drippy at the edges, smelling so sweet that two nearby bees filed a formal complaint about being kept up past their bedtime.

"I heard you make the bubbly cakes," Mango said, swaying. "The wiggly grid ones. I want to learn. I want to make joy too."

"Then you've come to the perfect stall!" Dan beamed. "First rule of egg waffles: you cannot make them in a hurry and you cannot make them in a grump. They only come out right if you're a little bit happy already."

"I'm extremely happy," said Mango. "I'm a mango. It's basically my whole personality."

"Then let's begin," said Dan, "with the BATTER."

He pulled out a big bowl, and together they got to work. In went the flour, and a crack of egg — *plip* — and a spoonful of sugar that Mango definitely did not sneak a taste of (she absolutely did), and a splash of milk, and a whisper of baking powder that Dan called "the bubble magic."

"Now we whisk," said Dan, handing Mango a whisk nearly as tall as she was. "Whisk it smooth. We want a batter like a golden river — pourable, swirly, no lumps allowed. Lumps are lovely fellows but they ruin a waffle."

Mango whisked. *Swish-swish-swoosh.* The batter swirled from clumpy to creamy, from grumpy to glossy, until it ribboned off the whisk in a smooth shiny stream.

"Ooooh," said Mango. "It looks like liquid sunshine."

"That," said Dan proudly, "is because it basically is."

---

"Now for the secret nobody tells you," Dan said, lowering his voice like he was sharing treasure. "We let the batter REST."

Mango blinked. "Rest? But I want to make it NOW."

"I know, I know." Dan patted her gently with a bubble. "But the batter just had a big workout. All that whisking! It's puffed and bubbly and it needs a little sit-down so the bubbles settle and the flour relaxes. A rested batter makes a crispier, fluffier waffle. Good things wait."

"That sounds suspiciously like a nap," said Mango.

"It is exactly a nap," said Dan. "Batter naps are real and they are important."

So they let the batter rest, and to pass the time Dan taught Mango the official Egg Waffle Wiggle, which is mostly jiggling in place while humming, and is — experts agree — the silliest dance in the entire night market. A passing snail watched the whole thing and decided his evening had been made.

---

Now came the part that made Dan stand up very straight and go a little bit serious in a warm, twinkly way.

"Mango," he said. "The next bit is HOT. We use a special iron — two heavy plates covered in little round dents, like an egg carton made of metal. It gets blazing hot. And blazing-hot things are absolutely, positively a grown-up's job."

"A grown-up's job," Mango repeated solemnly.

"So we find a wonderful one and we cheer them on," said Dan. "Cheering is OUR job. We are very good at it."

And right on cue, up stepped Auntie Yu — a kind grown-up with a polka-dot apron, twinkly eyes, and a laugh like a wind chime. She set the heavy egg-waffle iron over the flame and warmed it on both sides, brushing the little round dents with a slick of oil so the waffle would slip out smiling.

"Is it ready?" asked Mango, bouncing.

Auntie Yu flicked one tiny drop of batter onto the iron. *Tsssss!* It sang.

"That sizzle," said Dan, vibrating with delight, "means GO."

Auntie Yu poured the rested batter across the bottom plate — *glug-glug-glug* — filling every little round dent, all the way to the edges. The batter pooled and bubbled, and you could practically hear it deciding to become wonderful.

"Now the magic," whispered Dan. "Watch."

Auntie Yu pressed the top plate down — *clunk* — squishing the two halves together so the batter squeezed up into the dents and made the bubbles. Then, carefully, with two firm hands, she FLIPPED the whole iron over so it could cook golden on both sides.

"WHOOSH!" cheered Mango, who had never seen anything flip so grandly in her whole juicy life. "Do it again!"

"After it cooks," Dan laughed. "We can't rush the gold."

---

And so they waited, which — as every waffle-maker knows — is the hardest and most delicious part.

The iron hissed and crackled. The whole stall filled with a smell so good that the noodle stall stopped sneezing just to sniff it: warm sugar, toasted egg, and that cozy bakery-morning scent that makes your tummy tap you on the shoulder.

"Patience," said Dan, doing his little wiggle-hum. "Rush it and the bubbles come out pale and floppy. Wait for it and they come out golden and crisp on the outside, soft and steamy in the middle. The waiting IS the recipe."

"It smells like joy," said Mango quietly. "It actually smells like joy."

"That's how you know it's working," said Dan.

Auntie Yu peeked under the iron — *just a peek* — and her whole face went sunny.

"Golden," she said.

"GOLDEN!" everyone shouted, and a lantern down the row glowed a little brighter just to join in.

---

Auntie Yu opened the iron, and out came the most beautiful thing in the whole night market: a warm, golden sheet of bubbles, each one a crisp little dome, all puffed and proud and steaming.

"It's me!" cried Dan, hopping. "Well — it's me's cousin! Brand new! Fresh from the iron!"

"It's gorgeous," breathed Mango.

"And now," said Dan, eyes sparkling, "the very last step. The most important one of all." He paused dramatically. "We FOLD it into a cone — and then we GOBBLE it."

While the waffle was still warm and bendy, Auntie Yu rolled it up into a cozy cone shape, all the golden bubbles curled together like a bouquet of toasted moons. She handed it to Mango first.

"For the new waffle-maker," she said.

Mango held the warm cone in both hands like it was the most precious thing ever made. She took a bite right off the top — *crunch... then fluff.* Crispy outside. Soft and steamy inside. Warm all the way down.

"MMMF!" said Mango, exactly the way one is supposed to. "It's — it's CRUNCHY and then it's CLOUDY!"

"Crunch-then-fluff!" Dan cheered. "I TOLD you! That's the joy! That's the whole entire point!"

---

So they shared the bubbly grid of gold — a bubble for Mango, a bubble for Auntie Yu, a bubble for the lantern-seller, and one for the snail, who declared it the finest night of his slow and gentle life.

Dan looked around at his cozy little stall — the warm iron, the polka-dot apron, the swinging lanterns, his sticky-cheeked new friend grinning the biggest grin in the market.

"You know," he said softly, "I always thought egg waffles were about being crispy. But that's not really it." He patted Mango with a warm bubble. "They're about *bringing joy*. You make them with a friend, you fold them up warm, and you watch a face go *mmmf*. That's the real recipe."

"Then we made a LOT of joy tonight," said Mango, mango-juice on her chin and stars in her eyes.

"The whole grid of it," said Dan. "Golden, bubble by bubble."

## How to Make egg waffle 🍽️
*Ask a grown-up to help with anything hot or sharp!*
1. Whisk the batter — flour, egg, sugar, milk, and a little baking powder — until it's smooth, glossy, and pourable.
2. Let the batter rest a little while so the bubbles settle and the waffle comes out crispier.
3. Have a grown-up brush the egg-waffle iron with oil and heat it until a drop of batter sizzles.
4. Have the grown-up pour the rested batter across the iron, filling all the little round dents.
5. Have the grown-up close the iron and flip it so the waffle cooks golden on both sides.
6. Once it's golden and crisp, have the grown-up open the iron and roll the warm waffle into a cone.
7. Let it cool just a moment, then bite the top — crunch, then fluff — and gobble your grid of gold!
