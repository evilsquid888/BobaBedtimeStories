# Story 5: Niu Niu and the Warming Bowl

**Main Character:** Niu Niu (beef noodle soup)
**Guest Characters:** Cong Cong
**Setting:** A rainy noodle shop
**Theme:** Warmth is doubled
**Word Spotlight:** 牛肉麵 (niurou mian) = beef noodle soup

---

On the rainiest night of the whole rainy season, when the sky was wringing itself out like a soggy dishrag, a tiny noodle shop glowed on the corner like a hug with windows. Inside, in the steamiest, slurpiest, snuggliest bowl you ever saw, lived Niu Niu.

Niu Niu was a little spirit made of beef noodle soup, and he looked exactly as delicious as that sounds. His tummy was a warm brown broth that wobbled when he laughed. Springy golden noodles curled around his shoulders like a cozy scarf, and a chunk of tender beef sat on his head like the world's most relaxed hat. When Niu Niu got excited, steam puffed out of his ears — *poof, poof!* — which was often, because Niu Niu got excited about almost everything.

"Listen to that rain!" Niu Niu cheered, doing a little wobble that sloshed broth onto his slippers. "It's PERFECT soup weather. The only thing better than soup is soup *plus a friend.*"

Just then, the door went *ding-a-ling*, and in stumbled the wettest traveler in Taiwan.

---

The traveler was a long green onion with a damp, droopy top and the saddest little shiver you ever heard. He was so wet he squeaked when he walked.

"H-h-hello," the onion chattered. "My n-name is **葱葱** (cōng cōng) = scallion. I have walked a VERY long way, and I am c-c-cold all the way down to my roots."

"Oh, you poor noodle-less noodle!" gasped Niu Niu, steam shooting from both ears at once — *POOF POOF!* "You came to the right bowl. Tonight I am making **牛肉麵** (niúròu miàn) = beef noodle soup, and it is the warmest thing in the whole wide world. Stay! Help me cook! We'll be warm together!"

Cong Cong's droopy top perked up just a smidge. "Together?"

"Together," said Niu Niu, "is the secret ingredient. You'll see."

He led Cong Cong into the little kitchen, where a great big pot waited on the stove like a sleepy metal hippo. And here Niu Niu raised one noodly finger and made his Very Serious Face — which, on a bowl of soup, looked extremely silly.

"First rule of the kitchen," he announced. "Anything HOT or SHARP, we ask Auntie to help! She runs the shop, she has oven mitts the size of pillows, and she has done this a million times."

"AUNTIE!" called Niu Niu.

"Coming, dumpling!" boomed a warm voice, and in swept Auntie, a round and rosy grown-up with a ladle tucked behind her ear like a pencil. "Ooh, a guest! Let's make him a bowl that warms the night. Where do we begin?"

"At the beginning," said Niu Niu, "which is the BEEF."

**Step one: brown the beef and simmer it soft.** Auntie set a heavy pot on the stove — the hot part, so that was her job. In went chunks of beef, sizzling and spitting like a tiny rainstorm of their own. *Tssssss!*

"That's the beef saying hello," whispered Niu Niu. Cong Cong giggled, which was the first warm sound he'd made all night.

Auntie tumbled the beef until every side turned a deep, toasty brown. Then came the smelly-good parade. "Cong Cong, you can pass me the bits that aren't hot," she said, and oh, did Cong Cong love a job.

He passed her fat coins of **ginger** that smelled spicy and sunny. He passed her a splash of dark, salty **soy sauce** that glugged out like liquid midnight. He passed her a fat spoonful of brown **bean paste**, which looked a little like a mud pie and made everybody go "ooooh." Then Auntie poured in enough **water** to cover everything like a cozy blanket, and finally — *plop!* — one squashy red **tomato**, because, said Niu Niu, "a tomato makes the broth taste like a sunset."

"A SUNSET?" said Cong Cong. "In a SOUP?"

"Trust the soup," said Niu Niu, very wisely, while a noodle slid off his head.

Auntie put the lid on and turned the heat down low, low, low. And then she said the hardest word in all of cooking.

"Now," she said, "we wait."

Cong Cong's top drooped again. "Wait? But I'm cold NOW."

Niu Niu wobbled over and wrapped a warm noodle around the shivery onion like an arm. "That's the magic part," he said softly. "Beef noodle soup can't be rushed. The beef has to simmer and simmer until it's so tender it practically melts. While it cooks, the broth gets browner and richer and *braver.* And while we wait..." his eyes twinkled, "...we tell jokes."

So they did. They told jokes while the rain drummed on the roof and the pot went *blub, blub, blub.* Niu Niu told a joke so bad — "Why did the noodle blush? Because it saw the soup-er star!" — that Cong Cong laughed so hard his droopy top stood straight up. Then Cong Cong told one back: "What do you call a scallion who tells secrets? A *blab*-bage!" It wasn't very good either, but that only made them laugh harder, the way the silliest jokes always do between brand-new friends.

Every so often, Auntie lifted the lid just a crack to peek inside. Each time, a great fragrant cloud rolled out, and the broth was a deeper, browner, braver color than before — first the gold of weak tea, then the amber of honey, then a rich shiny mahogany that smelled like a hundred good dinners stacked on top of each other.

"How does it know when it's ready?" asked Cong Cong, his nose practically pressed to the pot.

"You poke the beef," said Niu Niu. "When it's so tender it falls apart at a nudge — like *that* — " Auntie gave a chunk the gentlest tap with her spoon and it tumbled into soft, juicy shreds — "then the soup is ready to share."

And slowly, breath by breath and *blub* by *blub*, the kitchen filled with the most wonderful smell in the universe. Warm. Beefy. A little sweet. A little like home.

By the time the beef was meltingly, fall-aparty tender, Cong Cong had stopped squeaking entirely. "I'm not cold anymore," he said, surprised. "And we haven't even eaten yet!"

"That," beamed Niu Niu, "is because warmth is doubled. The soup warmed the room, and the *company* warmed the rest." *Poof* went his happy ear-steam.

"Almost done, my darlings!" sang Auntie. "Time for the last bits!"

**Step two: greens, noodles, and the great big slurp.** First, Auntie dropped a handful of crisp green **bok choy** into the hot broth, where it went bright and tender in a wink. Then she set a fresh pot of bubbling water boiling, and in went the **noodles**, swimming and twirling like happy golden eels.

"Those are my cousins!" cried Niu Niu proudly. "Swim, cousins, swim!"

When the noodles were soft and slurpable, Auntie scooped them into two big bowls. Then — and this was the very best part — she **ladled** the rich brown broth right over the top, beef and bok choy and all, so the steam rose up in a great happy cloud.

Cong Cong stared at his bowl. His own reflection wobbled in the broth, looking warm and rosy and not one bit droopy.

"Now the most important step of ALL," said Niu Niu, leaning in.

"Wash my hands?" guessed Cong Cong.

"You already did. The *most* important step," said Niu Niu, lifting his bowl with both hands, "is the **slurp.** You have to slurp. It's the rule. Slurping means *thank you* in soup language. Ready? One, two, three — "

"*SLLLLUUUURRRRP!*"

The two of them slurped so loudly that Auntie laughed and a noodle flew clear off Cong Cong's spoon and landed — *splat* — right on his head, exactly like Niu Niu's beef hat.

"Look!" cheered Niu Niu. "Now you're soup too!"

---

Outside, the rain kept falling, soft and silver, drumming its sleepy song on the roof. But inside the little shop on the corner, two friends sat shoulder to shoulder, warm all the way down to their roots, sharing bowl after bowl of 牛肉麵 until the pot was empty and their tummies were full and their cheeks were pink as lanterns.

Auntie sat down too, with her own steaming bowl, and the three of them watched the rain together, slurping happily, in no hurry to go anywhere at all.

"Thank you, Niu Niu," yawned Cong Cong, his top finally dry and standing tall. "I came in cold and lonely. Now I'm warm and... *un*lonely."

"That's how it works," said Niu Niu sleepily, one noodle slipping over one eye. "A bowl of soup warms one belly. But a bowl of soup *shared* — that warms the whole rainy night."

And the little shop glowed on the corner, like a hug with windows, until morning.

## How to Make beef noodle soup 🍽️
*Ask a grown-up to help with anything hot or sharp!*
1. Ask a grown-up to brown chunks of beef in a pot, then simmer them low and slow with ginger, soy sauce, bean paste, water, and a tomato until the beef is super tender (this takes a while — tell jokes while you wait!).
2. Drop in some bok choy to cook, boil your noodles in a separate pot until soft, then ladle the hot broth, beef, and greens over the noodles in a bowl — and slurp!
