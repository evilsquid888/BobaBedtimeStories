# Story 30: The Grand Good-Night Kitchen

**Main Character:** Boba with the whole gang
**Guest Characters:** Everyone
**Setting:** A hotel kitchen
**Theme:** Made together
**Word Spotlight:** 辦桌 (ban zhuo) = a big shared banquet

---

Boba had never seen a kitchen so big. It was the kitchen of the grandest hotel in all of Taiwan, and it gleamed from floor to ceiling like the inside of a very tidy moon. There were silver counters longer than dragons, stoves with more knobs than a piano, and a clock on the wall that said it was nearly bedtime.

"Friends," Boba announced, bouncing onto a shiny counter with a *boing*, "tonight we are doing the most enormous thing we have ever done in our whole entire round little lives."

The whole gang leaned in. Bao Bao the pale-gold pillowy bun. Hong Hong with his crackly glass-sugar fruits. Xia Xia the whiskery blue-grey prawn in his fishing vest. Mala Malasada, golden and poofy with sugar. Serene jade Maccha-chan. Tall Gan Zhe with his leafy hair. Soft-glowing Deng Deng the lantern. And neat little Sa Sa the rice pillow.

"We," said Boba, "are throwing a **辦桌** (ban zhuo) = a big shared banquet. A whole feast! Made by all of us, together, before we say goodnight."

Everybody cheered so loudly that a stack of bowls did a tiny nervous jingle, and a single spoon fell off the counter and clattered to the floor with a *DING* so polite it almost sounded like applause.

"A banquet?" gasped Sa Sa, the neat little rice pillow. "An *enormous* one? But there are so many of us, and we all like such different snacks!"

"That," said Boba, beaming, "is exactly the point."

---

"Now here's the plan," said Boba, unrolling a napkin she had drawn all over with crayon. It was, frankly, a terrible drawing. There were squiggles and arrows and one thing that might have been a dumpling or possibly a sleepy potato. "It's too much food for one cook. Even a very bouncy one. So we split into *stations*. Every friend makes one thing they love best. Then" — she paused dramatically, the way you pause before the very best part of a story — "we carry it ALL to one big table."

"A whole *辦桌*," sighed Deng Deng, glowing a little brighter, the way he did when he was happy. "Made by everyone."

"Stations, ready!" Boba clapped her round little hands together, which for a tapioca pearl mostly meant bonking herself gently on both sides. "GO!"

The kitchen burst into the busiest, bounciest racket you ever heard. It sounded like a hundred happy spoons having a parade.

**At the drink station,** Boba and Maccha-chan got to work on the milk tea. A Grown-Up — who appeared the moment snacks were mentioned, the way Grown-Ups magically do — brewed a big pot of strong, dark black tea, holding the steamy kettle up high and away from small wobbly friends. "**熱** (rè) = hot," the Grown-Up reminded them, and Boba and Maccha-chan cheered from a safe and sensible distance, which is exactly where you cheer from when something steams. Once the tea had cooled, they stirred in creamy milk and a splash of sweet syrup, poured it over a clatter of ice, and shook it up — *shaka-shaka-BOING!* "A drink that isn't shaken," said Boba wisely, "is just a bunch of strangers standing in a cup."

**At the dumpling station,** Bao Bao took charge, because nobody understands dough like a baker. He showed Sa Sa how to spoon a little blob of filling into the middle of each round wrapper, dab the edge with water, fold it into a half-moon, and pinch tidy little pleats along the top. "Good things rise with time," Bao Bao hummed, "and good dumplings get pinched with patience." Sa Sa pinched away happily. "Pack it neat," she agreed, "share it sweet." When the dumplings were plump and pleated, the Grown-Up dropped them into a pot of bubbling water — *that* part was hot, so the friends only watched and waved — until they floated up to the top like tiny sleepy boats.

**At the tangyuan station,** Gan Zhe and Hong Hong rolled the little rice-flour balls. They mixed glutinous rice flour with warm water into a soft, squishy dough, then pinched off pieces and rolled them between their palms — *roll, roll, roll* — into perfect smooth marbles. "Squeeze the day," drawled Gan Zhe, rolling slow and easy. Hong Hong rolled fast and sparkly and made his fruit-on-a-stick jingle. The Grown-Up slipped the little balls into a sweet, gingery soup, and up they bobbed, soft and round and chewy.

**At the waffle station,** Mala Malasada was having the time of her life, which is to say she was covered head to toe in batter. She whisked flour, egg, sugar, and milk into a smooth golden batter, then the Grown-Up poured it into a hot waffle iron — *sizzle!* — and closed the lid. "Roll me in sugar!" Mala bounced. "Oh wait, that's me. Roll the WAFFLES in sugar!" When the lid lifted, out came a bubbly grid of gold, crisp and warm and dimpled with little square pockets just right for catching syrup.

**At the mochi station,** Xia Xia the prawn guided the pounding with his patient fishing wisdom. They steamed the sweet rice dough until it was soft and stretchy, and then — *thump, thump, thump* — pounded it gentle and rhythmic until it turned pillowy and bouncy. "Patience reels in the good stuff," said Xia Xia, his whiskers wobbling with each thump. They dusted the mochi in powder so it wouldn't stick, then pinched it into soft little clouds.

**At the shaved-ice station,** Maccha-chan zipped over to help once the milk tea was done. The Grown-Up cranked a big block of ice through a whirring machine — *shhhhhk!* — and out fluffed a snowy mountain of shaved ice, light as a whisper. "Whisk slow, froth grows," murmured Maccha-chan, dusting bright green matcha powder over the top like jade-colored snow. They piled on syrup and fruit until it looked like a tiny edible mountain wearing a party hat.

And all the while, Deng Deng the lantern floated softly above the whole busy kitchen, glowing warm and golden so everyone could see what they were doing. "A little light glows warmest," he said shyly, lighting up every station at once.

By the time the big clock ticked toward sleepy o'clock, the kitchen smelled like sugar and ginger and toasty waffles and a hundred good ideas. And there it all was: milk tea, dumplings, tangyuan, waffles, mochi, and a snowy mountain of shaved ice.

"NOW," cried Boba, "the most important step of the whole *辦桌*. We carry every single dish to ONE big table!"

So they did. It was a glorious, wobbly parade. Bao Bao balanced the dumplings. Sa Sa packed the mochi neat. Gan Zhe carried the tangyuan slow and steady so not a drop sloshed. Hong Hong sparkled along with the waffles. Xia Xia steadied the shaved ice. Maccha-chan and Boba floated the milk tea between them. And Deng Deng glowed above, lighting the way to the great long table in the middle of the grand kitchen.

They set every dish down, one beside the other, until the table was so full and so beautiful that everyone went quiet for one happy second. The milk tea glistened. The dumplings steamed. The tangyuan bobbed. The waffles gleamed. The mochi puffed. And the shaved-ice mountain wore its little party hat of green snow, very proud of itself.

"It's perfect," whispered Coconut Jelly, who had wobbled in just in time and nearly tipped over from sheer happiness. Even a few of Penguinsnowboard's penguins poked their beaks around the door — Piper, Barnaby, Willa, and Finnegan — and Uncle Piggle Wiggle and Timmy squeezed in too, sniffing the air.

"Everyone gather round," said Boba softly. She raised her little round self up tall. "On the count of three, we say goodnight. Ready? One... two... three..."

"**晚安** (wǎn'ān) = goodnight!" they all called together, and the whole kitchen rang with it.

Then they shared. Oh, how they shared! Dumplings passed left, tangyuan passed right, mochi pillows handed round, waffles broken into friendly pieces, milk tea slurped, shaved ice scooped until everyone wore a tiny snowy mustache. Nobody grabbed and nobody hurried. They just sat together, all the friends from all the universes, sharing the feast they had made with their own little hands.

And Boba, slurping a pearl up her straw with a happy *bloop*, looked round at every glowing, giggling face and thought it was the very best thing she had ever helped make. Not because it was the biggest *辦桌* in the grandest kitchen.

But because every single bit of it had been made together.

## How to Make a whole shared feast 🍽️
*Ask a grown-up to help with anything hot or sharp!*
1. Split into teams, each at its own station, so every friend makes one dish.
2. Make milk tea: brew strong black tea, let it cool, add milk and syrup, then pour over ice and shake.
3. Make dumplings: fill each wrapper, fold into a half-moon, pinch the pleats, and have a grown-up boil them until they float.
4. Make tangyuan: mix rice flour with warm water, roll into little balls, and have a grown-up cook them in sweet soup until they bob up.
5. Make waffles: whisk batter and have a grown-up pour it into a hot waffle iron until golden.
6. Make mochi: steam the sweet rice dough, pound it soft, dust with powder, and pinch into little pillows.
7. Make shaved ice: have a grown-up shave a block of ice into a fluffy mountain, then top with syrup and fruit.
8. Carry every dish to one big table, say *wǎn'ān* (goodnight) together, and share it all!

## Goodnight Blessing
Sleep now, little one, snug as a table full of friends. May your dreams be made the very best way — together, with all the people you love close by. The dumplings are quiet, the lanterns are dim, and the great kitchen rests at last. You made something wonderful today, and tomorrow holds even more. Close your sleepy eyes, dear heart. Goodnight.
