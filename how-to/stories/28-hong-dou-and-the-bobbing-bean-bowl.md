# Story 28: Hong Dou and the Bean Bowl

**Main Character:** Hong Dou (red bean)
**Guest Characters:** Cao
**Setting:** A cozy kitchen
**Theme:** Needs sweetness
**Word Spotlight:** 紅豆湯 (hongdou tang) = sweet red bean soup

---

Hong Dou was the roundest, reddest little bean in all the kitchen, and she had a teeny problem: she was not sweet yet.

"I'm a red bean," she announced to the soup pot, hopping up and down on the cutting board so her shiny skin caught the lamplight. "Red beans are SUPPOSED to be sweet! But I taste like a damp pebble. A nice damp pebble! But still a pebble."

From the cool dark fridge, a wobbly voice giggled. "A pebble can become a poem," said Cao, sliding out onto the counter. Cao was a slab of grass jelly — glossy, jiggly, the deep brown-black of midnight, and so wobbly that just talking made her shiver like jelly on a train. "I'm jiggly and plain too. But tonight, Hong Dou, we are going to be delicious together."

Hong Dou gasped so hard she nearly rolled off the board. "TOGETHER? Doing WHAT?"

Cao wobbled grandly. "We," she said, "are making **紅豆湯** (hóngdòu tāng) = sweet red bean soup. With grass jelly. Me."

---

Now, you must understand: Hong Dou was not just one bean. She was a whole bright bowlful of red beans, all rolling and bumping and cheering at once, like a tiny stadium of happy marbles. So when Cao said *sweet red bean soup*, the entire bowlful went, "OOOOOH!"

"But I don't FEEL sweet," Hong Dou worried, doing a nervous little spin. "What if I'm a pebble forever?"

"Sweetness," said Cao wisely, "is something you grow into. Step by step. Starting with a bath."

"A BATH?" The beans clattered. "We just had a bath last week!"

"Not a *clean* bath," Cao laughed, her whole body jiggling. "A **soak**. Beans are stubborn little stones until you soak them. Hop into this bowl of water and rest."

So Hong Dou and all her bean cousins cannonballed into a big bowl of cool water — *splish, splash, sploosh* — sending up a fountain that made Cao squeal and wobble out of the way.

"Now we wait," said Cao.

"For how LONG?" groaned Hong Dou, already bored, floating on her back.

"A good long while. Hours and hours. Maybe even a whole sleepy night. You'll drink up the water and get nice and plump and soft."

The beans grumbled, but slowly — *gloop, gloop, gloop* — they began to puff up, fatter and rounder, until Hong Dou was twice her grumpy little size and felt squishy as a tiny pillow.

"Look at me!" she cried, jiggling almost as much as Cao. "I'm POOFY!"

"You're getting there," Cao beamed. "Good things rise with time. Wait — that's Bao Bao's line. Borrowed it. Don't tell him."

When morning poured in golden through the window, the beans were plump and ready. And now came the part Hong Dou had been warned about.

"This next bit is HOT," said Cao very seriously, pointing one wobbly arm at the stove, where a tall pot waited. "Hot enough to turn a bean into bean soup. Which is the goal! But also hot enough to say *ouch.* So here is the most important rule of the whole kitchen."

Hong Dou leaned in. The whole bowlful of beans leaned in.

"We ask a grown-up," Cao whispered, like it was a magic spell.

"We ask a grown-up!" the beans echoed.

And right on cue, in shuffled a big warm pair of slippers — a real live grown-up, yawning and reaching for the kettle. Cao did a polite little curtsy-wobble. "Excuse me, kind grown-up! Could you please boil these beans? We're far too small and far too splashy to handle the hot bits."

The grown-up smiled (grown-ups always smile at jiggly desserts) and tipped Hong Dou and all her cousins into the pot with fresh water. Then *click* went the stove, and *whoosh* went the flame, and *blubble-blubble-BLUB* went the water as it came to a roaring boil.

"WHEEEE!" the beans bobbed and spun in the bubbling water like it was the world's tiniest hot spring. "This is the best swimming pool EVER!"

"Now, grown-up," Cao called up, "turn it low to a gentle simmer, please — so it goes *blip... blip... blip* instead of *BLUB.* Slow and patient is how beans get soft. Patience reels in the good stuff — wait, that's Xia Xia's line. Hmm. I really do borrow a lot."

So the grown-up turned the flame down low, and the pot bubbled gently — *blip, blip, blip* — and the beans simmered and softened, simmered and softened, until even tough little Hong Dou felt as squishy-soft as a marshmallow in pajamas.

"How do we know when they're done?" asked Cao, peering up (carefully, from a safe and not-hot distance).

The grown-up scooped one bean out, let it cool, and gave it a gentle squish. It smooshed like soft butter.

"SOFT!" cheered Hong Dou from inside the pot, doing a wobbly victory bob. "I'm officially squishable!"

"Then it's time," said Cao, "for the sweet part."

The grown-up spooned in **糖** (táng) = sugar — a generous, glittering snowfall of it — and stirred it round and round until it melted into the soup.

And oh — OH — something wonderful happened to Hong Dou.

"I feel..." she gasped, swirling in the warm sugary soup, "I feel SWEET! It's INSIDE me now! I'm not a pebble — I'm a *poem!* Cao, you were RIGHT!"

"Told you," Cao said softly, wobbling with joy. "Sweetness grows into you when you give it time."

Then the grown-up did one last cozy trick: with the back of the spoon, they gently mashed *just a few* of the beans against the side of the pot — *squish, squish, squish* — so the soup turned thick and velvety and creamy, with whole plump beans still bobbing through it like little swimmers.

"You mashed my cousins!" Hong Dou cried — but she was giggling, because the mashed ones were giggling too, all blended cozy together. "Now we're soup-AND-beans! Lumpy and smooth! The best of both!"

The kitchen smelled like warm sugar and sleepy comfort. The soup was done.

But not the *bowl.*

"My turn," wobbled Cao, scooting bravely to the cutting board. "But careful — this needs the knife. And a knife is sharp, which is a *grown-up job* if ever there was one."

"Ask a grown-up!" the beans chorused happily.

The grown-up took a knife and — *thunk, thunk, thunk* — cut wobbly Cao into neat little cubes, soft black squares that jiggled and bounced as they tumbled into the bottoms of two waiting bowls.

"That TICKLES!" Cao squealed between giggles. "I'm CUBES! Look at me, I'm a whole little checkerboard!"

Then came the grand finale. The grown-up took a ladle, scooped up the warm sweet red bean soup — beans and all — and poured it *gloosh* over the cool grass jelly cubes, filling each bowl right to the brim.

Steam curled up. Beans bobbed. Jelly wiggled. And there it was at last: 紅豆湯, sweet red bean soup with grass jelly, warm and cool and sweet and jiggly all in one cozy bowl.

---

Hong Dou floated in her bowl, swirling lazily around the little cubes of her friend, and she had never in her whole bean life felt so warm, so soft, or so *sweet.*

"We did it, Cao," she sighed happily. "We needed sweetness — and we grew it."

"And we shared it," Cao wobbled, snuggling up beside her in the soup. "That's the sweetest part of all."

The grown-up carried the two steaming bowls to the table, where two sleepy spoons waited — and somewhere a little reader, *you*, leaned in to take the very first warm, sweet, jiggly bite.

Slurp. Wobble. Sigh.

Perfect.

## How to Make sweet red bean soup with grass jelly 🍽️
*Ask a grown-up to help with anything hot or sharp!*
1. Soak the dried red beans in a bowl of water for a few hours (or overnight) until plump.
2. Ask a grown-up to boil the soaked beans in fresh water in a pot.
3. Have the grown-up lower the heat and gently simmer the beans until they are soft and squishable.
4. Stir in sugar until it melts into the soup, then mash just a few beans against the pot to make it thick and creamy.
5. Ask a grown-up to cut the grass jelly into little cubes and place them in bowls.
6. Ladle the warm sweet red bean soup over the grass jelly cubes — and share!
