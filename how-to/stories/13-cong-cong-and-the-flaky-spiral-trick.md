# Story 13: Cong Cong and the Flaky Spiral

**Main Character:** Cong Cong (scallion pancake)
**Guest Characters:** Bao Bao
**Setting:** A griddle cart
**Theme:** Share the fun
**Word Spotlight:** **congyoubing** (tsong-yoh-bing) — scallion pancake

---

On a breezy night at the edge of the night market, where the lanterns swung like sleepy fireflies, Cong Cong did a triple twirl and unrolled herself across the whole griddle cart by accident. *Floomp.* She was a warm, golden-brown **congyoubing** (tsong-yoh-bing) — scallion pancake — round and flaky, with crispy ruffled edges and little green bits of scallion freckled all across her cheeks. Being a scallion pancake meant Cong Cong was made of about a thousand flaky layers, which meant when she got excited she could unspool like a roll of party streamers and then giggle herself back together.

"Did you SEE that?" she crowed, rolling back into a neat round disc.

A little roasted sweet potato named Digua peeked over the edge of the cart — lumpy and warm, with toasty brown skin and a golden glow inside, still smelling of the charcoal cart next door. "I saw you turn into a rug," said Digua. "How do you get so many layers? I'm just one boring lump."

Cong Cong gasped with delight. "Oh, sweet potato," she said — because that's exactly what he was — "layers are the best secret in the whole market. You don't have to *be* the dough to make a pancake. Want me to teach you the flaky spiral trick?"

---

Digua bounced so hard he nearly fell off the cart. "Teach me teach me teach me!"

"First," laughed Cong Cong, catching him with a flaky wing, "we need a wise old hand. Making a great pancake is a lot like making great bread, and I know just the baker." She cupped her hands and called across the lanterns, "BAO BAO! We need your rolling pin!"

A pillowy, pale-gold bun came waddling over in a floury apron, leaving little powdery footprints. This was Bao Bao, the calmest baker in the market, who smelled like warm mornings. "Someone said rolling pin?" he said cozily. "Good things rise with time. Now — what are we making?"

"Congyoubing!" sang Cong Cong. "And Digua's going to do every single step himself."

"All of them?" squeaked Digua.

"All the safe ones," said Bao Bao with a wink. "The HOT bits, the sizzly griddle bits — those are mine. Hot is a grown-up's job. But the squishy fun bits? Those are *yours.*"

Digua puffed up with pride and immediately fell over. Being a roundish potato, he was very rolly.

"**Step one,**" announced Cong Cong, plopping a soft ball of dough onto the floured board. "We **ROLL IT THIN.** As thin as a paper lantern. Bao Bao, may we borrow the magic stick?"

Bao Bao handed over the rolling pin, which was nearly as long as Digua was tall. Digua grabbed it and pushed. The dough did not move. He pushed harder and the dough squirted out sideways like a watermelon seed and *bonked* Bao Bao on the nose.

"Oh no," whispered Digua. "I broke the pancake."

"You did not!" Cong Cong laughed so hard a scallion freckle popped off. "Dough is a friend, not a fight. Gentle and rolly. Push *forward,* like you're tucking a blanket." She rolled her flaky little hands over his, and together they pushed the pin smooth and slow until the blob spread out wide and flat and so thin you could almost see the lantern light glowing through it. "There. Thin as a whisper. *That's* the start of every layer."

"It's like a little blanket!" said Digua.

"Now **step two,**" said Cong Cong, eyes sparkling. "We **BRUSH IT WITH OIL.** This is the slippery secret. The oil sneaks between the layers so they puff apart instead of squishing together. No oil, no flakes — just a sad chewy lump." She handed Digua a soft brush and a little dish of oil, and he painted the whole thin blanket shiny and gleaming. He may have painted a smiley face first. Nobody minded.

"It's all slidey now!" Digua giggled.

"Slidey is exactly right," said Bao Bao warmly, dusting flour off his belly. "Patience and a little grease. Same as bread, only louder."

"**Step three,**" Cong Cong went on, practically vibrating with excitement, "is my FAVORITE — we **ADD THE SCALLIONS!**" She scattered a handful of fresh green **cong** (tsong) — scallion — all across the oily dough, little confetti bits of green from edge to edge. "These are where I get my freckles. And my flavor. And my whole personality, honestly." She sprinkled a pinch of salt too, like fairy dust. "A little salt to wake the scallions up. Not too much — we want a wink, not a shout."

Digua scattered his handful, then sneezed, and the scallions went everywhere — on the cart, on the lantern, on Bao Bao's nose (again).

"My nose," said Bao Bao serenely, "is having a wonderful night."

"**Step four,**" said Cong Cong, "is the trick that gives it the name *spiral.* We **ROLL IT INTO A ROPE.**" She caught one edge of the thin green-speckled blanket and rolled it up tight, the whole way across, until the flat blanket became a long skinny rope — like a green-and-gold jelly snake, with all those oily layers wound up snug inside. "See? Every wind of the rope is a brand-new layer hiding in there. Roll, roll, roll — nice and tight."

Digua rolled his own. It went a little crooked, fatter on one end like a snake that had swallowed a marble. "Mine's lumpy," he said, ears drooping.

"Lumpy ropes make handsome pancakes," said Cong Cong, and she meant it. "Soft dough forgives. Watch."

"**Step five** — the spiral!" she sang. "We **COIL THE ROPE** round and round into a little snail shell." She took her green rope and wound it up like a sleeping cat, tucking the tail underneath. It sat there on the board, a perfect coiled spiral, round and plump and proud.

"It's a SNAIL!" Digua coiled his rope too, winding it carefully round and round, tucking the lumpy end under so nobody would know. A wonky little snail. He loved it instantly.

"And **step six,**" said Cong Cong, "we **FLATTEN THE SNAIL** back into a circle. All those coiled-up layers, squished gently flat — and *that's* where the magic lives. When it cooks, the oil between every layer puffs up and pulls them apart, and *poof* — a hundred flaky leaves." She pressed her snail down soft and slow until it became a round, layered disc. Digua pressed his, tongue poking out in concentration, until his snail was a circle too.

Two raw pancakes sat side by side on the floury board, freckled green, ready and waiting.

"Now," said Cong Cong, suddenly very serious, "comes the part that is *only* for Bao Bao. Because **step seven** is HOT."

Bao Bao rolled up his floury sleeves. "Stand back, little ones. The griddle is mine."

He warmed a thin slick of oil on the big flat griddle until it shimmered. "**A grown-up pan-fries it golden,**" he narrated cozily, sliding the first pancake on. It hit the heat with a glorious *SSSSSSS* — the happiest sound in the whole night market. The edges began to ruffle and crisp and bubble, the layers lifting and puffing apart exactly like Cong Cong promised, turning a deep toasty gold.

"It's PUFFING!" Digua jumped. "The layers are coming apart!"

"That's the oil doing its slippery work," said Cong Cong, beaming. "Every layer breathing out into a flake."

Bao Bao flipped each pancake with a calm, practiced flick — golden side up, then golden side up again — until both were crisp and freckled and shiny all over. Then he slid them onto a wooden board to rest. "And the LAST hot job," he said, "is the knife." He cut each round pancake into neat little triangles, like sunbeams from the middle of the sun. "Sharp things are for steady old hands. You two just hold the plates and look hungry."

They held the plates. They looked *extremely* hungry.

---

Under the swinging lanterns, Cong Cong, Digua, and Bao Bao set the steaming golden triangles on a market plate and let them cool just enough to hold. The layers shimmered. The scallions sang. The whole cart smelled like a warm green hug.

Digua picked up a triangle — his very own wonky-snail pancake, now crisp and flaky and perfect — and took a bite. *Crunch.* Then soft. Then a hundred warm flaky layers, exactly the way it was supposed to be.

"I MADE LAYERS," he gasped, mouth full. "I'm not a boring lump! I'm a SPIRAL-MAKER!"

"You were never boring," said Cong Cong, hugging him with one flaky wing. "You're golden all the way through, sweet potato. You just hadn't met your layers yet." She passed a triangle to Bao Bao, who'd done all the brave hot bits, and one to a passing lantern friend, and one to a whisker-twitching prawn at the next cart, until the whole plate was shared all the way around.

Because the best part of any congyoubing — better than the crunch, better even than the layers — is the moment you hand a warm triangle to a friend.

"Share the fun," said Cong Cong happily, "and the pancake stretches twice as far."

## How to Make scallion pancake 🍽️
*Ask a grown-up to help with anything hot or sharp!*
1. Roll a ball of dough out thin and flat, as thin as you can.
2. Brush the thin dough all over with oil.
3. Scatter chopped scallions (and a tiny pinch of salt) across the top.
4. Roll the dough up tightly into a long rope.
5. Coil the rope round and round into a little spiral, like a snail shell.
6. Gently flatten the spiral back into a flat round pancake.
7. A grown-up pan-fries it in a little oil until golden and crispy on both sides, then cuts it into triangles to eat hot — and share!
