# Story 19: Xia Xia and the Shrimp Pond

**Main Character:** Xia Xia (shrimp-fishing guide)
**Guest Characters:** Boba
**Setting:** A shrimp parlor
**Theme:** Patience
**Word Spotlight:** 釣蝦 (diao xia) = shrimp fishing

---

On the steamiest, bounciest night of the whole night-market year, Boba came bowling through the door of the shrimp parlor so fast he nearly rolled straight into the pond. He was a glossy little tapioca pearl, round as a marble and dark as midnight syrup, and he wobbled to a stop with a wet *sploot*.

"I'm here! I'm here! Where are the shrimp? Are they here? Can I catch ten? Can I catch a hundred?" Boba puffed.

A long, blue-grey prawn looked up from the edge of the water. He had two curling whiskers, a fishing vest with about nine pockets, and the calmest smile in all of Taiwan.

"Welcome, little bubble," said Xia Xia, twirling one whisker. "Tonight I will teach you **釣蝦** (diào xiā) = shrimp fishing. But first — can you sit still?"

"Of COURSE I can sit still," said Boba, vibrating like a sneeze.

Xia Xia chuckled. "We'll see. Patience reels in the good stuff."

---

The shrimp parlor glowed warm and orange, all wooden stools and quiet ripples. A wide, shallow pond sat in the middle of the room, and down beneath the gentle water you could see them — fat, twitchy shrimp the color of bubblegum, scuttling about like they owned the place.

Boba pressed his whole round self against the glass. "Look at THAT one. He's enormous. He has *eyebrows*."

"That," said Xia Xia, "is Grandpa Twelve-Legs. He has been retiring from being caught for six years. He is the wisest shrimp in the pond, and the laziest, and tonight he is our teacher whether he likes it or not."

Xia Xia handed Boba a slim bamboo fishing rod with a thread of line, a tiny red-and-white **浮標** (fú biāo) = float bobbing at the end, and a teeny hook smaller than an eyelash.

"Now," said Xia Xia, "**Step One. The bait goes on the hook.** But the hook is very sharp and very tiny, so we do *not* poke it ourselves. We ask a grown-up to help."

He turned and called across the parlor. "Auntie Ami! One threading, please!"

A tall, kind soup-dumpling auntie came pleating over, plucked up a soft pink crumb of bait, and threaded it onto the little hook with two careful pleats of her fingers. *Tap-tap.* Done.

"Thank you, Auntie!" said Boba. "She has very steady hands. She did not poke herself even ONE time."

"That," said Xia Xia, "is the whole reason we ask. Sharp things are a grown-up job. Our job is the fun part." He winked. "And the fun part is mostly sitting."

Boba's float drooped. "Sitting? That's the fun part? I thought there'd be more... *splashing.*"

"Ahh," said Xia Xia, settling onto a stool with the slow creak of a very relaxed prawn. "Watch. **Step Two. Lower the line, hold still, and watch the float.**"

He lifted his bamboo rod and let the line slip down, down, *dip*, until the little red-and-white float rested on the water like a sleepy ladybug. Then he went absolutely, perfectly still. His whiskers didn't even twitch.

Boba tried to copy him. He lowered his line. He held still.

For about one and a half seconds.

"Is it time yet?" he whispered.

"No."

"Now?"

"No."

"What about—"

"Boba," said Xia Xia gently, "a shrimp can hear a wobble from across the pond. If you bounce, they think the whole sky is falling, and they hide under their little rocks and tell shrimp jokes about you for a week."

Boba gasped. "They tell jokes about me?"

"Terrible ones. *Knock knock — who's there — a tapioca pearl who can't sit still.*" Xia Xia shook his head sadly. "It barely even rhymes. The shrimp have no taste in comedy. But they have *excellent* ears. So we breathe. We hold still. We let the pond forget we're even here." Xia Xia closed his eyes halfway. "Stillness is its own kind of magic, little bubble. The float will tell us everything. We just have to be quiet enough to listen."

Boba thought about this very hard. "But what do I *do* while I wait? My bounces have to go *somewhere.*"

"Ahh." Xia Xia tapped his rod. "That is the secret nobody tells you. You don't squash the bounces. You pour them down the line. Every wiggle in you, you send into the water and into the watching. Then the bouncing becomes *paying attention* — and paying attention is the best part of 釣蝦 there is."

So Boba tried again.

He held still. The pond went smooth as cooled syrup. A paper lantern swayed somewhere far off. Boba felt his bouncy heart slow down, *thump... thump... thump,* until he wasn't a fizzing little pearl anymore — he was a calm, round, watching pearl, with his eyes locked on that one red float.

And the strangest thing happened.

He started to *like* it.

The waiting wasn't empty after all. It was full — full of soft ripples and the smell of warm rice and the tiny important question hiding under the water: *would the float move?*

"Xia Xia," he whispered, "I think... I think I could do this all night."

"NOW you're fishing," murmured Xia Xia, eyes still half-closed. "釣蝦 isn't about the catching. It's about the *waiting that's worth it.*"

Down below, Grandpa Twelve-Legs eyed Xia Xia's bait. He flicked an eyebrow. He scuttled closer. Then — *too wise, too lazy* — he turned around and went back to his rock for a nap.

"He's TOAST," whispered Boba. "I mean — he got away! How are you not even mad?"

"Mad?" Xia Xia smiled. "If we caught him on the first try, what would we do with the rest of the lovely evening? Patience reels in the good stuff. It does not rush the good stuff."

So they waited. A shrimp came and sniffed Boba's bait, decided it wasn't fancy enough, and flounced off with his little nose in the air. Boba did not bounce. Another shrimp swam three slow laps, like he was *thinking about it,* then changed his mind and went to find a snack at the other end of the pond. Boba did not bounce.

"That's two shrimp who ignored me," Boba whispered.

"That's two shrimp who are about to feel very silly," said Xia Xia. "The pond is like a steamer basket of buns, little bubble. You can't poke it open early just because you're hungry. Good things rise with — well. That's a different friend's saying. But it's true here too."

And so they waited. And waited. The float bobbed and the lanterns glowed and Boba did not bounce, not even once, which was possibly the greatest achievement of his entire small round life.

Then —

*dip.*

Boba's float twitched. The little red-and-white dot dimpled the water and ducked under like it had somewhere to be.

"XIA XIA! IT MOVED! IT DIPPED! IT'S DIPPING!"

"Easy, easy," breathed Xia Xia, leaning in. "**Step Three. When the float dips — we lift!** Not a big yank. A smooth, gentle lift. Up... *up.*"

Boba lifted his bamboo rod with all the calm he had been saving up, slow and steady and sure — and out of the water rose his line, and dangling at the end, wiggling and waggling and looking extremely surprised, was a fat pink shrimp.

"I CAUGHT ONE!" Boba shrieked, spinning in a happy circle. "I caught a shrimp! I was PATIENT and a shrimp HAPPENED!"

"You did, little bubble. You did." Xia Xia's whiskers wiggled with pride. "And now — **Step Four. A grown-up unhooks it.** That hook is still sharp and the shrimp is still wiggly, so we call Auntie."

"Auntie Ami! One unhooking, please!"

Auntie Ami came pleating back over, cupped the wiggly shrimp in her two soft hands, and *tip — tap —* slipped it free of the tiny hook without a single poke.

"There you are, sweet thing," she said. "A whopper. You'll make a fine snack."

Boba leaned toward his shrimp. The shrimp leaned toward Boba. They regarded each other warmly.

"No hard feelings?" Boba asked.

"None at all," said the shrimp, who was, frankly, delighted to be famous.

---

Then came the very best part of 釣蝦, the part Xia Xia had been saving like a secret.

"**Last step. We grill it — and we share.**"

Auntie Ami brought the shrimp to the little parlor grill — *that part's hot,* so the grown-up handled every sizzle — and laid it over the glowing coals with a fizz and a hiss and a smell so good it made Boba's whole round body sigh. The shrimp turned from bubblegum-pink to a deep, sunset, candied red, crackling and curling and *perfect.*

The whole parlor gathered round. Auntie Ami snipped the shrimp into little pieces with a happy snip-snip. And every single creature in that warm orange room — Xia Xia, and Boba, and the soup-dumpling aunties, and even Grandpa Twelve-Legs, who came out of retirement just for a nibble — shared the catch together.

It was the sweetest, smokiest, most patient shrimp anyone had ever tasted.

"It's even better," said Boba, with his mouth happily full, "because we *waited* for it."

Xia Xia smiled his calm prawn smile and twirled one whisker. "Now you understand, little bubble. The good stuff was never in a hurry. We just had to be still enough to meet it."

And Boba, the bounciest pearl in all of Taiwan, sat perfectly, peacefully still — and was glad.

## How to Make Taiwan-style indoor shrimp fishing 🍽️
*Ask a grown-up to help with anything hot or sharp!*
1. Have a grown-up thread the bait onto the tiny hook (it's sharp — that's a grown-up job).
2. Lower your line into the pond, hold very still, and watch the little float.
3. Keep waiting — be quiet and patient so the shrimp aren't scared away.
4. When the float dips, lift the rod up smoothly (no big yanks!).
5. Have a grown-up unhook the wiggly shrimp for you.
6. Have a grown-up grill the shrimp over the coals until it turns sunset-red — then share it with everyone!

## Goodnight Blessing
Sleep slow, little one, like a float resting on still water. Let the day's bounces settle soft and quiet, and trust that the good things are coming — you don't have to chase them. They'll find you while you rest. Breathe in, breathe out, and let the lanterns dim. The sweetest catch is the one you waited for, and the warmest one is sleep. Goodnight.
