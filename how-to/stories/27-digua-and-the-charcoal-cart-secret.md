# Story 27: Digua and the Charcoal Cart

**Main Character:** Digua (sweet potato)
**Guest Characters:** Hong Dou
**Setting:** A cart corner
**Theme:** Humble gold
**Word Spotlight:** 烤地瓜 (kao digua) = roasted sweet potato

---

On the coziest corner of the night market, where the lanterns leaned in like nosy aunties, sat a small charcoal cart that puffed and crackled and smelled like a hug. And bouncing beside it was Digua, the lumpiest, knobbliest, most cheerfully crooked sweet potato you ever did meet. Her skin was rough and brown as a tree root, her shape was bent like a comma, and she was, by any honest measure, not the prettiest snack at the market.

"I look like a potato that lost a wrestling match," Digua announced proudly, doing a little wobble-spin.

"You ARE a potato that lost a wrestling match," giggled Hong Dou, a round red bean as plump and shiny as a polished button, rolling up with her cousins jingling in a little cloth pouch. "Everybody says you're the plainest thing here."

Digua grinned a knobbly grin. "Plain on the outside," she whispered. "But inside? I'm hiding gold."

---

Hong Dou tilted her shiny red head. "Gold? You? You're brown and bumpy and you have a face like a thumb."

"Exactly!" said Digua, delighted. "That's the whole trick. The humblest-looking snacks keep the best surprises. Come on, Hong Dou — I'll show you how to make 烤地瓜 (**kao digua**) = roasted sweet potato. By the time we're done, you'll never call me plain again. Probably. Maybe. Let's find out!"

Just then a warm, floury figure ducked under the lanterns, apron dusted with flour, smelling of patient mornings. It was Bao Bao the bao baker, pale-gold and pillowy and soft as a nap.

"Did somebody say roasting?" Bao Bao yawned cozily. "Roasting is just baking's sleepy cousin. **Good things rise with time** — and good things *roast* with time too. Count me in. You'll need a grown-up for the hot parts, and I am, technically, an enormous warm grown-up bun."

"PERFECT," cheered Digua. "Step one. To the sink!"

She led them to the little washbasin behind the cart, where the water trickled cool and clear. "First," she said, plopping herself under the tap, "we **scrub**. A sweet potato spends its whole life underground, which is cozy but, let me tell you, *filthy*. I have dirt in places dirt should not be."

Hong Dou took a tiny brush and scrubbed Digua's bumpy back. Digua squealed and kicked her little leaf-feet.

"That TICKLES! Right there — no — THERE — oh, that's the spot, that's the — HAHA — okay, okay, I'm clean!" She popped up sparkling and damp, looking, frankly, exactly as bumpy as before but slightly more honest about it. "Scrub until the skin squeaks. A clean digua is a happy digua."

"Wait, we don't peel you?" Hong Dou asked, holding up a tiny peeler hopefully.

"PEEL me?" Digua clutched her own bumpy skin in mock horror. "Absolutely not! The skin is the best part! It goes all soft and chewy and toasty, like a cozy jacket I get to eat afterward. We never throw away the jacket, Hong Dou. The jacket has FEELINGS." She winked. "And vitamins. Mostly feelings, though."

"Step two?" asked Hong Dou, hopping with excitement.

"Step two," said Digua, taking a deep breath, "is the brave part. We poke holes."

Hong Dou gasped. "Holes? In YOU?"

"In me!" Digua puffed out her chest. "When a sweet potato roasts, she fills up with hot steam inside, like a tummy full of giggles. If there's nowhere for the steam to sneak out, well —" she made a *POOF* gesture with her leafy hands — "she might just burst with excitement. Literally. So we poke a few little holes with a fork to let the steam tiptoe out."

"That's the sharp part," Bao Bao reminded them gently, "so a grown-up holds the fork. Always. Forks are pokey little troublemakers."

A kind grown-up was found — there is always a kind grown-up at the night market, usually holding tea — and *poke, poke, poke,* tiny fork holes appeared all over Digua's bumpy skin.

"Does it hurt?" whispered Hong Dou.

"Not even a little," laughed Digua, wiggling. "It feels like polka dots! Now I'm a sweet potato with breathing holes. Very stylish. Very breezy."

"How many holes?" Hong Dou asked, counting on her tiny bean fingers.

"Oh, just a few all around — enough to let the steam whisper out," said Bao Bao, handing the fork carefully back to the grown-up, pointy-side down. "Not so many that she leaks. We want a polite amount of holes. A modest sprinkle of holes. The Goldilocks of holes."

"The Goldilocks of holes," Digua repeated solemnly. "Put that on my tombstone." Then she burst out laughing, because sweet potatoes do not have tombstones, they have *dinner plates*, and that is a much happier place to end up.

"Step three," Digua continued, "we get cozy. We wrap up in foil."

Bao Bao unrolled a long sheet of shiny silver foil, crinkling like frost. Hong Dou rolled Digua gently into the middle and the two friends tucked the foil snug around her, folding the edges down like the corners of a bedtime blanket.

"Wrap me up tight," sighed Digua happily, now a glittering silver lump. "The foil holds in all my warmth and steam so I roast soft and sweet instead of dry and grumpy. I'm basically a baked potato burrito. A digua dumpling. A shiny little gift."

"You look like a present!" Hong Dou clapped.

"I AM a present," Digua said. "The best presents come in lumpy wrapping."

Hong Dou tucked the last shiny corner of foil into place and gave Digua a little pat. "Comfy?"

"Comfy," came Digua's muffled, happy voice. "It's dark and snug in here. Wake me when I'm gold."

Now came the hot part — the part with the real fire — so all three friends turned to Bao Bao, because Bao Bao was the warm grown-up bun, and the rule of the night market is simple: *the hot oven belongs to the grown-up.*

"Into the hot oven you go, little gold," Bao Bao said, sliding foil-wrapped Digua carefully onto a tray. The cart's belly glowed orange with charcoal, hot as a summer afternoon. "A really hot oven — the kind that makes the air shimmer. And then..."

"And then?" asked Hong Dou, bouncing.

"And then we *wait*," Bao Bao said, with a slow, cozy smile. "A whole hour. Roasting can't be rushed. **Good things rise with time.** Patience is the secret ingredient nobody ever puts on the label."

So they waited. And oh, an hour is a long time when you are a small red bean who loves snacks. Hong Dou tried counting her cousins. She tried napping. She tried asking "is it done yet?" exactly forty-one times, and every time Bao Bao chuckled, "Not yet, little one. Let her dream a while longer."

To pass the time, Bao Bao told stories about the patient things she had baked over the years — bread that needed all night to rise, buns that puffed up only when nobody was watching. "The oven does the magic," she said, "but the waiting does the loving. You can't poke a roasting potato every minute. You have to trust the warmth."

Hong Dou flopped onto her round red back and stared up at the lanterns. "Trust the warmth," she sighed. "That's the hardest step of all."

"It usually is," Bao Bao agreed, and she rocked the little red bean gently while the cart hummed its warm orange hum.

Slowly, slowly, a smell crept out of the cart. A warm, caramel, sugary, sleepy smell that wrapped around the whole corner of the market. Lanterns leaned closer. A passing moth sighed. Hong Dou's tummy rumbled like a tiny drum.

"That smell," Hong Dou whispered. "That's the smell of gold."

"That," said Bao Bao, "is the smell of *almost ready.*"

When at last the hour was up, Bao Bao reached two soft pillowy hands into the warm cart and drew out the foil bundle, steaming and crackling. "Careful — still hot," she warned, setting it down to cool just enough.

"Final step," said Digua's muffled, joyful voice from inside the foil. "Unwrap me and SPLIT me open. Right down the middle. That's where the magic lives."

A grown-up peeled back the warm silver foil. And then, very gently, they split the sweet potato open along her bumpy back — and Hong Dou *gasped*.

Inside the plain brown skin, the lumpy crooked nobody-special sweet potato, was the most beautiful sight in the whole market: soft, glowing, steaming flesh the deep warm color of *gold*. Honey-gold. Sunset-gold. Treasure-gold. It steamed and glistened like the inside of a tiny sunrise.

"You... you really WERE hiding gold," Hong Dou breathed. "The whole time."

"Told you so," said Digua, and even though she'd been split right open, she was beaming, because that is the joyful, funny secret of being a snack: the very best ending is being shared. "Humble on the outside. Gold on the inside. Now — eat me plain, or with a little melty pat of butter. Both are perfect."

Bao Bao added a soft golden curl of butter, and it melted into the steaming gold with a happy little sizzle.

Hong Dou took the first bite, eyes going wide as lanterns. "It's sweet," she mumbled, cheeks full, "and warm, and SOFT, and it tastes like — like a cozy nap and a sunny field and a hug from the inside!"

"And the skin?" Digua prompted, wiggling proudly.

Hong Dou nibbled the soft toasty jacket. "CHEWY," she announced, butter glistening on her chin. "The jacket really did have feelings. Delicious feelings."

Bao Bao took her own bite, slow and savoring, the way patient bakers do everything. "Mmm," she rumbled, eyes closing. "Worth every minute of the hour. The best gold is the gold you have to wait for."

"That's 烤地瓜," said Digua proudly, as the three friends huddled in the warm cart corner, passing the gold between them, butter on their chins, the lanterns glowing low. "The humblest treasure at the market. Never judge a snack by her bumpy skin."

Hong Dou looked around the cozy cart corner — at the soft lanterns, the glowing charcoal, the steam curling up into the dark like sleepy ribbons — and she felt suddenly very warm, all the way down to her round red middle. "I'm sorry I called you plain," she said quietly.

"Don't be," Digua laughed. "Plain is just gold wearing pajamas. And anyway — now you know how to make me. Scrub, poke, wrap, roast, split, share. Six little steps and a whole lot of patience. That's the secret of the humblest treasure at the market."

"Scrub, poke, wrap, roast, split, share," Hong Dou repeated, tucking the steps into her heart like coins into a pouch. "I'll remember forever."

And that is how a lumpy little sweet potato taught a shiny red bean that the plainest wrapping can hold the warmest gold — one scrubbed, poked, wrapped, slow-roasted, golden, buttery bite at a time.

## How to Make roasted sweet potato 🍽️
*Ask a grown-up to help with anything hot or sharp!*
1. Scrub the sweet potatoes under cool water until the skin squeaks clean.
2. Ask a grown-up to poke a few small holes all over each potato with a fork (this lets the steam escape).
3. Wrap each potato snugly in foil, like tucking it into a little blanket.
4. Ask a grown-up to roast them in a hot oven for about an hour, until soft.
5. Let them cool a moment, then split each one open down the middle to reveal the gold inside.
6. Eat plain, or add a small pat of butter and let it melt. Share and enjoy!
