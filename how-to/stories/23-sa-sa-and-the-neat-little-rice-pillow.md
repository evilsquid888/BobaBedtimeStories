# Story 23: Sa Sa and the Rice Pillow

**Main Character:** Sa Sa (spam musubi)
**Guest Characters:** Tama Onigiri
**Setting:** A beach picnic
**Theme:** Pack and share
**Word Spotlight:** **omusubi** (oh-moo-soo-bee) — pressed rice snack

---

On the sunniest, sea-breeziest corner of the whole beach, a tidy little rice block came marching across the sand wearing a backpack twice his size. This was Sa Sa, a neat square of warm white rice with a glossy slice of Spam riding on top like a saddle and a smart black belt of nori snugged round his middle. He hummed as he walked. He liked walking. He liked humming. Most of all, he liked PACKING — and his backpack was packed so beautifully that nothing inside dared to wiggle.

"Picnic blanket — neat," he said, ticking off his checklist. "Snacks — neat. Sunhat — extremely neat."

Beside him bounced his best beach buddy, Coconut Jelly, a wobbly cube the color of moonlight who jiggled with every step and left tiny giggly footprints. Coconut Jelly was the opposite of neat. Coconut Jelly was a delight and a disaster, usually at the same time.

"Sa Sa!" wobbled Coconut Jelly. "Teach me to make YOU! I mean — not *you* you. The snack you. The squishy pillow you!"

Sa Sa puffed up proudly. "You mean **omusubi** (oh-moo-soo-bee) — pressed rice snack? Today, my jiggly friend, you learn the neatest snack on the beach."

---

They spread the blanket where the sand was soft, and a third friend was already waiting there: Tama Onigiri, a plump little rice triangle with rosy cheeks, a single black nori smile, and a calm that came from sitting very still so she wouldn't fall over.

"Ah, musubi day," said Tama warmly. "My favorite kind of day. We onigiri and musubi are cousins, you know. We're all part of the great squishy rice family."

"Cousins!" cried Coconut Jelly, and tried to hug Tama, and nearly rolled her into the ocean. Tama smiled patiently, because that is the onigiri way.

"We're a big family, the squishy rice folk," Tama went on, settling herself upright in the sand. "There's me, the triangle. There's Sa Sa, the block. There's roll-ups and ball-ups and pillows and pyramids. But we all share one secret: we hold together better when somebody makes us with care." She tapped her nori smile. "And we taste best when we're shared. A musubi eaten alone is just a snack. A musubi eaten with friends is a *picnic*."

Coconut Jelly nodded so hard he nearly tipped over again.

Sa Sa unzipped his backpack and — *of course* — everything inside was arranged in perfect rows. A warm pot of cooked rice. A neat stack of nori sheets, black and shiny as ribbon. A little plastic musubi mold shaped like an open box. And one chilly package of Spam.

"Step one," Sa Sa announced, "is the part where we call in the captain."

"Captain?" asked Coconut Jelly, looking around for a boat.

"A GROWN-UP captain," said Sa Sa. "Because step one has a hot pan and a slicing knife, and hot pans and slicing knives are strictly Grown-Up Equipment. We don't touch the fire. We supervise the fire. From a comfy distance. With snacks."

Right on cue, Auntie Mango — the picnic grown-up, tall and bright in a flowery sunhat — knelt by the little camp stove. She sliced the Spam into neat pink slabs (Coconut Jelly covered his eyes and peeked between his wobbles), laid them sizzling in the pan, and fried them until the edges turned crispy and golden-brown. Then she spooned over a shiny splash of sweet-salty sauce that bubbled and glazed each slice until it gleamed like a little sunset.

"Ooooh," said everyone, including a passing crab.

The whole beach seemed to lean in toward that golden, glazy, sweet-salty smell. A seagull stopped mid-waddle. A second crab climbed out of a tide pool just to investigate. Even the wind seemed to slow down and sniff.

"That's a glaze of **shoyu** (SHOH-yoo) — soy sauce," said Sa Sa. "It makes the Spam sparkle. The grown-up cooks it nice and hot so the edges go crispy, then glazes it shiny. We just watch — and we drool politely. Thank you, Captain Auntie!"

"Anytime, neat one," said Auntie Mango, and she set the glazed Spam on a plate to rest, safely away from squishy little hands.

Now came the part Sa Sa had been bouncing for. He set the musubi mold down on a nori sheet, open-side up, so the mold sat in the very middle with the black ribbon waiting on both sides.

"Step two!" he said. "We press the rice. The rice has to be WARM — warm rice hugs itself together. Cold rice just sulks in a pile." He scooped a fat spoonful of warm rice into the mold. "Coconut Jelly, you press."

Coconut Jelly looked at the little flat presser-lid. He looked at his own wobbly hands. "I press?"

"Gently. Like tucking a tiny rice baby into bed."

Coconut Jelly pressed — *too* hard — and rice squirted out the sides like a happy little volcano. "WHOOPS." A grain landed on Tama's nose. Tama did not move. Tama is a professional.

"Softer," laughed Sa Sa, scraping the escapees back in. "Press it FLAT and SNUG, not flat and FLAT. There — feel how it holds its shape? That's a rice **zhentou** (jen-toh) — pillow. A neat little pillow you can eat."

They pressed a second scoop, and a third. Coconut Jelly got the hang of it on the third try, pressing just gently enough that no rice escaped — and when he lifted the presser-lid and saw a perfect plump rectangle sitting underneath, he gasped like he'd discovered treasure.

"I made a SHAPE," he whispered. "A real shape! With CORNERS!"

"Four whole corners," Sa Sa agreed solemnly. "That's more corners than most jellies ever make in a lifetime."

They pressed until each one was a perfect plump rectangle, lined up neat as little mattresses. Then Sa Sa laid one shining slice of glazed Spam right on top of each rice pillow, like a cozy golden blanket.

"Spam on top, rice below, nori waiting all around," he sang. "This is the neatest moment of my whole entire life."

"You said that yesterday about folding the picnic blanket," said Tama.

"And I *meant* it yesterday too," said Sa Sa.

Now came the wrapping. Sa Sa lifted the mold straight up and *off* — and there sat the rice pillow with its Spam blanket, sitting smack in the middle of the nori ribbon. He took one end of the nori and folded it up and over the top. Then the other end. He pressed it down so the two ends overlapped and stuck like a tidy black belt.

"Snug," he said happily, patting it. "Pack it snug so it travels well. A wobbly musubi falls apart in the backpack. A SNUG musubi arrives like a tiny gift." He gave it a little wiggle to prove it. The musubi held tight, not a grain out of place. "See? You could carry this one to the top of a mountain and it would still look this neat. Probably. I haven't tested the mountain part. But I believe in it."

Tama wrapped one too, slow and sure, tucking the nori ends under with the calm confidence of a rice triangle who has done this a thousand quiet times. "Snug and shareable," she murmured. "That's the whole point of us."

"Pack it neat, share it sweet!" said Coconut Jelly, who had been listening after all.

"That's MY catchphrase," said Sa Sa, glowing. "You learned my catchphrase!"

Coconut Jelly tried his own. He scooped rice, laid the Spam, and folded the nori up and over — slowly, wobbling, his little jelly hands shaking with concentration. The first end folded fine. The second end folded... sideways. The nori belt ended up on the *diagonal*, like a tiny musubi wearing a sash to a fancy party.

Everyone went quiet. Coconut Jelly's wobble drooped. "It's not neat," he whispered. "It's lopsided. You like things neat, and I made a mess again."

But Sa Sa picked up the lopsided musubi and held it like treasure. "Are you kidding? Look how SNUG you packed it. The belt's a little crooked, sure — but nothing's falling out. It'll travel just fine." He grinned. "Neat isn't about being perfect, Coconut Jelly. Neat is about holding together so you can SHARE. And this little guy is ready to share."

Tama nodded her one nori smile. "A musubi made by a friend tastes better than a musubi made by a machine. That's just rice science."

Coconut Jelly's wobble came all the way back, bouncier than ever. "Then let's make a WHOLE bunch! Enough for everybody!"

So they did. They pressed and laid and folded, an assembly line of rice and Spam and nori, Sa Sa calling out the steps like a tiny picnic conductor — *press, top, lift, fold, fold, snug!* — while Tama wrapped steadily and Coconut Jelly wobbled along beside them, getting a little neater each time but always leaving at least one charming crooked belt, because that, Sa Sa decided, was simply his signature.

They wrapped each finished musubi in a little square of wax paper, folded neat, so they'd be easy to hand out. "Wrapped tidy means shared easy," Sa Sa explained. "Nobody likes a snack that falls apart before it gets to your friend."

They lined the finished musubi up in a happy row on the plate — Sa Sa's tidy ones, Tama's tidy ones, and Coconut Jelly's beautifully crooked ones right in the middle of honor. The sea breeze carried the smell of warm rice and glazed Spam down the whole beach, and three crabs, a seagull, a hermit crab in a borrowed shell, and a very interested sandcastle all leaned in.

"To share," said Sa Sa, lifting his musubi high.

"To share!" wobbled Coconut Jelly, and lifted his crooked one even higher.

They each took a big bite. The rice was soft and warm, the Spam was sweet and salty and golden, the nori was crisp as a sea breeze — and the lopsided one, everyone agreed, was the most delicious of all.

Then they shared the rest. They gave one to Auntie Mango, who'd earned it. They left a tiny crumb for each polite crab. The seagull got a single grain and flew off feeling like a king. And as the sun slid lower and turned the whole beach the color of glazed Spam, Sa Sa zipped his backpack shut — neat, of course — with nothing inside but happy emptiness, because every last musubi had found a friend.

"Best picnic ever," wobbled Coconut Jelly sleepily, leaning against Tama's warm rice side.

"Pack it neat," Sa Sa yawned, "share it sweet."

## How to Make spam musubi 🍽️
*Ask a grown-up to help with anything hot or sharp!*
1. Have a grown-up slice the Spam, fry it until golden, and glaze it with sweet soy sauce — then let it cool a little.
2. Set a musubi mold on a sheet of nori and press warm cooked rice into the mold until it holds a snug, flat shape.
3. Lay a glazed slice of Spam on top of the pressed rice.
4. Lift the mold off, fold one end of the nori up and over, then the other, and press so the ends overlap into a neat belt.
5. Pack each musubi snug so it travels well — then line them up and share them with your friends!
