# Story 26: Deng Deng and the Lantern

**Main Character:** Deng Deng (paper lantern)
**Guest Characters:** Tang Yuan
**Setting:** A festival workshop
**Theme:** Handmade glows
**Word Spotlight:** 燈籠 (denglong) = lantern

---

On the very last evening before the Lantern Festival, the workshop behind the night market smelled of rice glue, fresh paper, and just a little bit of mischief. Deng Deng the paper lantern bobbed near the rafters, his red paper sides glowing softly, his gold tassels swishing like two tiny ponytails. He was, if we are being honest, showing off — gliding figure-eights through the warm air and humming the festival song slightly off-key.

"Deng Deng!" squeaked a round little voice from a bowl on the table. "How do you DO that? How do you glow like a tiny happy moon?"

It was Tang Yuan — a plump, pearly-white rice ball, soft and bouncy and so squishably round that he wobbled when he got excited, which was always. Tonight he was excited enough to nearly roll off the table.

Deng Deng floated down with a shy little dip. "A little light glows warmest," he said. "Would you like to make your very own 燈籠 (denglong) = lantern? A real one. With your own two... well, with your own paste-y rice-ball hands?"

Tang Yuan gasped so hard he hiccuped. "MORE than yes!"

---

"First," said Deng Deng, gliding to a shelf and tugging down a big square of red paper with one tassel, "every lantern begins with **紙** (zhǐ) = paper. Red paper, because red means luck, and luck means glow, and glow means... well, me."

The paper drifted down and landed right on top of Tang Yuan, who vanished completely. "I am a ghost lantern now," came his muffled voice. "WooOOoo."

"You are a dumpling under a blanket," said Deng Deng kindly, lifting the paper off. Tang Yuan popped out, grinning, with one corner of the paper stuck to his sticky cheek. Deng Deng peeled it loose. "Step one. We **fold** the paper in half — the long way, like closing a book. Match the corners. Press the crease nice and flat."

Tang Yuan flopped his whole soft body onto the fold to flatten it, which was not technically the right tool but worked surprisingly well. *Squish.* "Like this?"

"Exactly like that," said Deng Deng, "if a bit drooly."

The red paper now lay folded into a long doubled rectangle, with the folded edge along the bottom and the two loose edges along the top.

"Now," Deng Deng went on, lowering his glow to a serious, careful warmth, "comes the snipping. And snipping means scissors, and scissors are **sharp** — so this is a job where we cheerfully holler for a grown-up."

"GROWN-UP!" Tang Yuan hollered immediately, joyfully, before Deng Deng had even finished.

The workshop auntie wandered over, wiping floury hands on her apron. "You two rang?"

"We need brave grown-up scissor fingers," said Deng Deng. "Tang Yuan will show you where, and you'll do the cutting."

Tang Yuan took this duty *very* seriously. He bounced along the folded edge — the bottom, the part that did NOT open — and pressed his little belly down to make a row of dents. "Here. And here. And here-here-here." Auntie cut a straight line at each dent, starting from the folded edge and snipping *upward toward* the loose top edge — but she stopped before reaching it, leaving a strip of uncut paper about a thumb-width wide along the top.

"Why do we stop?" asked Tang Yuan, peering at the gap.

"Because that uncut top," said Deng Deng, "is what holds the whole lantern together. Cut all the way and you don't get a 燈籠 — you get confetti."

"I love confetti," said Tang Yuan.

"On a different night," said Deng Deng.

*Snip, snip, snip,* went Auntie, all the way along, until the folded paper had a whole comb of cuts marching across it, evenly spaced, like piano keys. Then she set the scissors safely point-down in a cup and gave a little bow. "Back to my buns," she said, and off she went.

"Now the magic," whispered Deng Deng. "**Unfold** it."

Tang Yuan unhooked the fold and opened the paper flat — and squeaked. Where there had been simple cuts, the open paper now had a row of red strips down the middle, all connected at the top and the bottom, like the bars of a tiny cage, or the strings of a tiny harp.

"It's a fence!" said Tang Yuan. "A friendly red fence!"

"It's about to be rounder than any fence," said Deng Deng. "Step three: we **roll** it into a tube. Bring the two short ends together — the left edge around to meet the right edge — so it makes a circle. The strips will swell out in the middle all on their own. That belly is what makes a lantern a lantern."

Tang Yuan tried to roll the paper, but being round and sticky and overexcited, he rolled *himself* up inside it instead and went tumbling across the table — *bumpa-bumpa-bump* — a rice ball burrito in a swirl of red.

"Tang Yuan," said Deng Deng, holding very still so as not to laugh, "you are inside the lantern."

"It's cozy in here," said Tang Yuan. "Ten out of ten. Would roll again."

Deng Deng gently unrolled him, dusted him off, and showed him how to curve the paper the *other* way — around the outside — until the two short edges kissed and the middle puffed into a perfect plump barrel. "Now tape the edges where they meet. Top and bottom both. **膠帶** (jiāodài) = tape. A grown-up isn't needed for tape, but a steady friend is."

Tang Yuan held the seam closed while Deng Deng wound the tape — *zzzip, zzzip* — and there it was. A round red 燈籠, glowing faintly in the lamplight, its strips bowing out like the ribs of a small happy whale.

Tang Yuan went very quiet. "We *made* that," he whispered. "Out of one flat boring square."

"Nothing flat is ever boring," said Deng Deng softly, "if you fold it with love. But we're not done. A lantern needs to be *carried*, and a lantern needs to be *dressed*."

So Deng Deng cut a thin strip of leftover paper (Auntie supervised, naturally — "GROWN-UP!" "I never left, dear") and they taped it across the top opening to make a **handle**, an arch you could hook a finger under. Then came the dressing-up. Tang Yuan pressed shiny gold stickers all over the red, plus three glittery stars, plus one sticker of a smiling dumpling that looked suspiciously like himself.

"That's me!" he beamed. "Now I'm IN the festival forever."

"Last step," said Deng Deng, and his voice went warm and glowy and proud. "The light. And here is the most important rule in the whole workshop: **no flame**. Paper and fire are not friends. So inside our lantern we put a **battery tea-light** — a little pretend candle that glows but never burns. Cool to the touch. Safe as a hug."

Tang Yuan clicked the tiny switch. A soft golden light bloomed up through the red paper, and every strip glowed, and every gold star sparkled, and the smiling-dumpling sticker beamed, and the whole lantern lit the workshop in a warm rosy hush.

"Oh," breathed Tang Yuan. He didn't bounce. He didn't wobble. He just stared, his pearly face washed in rose-gold. "Oh, Deng Deng. It glows just like *you*."

"Better," said Deng Deng, his own glow shining wet and happy at the rim. "It glows like *you made it*. And handmade glows warmest of all."

Out into the night market they went, Deng Deng floating and Tang Yuan rolling, the brand-new 燈籠 swinging from a stick between them, painting little circles of red light on the cobblestones. Bao Bao waved from her bun cart. Hong Hong's candied fruits caught the glow and threw it back in sparkles. And all the festival lanterns above seemed to lean down to welcome the smallest, newest, most-loved one of all.

They shared it with everyone — which is the very best thing to do with a glow.

## How to Make a paper lantern 🍽️
*Ask a grown-up to help with anything hot or sharp!*
1. Take a square or rectangle of red paper and fold it in half the long way; press the crease flat.
2. With a grown-up's help, cut straight lines from the FOLDED edge toward the open edges — but stop before the end, leaving about a thumb-width of paper uncut along the top.
3. Unfold the paper flat; you'll see a row of connected strips.
4. Roll the paper into a tube, bringing the two short ends together so the strips bow out in the middle. Tape the seam at the top and bottom.
5. Tape a thin paper strip across the top opening to make a handle.
6. Decorate with stickers, stars, or drawings.
7. Pop a battery tea-light inside (NO flame — paper and fire are not friends!) and switch it on to make it glow.

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, the day is folded up and creased flat and put away. The stars are taped softly to the dark, and the moon is your battery tea-light, glowing but never burning, warm but never far. You made today out of one plain square of morning, and look how it glowed. Tuck your hands beneath your cheek. A little light glows warmest, and yours is shining still. Goodnight.
