# Story 3: Bing and the Shaved Snow

**Main Character:** Bing (shaved ice)
**Guest Characters:** Mango
**Setting:** A hot night market
**Theme:** Sharing is bigger
**Word Spotlight:** 剉冰 (cuobing) = shaved ice

---

It was the hottest night the night market had ever sweated through. The lanterns drooped. The dumplings fanned themselves. Even the steam from the noodle stalls flopped over sideways and gave up.

And right in the middle of it all stood Bing, the coolest soul in the whole sizzling place — because Bing was made of ice.

Bing was a soft snowy mound, white as a cloud that had taken a nap in the freezer, with two twinkly eyes like tiny frosted blueberries and a chill so refreshing that flies fainted happily nearby. When Bing breathed out, little snowflakes puffed into the steamy air and made a tiny *tinkle* sound, like the world's smallest wind chime.

"Whew," said a wobbly voice. "It is so hot tonight I think I'm about to ripen TWICE."

Bing turned around. And there, melting dramatically against a lamppost, was Mango.

---

Mango was a plump, sunshine-orange fellow, glossy as a polished marble, with a single green leaf flopping over one eye like the world's laziest hat. He had a smile so juicy it practically dripped.

"Bing!" Mango cried, peeling himself off the lamppost. "You're cold! You're MARVELOUS! Stand near me before I turn into a smoothie all by myself!"

Bing giggled, and three snowflakes plinked out. "I can do better than stand near you," Bing said. "How would you like to learn how to make **剉冰** (cuobing) = shaved ice? It's the most refreshing treat in the whole night market. And the best part is — you can't make it for ONE. It only works if you share."

Mango's leaf-hat perked straight up. "Teach me! Teach me! I am an excellent student. I am also delicious, but that's not relevant right now."

"Step one," said Bing, leading Mango around the back of the dessert stall, "we have to *freeze* something."

"Freeze WHAT?" gasped Mango. "Me? Please me? I'm so warm I'd freeze in eleven seconds."

"Not you, silly," Bing laughed. "We freeze water — or even better, milk, for the creamy kind. We pour it into a big round tub and pop it in the freezer until it turns into a solid block. A nice, fat, frozen brick."

So they poured. Mango held the milk jug with both stubby arms and tipped it veeeery slowly, mostly into the tub and only a little onto his own feet. Then together they slid the tub into the great frosty freezer, which breathed out a cold *whoosh* so cozy that Bing sighed, "Ahhh, home sweet home."

"Now what?" asked Mango, hopping.

"Now," said Bing, "the very best part of being shaved ice. We *wait.*"

"WAIT?" Mango wilted. "But I'm melting NOW!"

Bing patted him with a cool snowy paw, which felt so nice that Mango stopped melting entirely and just went *ahhhhh.* "Good things are worth a little wait," Bing whispered. "Especially the frozen ones."

They waited. They played thumb war. Mango lost, because he doesn't have thumbs, only juicy nubs. They counted lanterns, all the way up to "lots." They sang a song about how hot it was, which only had one word in it, and that word was "hooooot."

"Is it frozen yet?" Mango asked.

"Not yet," said Bing.

"Is it frozen NOW?"

"Still not yet."

"Is it—"

"Mango," giggled Bing, "ice is shy. It freezes faster when nobody's staring at it. That's a snowflake secret." (This is not true. But it is what every patient ice cube tells every wiggly mango, and it works every single time.)

So they turned their backs on the freezer and told three jokes and watched a moth do loop-the-loops around a lantern. And wouldn't you know it — being patient made the waiting fly right by.

And at last — *clunk!* — out came the milk, frozen into a glorious solid block, smooth as the moon.

"Now THIS," said Bing, "is the part where we need a grown-up."

"Ooh, why?" asked Mango.

"Because the next step uses a *shaving machine* with a sharp, spinning blade," said Bing, "and sharp things are strictly a grown-up's job. We just point and cheer." Bing turned and called out, "Auntie Cuo! Could you help us shave the ice, please?"

Auntie Cuo, the kindly old stallkeeper with flour on her elbows and a cheerful crank-handle in her hand, ambled over. "Of course, little snowdrop," she chuckled. She lifted the frozen block — *oof* — and set it onto the shaving machine, the one with the bright, spinning blade that Bing and Mango stayed a careful, polite distance from.

"Watch THIS," whispered Bing, practically vibrating with joy.

Auntie Cuo turned the crank. *Whirr-whirr-whirr* went the blade, and the frozen block began to shave into the fluffiest, softest, snowiest curls you ever saw. Down they drifted into the bowl below — *fwoof, fwoof, fwoof* — building up and up into a tall, trembling, magnificent mountain of snow.

"It's SNOWING!" shrieked Mango, catching flakes on his leaf. "Indoors! In summer! Bing, you ARE the night market's secret winter!"

The mountain grew taller. And taller. Until it was a soft white peak nearly as tall as Mango himself, wobbling gently like it might giggle.

"And THAT," said Bing proudly, as Auntie Cuo gave the bowl a pat and stepped away, "is **剉冰** (cuobing) = shaved ice. Well — half of it. Now comes my favorite part. The TOPPINGS."

Mango's eyes went enormous. "I LOVE toppings. I am ALSO a topping. This is the best day of my life."

"Then up you go!" Bing said. So Mango took a deep, brave breath, and Bing helped him slice into juicy golden cubes — *plip, plip, plip* — which they piled, glistening, all over the snowy peak. (Mango cubes are very giggly, and they kept rolling, so it took several tries.)

Next came a scoop of sweet **紅豆** (hongdou) = red bean, soft and shiny, tumbling down the mountainside like little brown boulders in a tiny, delicious avalanche. Mango cheered, "AVALANCHE!" far too loudly, and one red bean rolled clean off the table and had to be rescued.

Then came squishy chewy **麻糬** (mashu) = mochi balls, bouncing aboard one by one with happy little *boing*s. One mochi bounced so high it nearly escaped over the edge, but Bing caught it with a cool snowy paw and plopped it gently back on the summit, where it sat looking slightly embarrassed. Bing tucked in some wobbly grass jelly that giggled when poked, a wink of pineapple, and a single shy strawberry on top like a hat.

"And the finishing touch," said Bing, lifting a tiny pitcher, "a drizzle of sweet condensed milk, swirling all the way down."

*Driiiiizzle.* The creamy ribbon wound down the snowy slopes, and the whole towering treat sparkled under the lanterns like a tiny delicious mountain at sunrise.

Mango stared. Then his juicy face crumpled. "Bing," he sniffled happily. "It's so beautiful. But — but it's SO BIG. I could never eat all of it myself! What a waste—"

"Aha," said Bing, with a twinkly frosted-blueberry wink. "That's the whole secret, Mango. **剉冰** isn't meant for one. It's meant for sharing. You can't make a tiny one — it'd be silly! The bigger the mountain, the more friends it feeds. *Sharing is bigger.*"

And right then, as if the mountain had rung a bell, the night market gathered round. Bao Bao the bun toddled over in his floury apron. Hong Hong the candied-fruit friend arrived all sparkle and crackle. Mala Malasada bounced in, leaving sugar everywhere, hollering "Roll ME in shaved ice next!" Even a tired noodle vendor and two fanning dumplings squeezed in close.

"Quick!" laughed Bing. "There's one more rule. You have to eat **剉冰** FAST — before it melts back into a puddle! Spoons up, everyone!"

*Clink-clink-clink* went a dozen spoons. The mountain trembled, it tumbled, it disappeared into happy slurping mouths, cold and sweet and chewy and creamy all at once. Bao Bao got a snowflake on his nose and sneezed a tiny floury *poof.* Hong Hong crunched a hidden mango cube and sparkled even brighter. Mala Malasada took such an enormous bite that he got a brain-freeze and hopped in a circle going "ooh-ooh-ooh!" until Bing pressed a cool paw to his forehead and the wobbles melted away. Mango ate a piece of HIMSELF by accident and declared it "honestly, delightful — I had no idea I was this good."

By the time the last snowy spoonful melted away, the whole night market had stopped sweating and started smiling. Everyone was cool. Everyone was full. Everyone was friends.

"See?" Bing whispered, as Mango leaned sleepy and happy against his cool soft side. "One bowl. So many friends. That's how shaved ice works. The mountain shrank — but the sharing grew."

Mango yawned a juicy yawn. "Bing," he murmured, "you're the best secret winter a hot night ever had."

## How to Make shaved ice 🍽️
*Ask a grown-up to help with anything hot or sharp!*
1. Pour water or milk into a round tub and freeze it solid into a block (this takes time — be patient!).
2. Ask a grown-up to shave the frozen block on a shaving machine with a sharp blade, until it piles into a tall, fluffy mountain of snow in a bowl.
3. Pile on toppings: fresh fruit like mango, sweet red bean, chewy mochi, jelly — whatever you love.
4. Drizzle sweet condensed milk all over the top.
5. Grab spoons, share with friends, and eat fast before it melts!
