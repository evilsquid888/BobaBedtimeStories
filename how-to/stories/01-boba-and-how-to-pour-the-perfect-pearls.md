# Story 1: Boba and the Bubble-Tea Lesson

**Main Character:** Boba (tapioca pearl)
**Guest Characters:** Brown Sugar
**Setting:** Boba shop
**Theme:** Learn the steps
**Word Spotlight:** **zhenzhu naicha** (jen-joo nai-chah) — boba milk tea

---

Boba was the bounciest little pearl in all of Taiwan, and she knew it. She was small and round and a deep glossy brown, exactly the color of a polished chestnut, and when she got excited she boinged straight up off the counter like a sneeze made of jelly. Right now she was very excited, because today was the day she had promised to teach her best friend, Coconut Jelly, the most important secret in the whole wide night-market world.

"Are you ready?" Boba bounced. *Boing.* "Are you super ready?" *Boing, boing.* "Are you ready-ready-with-a-cherry-on-top ready?"

Coconut Jelly was a wobbly white cube, soft as a cloud that had decided to take a nap. He jiggled nervously. "I think so? What are we making, exactly?"

Boba puffed out her round little chest. "We," she announced, "are making **zhenzhu naicha** (jen-joo nai-chah) — boba milk tea. The chewiest, dreamiest, slurpiest drink ever invented. And by the end, *you'll* know how too!"

---

The boba shop smelled like warm sugar and good ideas. Steam curled up to the ceiling like lazy little ghosts, and a big pot bubbled away on the stove with a friendly *gloop, gloop, gloop*.

"First things first," said Boba, hopping onto a stool. "Cooking the pearls. That means me. I have *cousins* in that pot." She waved cheerfully at the bubbling water. A few tapioca pearls bobbed up and waved back. "Hi, Auntie Glug! Hi, Cousin Plop!"

But the pot was hot — *very* hot, hot enough to make your whiskers wilt — and the stove was no place for two small wobbly friends. So Boba did the smartest thing a chewy pearl can do.

"COCONUT JELLY," she said importantly, "we must summon... a Grown-Up."

A Grown-Up appeared, which is a magical thing Grown-Ups do, especially near snacks. This one wore a flowery apron and had warm, crinkly eyes.

"We need the pearls cooked, please!" said Boba. "Bubble, bubble, then a nice long rest, until they're squishy in the middle and chewy all over."

The Grown-Up stirred the pot so the pearls swirled like little brown planets. They bubbled and danced and slowly turned soft and shiny. Coconut Jelly watched, fascinated. "They're doing a swimming lesson!" he whispered.

"Backstroke," Boba agreed proudly. "It's the family talent."

When the pearls were perfectly tender, the Grown-Up scooped them into a bowl. And that was the cue for the star of the show to arrive.

"Did somebody say... *sweetness?*"

Down the counter rolled **Brown Sugar** — a glossy, dark, syrupy fellow who shimmered like a wet stone in the rain. He moved slow and rich and left a sticky golden trail behind him, and he smelled like caramel and campfires and Sunday mornings.

"Brown Sugar!" cheered Boba. "We need your famous syrup!"

"Of course you do," he drawled, doing a slow swirl. "Nobody ever throws a party and forgets the sugar. That would be a *bitter* mistake." He chuckled at his own joke for a worryingly long time.

The Grown-Up melted Brown Sugar gently — *low and slow*, with a splash of water — until he turned into a thick, glossy syrup the color of treasure. Then they poured him right over the warm pearls and stirred until every single pearl wore a shiny brown sugar coat.

"Oooh," sighed Coconut Jelly. "They look like they're dressed for a wedding."

"Their own wedding," said Boba. "To a cup of tea. It's a love story, really."

The pearls glistened, all dark and sticky and gleaming, soaking up the sweetness. Brown Sugar admired his work. "Shine outside," he said, "sweet inside. That's the only way to live, if you ask me."

"Now," said Boba, hopping to the next station, "the tea!"

While the pearls rested in their sugary blanket, the Grown-Up brewed a big pot of black tea — strong and dark and smelling like a cozy library. "**Re** (reh) — hot!" the Grown-Up reminded them, holding the kettle high and away from little wobbly friends. So Boba and Coconut Jelly cheered from a safe and sensible distance, which is exactly where you cheer from when something is steamy.

"Why does it have to be *strong* tea?" asked Coconut Jelly.

"Because!" said Boba. "We're going to add cold milk and lots of ice, and they're *bullies*. They water everything down. Strong tea fights back." She struck a tiny pearl karate pose. "*Hi-yah!*"

Then came the hardest part of all, which was waiting. The hot tea had to cool down before it could become a proper iced drink. Boba did not love waiting. Boba was a *boing*, and boings are not famous for their patience.

"Is it cool yet?" *Boing.* "Is it cool NOW?" *Boing, boing.* "How about this exact second?"

"Patience," giggled Coconut Jelly, who had read about that somewhere. "Good things take a little time, you know."

So they passed the minutes the very best way: by tasting one single test-pearl, purely for science. It was warm and chewy and ridiculously delicious, and they both agreed, very seriously, that more scientific tasting would probably be required later.

At last the tea was cool — cool enough to be friends with ice instead of melting it. "It's time!" Boba practically backflipped. "The grand assembly! Pay attention, this is the magic part!"

She lined up a tall, clear cup. "Step one of the finish: the pearls go in *first*. Right at the bottom, like treasure in a chest." The Grown-Up spooned the glossy brown sugar pearls into the cup, and they slid down and painted gorgeous dark stripes up the sides — *tiger stripes*, the kind that make a boba milk tea look extra fancy.

"Whoa," breathed Coconut Jelly. "It looks like a sunset that fell into a cup."

"Step two: ICE." In tumbled the ice cubes with a *clatter-clatter-clink*, like tiny applause. "Step three: the tea and the milk!" The Grown-Up poured the cooled black tea and a generous splash of creamy milk right over the top, and the whole cup swirled from dark to soft caramel-brown, the pearls peeking up through the clouds.

"And step four," said Boba, her eyes sparkling, "the part that makes it a *real* boba milk tea." She grinned the grin of a pearl about to do her favorite thing in the universe.

"We SHAKE it."

The Grown-Up popped a lid on tight and shook the cup — *shaka-shaka-shaka-SHAKA!* — and Boba could not help herself. She hopped up onto the counter and shook her whole round self in time. "Shaka-shaka-BOING! Shaka-shaka-BOING!" Coconut Jelly wobbled along, jiggling so hard he nearly bounced off the stool, and even Brown Sugar swayed slowly side to side like a syrupy disco.

"Why do we shake it?" Coconut Jelly panted.

"So everybody makes friends!" said Boba. "The tea, the milk, the sugar — all mixed up cozy together. A drink that isn't shaken is just a bunch of strangers standing in a cup."

When they popped the lid, the boba milk tea was *perfect*: creamy and swirly and crowned with foam, the dark sugary pearls waiting at the bottom like a treasure hunt with a chewy prize. The Grown-Up added one last thing — a fat, friendly straw, the kind wide enough to slurp a whole pearl right up.

"Coconut Jelly," said Boba softly, holding up the cup. "You did it. You learned the whole thing. Cook the pearls, sweeten them up, brew and cool the tea, then layer, ice, and shake. *That's* boba milk tea."

Coconut Jelly's whole wobbly self glowed. "I really did, didn't I?"

"Now," said Boba, "the most important step of all. The one nobody writes down."

"What's that?"

Boba slid the straw toward her best friend. "You share the very first sip with somebody you love."

So they did. They slurped together — *bloop!* went a pearl up the straw — and Coconut Jelly's eyes went as wide as moons. Chewy and sweet and creamy and cold and warm in the heart all at once. Brown Sugar happily plopped himself a tiny drop into the foam, just to say he was in there too. And the three of them sat on the counter as the night-market lanterns glowed, passing the cup back and forth, slurping and giggling, until every last pearl was gone.

Which, of course, simply meant it was time to make another.

## How to Make boba milk tea 🍽️
*Ask a grown-up to help with anything hot or sharp!*
1. Have a grown-up cook the tapioca pearls in boiling water until soft and chewy, then stir them in warm brown sugar syrup until coated and glossy.
2. Have a grown-up brew a pot of strong black tea, then let it cool down completely.
3. Spoon the brown sugar pearls into the bottom of a tall cup, then fill it up with ice.
4. Pour the cooled tea and a splash of milk over the top, put on a lid, and shake it up.
5. Add a wide straw, share the first sip with someone you love, and slurp!
