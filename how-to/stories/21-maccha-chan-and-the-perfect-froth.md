# Story 21: Maccha-chan and the Froth

**Main Character:** Maccha-chan (matcha whisk)
**Guest Characters:** Niko Nigiri
**Setting:** A teahouse
**Theme:** Slow and breathe
**Word Spotlight:** **matcha** (MAH-chah) — powdered green tea

---

In the calmest corner of the night market, where the lanterns glowed like sleepy fireflies, there was a teahouse so quiet you could hear a sugar crystal drop. And inside that teahouse lived the bounciest problem the quiet had ever met.

His name was Niko Nigiri, and he was a little block of rice wearing a slice of fish like a cozy blanket. Niko could NOT sit still. He hopped. He wiggled. He spun in circles until his nori belt came loose.

"I'm SO excited," Niko buzzed, vibrating like a teacup on a drum. "What are we making? Is it loud? Is it fast? Is it BOTH?"

He bounced so high his fish slice flapped up like a little flag and then flopped back down over one eye. He didn't even notice. He just kept bouncing.

"Niko," called a gentle voice, "you've been hopping since breakfast. Aren't you the tiniest bit tired?"

"Tired?" Niko gasped, as if he'd never heard such a strange word. "I don't get tired. I'm POWERED BY RICE."

From a shelf of teabowls, a small jade spirit floated down. She had a body made of a hundred slender bamboo prongs — a whisk! — and a soft green cap of foam, like a cloud that had eaten too many vegetables.

"We're making **matcha** (MAH-chah) — powdered green tea," said Maccha-chan, in a voice as smooth as still water. "And the first ingredient... is slow."

Niko blinked. "Slow isn't an ingredient."

"It's the BEST ingredient," she whispered. "Watch."

She drifted closer, and a little curl of foam slid off her cap and bopped Niko softly on the nose. He went cross-eyed trying to look at it.

"Did you just boop me with a tea-cloud?"

"I might have," she said, hiding a smile behind a prong.

---

Maccha-chan glided to the little tea table, where everything waited in a tidy, sleepy row: a tin of bright green powder, a fat-bottomed bowl called a **chawan** (chah-WAHN) — tea bowl, a tiny scoop, and a small mesh sieve no bigger than a button.

"First," she said, "we wake up the matcha. But gently. Like waking a friend on a Sunday."

She unscrewed the tin. Inside was powder so green it practically hummed — the color of spring grass, of frog tummies, of the very greenest crayon in the box. Niko leaned in so fast he nearly faceplanted into it.

"WHOA. Can I just eat the whole tin?"

"You could," said Maccha-chan, "but raw matcha is lumpy and clumpy, and lumps are no fun in a smooth cup. So first, we **sift** it."

She scooped a little powder into the tiny sieve and tapped the side — *tink, tink, tink* — and a soft green snow drifted down into the bowl. The lumps got caught in the mesh and broke apart into the finest, fluffiest dust.

"Sifting takes ALL the clumps out," she explained, tapping in a calm rhythm. "*Tink. Tink. Tink.* See? You can't hurry a sift. You just... breathe with it."

Niko tried tapping fast. *TINKTINKTINK!* A green puff exploded right up his nose, and he sneezed a tiny green cloud — *AH-CHOO!* — which left him polka-dotted in matcha freckles.

"I'm a kiwi now," Niko said, looking at his green-speckled rice. Maccha-chan let out a froth-soft giggle.

"Slow down, little rice ball. Breathe in..." She floated up. "...breathe out." She floated down. Niko copied her. In. Out. And — funny thing — his wiggles got a little smaller. Just a little.

"Huh," he said, surprised. "My bounce is leaking out."

"That's not a leak," Maccha-chan said warmly. "That's calm sneaking in."

---

"Now," said Maccha-chan, "we need water. Warm water. NOT boiling."

"Why not boiling?" asked Niko. "Boiling is exciting!"

"Boiling water is too hot for matcha," she said. "It scorches the leaves and turns them bitter and grumpy. We want the water hot like a cozy bath, not hot like a dragon's hiccup." She tilted a prong toward the kitchen door. "And THAT is a grown-up job, because hot water is for careful paws only."

So they called Auntie Oolong, the kindly old teapot who ran the teahouse. She bustled in, steaming softly, and warmed the water until it was just right — hot, but happy, never angry-bubbling.

"How do you know when it's perfect?" Niko asked, standing on his tippy-toes (which, for a rice ball, is mostly just leaning).

"When the steam curls up lazy and slow," said Auntie Oolong, "like a kitten stretching after a nap. If the water's jumping and roaring, it's too cross for tea." She tested it with one careful spout. "Ahh. That's a sleepy kettle. Just right."

"Perfect temperature, dears," Auntie Oolong said, pouring a careful little splash into the bowl. "Not a drop more till you've made your froth."

"Wait — only a LITTLE water?" said Niko. "That's barely a puddle!"

"That's the secret," said Maccha-chan, eyes twinkling. "We use just a splash first. A little water makes a thick green paste, and a thick paste is the only way to whisk up a really good foam. Pour it all in now and you get sad, flat, lonely tea." She shivered her prongs dramatically. "And nobody wants lonely tea."

---

Then came Maccha-chan's favorite part. The part she was BORN for.

She dipped her whisk-body into the bowl, into the little pool of green, and began to move — not in circles, but back and forth, back and forth.

"You whisk in the shape of a letter," she sang softly. "Not an O. Not a swirly squiggle. You whisk a **W**. Wuh. Wuh. Wuh."

"A W?!" Niko gasped. "Like in *wiggle*? That's MY favorite letter!"

"Then this part is yours," she said, and let him hold her gently. Together they whisked — *W, W, W* — fast little zigzags across the bottom of the bowl, never stirring round and round, just W-ing back and forth like windshield wipers in a hurry.

And slowly, magically, the green paste began to change. Bubbles appeared. Tiny ones, then tinier ones, then a thousand whispering ones, until the whole surface fluffed up into a soft, jade-colored foam — like a tiny green cloud had come to take a nap on the tea.

"FROTH!" Niko shouted, so thrilled he did a full backflip. "WE MADE A CLOUD!"

"We made a cloud," Maccha-chan agreed, beaming. Her catchphrase floated out of her like steam: "**Whisk slow, froth grows.**"

"But we whisked FAST," said Niko.

"Fast hands," she winked, "calm heart. The whisk hurries. *We* don't."

Niko peered into the bowl and watched the foam wobble. "It looks like a tiny green sky," he whispered. "Can I poke it?"

"You may admire it," said Maccha-chan, "but if you poke a cloud, it pops, and then you've poked your own tea. Tea has feelings, you know." She paused. "...That's not true. But it's nicer than poking."

---

"Now the rest of the water," said Maccha-chan, and Auntie Oolong tipped the warm kettle once more, filling the bowl the rest of the way. The foam rose to the top and sat there like a fluffy green hat.

The bowl steamed. The lanterns glowed. And the whole teahouse smelled like a forest right after rain.

"It's ready," Maccha-chan said. "But here is the very last step, and it might be the most important one of all."

Niko held his breath. "Is it a secret ninja sip?"

"It's this," she said softly. "You sip it... **slowly**. You don't gulp matcha. You hold the warm bowl in both paws, you breathe in the green-tea smell, and you take one slow, gentle sip. And while you sip — you feel calm spread all the way from your tummy to your toes."

She handed Niko the bowl. He, the wiggliest rice ball in the entire night market, wrapped both little hands around the warm chawan. He breathed in. The steam tickled his nose. And he took one slow, slow sip.

His eyes went wide.

Then... soft.

Then... sleepy-happy.

"Ohhh," he sighed, and for the first time all day, Niko Nigiri sat perfectly, completely still. His shoulders dropped. His nori belt relaxed. A tiny, peaceful smile spread across his rice-y face. "It tastes like... a green nap. Like a hug made of leaves."

"That," whispered Maccha-chan, "is what slow tastes like."

They passed the bowl back and forth under the lantern light — one slow sip for Niko, one slow sip for Maccha-chan, one for Auntie Oolong too — sharing the warm green cloud until every last froth-bubble was gone.

And outside, the busy market kept buzzing and clanging and zooming. But inside the quiet teahouse, two friends sat close, warm bowl between them, breathing slow.

In. Out. In. Out.

Sweet, green, and perfectly still.

## How to Make matcha 🍽️
*Ask a grown-up to help with anything hot or sharp!*
1. Sift a little matcha powder through a small mesh sieve into a wide bowl to break up the lumps.
2. Ask a grown-up to heat water until it's warm but NOT boiling (too-hot water makes matcha bitter).
3. Pour just a little of the warm water into the bowl first.
4. Whisk briskly back and forth in a "W" shape (not circles!) until the surface turns foamy and frothy.
5. Add the rest of the warm water and gently mix.
6. Hold the warm bowl in both hands, breathe in, and sip slowly — feel yourself grow calm.
