# Story 2: Bao Bao and the Sleepy Dough

**Main Character:** Bao Bao (bread baker)
**Guest Characters:** Hong Dou
**Setting:** A midnight bakery
**Theme:** Rise with time
**Word Spotlight:** **mianbao** (myen-baow) — bread

---

At exactly midnight, when the rest of the night market had pulled its blankets up to its chin, one little window still glowed warm and gold. It belonged to a bakery no bigger than a teapot, and inside it stood Bao Bao — a pale-gold, pillowy bun of a baker with a floury apron and cheeks so soft you wanted to poke them (but you mustn't, it's rude to poke a baker).

Bao Bao was waiting. Bao Bao was always waiting. Waiting was practically Bao Bao's hobby.

Suddenly — *bonk-a-bonk-a-bonk!* — a small round somebody came tumbling through the flap of the door and rolled straight into a sack of flour with a soft *poof*.

"I'm awake!" announced the round somebody, sneezing out a tiny flour cloud. "I'm Hong Dou, and I want to learn to make **mianbao** (myen-baow) — bread! And I want to learn it RIGHT NOW, this very second, immediately, please and thank you!"

Bao Bao smiled the slow smile of someone who has never once done anything *this very second.*

"Good evening, Red Bean," said Bao Bao warmly. "Bread is wonderful. But I should warn you about one tiny thing."

---

"What tiny thing?" Hong Dou bounced. Red Bean was a glossy, plump little bean the color of a sunset, round as a marble and twice as excitable.

"Bread is sleepy," said Bao Bao. "It loves to nap. And you cannot rush a nap." Bao Bao gave a cozy wink. "Good things rise with time."

"I am NOT a sleepy sort of bean," said Hong Dou, vibrating slightly.

"We'll see," said Bao Bao. "First — we wake up the **jiaomu** (jyow-moo) — the yeast."

Bao Bao set down a little bowl of warm water — *warm,* mind you, never hot, the kind of warm a kitten's tummy is. Into it went a spoonful of sugar and a sprinkle of tiny, sandy, sleepy yeast grains.

"Now we wait for it to foam," said Bao Bao.

"WAIT?" gasped Hong Dou, as if Bao Bao had asked the bean to eat a sock.

"Just ten minutes. The yeast is having breakfast. When it's full and happy, it burps."

"Yeast *burps*?"

"Politely," said Bao Bao. "Watch."

They watched. Hong Dou watched so hard the bean went cross-eyed. And slowly — *blip... blip... blip-blip-blip* — the bowl grew a soft, foamy hat, like a tiny cappuccino, bubbling and breathing.

"It's ALIVE!" whispered Hong Dou, delighted. "It's a foam beard! Hello, foam beard!"

"That's how you know it's working," said Bao Bao. "Sleepy yeast says no. Foamy yeast says yes."

Next came the big bowl. Bao Bao tipped in a soft white mountain of flour, a small pinch of **yan** (yen) — salt — and a little glug of oil so the bread would be tender.

"Salt?" said Hong Dou. "In bread? But salt is for soup!"

"A pinch makes the bread taste like *bread* instead of like a cloud," said Bao Bao. "Trust the pinch."

Then they poured the foamy, burpy yeast water right into the middle of the flour mountain, and Bao Bao swirled it all together with a wooden spoon until it became a shaggy, lumpy, sticky, ridiculous-looking dough.

"That is the ugliest thing I have ever seen," said Hong Dou, with enormous respect.

"It gets uglier before it gets prettier," said Bao Bao. "That's called *kneading.* Hop in."

---

What followed was the noisiest part of the night.

Kneading, it turns out, is when you push the dough with the heel of your hand, fold it over, give it a quarter-turn, and push again. Push, fold, turn. Push, fold, turn. Bao Bao did it like a slow, peaceful dance. Hong Dou did it like a tiny bean in a wrestling match.

"Take — THAT — dough!" puffed Hong Dou, rolling clean over the top of it. "I'll — knead — you — into — submission!"

"Gentler," laughed Bao Bao. "You're not fighting it. You're tucking it in."

"I am tucking it in *aggressively,*" said Hong Dou, now wearing the dough as a hat.

But here's the funny thing about kneading: the more they did it, the smoother and stretchier and bouncier the dough became, until at last it was soft as Bao Bao's own cheek and didn't stick to anybody's fingers at all. It even sprang back when you poked it, going *boop.*

"Boop," said Hong Dou.

"*Boop,*" agreed Bao Bao. "Now — the first nap."

Bao Bao rolled the dough into a tidy ball, tucked it into a clean bowl, covered it with a cozy cloth like a little blanket, and set it in a warm corner.

"How long is the nap?" asked Hong Dou.

"About an hour. Until it's twice as big."

Hong Dou's whole face fell. "An *hour?* I can't wait an hour. I will *expire.* I will turn to dust. They'll find me here as an ancient bean fossil and put me in a museum—"

"Then we'll wait *together,*" said Bao Bao gently. "That's the secret. Waiting is lonely. Rising is friendly."

So they sat by the warm window, and Bao Bao told sleepy stories about loaves of long ago, and the lantern glow wobbled, and somewhere in the dark the dough breathed in slow and grew and grew. The yeast was burping its quiet, busy burps, blowing the dough up like a balloon from the inside.

When Bao Bao lifted the cloth — *gasp!* — the dough had puffed up DOUBLE, soft and domed and proud.

"It got fat!" cried Hong Dou. "It napped and it got fat! That's the best kind of nap!"

"Now we press the air out — gently — and shape it into a loaf," said Bao Bao.

They patted it down (it sighed out a tiny *pffft*, which made Hong Dou laugh so hard the bean fell off the table), then folded it into a smooth, plump log and laid it snug in a bread tin.

"And now," said Bao Bao, "the *second* nap."

Hong Dou flopped over dramatically. "Another one? This bread sleeps more than my grandmother!"

But this time — and Bao Bao noticed this with a secret, proud little smile — Hong Dou didn't complain so much. The bean curled up by the warm tin and watched the dough rise again, slow and patient, like a friend keeping company. The bouncing had gone all soft and dozy.

"You're learning," whispered Bao Bao.

"Mmf," said Hong Dou, who was nearly asleep.

When the loaf had risen tall and pillowy a second time, it was finally ready for the one part Bao Bao never, ever did alone.

"This next bit is HOT," said Bao Bao seriously. "The oven is roaring like a friendly dragon. So we do the smart thing — we go and wake up Grandpa Stove-Tender, who is a grown-up, and we ask very nicely for help."

And they did! Grandpa Stove-Tender, big and warm and yawny, slid the tin into the hot oven with his thick mitts while Bao Bao and Hong Dou cheered from a safe, sensible distance.

"Good thinking, you two," he rumbled. "Hot things are grown-up jobs."

---

The bakery filled with the most magnificent smell in the entire world — warm, toasty, golden, the smell of *almost.* Hong Dou pressed against the glass and watched the loaf turn from pale to honey to a deep, glossy, gorgeous gold.

When Grandpa Stove-Tender lifted it out (with his mitts, of course) and *thunk* — knocked the bottom — it sounded hollow, like a tiny drum.

"That hollow knock means it's done," said Bao Bao. "Now the hardest wait of all. We let it cool."

"I cannot believe there is MORE waiting," moaned Hong Dou — but the bean was grinning now.

"If we slice it too soon, it squishes," said Bao Bao. "Cool bread slices clean. Good things rise with time... and *rest* with time, too."

So they waited one last cozy while. And when at last Grandpa Stove-Tender brought the bread knife (a sharp grown-up tool, so *he* did the slicing), the loaf fell open in soft, steamy, fluffy slices — pale and tender and full of tiny airy holes, each one a place where the yeast had burped a happy burp.

Bao Bao spread one warm slice with sweet red bean paste — Hong Dou's very favorite — and they sat by the glowing window and ate their mianbao together as the sky turned the softest pre-morning blue.

"It was worth the wait," Hong Dou admitted, mouth full, eyes happy.

"It always is," said Bao Bao. "Good things rise with time."

And the sleepiest bean in the whole night market leaned against the warmest baker in the whole night market, and the bread was perfect, and so was the quiet.

## How to Make bread 🍽️
*Ask a grown-up to help with anything hot or sharp!*
1. Foam the yeast: stir a spoonful of yeast and a little sugar into warm (not hot!) water and wait about 10 minutes until it gets a foamy top.
2. Make the dough: mix flour, a pinch of salt, and a little oil with the foamy yeast water until it forms a shaggy ball.
3. Knead it: push, fold, and turn the dough for several minutes until it's smooth, stretchy, and springs back when poked.
4. First rise: cover the dough with a cloth and let it nap in a warm spot for about an hour, until it doubles in size.
5. Shape it: gently press out the air and fold the dough into a smooth loaf, then place it in a bread tin.
6. Second rise: cover and let it nap again until it puffs up tall.
7. Bake it: ask a grown-up to put the tin in a hot oven and bake until the loaf is golden and sounds hollow when knocked.
8. Cool and slice: let the bread cool, then have a grown-up slice it. Spread with your favorite topping and share!
