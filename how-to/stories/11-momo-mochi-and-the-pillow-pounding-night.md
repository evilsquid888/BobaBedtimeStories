# Story 11: Momo and the Pounding Night

**Main Character:** Momo Mochi (mochi)
**Guest Characters:** Mochi
**Setting:** A moonlit garden
**Theme:** Soft is strong
**Word Spotlight:** もち (mochi) = pounded rice cake

---

On the softest night of the whole year, when the moon hung in the garden like a giant rice ball someone had forgotten to eat, Momo Mochi did a cartwheel and landed on her own face. This did not hurt one bit. Momo was a plump, pearl-white **もち** (mochi) = pounded rice cake, and being pounded rice cake meant she was squishy as a marshmallow pillow that had eaten another pillow for breakfast. She bounced. She wobbled. She giggled so hard a dewdrop fell off a leaf in surprise.

"Watch THIS," said Momo, and she squished herself completely flat, then *boing*ed back into a perfect round.

"How are you SO soft?" asked her little cousin, a tiny new mochi named — well — Mochi, who had only been a rice cake for about three hours and was still very lumpy and unsure.

Momo grinned. "Soft," she whispered, "is the strongest thing in the whole garden. Want me to teach you how it's made?"

---

Little Mochi's eyes went round as two sesame seeds. "You can MAKE more of us? On purpose?"

"On purpose, by moonlight, the old way," said Momo, and she rolled — literally rolled, like a happy snowball — toward the middle of the garden where a stone table waited under the moon. On it sat a bowl of pale paste, a tin of powdery starch, a little dish of sweet red bean filling, and one very sleepy grown-up firefly named Lumi who ran the garden's only stove.

"First things first," said Momo. "Making mochi starts with **mochiko** (moh-chee-koh) = sweet rice flour, all mixed with water into a paste. See this bowl? That's the paste, before it becomes us. Right now it's pale and sloppy and a little grumpy, like somebody woken up too early."

The paste blooped a sad little bubble. *Blurp.*

"Hello, future cousin," said Mochi politely, and bowed to the bowl.

"Now," Momo went on, "the paste has to be **STEAMED** until it turns glossy and stretchy. And steam is HOT — hot enough to say ouch and mean it. So this is a job for a grown-up."

She turned and called, "Lumi! We need your warm bits!"

Lumi the firefly blinked awake, her tail glowing a cozy orange. "Steam duty?" she yawned. "My absolute favorite." She fluttered the bowl over to the little stove, popped it above a pot of bubbling water, and set a lid on top. "You two stay back and supervise from the soft seats," she said. "Hot steam is for fireflies, not for fluffballs."

So Momo and Mochi plopped onto two squishy mushroom cushions and supervised *extremely* hard, which mostly meant wiggling and asking "is it ready yet" eleven times.

"Patience," said Momo, doing her best to sound wise and only slightly bouncing. "Good things take steam. You can't rush a rice cake. Trust me, I've tried — I once jumped in the pot to hurry myself up and came out the exact same speed, just dizzier."

Mochi laughed so hard he fell off his mushroom and landed with a soft *fwump*. He did not get hurt either. That's the lovely thing about being soft: the ground is mostly just a friendly suggestion.

After a while, Lumi lifted the lid, and a curl of steam danced up like a tiny silver dragon. The paste inside had changed. It was no longer pale and grumpy — now it was *glossy*, shiny as the moon's own reflection, stretching and gleaming and stuck-togethery.

"Ohhh," breathed Mochi. "It's all shimmery now."

"That's how you know it's done," said Momo. "Pale and sloppy means *not yet*. Glossy and stretchy means *yes, please*. Lumi, set our shiny friend down to cool just a smidge — too hot to touch is still a grown-up's job."

Lumi set the bowl on the cool stone and shooed them with a wing. "Wait till it's warm-not-hot," she said. "Count to a hundred mushrooms."

They counted to a hundred mushrooms. (They got to forty, then started over twice because Mochi kept sneezing the number, but the spirit was correct.)

When the glossy paste was warm instead of hot, Momo clapped her squishy hands. "Now comes the fun part. Step two: we **DUST** everything with starch so nothing sticks." She grabbed the tin of fine white powder — **片栗粉** (katakuriko) = potato starch — and gave it a shake over the table. *Poof!* A snowy cloud drifted down and settled over the stone, over the bowl, over Mochi, over Momo's nose, over the entire idea of staying clean.

"Why the powder?" Mochi sneezed a small white sneeze.

"Because mochi is sticky," said Momo, "and sticky is wonderful for hugs but terrible for tables. The starch is a tickly little jacket. It keeps us from gluing ourselves to the whole garden." She dusted her hands. "Watch your fingers stay un-stuck. See? Magic powder."

Then, with starch-jacketed hands, Momo scooped a warm glossy blob from the bowl and plopped it onto the dusted stone. She pressed it down — *squidge* — until it flattened into a soft round disc, like a tiny moon pancake.

"That's a round," she said. "Flatten the warm dough into little rounds, one for each cousin you want to make. Big enough to wrap a treat inside, small enough to fit in a happy mouth. Your turn, Mochi."

Mochi reached in with his powdery hands, pulled out his very own glossy blob, and pressed. It came out lopsided, with one fat lump and one thin edge, looking less like a moon and more like a moon that had stubbed its toe.

"Mine's *wonky*," he said, ears drooping.

"Mine was wonky too, my first hundred times," said Momo warmly. "Soft means you can always squish it round again. That's not a mistake, cousin — that's a second chance with frosting on it." She gently helped him pat the edges even, and *poof*, a proper little round.

Now the table was lined with soft warm discs, glowing faintly in the moonlight. Momo rubbed her hands together like a tiny chef about to do something marvelous.

"Last steps," she announced. "Step three: the **TREASURE**." She lifted the dish of sweet red bean filling — **紅豆** (hóng dòu) = red bean — dark and shiny and sweet as a secret. "We put a little spoon of this right in the middle of each round. This is the cozy heart. The reason anybody bothers."

She set a small scoop of red bean in the center of a disc. Mochi did the same on his, and only ate a *tiny* bit first, which Momo pretended very loudly not to notice.

"And step four: we **SEAL** them up." Momo folded the soft edges of her disc up and over the red bean, gathering them at the top and pinching them shut, then rolling the whole thing between her palms. The seam vanished. What was flat and open became a smooth, plump, perfect little ball — a soft white moon with a sweet secret hidden snug inside.

"It's all closed up," whispered Mochi, amazed. "You can't even see where it went in. It's just... soft all the way around, holding the sweet part safe."

"That," said Momo softly, "is the whole entire point. The soft outside isn't weak. It's the strongest hug there is. It wraps right around the good stuff and keeps it cozy forever. Soft is strong, little one. Soft is the thing that holds." She gave one last roll, then dusted the finished ball lightly so it wouldn't stick. "And ALWAYS pat off the extra starch at the end, or you'll cough a snow cloud when you bite in."

Mochi sealed his own. His seam showed a little. His ball was a smidge potato-shaped. He looked up, worried.

But Momo just popped it onto the moonlit plate next to her own and beamed. "Beautiful," she said, and she meant every word. "Every mochi is a little different. That's how we tell each other apart at family reunions."

So under the soft, round, forgetful-rice-ball moon, the two cousins lined up their finished **もち** (mochi) = pounded rice cake on a leaf-green plate — plump and dusted and glowing — and Lumi the firefly dimmed her tail to a candle-flicker so they could see the moon shine on the sugar.

They each picked one up. They each took a bite. The outside was pillow-soft, the inside was sweet-bean-warm, and the whole garden seemed to sigh a happy, full-bellied sigh.

"Soft is strong," said Mochi, with red bean on his cheek and the biggest sleepy smile in the world.

"Now *and* always," said Momo, and she pulled her little cousin into a squish so soft that they bounced.

## How to Make mochi 🍽️
*Ask a grown-up to help with anything hot or sharp!*
1. Mix sweet rice flour (mochiko) with water into a paste. A grown-up steams it until it turns glossy and stretchy, then lets it cool to warm-not-hot.
2. Dust the table and your hands with starch (so nothing sticks), then press the warm dough into little flat rounds.
3. Put a small spoon of sweet red bean filling in the center of each round.
4. Fold the soft edges up over the filling and pinch them shut, then roll into a smooth ball. Dust off the extra starch — and share!
