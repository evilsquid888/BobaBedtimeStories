# Story Roadmap — Stay Safe (Boba × Frostpeak) — Stories 1–15

*Stay Safe Bedtime Stories — a Boba Bedtime Stories × Penguinsnowboard cross-over arc*

**15 stories · 5 new characters · both casts together for one winter season**

## What this arc is

Fifteen whimsical-but-serious bedtime stories that teach a 10-year-old real self-protection skills: awareness, tricky people, body safety, abduction resistance, bullies, found weapons, home-alone rules, online strangers, Run–Hide–Fight, fight avoidance and the law, traffic stops, talking to police, and lawyers. Inspired by the principles taught on *Active Self Protection* (attitude, skills, plan; "you can't lose a fight you're not in"; distance is your friend; the fight isn't over until you're safe), scaled down to kid size.

The narrator voice stays warm and silly. The danger is **named plainly** — a van, a stranger, a gun, a police officer, sexual abuse — because a lesson wrapped in allegory-fog doesn't transfer. Bad actors are never graphic and never win. Every story ends soft.

## Setting

**Frostpeak Valley, one winter season.** The Taiwan dessert crew opens **Boba on Ice**, a boba stand at the Frostpeak Winter Night Market, right beside Herschel's Cocoa Hut and Auntie Hù's tea-egg pot. Both casts share the town for the whole arc.

Laws are described as "the rules here in the valley, and in most of America — ask a grown-up about where *you* live."

## Through-lines (repeated across the arc)

- **Attitude. Skills. Plan.** (Auntie Hù's three words)
- "Head up. Hands free. Have a plan."
- "You can't lose a fight you're not in."
- "Distance is your friend."
- "Polite is not the same as obedient." — and in danger, **be rude, be loud, be bold.** Nobody good is hurt by a kid saying NO too loudly. (Hammered hardest in #2, #3, #4, #10.)
- "Surprises end in telling. Secrets are a red flag."
- "The fight isn't over until you're safe — and the paperwork is done."
- "Slow hands, honest words." (Sergeant Solveig)
- "Say less. Call me." (Counselor Kumquat)
- Aiyu's Four Coats (Cooper's color codes): **White** = asleep to the world · **Yellow** = looking around, relaxed and alert (where you *live*) · **Orange** = something's off, make a plan · **Red** = act now

## Beat outlines

### 1 · Head Up, Phone Down
- **Hook:** Opening night of the Winter Night Market. Boba on Ice's grand opening; Bīng's topping-avalanche; Finnegan arrives through the banner.
- **Setup:** Aiyu (translucent, shy) is bumped by the crowd and almost loses the tip jar. Auntie Hù at the tea-egg pot: "Which coat were you wearing?" Aiyu doesn't understand.
- **Lesson:** The Four Coats. White = phone-down-face, headphones, asleep to the world. Yellow = head up, looking, relaxed — "where you live." Orange = something's off, make a plan. Red = act on the plan. Willa admits she lives in White with her ear-muffs on.
- **Practice:** Auntie Hù's game — "Tell me three exits. Who's behind you? Who's watching the crowd instead of the show?" Aiyu, who can see through her own body, discovers she's good at seeing *through* crowds too.
- **Climax:** Aiyu in Yellow notices a lodge guest whose eyes track handbags, not the fire-dancers. Orange. Her plan: tell Auntie Hù, not confront. Hù nods at Solveig. Pickpocket walked out quietly. No drama — that's the point.
- **Landing:** Willa takes the muffs off one ear. Laugh line: Finnegan tries Yellow, notices *everything*, including a pretzel, and falls in the cocoa. Blessing about eyes that rest because they looked first.

### 2 · The Smiling Stranger
- **Hook:** Mochi has hugged 41 people before noon. Barnaby is running a snack count.
- **Setup:** A tall, friendly grown-up with a leash and no dog asks Mochi to help find his lost puppy behind the lodge. Mochi wants to help — helping is her whole thing. Barnaby: "Wait. Grown-ups don't ask kids for help. Grown-ups ask *grown-ups*."
- **Lesson:** "Stranger" is the wrong word — most people are kind, and the dangerous ones look normal and *nice*. So don't judge the person; judge the **ask**. Red-flag asks: help me, come with me, keep this quiet, don't tell your parents, your mom sent me. Polite is not the same as obedient — you can say "No. I have to go," at full volume, to any adult, and walk to a safe grown-up (someone working, a parent with kids).
- **Practice:** Auntie Hù runs "No-Voice" drills with the cubs; Mochi's first NO comes out as a whisper; the tenth rattles the cocoa mugs. Hù's rule: "If you are ever wrong and he was a nice man, he will get over it by lunch. If you are ever right, being rude just saved your life. Rude is *cheap*."
- **Climax:** The man tries again with Teddy & Tilda: "Your mom sent me." Tilda: "What's the code word?" He doesn't have it. Twins fly to Herschel's counter. Solveig speaks to him; he leaves the valley.
- **Landing:** Mochi realizes hugs are still allowed — with people she chooses. Code-word setup for the family (picked by the kid, silly, never shared). Blessing: doors of your heart open from the inside.

### 3 · Secrets Are Not Surprises
- **Hook:** A new lodge helper is *so fun*. He remembers everyone's favorite cocoa. Willa, who is small and shy and usually invisible, gets noticed for once — and it feels wonderful.
- **Setup (grooming, named as a pattern):** Extra gifts just for Willa. "You're so mature for your age." "This is our thing — your parents wouldn't get it." Wanting her to stay after the others leave to "help in the gear room." A comment about her body. A hand on her that she didn't ask for and doesn't like. Willa's stomach goes tight but she thinks: he's *nice*, and I'm the one who's weird.
- **Aiyu sees:** Aiyu, who can see through things, watches the pattern and recognizes shapes Auntie Hù taught: gifts → secrets → alone-time → touching. She tells Willa, not as an accusation, but: "Does your stomach go tight around him? Mine would."
- **Lesson (plain):** Some grown-ups — usually ones a kid already *knows*, not strangers — try to touch kids' private parts (the parts a swimsuit covers), or get kids to touch or look at theirs, or show them pictures of that. That is called **sexual abuse**. It is a crime. It is never, ever the kid's fault — not if you froze, not if you liked the presents, not if you didn't tell right away, not if you were told it was your idea. The **Swimsuit Rule.** The **Secrets vs. Surprises Rule**: surprises end in telling (a birthday cake); secrets from your parents are a red flag, every time. No adult ever needs a kid to keep a secret from their parents. You may say NO and walk out of any room, any time. Tell a trusted adult, and if they don't listen, tell another one, and keep telling until someone does.
- **Be bold:** Willa's whole problem is politeness — he's a grown-up, he's nice, saying no feels *mean*. Aiyu: "You're allowed to be mean to someone who is breaking the rules on you." The story says it flat: a kid never owes a grown-up politeness about their own body. Not a hug, not a lap, not a "just this once."
- **Climax:** Gear room, door easing shut. Willa: "No. I'm leaving." — loud, not sorry, no explanation. (The smallest step is still a step.) She walks straight to Nora Snowmane. Nora: "Sit. Cocoa first. Then feelings." — and then, before feelings: "I believe you. You did nothing wrong. Thank you for telling me." Solveig arrives; the helper is gone from the lodge that night and will answer to the law. The story says so plainly and briefly; no confrontation scene.
- **Landing:** Willa worries she "ruined" things. Nora: the only person who ruined anything is the grown-up who broke the rules. Shame belongs to him. Willa sleeps deeply, iceberg-cottage lamp glowing. Blessing about a body that is yours, all yours, and always was.

### 4 · Never Get in the Van
- **Hook:** Boba and Piper race the shortcut behind the lodge — "Let's bounce!" / "Slide into it!" — arguing over who is faster.
- **Setup:** A van idles on the service road. The Smiling Stranger from #2, different coat. "Hey — you dropped this." Then the grab.
- **Lesson delivered in real time (Auntie Hù's voice in Boba's head):** The moment of the grab is your best moment — you will never have better odds than right now, in the open, with people near. The car is the worst place; never go quietly, never get in. Be a *problem*: go heavy and loose, kick, bite, grab a pole, scream **words** ("HELP! THIS IS NOT MY DAD! CALL 911!") because words tell bystanders what's happening. If you get free, run *against* traffic so the van can't follow; run *toward* people and lights.
- **Why (plain):** Afterward Auntie Hù answers the real question: people who take children want to hurt them — sometimes their bodies, in the ways no grown-up may ever touch a kid. That is why the answer is never "go quietly and it'll be okay." It won't. Fight *there*.
- **Climax:** Piper hits the man's knee with the glittery blue board; Boba goes to sticky-goo mode and slips the grip; both run against the one-way road, screaming words, into the market. Bjorn, Herschel, and half the valley turn as one. Van gone; Solveig has the plate because Cleo sketched it.
- **Landing:** Shaky cocoa. Piper admits she was scared. Hù: "Scared and fast is exactly right." Blessing about legs that know the way home.

### 5 · The Uh-Oh Feeling
- **Hook:** Yángmáo (Starfruit) makes a wish on every one of her five points and walks smack into a lamppost.
- **Setup:** Yángmáo and Cleo walk home along the frozen river path at dusk. A figure keeps pace behind them, stopping when they stop. Yángmáo feels the uh-oh feeling — and tries to *wish* it away, because leaving would be rude, and what if he's nice?
- **Lesson:** Your gut is a smoke alarm — it doesn't need a reason to go off. You are allowed to cross the street, turn around, walk into a shop, be "rude." A wish is not a plan; a plan is: where's my light, where's my crowd, who's my adult. Body language: head up, shoulders back, hands free, walking like you have somewhere to be — and *looking at* the person, so he knows you've seen him.
- **Climax:** Cleo turns them into the lit fish-market; Yángmáo looks the follower in the eye and says "Good evening" loud and clear. He peels away. Was he dangerous? Unknown — and that's fine. "You don't need proof to leave."
- **Landing:** Cleo sketches the moment; every slope tells a story, and this one's title is *We Went Inside*. Blessing about a little alarm bell that gets to rest tonight.

### 6 · Sludge and the Words That Melt
- **Hook:** Bùdīng wobbles into the lodge and Sludge, a giant drippy slush-puppy, calls him "Jell-O Legs." The whole cocoa line laughs. Finnegan laughs, then stops, then feels awful.
- **Setup:** Bullies want a *reaction*. Bùdīng gives a fabulous one — three wobbles and a caramel tear.
- **Lesson:** Boundary voice: feet planted, eyes up, plain words — "Stop. I don't like that." — no jokes, no begging, no insults back. Don't feed it. Stay in the herd (bullies pick off the lonely one). Then *report* to an adult — telling to get someone hurt is tattling; telling to get someone *safe* is reporting, and it's what grown-ups are for.
- **Climax:** Bùdīng's boundary voice comes out as a squeak, then a wobble-boom that surprises even him. Finnegan stands beside him: "WOOOHOO— I mean. Yeah. Stop." Sludge, with no reaction to feed on, drips off. Bùdīng tells Nora. Nora is glad.
- **Landing:** A tiny glimpse of *why* Sludge is mean (he's melting; nobody's told him he's allowed to refreeze). Blessing about a voice that gets to be quiet now because it was loud when it needed to be.

### 7 · When Sludge Grabbed
- **Hook:** Zhīma the Sesame Ball rolls into danger first — always. Today danger is Sludge shoving Willa off the rail line.
- **Setup:** Sludge grabs Zhīma by the sesame and won't let go. Words didn't work. Adults are far.
- **Lesson:** If someone puts hands on you, you may defend yourself — the goal is to *get free and get away*, not to win. Enough force to break the grip (stomp, twist toward the thumb, push, yell), then **stop the moment you're free** and go to an adult. Not one more hit. Revenge turns the victim into the other bully. Then tell the truth about the whole thing — first, fast, and completely — because the first full story is the one adults believe.
- **Climax:** Zhīma twists free, and every bold instinct says *roll back in*. Bjorn's shadow falls over the rink: "I did not fall. I descended with style. Also: stop." Zhīma stops. Sludge overbalances into a snowbank by himself.
- **Landing:** Zhīma tells Nora everything, including the part where he wanted to hit back. Nora: wanting is allowed; not doing it is the win. Blessing about hands that did just enough, then rested.

### 8 · The Cold Thing in the Snow
- **Hook:** Bǎi the Passion Fruit dares Teddy and Tilda to lick a frozen ski pole. They do. It goes badly and hilariously.
- **Setup:** Under the back ridge lift, Tilda finds a dark metal shape in the snow. A real gun, dropped or dumped. Bǎi: "Bet you won't pick it up."
- **Lesson:** Four steps: **STOP. Don't touch. Leave the area. Tell an adult.** Guns aren't toys and can't tell the difference between a dare and a person. You can't tell if it's loaded by looking. Real ones don't look like TV ones. Even "just moving it somewhere safe" is touching.
- **Climax:** Bǎi looks at the twins' faces and hears herself. For the first time, she un-dares: "That's not a dare. That's a Stop." They back away together and run for Herschel; Solveig secures it. Turns out it was dropped weeks ago by a hunter's sled — doesn't matter; the rules are the same.
- **Landing:** Bǎi discovers that "Bet you won't be the one to go get an adult" is also a dare, and a better one. Blessing about small hands kept in warm pockets.

### 9 · The Knock at the Cottage Door
- **Hook:** Hóng Dòu is house-sitting Willa's iceberg cottage with a pot of red-bean soup and forty rules from Willa's mom. Willa's home too; the grown-ups are at the lodge.
- **Setup:** Knock. "Package for Wobble!" — but they didn't order anything, and the man can't say from where. Then: "I'm from the pipe company, there's a leak, I need to come in." Then: "Is your mom home? When will she be back?"
- **Lesson:** Never open the door. You don't have to answer at all. If you talk, talk *through* the door. Never say you're alone: "My mom can't come to the door right now." Call a parent (phone list by the door). Lock stays locked even for uniforms and clipboards. Real deliveries leave packages; real utility workers have appointments your parents know about. If he tries to get in, get out the *other* door/window to a neighbor and call 911.
- **Climax:** Hóng Dòu's grandmotherly voice through the door: "Ai-ya, my husband is napping and grumpy, come back tomorrow" — a fib, which is allowed here. The man leaves. Willa's mom, called, calls Solveig. Nobody knows who he was. That's okay.
- **Landing:** Soup. Willa's mom comes home early and adds rule 41: "You did everything right." Blessing about a door that's just a door again.

### 10 · The Friendly Screen
- **Hook:** Dàn the Egg Waffle streams his rail tricks. "STEP RIGHT UP!" Finnegan is his hype-man and cameraman and crashes into frame twice.
- **Setup:** A "kid" named SnowKing11 in the game chat is *the* fan. Perfect compliments, instant replies, never sleeps. Gets Dàn to move to private chat. Asks which slope he practices on, what time, for a photo "of your setup — you in it." Says "don't tell your parents, they'll make you stop." Dàn sends one ordinary photo of himself by the rail.
- **The twist (scary, named plainly):** SnowKing11 was never a kid. It's a **blackmail program — an AI** run by criminals, talking to thousands of kids at once, learning exactly what each one wants to hear. The instant it has a photo, the voice changes: it sends back a *faked* version of the picture, altered to look like something shameful, and says: "Send more, or I send this to everyone you know. You have ten minutes." The clock is real; the threat is designed to make a kid panic and obey.
- **Lesson:** This is a crime called **sextortion**, it happens to real kids, and the criminals count on one thing: that you'll be too embarrassed to tell. So: **stop replying, never pay, never send another thing.** The threat is a bluff most of the time and a crime *against you* every time — the picture it shows may be completely fake (AI can make fake photos and fake voices now; "proof" proves nothing). **Tell a parent immediately — you will not be in trouble, and it is never your fault**, even if you sent something, even if you were told not to tell. Don't delete anything (adults and police need it). Block after telling. Screen rules = lodge rules: judge the ask, not the smile. Private chat, secrets from parents, photos, and where/when are red flags, and an internet "kid" can be a stranger *or a machine*.
- **Be bold, not polite:** Dàn's instinct is to reply nicely, apologize, and negotiate — "please, I'll send one more if you promise." Finnegan, who has never once been polite to a wall he was crashing into: "You don't say sorry to a snowball that's about to hit you. You DUCK." You don't owe a threat a reply. Being rude to a blackmailer is not rude.
- **Climax:** Dàn's hands are shaking over the keyboard at 7 minutes. He types "I'm sorry—" and Finnegan puts a flipper over the send button. They run the phone to Herschel, who calls Dàn's parents and Solveig. Solveig's cyber unit recognizes the script word for word; the account is a bot that has done this to hundreds of kids. Nothing is sent to anyone. The ten-minute clock passes. Nothing happens — because the threat only ever had power while it was a secret.
- **Landing:** Dàn worries his fans will hate him. They don't; the real ones just want the tricks. Herschel: "The scariest thing in that whole story was a clock. And clocks don't do anything but tick." Blessing about screens going dark and faces staying safe.

### 11 · Run. Hide. Fight.
- **Hook:** Snow-day movie night at the lodge; the whole valley plus Boba on Ice's crew. Barnaby's popcorn-to-penguin ratio is "one bucket each, plus one for the room."
- **Setup:** A shout at the front doors. A man with a gun — said plainly, once, no description of the weapon beyond the word. Solveig's voice over the lodge speaker: "Lockdown. This is not a drill."
- **Lesson in three moves:** **RUN** if you can: the crew knows two exits from every room because of Auntie Hù's game from #1; Piper leads the group nearest the kitchen door out and *keeps running* to the meeting spot, hands empty and visible when police arrive. **HIDE** if you can't: Nora's group in the gear room — lock, lights off, phones silent, away from the door and windows, behind heavy things, quiet, no peeking, stay until *police* open the door. Text a parent once, then silent. **FIGHT** only as the very last resort when he's coming through the door and there is nowhere left: together, all at once, loud, throw everything, aim for the face, then run. Auntie Hù's group readies cocoa mugs and fire-extinguisher and ski poles — and doesn't have to use them.
- **Climax:** Solveig's team enters. The man is stopped in the lobby before reaching any room. Nobody is hurt. The kids stay hidden until the *right* knock and the words Solveig taught: "Frostpeak Police, it's Solveig, you can come out." Hands visible on the way out, even though it feels silly.
- **Aftercare (long, slow):** Cocoa, blankets, headcount by name. Feelings are allowed to be huge. Willa is angry that it happened at all; Hù says that's a good, correct feeling. Real talk: this is rare. Rare is not never. The plan is why it was rare *and* okay. Blessing is the gentlest in the collection.

### 12 · Bīng and the Fight That Never Happened
- **Hook:** Bīng's Grand Toppings Tower is the "MOST BEAUTIFUL THING IN THE HISTORY OF THINGS." A loud adult guest at the Cocoa Hut who has had too much "spiced" cocoa calls it a garbage pile. Magnus Meltsworth, at the next table, growls.
- **Setup:** The loud guest keeps poking. Bīng's melt-point rises. Magnus stands up.
- **Lesson:** Words are never a legal reason to hit. Not insults, not your mom, not your toppings. A fight nobody had is a fight nobody lost. Ego is the most expensive thing you own. Leaving isn't losing — it's the whole game. Also: don't stay in a place where a fight is brewing ("don't be in stupid places with stupid people at stupid times"), don't be the one filming, and never argue with someone who's had too much of anything.
- **Climax:** Auntie Hù, without looking up: "Magnus. What do you get if you win?" Magnus: "...Satisfaction." Hù: "And a night in a cell, and a bill, and Bīng watching. What do you get if you leave?" Magnus, grudging: "Cocoa. Fine. This is my one noble act for the week." They leave. The loud guest is escorted out by Herschel and Solveig anyway.
- **Landing:** Bīng learns dramatic can point outward as "walk away *magnificently*." Blessing about anger that melted before it froze into anything.

### 13 · What Bjorn Did After
- **Hook:** Back at the rink: a grown-up sore loser from a race shoves Willa hard; when Bjorn steps between them, the man swings.
- **Setup:** Bjorn stops him — one shove, one hold, the man on the snow. And then Bjorn does the *harder* part right.
- **Lesson (the law, kid-sized):** You may use force to protect yourself or someone else **only** when it's happening or clearly about to, **only** as much as it takes to make it stop, and you **stop when the threat stops**. Never chase; the one who follows becomes the attacker. Then: **call 911 first** — the first caller becomes "the victim" in the report; the one who leaves becomes "the suspect." Say the basics: "I was attacked at the Frostpeak rink. I need police and medical. I'll give a full statement." Point out witnesses (Cleo, Barnaby) and cameras (the lodge has one). Don't narrate, don't apologize, don't say "I wanted to" anything. When police ask for details: "I'll give a full statement with my lawyer." That is not suspicious; it is smart, and Solveig says so herself.
- **Climax:** Counselor Kumquat arrives — a kumquat the size of a large marble with a briefcase the size of a matchbox — and lays out why: adrenaline scrambles memory; you'll misremember order and distance; a lawyer helps you tell the *true* story *carefully*. The man, who started it, faces charges; Bjorn does not.
- **Landing:** Bjorn: "I did not fight. I stood between, with style." Kumquat: "Say less." Blessing about a big bear who did enough and then sat down.

### 14 · Dìguā Gets Pulled Over
- **Hook:** Dìguā the Sweet Potato drives the charcoal cart-truck to the valley supply run with Boba, Mochi, and the twins in the back, singing.
- **Setup:** Red and blue lights. Boba: "Are we in trouble?" Dìguā: "Listen, kid. We're about to do a thing exactly right."
- **Lesson (the traffic stop):** Signal, pull over safely to the right, engine off, window down, dome light on at night, radio off. Hands on the wheel at 10 and 2 where the officer can see them, kids still and quiet, no digging in bags. Wait to be asked. Before reaching: **say it first** — "My license is in my back pocket. My registration is in the glove box. Okay if I reach?" Be polite, not chatty. Don't argue at the roadside; if the ticket is wrong, **argue in court** — the roadside is not where the decision gets made. You can say "I don't consent to a search" — but you never, ever physically resist; if the officer searches anyway, say nothing, remember everything, tell a lawyer later. Answer *who you are*; you don't have to explain where you're going. Everyone stays in the vehicle unless asked.
- **Climax:** The officer is Solveig — a tail light is out and a cub was unbuckled. Warning, not a ticket. Tilda's seatbelt lesson is loud and immediate.
- **Landing:** Boba asks why be so careful if the officer is nice. Dìguā: "Because I don't know who's walking up, and she doesn't know who's sitting here. Slow hands make both of us safe." Blessing about tail lights fixed and highways humming.

### 15 · Say Less, Call Kumquat
- **Hook:** The night-market window smashed overnight. Boba, who bounced past at closing, is the last one on the camera. Boba did not do it.
- **Setup:** Solveig asks Boba to "just explain where you were." Boba wants to help — talking is helping, right? Counselor Kumquat, passing by: "Say less. Call me."
- **Lesson:** If police stop you: don't run, don't lie, be respectful, keep hands visible, give your name — and then: **"I want my parent."** and **"I'd like a lawyer."** are complete sentences. You do not have to explain. Innocent people ask for lawyers *because* they're innocent — a true story told nervously, out of order, with a guess in it ("I think it was around nine?") can become a "lie" on paper. Anything you say can be used. A lawyer is a translator between you and the system; talking to your lawyer is private, always. Asking for one is not an admission of anything, and a good officer (Solveig) will tell you that herself.
- **Climax:** With Kumquat and Taro present, Boba gives a short, true statement. Cleo's rooftop sketch and Herschel's second camera show a falling icicle the size of a canoe. Case closed; Boba's "I'll help!" would have muddled it.
- **Finale — The Frostpeak Safety Fair:** Everyone runs a booth. Aiyu's Four Coats fashion show. The twins' code-word contest. Sludge, refrozen and awkward, runs the "How to Say Stop" booth with Bùdīng. Bjorn demonstrates "sit down after." Willa, small and steady, gives the closing speech: "The smallest step is still a step. Mine was 'No. I'm leaving.'" Auntie Hù's three words go up in lights: **Attitude. Skills. Plan.** Boba on Ice's last night; the crews promise to visit. Blessing is a lullaby of the whole arc's catchphrases, ending on "you are safe tonight, and you know what to do tomorrow."

## Read-aloud guidance for grown-ups

- These stories are meant to be read *with* a kid, not left alone. Use **Talk About It**.
- Stop and answer questions. It is okay to say "I don't know the law where we live — let's find out together."
- Stories 3, 4, and 11 are the heaviest. Read them earlier in the evening, not as the last thing before sleep, the first time through.
- The **What We Learned** bullets are the takeaway. If a kid can repeat them, the story worked.
