# Story 48: Kamaron the Garlic Shrimp and the Cheering Truck

**Main Character:** Kamaron the Garlic Shrimp
**Guest Characters:** Musubi Manu; Kai the Shave Ice; Mac the Macadamia
**Setting:** A North Shore beach-games day beside the famous garlic-shrimp trucks
**Theme:** Cheering someone else across the finish line is its own kind of winning.
**Hawaiian Spotlight:** **ho'omaika'i** (hoh-oh-my-KAH-ee) = to congratulate / to praise

---

Kamaron the Garlic Shrimp had won eleven races before breakfast, and he suspected nobody had noticed.

He was sunset-orange, dusted in sparkly garlic crumbs, with two bouncy antennae and a tail like a spring.

The whistle blew for race twelve. Kamaron fwipped his tail.

He won before the other racers had finished sneezing off the starting line.

"Twelve and zero!" he announced, panting. "I mean, eleven and one. No. Twelve and zero!"

The whole beach was looking the other way.

---

Because the real cheering, the kind that scattered seabirds off the lifeguard tower, came for the LAST finisher.

Kai the Shave Ice came puddling across the line, dead last, dripping rainbow all the way.

The garlic-shrimp truck honked its horn like a happy goose. The beach went bananas.

"Chill out and rainbow on," said Kai, melting proudly under a flower lei.

"But I won that one," said Kamaron, raising one little claw. "Twelve minutes ago. I'm right here."

Mac the Macadamia hopped past. "You smell amazing," he said kindly, then hopped off to hug the puddle.

"Thank you," Kamaron called after him. "I also WON, by the way. There was a ribbon."

---

By race nineteen, Kamaron had a trophy pile so tall it cast a shade you could nap in.

He won the three-legged race, which is impressive when you have ten legs and must tie up nine.

He won the very-serious-walking race, where you must look extremely serious. Then he looked extremely sad.

Then Mac wobbled in thirtieth out of thirty, and the truck honked, and the beach roared.

"Winning is the whole point of a race," muttered Kamaron. "It says so in the word. I think."

---

"You watch the wrong thing," said Musubi Manu, lining up water cups in perfect rows.

"You watch the finish line," said Manu. "Look at them, Kamaron. They're not watching the line at all."

Kamaron looked. Oh. Oh.

The whole beach had its back to the finish ribbon. They were watching each other.

"You cross the line alone," said Manu. "So nobody gets to cross it with you."

---

A conch sounded for the Grand Relay: everybody runs, passing one coconut down the beach to the truck.

Kai dripped at the start. Mac tucked deep in his shell. Kamaron, at the front, as always.

The whistle blew. Kamaron felt the old pull. Fwip. Be first. Touch the ribbon.

But the trophy pile cast such a lonely shade.

So Kamaron did the most surprising thing he had ever done. He stopped.

---

He turned around to face his whole wobbling team, and he started to cheer.

"GO, little nut, GO!" He flung garlic crumbs into the air like confetti.

Kai fumbled the coconut toward the waves. Kamaron skidded, flicked it back with his tail, kept cheering.

"Rainbow cone! Don't melt on me now! You've GOT this!" He galloped backward beside Kai.

Mac heard his name screamed at the start, and the middle, and every tumbling hop in between.

"**ho'omaika'i** (hoh-oh-my-KAH-ee) = to congratulate!" roared Kamaron. "I'm gonna ho'omaika'i you so loud the island hears!"

The truck honked. The seabirds scattered. Mac hopped across the line, dead, dead last.

The beach erupted.

---

This time Kamaron was in the middle of it, buried in a warm, sandy, garlicky pile of friends.

"You gave up the win," puffed Mac, peeking out of his shell. "Why?"

"First across the line is the kind you do alone," said Kamaron. "Cheering somebody across, you do together."

Manu set a water cup beside each of them, in a perfect row, and sat down too.

Kamaron, the fastest snack on the beach, had finally come in last, and it felt like first.

---
