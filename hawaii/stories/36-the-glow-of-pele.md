# Story 36: The Glow of Pele

**Main Character:** Pele's Glow (Lava the gentle ember)
**Guest Characters:** Honu Haupia, Dole Whip Dani
**Setting:** The warm, glowing edges of Kilauea volcano on the Big Island
**Theme:** Even powerful things can be gentle and welcoming.
**Hawaiian Spotlight:** pele (PEH-leh) = volcano (and the goddess of fire)

---

Now, between you and me, Dole Whip Dani had a teeny-tiny problem with the night hike up the mountain — which was that she kept melting onto her own toes.

"Whoopsie-swirl!" she giggled, scooping a drip of golden pineapple soft-serve back onto her own peak. "Okay! Okay! I am COMPLETELY in control of my drips!"

She was not completely in control of her drips.

But she WAS sun-bright and bubbly and bouncing up the dark trail like a little melty lantern, her tiny pineapple-leaf tuft wobbling with every hop. Behind her, gliding slow as the tide, came Honu Haupia — a snow-white coconut-pudding sea turtle whose jiggly shell shimmered in the starlight like a tray of tiny moons.

"Slow flippers, far journeys," Honu hummed. "And tonight, far journeys go UP. Up the warm side of the **pele** (PEH-leh) = volcano."

Dani gasped a big sparkly gasp.

"A volcano?! Is it gonna ROAR? Is it gonna RUMBLE? Are we gonna be toasted pineapple?!"

Honu's ancient eyes crinkled. "We are going to make a friend."

---

The trail wound higher, and the air grew cozy-warm, the way the kitchen feels when somebody's baking something delicious you haven't seen yet. Little curls of steam drifted up from cracks in the dark rock, soft as breath. Dani peeked over the edge of a glowing ledge — and far, far below, deep in the heart of Kilauea, something shimmered orange-gold like a sleepy sunset that had decided to live underground.

"Okay that," whispered Dani, "is the most beautiful melty thing I have ever seen, and I am MADE of melty things."

That was when a tiny voice, warm and crackly as a friendly campfire, said: "Aloha, little travelers. You came all this way... just to say hello?"

Dani shrieked and did a backflip directly into Honu's shell.

Because rising up out of a glowing crack — slow, soft, and small as a teacup — floated a little ember. It had a flickery body of orange-gold light, cooling to cozy black-and-red around the edges, and it twinkled, honest-to-goodness twinkled, like a star that had wandered down for a snuggle.

"Don't be scared," said the ember gently. "I'm Pele's Glow. I'm the smallest piece of the fire inside the mountain. And I would never, ever burn a friend." It gave a little flicker that was almost certainly a smile. "Here. Feel."

Dani peeked one sparkly eye out from behind Honu. Very, very slowly, she stretched out one drippy soft-serve finger.

The Glow was warm.

Not burny-warm. Not toasty-pineapple warm. *Hug* warm. The kind of warm that wraps all the way around your middle and tucks itself in.

"Oh," breathed Dani. "Oh, you're like... a blanket made of sunset."

"That," said Pele's Glow, "is the nicest thing anyone has said to me all week. Mostly people run away yelling." It sighed a tiny sparky sigh, and a little spark hopped off it and twirled in the air like a firefly. "I get that a lot."

"Run away?!" Dani planted her drippy hands on her cup-hips, scandalized. "From YOU? But you're the size of my breakfast! Who's scared of breakfast?!"

"You'd be surprised," said the Glow. "Last week a whole family of crabs scuttled into the sea sideways the moment they saw me coming. Sideways! And I had only floated up to say good evening." It flickered sadly. "I do love good evenings."

"Well, I think running from a good evening is just plain silly," Dani announced. "Honu! Tell the majestic cupcake that running is silly."

"It is a little silly," Honu agreed, gliding closer in her slow, kindly way. "Though I understand it." Her flippers swept slow circles in the warm air, and her haupia shell jiggled gently with each word. "When something is very, very big, the heart says *be careful* before the heart says *hello*. That is not foolishness, sunny one. That is the same caution that keeps a small turtle from swimming into a storm. But —" and here her ancient eyes twinkled — "the heart that learns to say *hello* afterward... that is the heart that finds the very best friends."

---

Honu Haupia drifted closer still, her flippers slow and her voice soft. "And why," she asked, "do you suppose they run, little Glow?"

The ember dimmed, just a flicker. "Because the **pele** is big. The mountain is big, and the fire is big, and big things look... fierce. Everyone thinks because I am powerful, I must be scary." It looked down at its own tiny twinkly self. "But I'm not scary. I'm just... busy."

"Busy?" said Dani, who had now climbed fully out of Honu's shell and was sitting cross-legged on the warm rock like she was at story time. "Busy doing WHAT? Glowing? Because you're EXCELLENT at it. Ten out of ten. Best glow."

Pele's Glow laughed — a soft crackly laugh like a log settling in a fire. "Come," it said. "Let me show you what the fire is really for. But we must go slow. Honu, you'll like this part."

"I like all slow parts," said Honu serenely. "Slow is where the good things hide."

"Slow is also where the DRIPS happen," muttered Dani, scooping a little golden run off her elbow and plopping it back on her peak. "But fine! Slow! I am the slowest, calmest, least-melty soft-serve on the whole mountain." She took one careful, dignified step. Then immediately bounced three times out of pure excitement. "Okay I tried."

---

They followed the little Glow along the warm edge of the crater, where the rock was new — so new it was still cooling, popping and ticking and pinging like a cookie sheet fresh from the oven. Pele's Glow floated low over a stretch of fresh black stone, and where its warm light touched, the friends could see tiny lines, tiny ridges, tiny ripples, all frozen in place.

"This," said the Glow proudly, "was liquid fire yesterday. And today? Today it is *land*. Brand-new land. Nobody has ever stood here before. You three are the very first."

Dani looked down at her own drippy feet. "Wait. Wait wait wait. We're standing on a piece of the island that is *one day old*?"

"Younger," said the Glow, twinkling. "It's a baby. Be gentle."

Dani immediately patted the rock very softly. "Hello, baby island," she whispered. "You're doing so good. You're gonna grow up to have palm trees and shave ice stands and EVERYTHING."

"And tide pools," added Honu. "Do not forget the tide pools. A baby island always wants tide pools."

"And tide pools!" Dani agreed, patting the rock again. "The works."

Pele's Glow brightened so warm and happy it lit up all their faces. "You see? You see how you're being with it? Gentle. That is exactly how the fire is, deep down. People hear *volcano* and they picture roaring and crashing and the sky on fire. But mostly?" It drifted in a slow, peaceful loop. "Mostly the **pele** works like a grandmother knitting in the evening. Slow. Patient. One warm stitch at a time, until — surprise — there's a whole cozy blanket where there used to be only sea."

Honu Haupia's gentle ancient eyes went misty. "So this is the secret of the fire," she murmured. "It does not destroy. It *builds*."

"That's it. That's the whole job." The Glow drifted up, brightening, and now its warm light spilled across the whole dark mountainside, and oh — the friends could SEE. They could see that the entire island, every cove and cliff and curl of beach, every place a coconut had ever rolled or a turtle had ever napped, had once flowed warm and orange out of the heart of the **pele**.

"Every beach you've ever wiggled your toes in," said the Glow softly, "I made. Slowly. One warm night at a time. Drop by drop by drop, the islands grow. New land, new light, every night."

Dani sat down very suddenly. "I think," she said, in a hushed voice, "I just learned the biggest thing I've ever learned and I learned it in the DARK on a MOUNTAIN from a piece of FIRE the size of a CUPCAKE."

"I prefer 'majestic,'" said the Glow.

"Majestic cupcake," Dani agreed solemnly.

Honu drifted between them, her shell catching the warm light. "There is an old, old saying," she said softly, the way she always said the old, old things. "*He ali'i ka 'aina, he kauwa ke kanaka.* The land is chief; we are its keepers. The land takes care of us, little ones — and so we take care of the land." She turned her gentle eyes to the glowing crater far below. "Tonight we have met the one who *makes* the land. I think that is the greatest honor a small turtle could swim toward."

"I'm not a turtle but I feel SO honored," Dani whispered, sniffling a happy little soft-serve sniffle that nearly dripped down her chin.

---

But just then — *plip, plip, plip* — Dani felt something terrible. The cozy warmth of the mountain, mixed with her excitement, mixed with all that bouncing, had finally done her in.

She was melting. Fast.

"Oh no oh no oh swirl!" she cried, golden soft-serve sliding right off her peak. "I'm gonna puddle! I'm gonna be Dole *Soup* Dani!"

Honu reached out a flipper, but haupia pudding can't exactly stop a melt. And the night was warm, and getting warmer the closer they stood to the friendly fire.

That's when Pele's Glow did the gentlest, cleverest thing.

It dimmed itself way, way down — soft as a nightlight — and floated a little distance away, over a fresh-cooled ledge where a breeze came up off the dark sea. "Here, friend," it called warmly. "Come stand in my shade-side. Fire makes shadows too, you know. And the wind off the water is cool as a kiss."

Dani wobbled over on her melty feet — and oh, the sea breeze hit her, fresh and salty and cool, and her drips slowed, and her peak firmed right back up into its proud glossy curl.

"You SAVED me," she gasped. "A fire saved me from melting! That's — that's the silliest most wonderful thing, you're a fire and you helped a soft-serve stay cool, that's — I can't even — *stay cool and swirl on!*"

"That's my whole point, sunny one," the Glow twinkled. "Powerful doesn't mean unkind. The strongest thing on the whole island... is also the warmest welcome."

Dani twirled in her perfect cool spot, her glossy curl firm and proud again. "You know what you are?" she declared. "You're the BIGGEST and the GENTLEST at the same time, and that's the best kind of thing to be. I'm gonna remember that forever. Whenever I meet something big and scary-looking, I'm gonna say, *Hi! Are you secretly a majestic cupcake?* And probably it'll say yes!"

"Probably it will," agreed Honu, who had seen a great many things in her long slow life and found this to be surprisingly true.

The Glow flickered so warmly it was almost bashful. "Most folks only ever see the fire from far away, and far away, fire looks like danger," it said. "But up close — close enough to share an evening — fire looks like *home.* You just have to be brave enough to come close." It dipped, soft and shy. "Thank you for coming close."

---

They stayed until the sky began to soften at the edges, the three of them tucked on the warm new rock — Honu humming an old ocean song, Dani perfectly cool in the breeze-shade, and Pele's Glow flickering low and gold between them like the world's coziest campfire.

"Will you be lonely," Dani asked, "when we hike back down?"

"Never," said the Glow. "I'll be busy. Building. And every grain of sand you ever sleep on, every warm beach where your **'ohana** ('OH-hah-nah) = family gathers — that'll be a little hello from me." It glowed brighter, soft and proud. "From the **pele**, with all the **aloha** (ah-LOH-hah) = love in the world."

And as the first pink light crept up over Kilauea, the gang waved goodbye to the gentlest, warmest, most majestic-cupcake-sized friend on the whole Big Island — who was, even then, quietly making more of Hawaii, one twinkling drop at a time.
