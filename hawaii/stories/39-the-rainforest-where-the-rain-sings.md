# Story 39: The Rainforest Where the Rain Sings

**Main Character:** Lani the Loco Moco
**Guest Characters:** Lilikoi Lani, Malia the Malasada
**Setting:** A misty Big Island rainforest full of ferns and tiny waterfalls
**Theme:** Rain is the land's way of getting a good drink.
**Hawaiian Spotlight:** **ua** (OO-ah) = rain

---

Lani the Loco Moco was, by his own happy admission, the laziest hiker in Hawaii.

He hiked the way a cloud travels: slowly, with many breaks and a lot of comfortable groaning.

"Today," he announced, egg-hat wobbling, "I am LEADING an adventure." His friends fell over from shock.

"You? Leading?" Lilikoi bounced in circles. "You once led us to a hammock and called it a journey!"

"A fine journey," said Lani. "But today I want you to hear the **ua** (OO-ah) = rain sing."

"Rain that SINGS?" Malia gasped, shedding sugar. "Count me in! I'll bring snacks. And hugs. And more snacks!"

---

The trail climbed into cool green mist, ferns unfurling, tiny waterfalls splashing into moss bowls.

Lani plopped onto a moss cushion with a satisfied SQUISH. "Now. Listen."

The clouds leaned down to the treetops, and the softest ua began. Tip. Tip-tip. Tap.

Drops bonked a hollow log, bonk-bonk, pinged off fern tips, and booped a mushroom cap. Boop.

"It's a SONG!" cried Malia, swaying, sugar sparkling off her cheeks. "With a beat and everything!"

Lilikoi caught a drop on her tongue and made the sourest face in history. "BLECH! It's WATER!"

"Where's the ZING?" she said. "I'll be over there, somewhere DRY, being interesting."

And ZIP, off she went down a side path, leaving a tangy scent trail like ribbon.

---

Lani heaved upright, gravy sloshing. "We don't leave ohana in the rain. Not even prickly ones."

They followed the scent. Malia parted a curtain of fronds. A startled snail. No Lilikoi.

"Lilikoooi! We've got SNACKS!" called Malia. "I always lead with snacks. Works on me."

Lani peered into a hollow log. A fat frog blinked back and burped. Lani burped politely in reply.

Under a mushroom the size of a table: a dry spot, a lilikoi-shaped dent, and nobody in it.

---

Then the ferns opened onto a grove of **'ōhi'a lehua** (OH-hee-ah leh-HOO-ah) = red lehua blossoms, bright as fireworks.

"The ua feeds them," said Lani. "No rain, no flowers. No flowers, no fruit. No fruit..."

"...no LILIKOI!" gasped Malia. "The rain MAKES her! It makes all of us!"

"Every drop you hear singing," said Lani, "is the mountain taking a good long drink."

Then, from somewhere ahead, came a small soggy voice. "...help?"

---

Behind a mossy rock, Lilikoi sat marooned on a tiny stone, a rain-puddle moat all around her.

"It keeps RAINING," she wailed. "The puddle's too wide, my legs are too short, I'm soggy FOREVER!"

She jumped anyway. SPLOOSH. Back onto her stone, sourer and wetter than ever.

"Nobody stays stuck on Malia's watch!" Malia lay down as a bridge and went soggy in four seconds.

"Fresh, warm, and never alone," she said from the puddle, "but mostly, right now, damp."

Lani lay down across the water, a warm rice bridge, gravy and all. "Hop across. Mind the egg."

"Voted Most Likely To Be Napped On," he added. "Three years running."

---

Tip-tap-tip went Lilikoi over his back. "Careful of the yolk," mumbled Lani. "It's ticklish."

Too late. Giggles rumbled up through the rice. The whole bridge wobbled like jelly.

BOING! Lilikoi launched off the end and landed safe in Malia's sugary, soggy hug.

"You came back for me," whispered Lilikoi. "Even though I was rude about the rain."

Lani heaved upright with a grunt. "The ua comes back too. Again and again. That's how forests grow."

Lilikoi caught a drop off a lehua petal. Her face lit up. "It tastes like the WHOLE FOREST!"

"Sour first," said Lani gently. "Sweet always."

---

The rain hushed to a whisper, and one gold sunbeam slanted through, lighting every wet leaf like jewelry.

Lani lay back in the warm moss, friends snuggled against his sides. "Best journey ever."

"Even better than the hammock?" teased Lilikoi.

"Let's not go crazy," said Lani, and closed his sleepy eyes.

---
