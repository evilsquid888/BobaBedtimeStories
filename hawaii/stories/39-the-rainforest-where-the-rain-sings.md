# Story 39: The Rainforest Where the Rain Sings

**Main Character:** Lani the Loco Moco
**Guest Characters:** Lilikoi Lani, Malia the Malasada
**Setting:** A misty Big Island rainforest full of ferns and tiny waterfalls
**Theme:** Rain is the land's way of getting a good drink.
**Hawaiian Spotlight:** ua (OO-ah) = rain

---

Lani the Loco Moco was, by his own happy admission, the laziest hiker on the whole Big Island. He hiked the way some people nap — slowly, with many breaks, and a great deal of comfortable groaning. So when he announced he was leading an adventure into the rainforest, his friends nearly fell over from shock.

"You? Leading?" said Lilikoi Lani, the little purple passion fruit, bouncing in zippy circles. "You once led us to a hammock and called it a journey!"

"That *was* a journey," Lani said, very seriously, the sunny-side egg on top of his rice-mountain head wobbling like a golden hat. Gravy rivers ran cozily down his sides. "A journey to the most comfortable place on Earth. But today—" he gave a sleepy wink, his runny yolk catching the light "—today we go where the **ua** (OO-ah) = rain *sings*."

Malia the Malasada gasped, dropping a sprinkle of sugar crystals from her cheeks. "Rain that SINGS? Oh, count me in! I'll bring snacks. I'll bring hugs. I'll bring more snacks!"

"Pile it high, sleepy guy," Lani rumbled. "Let's go drink the green."

---

The trail climbed gently up the mountain's flank, and the air grew cool and soft and smelled like wet leaves and waking flowers. All around them, ferns unfurled like sleepy green fists opening into hands. Tiny waterfalls trickled off the rocks, no taller than Mac the Macadamia, splashing into bowls of moss.

"It's so *green*," whispered Malia, her custard heart peeking out as she smiled. "Greener than green. Green's fancy cousin."

"That's because of the ua," said Lani, plopping down on a cushion of moss with a satisfied *squish*. "Watch."

Right on cue, the mist thickened. The clouds leaned down low to the treetops, almost shyly, the way a grandmother leans down to whisper. And then it began — not a storm, not a downpour, but the gentlest, softest **ua** you ever heard.

*Tip. Tip-tip. Tip. Tap-tip.*

The rain landed on broad leaves and made one note. It landed on the little waterfalls and made another. It pattered on hollow logs — *bonk-bonk* — and dripped off curled fern tips — *ping* — and trickled down stone — *shhhhhh*. A fat drop bonked a mushroom cap and went *boop*. A whole row of them down a leaf-stem went *plip-plip-plip-PLIP*, like someone tiptoeing up a tiny staircase.

"It's a SONG," Malia breathed. "A real one! With a beat and everything!" And she began to sway, sugar crystals sparkling off her cheeks, humming along with the drips until she had the whole tune. "*Tip-tap-tippy, ping-ping-shhh* — oh, I could dance to this all night!"

"Told you," said Lani, beaming so wide his egg jiggled. "On the Big Island we've got rains with *names*, you know. There's the kind that mists so soft you can't tell if it's even falling, and the kind that drums on the leaves like a luau, and the kind that walks across the forest in a gray curtain and you can watch it coming from a mile off. This one—" he tilted his sleepy head, listening "—this one's a humming rain. A drink-it-slow rain. My favorite kind."

Lilikoi Lani stuck out her tongue to catch a drop, then made the sourest, most dramatic face in the history of faces. "BLECH! It's WATER! Plain old WET water! Where's the *flavor*? Where's the *zing*? Rain is so boring it puts itself to sleep!" She crossed her little arms. "I am leaving this drippy concert. I'll be over there, somewhere DRY, being interesting."

And before anyone could stop her, the cheeky little passion fruit zipped off down a side path, leaving her tangy-sweet scent trailing behind her like ribbon.

"Lani! She wandered off!" cried Malia.

Lani sighed the long, comfortable sigh of someone who really did not want to get up. "Ohhh, that girl. Sour first, every time." He hauled himself upright, gravy sloshing. "Come on. We don't leave 'ohana in the rain alone — not even the prickly ones."

---

They followed the scent trail through the dripping ferns, calling her name. The rainforest grew thicker and more wonderful with every step. Ropy vines hung down like nature's swing set. Mushrooms glowed faintly on fallen logs. Silvery threads of mist drifted between the trees like slow, sleepy ghosts who'd come only to say hello. Everything *drank* — Lani could practically hear it, the whole forest sipping the soft rain like a thousand happy straws.

"Lilikoi!" called Malia, cupping her hands. "Lilikoooi! We've got snacks!" She paused. "I always lead with snacks. Works on me, anyway."

"Smart," rumbled Lani, plodding along behind her, his gravy leaving cozy little warm footprints in the cool moss. He stopped to admire a curl of fern shaped exactly like a question mark. He stopped to watch a tiny waterfall splash into a stone bowl. He stopped, frankly, rather a lot. "You can't hurry a rainforest," he explained to nobody. "And you really can't hurry a Loco Moco."

"Look at the flowers," Lani said, pausing — partly to admire them, partly because pausing was his favorite hobby. The blossoms were fat and bright and bursting with color: red **'ōhi'a lehua** (OH-hee-ah leh-HOO-ah) = the red pompom flower of the lehua tree, puffing out like tiny fireworks frozen mid-pop. "These only get this ono — this *delicious-looking* — because the ua feeds them. No rain, no flowers. No flowers, no fruit. No fruit—"

"—no LILIKOI!" gasped Malia, suddenly understanding. "The rain MAKES her. It makes all of us!"

Lani nodded slowly, the way he did everything. "The land gets thirsty, just like you and me. The ua is how it takes a good, long drink. Every drop you hear singing? That's the mountain saying *mahalo* — thank you." He patted the moss fondly. "See how plump and green this moss is? That's a forest that's well watered. A happy forest. You can't fake it. The rain shows the truth of a place."

Malia knelt by a fern dripping diamonds off every tip. "So when it rains and rains and everybody grumbles and says *oh no, not again*..."

"...the flowers are throwing a party," Lani finished, with his slow, comfortable smile. "The taro's drinking deep. The fruit's getting sweeter by the minute. Grumbling at the ua is like grumbling at your own dinner."

Just then, from somewhere ahead, came a small, soggy voice.

"...help?"

---

They hurried (well, Lani *hurried*, which looked a lot like a brisk waddle) around a curtain of ferns — and there was Lilikoi Lani. She'd zipped right into a hollow under a big mossy rock, thinking it would be a nice dry hideout. But the gentle ua had pooled around the entrance into a little puddle-moat, and now the puddle had her surrounded. She was perched on a tiny island of stone, looking very damp and very grumpy and, underneath it all, just a teensy bit scared.

"It KEEPS RAINING," she wailed. "I tried to wait it out but it just got *wetter*! And the puddle's too wide to jump and my legs are too short and I'm going to be stuck here being soggy FOREVER!"

"Oh, sweet zesty girl," said Malia warmly, "nobody stays stuck on Malia's watch. Fresh, warm, and never alone!"

But the puddle truly was too wide. Lilikoi couldn't jump it. Malia was too squishy to make a bridge that wouldn't go soggy. And Lani — well, Lani looked at the puddle, and then at his own cozy, sturdy, mountain-shaped self, and a sleepy little smile spread across his face.

"Pile it high," he murmured, "sleepy guy."

He lay down right across the puddle. His big rice-body made a warm, comfy bridge from one side to the other, gravy and all. "Hop across my back, little one. Mind the egg — it's the wobbly part."

Lilikoi Lani stared. "You're... letting me walk on you?"

"I'm the comfiest bridge on the island," Lani said proudly. "Voted Most Likely To Be Napped On, three years running."

So she crossed — *tip-tap-tip* — her little feet padding right over Lani's soft back, hopping carefully around the jiggly egg.

"Careful of the yolk," Lani mumbled into the moss. "It's ticklish."

"You're *ticklish*?" Lilikoi giggled.

"Everybody's ticklish somewhere," he said with great dignity. "Mine's the yolk. Keep moving, please, before I laugh and tip you in."

And then *boing!* — she leapt off the end of him, safe on the other side, landing right in Malia's sugary, waiting hug.

"You came back for me," Lilikoi whispered, her cheeky sparkle going soft. "Even though I was rude about the rain."

"'Course we did," said Lani, rolling back upright with a contented grunt. "That's what the ua does too, you know. It comes back. Again and again. Never gives up on the land, even when the land doesn't say thank you. That's how love grows — low and slow."

---

Lilikoi looked up at the singing rain — *tip, tap-tip, ping, shhhh* — really *listening* this time. And this time it didn't sound boring at all. It sounded like a lullaby the whole mountain was humming to itself.

"...Okay," she admitted, with a sour-sweet little grin. "Maybe it's a *little* bit beautiful. Maybe."

"It's also delicious," Malia announced, tilting her face up. The rain pattered onto her warm doughnut body, melting just a few sugar crystals into sweet, sparkly drops. "Try a raindrop off a lehua leaf. Go on!"

This time Lilikoi caught a drop that had rolled down a red blossom — and her wrinkly purple face lit up like sunrise. "It tastes like... like *flowers*! And rain! And green! It tastes like the whole forest in one sip!"

"Sour first," said Lani gently, "sweet always. Right?"

"Sweet always," she agreed, and zipped a happy loop-the-loop, this time *toward* the rain instead of away from it.

They stayed until the ua softened to a whisper and then to nothing at all, and the mist lifted just enough for a single gold sunbeam to come slanting through the ferns, making every wet leaf and every tiny waterfall sparkle like the forest was wearing all its jewelry at once. A bird somewhere shook itself dry and sang. The 'ōhi'a blossoms stood up a little straighter, fat and red and freshly fed. The whole rainforest looked drunk-full and dewy and so very, very ono — like the whole green world had just finished a tall, cool drink and let out a happy *ahhh*.

"It's like the forest just woke up from the best nap ever," Malia sighed.

"That," said Lani, with deep approval, "is the nicest thing anyone has ever said about a forest. Or about napping."

And Lani the Loco Moco, the laziest hiker on the Big Island, lay back down in the warm moss with a sigh of pure joy, his friends snuggled against his cozy sides, listening to the land take its last gentle sips of the singing rain.

"Best journey ever," he murmured.

"Even better than the hammock?" teased Lilikoi.

"...Let's not go crazy," said Lani, and closed his sleepy eyes.

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, listen — somewhere on a green mountain tonight, the soft rain is singing the land to sleep. *Tip, tap-tip, ping.* Every drop is a kindness, every patter a quiet thank-you, feeding the flowers and the fruit and all the sweet things waiting to grow. May you drink in good dreams the way the forest drinks the ua, slow and gentle and full of love. You are watered, you are growing, and you are never, ever alone. Goodnight.
