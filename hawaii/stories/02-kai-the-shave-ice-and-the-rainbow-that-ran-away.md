# Story 2: Kai the Shave Ice and the Rainbow That Ran Away

**Main Character:** Kai the Shave Ice
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A shave-ice cart on Waikiki Beach after a rain shower
**Theme:** Your colors are yours, even when you can't see them.
**Hawaiian Spotlight:** anuenue (AH-noo-EH-noo-eh) = rainbow

---

On a bright afternoon on Waikiki Beach, Kai the Shave Ice was having the best day of his fluffy little life — which, for a tall dome of snow drenched in rainbow syrup, is really saying something. He sat in his paper cone on the edge of the shave-ice cart, paper-umbrella hat tilted just so, tiny spoon tucked behind one ear, watching the surfers bob like little corks.

"Brah," Kai sighed happily, wiggling so his stripes shimmered. Strawberry red ran down his left side. Blue coconut zigged across his middle. Tangy gold lilikoi swirled all over the top like sunshine that learned to dance. "I am, without a doubt, the most colorful guy on this entire beach. Chill out and rainbow on!"

A seagull squawked, unimpressed.

"You're just jealous," Kai told it warmly, "because you're only one color, and I am *all* of them."

That was the exact moment the sky decided to teach Kai a little lesson.

A fat gray cloud rolled in over Diamond Head, faster than a malasada disappears at breakfast. The wind went cool. And then —

*PLISH-PLISH-PLISH-PLOOSH!*

Down came the rain, warm and sudden, the way it always does in the islands. A quick island shower, here and gone in a wink. The surfers laughed and dove. The seagull tucked its head. And Kai — poor, proud, colorful Kai — sat right out in the open on the edge of his cart with absolutely no roof at all.

"Oh no," he said. "Oh no-no-no. My hat! My hat is *paper*!"

The rain pitter-pattered down his snowy slopes. And with every drop, something terrible and ticklish happened. The strawberry red went pink, then pale, then *gone*. The blue coconut ran in little streams down into the sand. The golden lilikoi melted away like a sunset that forgot to set.

When the cloud finished its business and floated off — happy as you please — the sun came back out and lit up the wet beach. Everything sparkled. A beautiful **anuenue** (AH-noo-EH-noo-eh) = rainbow arched all the way across the sky, from the green mountains clear out over the blue, blue sea.

And Kai? Kai was white.

Just... white. Plain, snowy, blank white, all the way down to his paper cone.

"My colors," Kai whispered, his big eyes going huge. "My colors ran away!"

He looked up at that gorgeous rainbow stretched across the sky. And a teeny, slightly soggy idea formed in his slushy little head.

"They didn't disappear," he breathed. "They *escaped*. They washed off me and floated up into the sky and became — THE ANUENUE! My colors are up there! I have to get them back!"

Now, you and I might know that's not *quite* how rainbows work. But Kai was a very fluffy fellow with a very big imagination, and once he had a plan, well — chill out and rainbow on, indeed.

He hopped — *fwump, fwump* — right off the cart and onto the warm wet sand. "I'm coming, colors!" he hollered. "Stay right where you are!"

He chased the anuenue down the beach. He chased it past the surfboards leaning in a sandy row. He chased it past a tide pool, where a little crab waved hello with one claw.

"Excuse me," Kai puffed, sliding to a stop beside the tide pool. "Have you seen my colors? They look exactly like that, up there." He pointed his spoon at the sky.

The crab blinked its stalky eyes. It did not answer, on account of being a crab. But it did reach into the tide pool with one claw and very gently set something on the sand in front of Kai.

It was a tiny seashell, swirled inside with the softest pink-and-pearl shine. Pink. The first hint of pink Kai had seen since the rain.

"Pink!" Kai gasped. "That's — that's strawberry pink! Close enough!" He held the shell up to his white slope, hopeful, pressing it against the spot where his very best strawberry stripe used to live. He even gave the shell a little encouraging pat, the way you'd pat a shy puppy. "Go on. Hop on up. I've got plenty of room."

But of course a shell can't paint a shave ice. The pink stayed in the shell, snug as ever, and Kai stayed white as a winter morning. He sighed a small, slushy sigh that fogged up the air in front of him.

"Thank you anyway, little crab. You're a real one." He set the shell gently back by the tide pool. The crab tucked it away under a pebble, the way you'd tuck a treasure under your pillow, and Kai felt his slushy heart squeeze just a little. Even a crab knew that some colors were worth keeping safe.

He kept going. The anuenue seemed to lean toward the mountains now, so Kai turned and chased it up the beach, where the sand met a row of swaying palms.

There, in the shade of the biggest palm, sat a flower — a great big **pua** (POO-ah) = flower, a hibiscus as red and gold and orange as a whole bowl of syrup. Bees bumbled around it lazily.

"COLORS!" Kai shrieked with joy, throwing his little arms wide. He flumped right up to the flower and pressed his snowy cheek against a petal. "I found you! Come back to me!"

The petal was soft and warm. It smelled like honey and afternoon, and a tickly bee bumbled right up to Kai's nose to investigate.

"Bzzt?" said the bee, which is bee for *and who might you be?*

"I'm a rainbow," Kai explained proudly. "Or I will be, just as soon as I borrow some color off this flower."

The bee bumbled off, clearly deciding that white snow mountains who talk to flowers were not worth the trouble. And when Kai pulled back from the petal — he was still white, and the flower was still a flower, and not one drop of red had jumped over to him.

"Aw, **shoots**," Kai mumbled (that's island for *darn it*). His umbrella hat drooped down over one eye. "You're beautiful. You're so beautiful it's almost rude. But you're not *my* colors."

He was getting tired now. His slopes were melting just a touch at the edges, the way shave ice does when it's been working too hard in the sun. The anuenue arched on, shimmering, just out of reach, the way rainbows always are — and for the first time all day, Kai felt a little less than sparkly.

He plopped down in the cool sand beneath the palm. "Maybe," he said softly, "maybe I'm just a plain white guy now. Maybe my colors live in the sky, and I'll never wear them again."

That's when he heard the sweetest sound — a bubbling, gurgling, sloshing little giggle. Right beside him, half-tucked into the sand, was a small puddle. The rain had left it behind, clear and still, with the whole beach reflected in its glassy top.

"You look down in the dumps, **keiki** (KAY-kee) = child," said the puddle. It had a kind, watery voice, soft as a wave winding down. "Come. Look in me. Tell me what you see."

Kai didn't really feel like looking at his plain white self. But the puddle seemed nice, and his auntie always said you should never be rude to water. So he leaned over and peered in.

And he stopped.

Because there, where the sun came pouring down through Kai's wet, snowy slopes, the light did the most magical thing. It went *into* him — and bent — and split — and came back out the other side in a hundred soft glimmering bands. Red. Orange. Gold. Green. Blue. Purple. A whole rainbow, shining right out of plain white Kai, dancing on the surface of the little puddle.

"That's — that's an anuenue," Kai whispered. "A rainbow! But it's coming *out of me*."

"Of course it is," the puddle gurgled gently. "Where do you think rainbows come from, little one? Not from syrup. Not from flowers. Not from a fancy hat or a tucked-behind-the-ear spoon. A rainbow is only ever light, finding its way through something clear and true — like rain. Like water. Like a thousand little raindrops still drifting in the sky after a storm." It rippled with a small, knowing laugh, and the reflected rainbow swayed along with it. "Like *you*, keiki. You are made of snow and water and light. You were *built* for rainbows."

Kai stared at the colors spilling out of his own snowy self. "But the syrup washed away," he said. "I thought my colors washed away with it."

"The syrup was lovely," said the puddle kindly. "But it sat *on* you. These colors — these come from *inside* you, from the light passing through. The rain didn't take your colors, keiki. The rain washed you so clean that now everyone can see they were inside you all along."

Kai's huge eyes filled up — not with sad tears, but with the happy, sparkly kind. He looked up at the great anuenue in the sky, then down at the little anuenue shining out of his own body, and he laughed the biggest laugh a fluffy snow-mountain has ever laughed.

"My colors never ran away!" he cried, bouncing in the sand. "They were just hiding! In the best hiding spot of all — INSIDE ME!"

He wiggled, and his rainbow wiggled. He spun, and his rainbow spun, splashing color all over the puddle and the palm and the happy little crab who had followed him all that way. He did a slow, careful twirl, and ribbons of red and gold and purple swept across the sand like he was painting the whole beach with his very own self.

"Look at me!" he laughed. "I'm a *walking* anuenue! I don't need to chase a rainbow — I AM one!"

The crab clapped both claws. The bee came back just to watch. Even the seagull from earlier circled overhead, and this time it didn't look the least bit unimpressed.

"Thank you, puddle," Kai said, bowing his umbrella hat. "Thank you, brah. You're the wisest puddle on the whole beach."

"All puddles are wise," said the puddle, with a tiny modest ripple. "We just had farther to fall."

Kai hopped and slid and shimmered all the way back down the beach to his cart, leaving little rainbow sparkles wherever the sun hit him. And by the time he climbed back into his paper cone, the shave-ice man had come along with his bottles of syrup — strawberry, blue coconut, golden lilikoi — and was glad to see his favorite shave ice come home.

But Kai didn't even mind that the man started drizzling fresh stripes back on. Syrup was nice. Syrup was sweet. But Kai had a secret now, tucked deep in his snowy heart, where no rain could ever wash it out.

His colors weren't *on* him.

His colors were *in* him.

And as the sun sank low and gold over Waikiki, and the anuenue faded softly into evening, Kai the Shave Ice sat happy in his cone, glowing from the inside, the most colorful guy on the entire beach — rain or shine.

"Chill out," he murmured sleepily to the sea, "and rainbow on."

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, the rain may wash a great many things away, but it can never wash away what is truly yours. Your colors live inside you — your kindness, your laugh, your bright and shining heart — and no cloudy day can ever take them. So close your eyes and let the warm island night tuck you in, and know that even in the dark, your rainbow is still there, glowing soft and safe within you. Sleep deep, dear keiki, like fresh snow on a quiet mountain. Dream of puddles full of light and crabs that wave hello. Goodnight.
