# Story 2: Kai the Shave Ice and the Rainbow That Ran Away

**Main Character:** Kai the Shave Ice
**Guest Characters:** Ama, a little tide-pool crab; Auntie Puddle, a wise rain puddle
**Setting:** A shave-ice cart on Waikiki Beach after a rain shower
**Theme:** Your colors are yours, even when you can't see them.
**Hawaiian Spotlight:** **anuenue** (AH-noo-EH-noo-eh) = rainbow

---

On the edge of the shave-ice cart sat Kai, a snowy mountain dripping red, blue, and gold syrup.

"Brah," he sighed to a seagull, wiggling his stripes. "I'm the most colorful fella on this beach."

The seagull squawked. "Jealous," said Kai. "You're one color. I'm ALL of them."

A fat gray cloud rolled over Diamond Head. PLISH-PLISH-PLOOSH. "My hat is PAPER!" wailed Kai.

The strawberry ran pink, then gone. The blue streamed away. The gold melted like a lazy sunset.

The sun returned, an **anuenue** (AH-noo-EH-noo-eh) = rainbow arched over the sea, and Kai was plain white.

"My colors ran AWAY!" he gasped. "They're up there in the sky. I'm getting them BACK!"

He hopped off the cart, fwump, fwump, onto the wet sand. "Stay right there, colors!"

---

He chased the anuenue past a row of surfboards and skidded straight into a tide pool. SPLOOSH.

A little crab named Ama blinked up at him with stalky eyes.

"Have you seen my colors?" Kai panted, pointing his spoon at the sky.

Ama said nothing, being a crab, but set a tiny pink-and-pearl seashell on the sand.

"PINK! That's strawberry pink! Close enough!" Kai pressed the shell to his white slope. "Hop on up."

The pink stayed in the shell. Kai stayed white. He gave the shell an encouraging pat anyway.

"Thanks anyway, Ama. You're a real one." Ama tucked the shell under a pebble like treasure.

---

The anuenue leaned toward the mountains. Kai chased it up the beach to the swaying palms.

Under the biggest palm bloomed a **pua** (POO-ah) = flower, a hibiscus as red and gold as syrup.

"COLORS!" Kai flumped up and pressed his snowy cheek against a petal. "Come back to me!"

A bee bumbled up to his nose, looked him over, and bumbled off, unimpressed.

Kai pulled back. Still white. "Aw, shoots. You're beautiful. But you're not MY colors."

So Kai climbed the palm, slope over slope, to reach the rainbow himself.

He got halfway. The trunk was slippery. He slid down and plopped into the sand.

"Maybe I'm just a plain white fella now," he said softly, his umbrella hat drooping over one eye.

---

Beside him, a little leftover rain puddle gurgled a sloshing giggle.

"You look down in the dumps, **keiki** (KAY-kee) = little one," said Auntie Puddle. "Come look in me."

Kai leaned over. Sunlight poured through his wet snowy slopes, bent, split, and spilled out in colors.

Red. Orange. Gold. Green. Blue. Purple. A whole rainbow, shining right out of plain white Kai.

"That's an anuenue," Kai whispered. "And it's coming OUT of me."

"Where do you think rainbows come from, keiki?" gurgled the puddle. "Light, through something clear and true."

"The syrup sat ON you. These colors come from INSIDE you. The rain only washed you clean."

Kai laughed the biggest laugh a snow-mountain ever laughed. "My colors were HIDING. Inside ME!"

---

He wiggled, and his rainbow wiggled. He bounced, and ribbons of color swept across the sand.

"I'm a WALKING anuenue!" he hollered. "I don't need to chase a rainbow. I AM one!"

Ama clapped both claws. The bee came back to watch. Even the seagull circled overhead, impressed.

"Thank you, Auntie Puddle. You're the wisest puddle on the beach," said Kai, bowing his hat.

"All puddles are wise," said the puddle. "We just had farther to fall."

---

Kai hopped home to his cart, leaving little rainbow sparkles wherever the sun hit him.

Ama had followed. It tipped the strawberry bottle over Kai's slopes, then the blue, then the gold.

"Syrup's nice," said Kai. "But my colors aren't ON me. They're IN me."

The sun sank low and gold over Waikiki, and the great anuenue faded softly away.

"Chill out," Kai murmured to the sea, "and rainbow on."
