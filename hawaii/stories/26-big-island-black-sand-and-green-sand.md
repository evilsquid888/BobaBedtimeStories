# Story 26: Big Island, Black Sand and Green Sand

**Main Character:** Kai the Shave Ice
**Guest Characters:** Honu Haupia, Lilikoi Lani
**Setting:** The black-sand and green-sand beaches of the Big Island
**Theme:** Different doesn't mean less — it means special.
**Hawaiian Spotlight:** **one** (OH-neh) = sand

---

Now, you may already know that Kai the Shave Ice is the most colorful fellow on any beach. He's a tall fluffy dome of snow-white ice drenched in rainbow stripes — lilikoi gold, blue-coconut blue, and strawberry red — sitting in a paper cone with a paper-umbrella hat and a tiny spoon tucked behind one ear. Wherever Kai goes, color goes with him.

So you can imagine his surprise when he hopped off the canoe onto the Big Island, looked down at the beach, and yelped, "WHOA. The **one** (OH-neh) = sand — it's BLACK! Somebody spilled the night all over the daytime!"

He poked it with one drippy toe. Black. He scooped a handful. Black, black, sparkly black. He held it up to his huge sparkly eyes, absolutely certain that if he stared hard enough, a rainbow would come peeking out.

It did not.

"Chill out and rainbow on," Kai whispered to himself, which is what he always says when he is one hundred percent not chill.

---

A slow, smooth voice drifted up from the shoreline. "The land made that one, little wave."

It was Honu Haupia, the wobbly snow-white coconut-pudding sea turtle, gliding up the black beach like she was still underwater. Her haupia-square shell jiggled gently, and her ancient eyes shone with that faraway ocean kindness.

"Made it?" Kai gasped. "Out of WHAT? Did the island run out of regular sand?"

Honu chuckled, slow as honey. "When the warm lava meets the cool sea, it cracks and tumbles and cools into shiny black grains. Black one is young one, Kai. Fresh from the fire of the land. Slow flippers, far journeys — this beach is brand new in island time."

Kai blinked at the glittering black under his paper umbrella. "But it's not... colorful."

"Isn't it?" said Honu.

And just then, the sun caught the beach, and every single black grain twinkled like a sky full of upside-down stars, and Kai's mouth fell open so wide his spoon nearly slid off his ear.

"Okay," he admitted. "That's a *little* show-offy. In a good way."

He crouched down and let the black one trickle through his drippy fingers. It was warm — warmer than any sand he'd ever felt back home, like the beach had a tiny cozy heartbeat tucked underneath it.

"Why is it warm, Honu?" he asked, wiggling his toes into it.

"Because it remembers the fire," said Honu. "The land here was born from a volcano, little wave. Pele's glow flowed down to the sea, hissing and steaming, and where it met the cold water it shattered into a million shiny pieces. This whole beach was once a river of light." She tilted her ancient head. "Some beaches are old as the deep. This one is hardly older than you."

Kai looked at the warm black sand in a whole new way. "So it's a baby beach."

"The babiest," agreed Honu solemnly.

"Hi, baby beach!" Kai waved at the ground, which is a slightly silly thing to do, but nobody on the Big Island minded one bit.

---

That's when a tiny purple blur came zipping across the dark sand, kicking up sparkly black puffs and leaving a tangy-sweet smell trail behind it.

"BLACK BEACH! BLACK BEACH!" squealed Lilikoi Lani, the little round purple-wrinkly passion fruit, skidding to a stop and cracking herself open just enough to flash her bright orange seeds. "I dare somebody to find a *more* dramatic beach. You can't. It's impossible. I already win."

"Lani," said Kai, "where did you even come from?"

"Stowaway," said Lani proudly. "Tucked myself in Honu's shell wrinkle. Sour first, sweet always!" She gave a little zesty spin. "Now — I heard a rumor. A WILD rumor. They say somewhere on this island there's a beach that's... wait for it..." She paused for dramatic effect. "...*green*."

Kai laughed so hard a stripe of strawberry syrup wiggled clean down his side. "Green sand? Lani, that's the silliest thing I've ever heard, and I once met a poke bowl who arranged his sesame seeds by mood."

"It's true," rumbled Honu. "Papakolea. The green-sand beach. Far down the island, past the cliffs, where the wind walks."

Kai stopped laughing. "...Green like, GREEN green?"

"Olive green," said Honu. "Like a turtle's shell in the deep."

Lani was already bouncing. "ROAD TRIP! Honu, you drive! I'll be the snacks AND the entertainment!"

---

So off they went — Honu gliding slow and steady, Kai melting a tiny bit at the edges from excitement (and the warm Big Island sun), and Lani zipping in loop-the-loops around them, narrating everything like a one-fruit parade.

The walk to the green-sand beach was long. The black sand gave way to red dirt, and the red dirt gave way to golden grass, and the wind got bolder and bolder until it tried to steal Kai's paper umbrella right off his head.

"Hold on, little wave," called Honu.

"I'm holding! I'm holding!" Kai clamped both drippy hands on his hat. "Is this normal?! Does the wind always grab people's hats?!"

"Only the fashionable ones," said Lani, who got picked up by a gust and carried fifteen feet sideways with a delighted "WHEEEEE!"

Along the way, Kai kept noticing how the land changed its colors like he changed his syrups. Here the dirt was rusty-red, there it was the soft brown of malasada sugar, and over there it was pale as Honu's pudding shell. He'd always thought of land as just *land* — one plain color, nothing to see. But the Big Island, it turned out, was a giant box of crayons that had been left out in the sun and melted into one another.

"Honu," he said, a little out of breath from the climbing, "how does ONE island make so many different things?"

"Patience," said Honu, gliding along without rushing a single inch. "The land doesn't hurry. It heats and cools, it crumbles and grows, it lets the rain carve it and the wind shape it, for years and years and years. Slow flippers, far journeys. Everything beautiful here took its sweet time."

"That's basically my whole personality," said Lani, who had been carried backward up the hill by the wind and didn't seem to mind at all. "Slow? No. But beautiful? Obviously."

They climbed down a winding path into a great green bowl of cliffs, like a giant scoop had taken a bite out of the land. And there — far below, kissed by foamy waves —

Kai froze.

The beach was GREEN.

Not painted green. Not grass green. Real, sparkling, olive-and-emerald *sand*, shimmering in the sun like the whole shore had been dusted with tiny precious jewels.

"It's..." Kai's voice came out very small for such a tall dome of ice. "It's beautiful."

---

They scampered down to the green one, and Kai scooped a whole handful, holding it up to the light. Hundreds of tiny green crystals winked back at him.

"Those are **olivine** crystals," explained Honu gently. "Born deep inside the volcano, harder than the black sand around them. So when the soft black washes away to sea... the tough little green gems stay behind. This beach is made of survivors."

Lani peered at a single green grain balanced on her seed. "So the green is the part that's TOO TOUGH to leave?"

"That's right," said Honu. "Different sand. Special sand."

Kai looked at the green sand. Then he looked back up the long path, toward the black sand. Then, slowly, he looked down at himself — his rainbow stripes, his snow-white fluff, his slightly drippy edges.

And something gentle clicked into place behind his sparkly eyes.

"I think I get it," he said softly. "I always thought a beach had to be the regular golden kind to be a *real* beach. Like rainbow was the only way to be pretty." He smiled. "But the black one is brand-new fire. And the green one is the toughest gems that wouldn't wash away. And the gold ones somewhere else are made of crunched-up coral and shells." He spread his arms wide. "The island makes beauty in EVERY color! Even ones I never imagined!"

"Now you're rainbow-ing," said Honu warmly.

"Different doesn't mean less," Kai declared, in the voice of someone who has just figured out something enormous. "It means *special*!"

"FINALLY," said Lani, flopping dramatically into the green sand. "I've known that my whole life. I'm purple AND orange. I'm basically a walking lesson."

---

To celebrate, they did what any group of friends does at a once-in-a-lifetime beach: they played.

Kai dribbled a little melt onto the black sand and made the world's first black-sand snow cone, which Lani declared "extremely goth and I love it." They buried Lani up to her wrinkly chin in green olivine until only her orange-seed grin showed, and she popped out shouting "I'm a GEM now! Bow before me!" which made Kai laugh so hard he nearly tipped out of his cone.

Honu drew slow flipper-pictures of turtles in the dark sand, and the waves wiped them away, and she drew them again, perfectly patient, perfectly happy. "Aren't you sad when the waves erase them?" Kai asked.

"Never," said Honu. "The sea isn't taking my pictures. It's just asking me to draw more." And she smiled her slow, ancient smile, and drew another.

And when the sun began to sink, the most wonderful thing happened.

The black sand turned to spilled starlight. The green sand glowed soft as sea-glass. The sky went lilikoi-gold and strawberry-pink and blue-coconut blue — all of Kai's own colors, stretched across the whole entire heavens.

Kai gasped. "Honu! The sky! It's wearing MY stripes!"

"The sky has always been a shave ice," said Honu, eyes twinkling. "It just waits for sunset to show its syrups."

Kai laughed his biggest laugh yet, fluffy and bright, and for once he didn't feel like the only colorful thing on the beach. He felt like one beautiful color among a whole island of them — black and green and gold and purple and rainbow, every single one exactly as special as the next.

"Chill out and rainbow on," he whispered, and this time he meant it all the way down to his paper cone.

---

That night, the three friends curled up together on the warm dark sand — Honu's jiggly shell, Lani tucked into a wrinkle of it, and Kai melting just the tiniest bit at the edges, the way he always did when he was truly, perfectly content.

Above them, the stars came out by the thousands, and the black one beneath them sparkled right back, so that Kai couldn't quite tell where the beach ended and the sky began.

"Goodnight, black sand," he murmured. "Goodnight, green sand. Goodnight, every color I haven't met yet."

And the warm island wind tucked them in.

---

## Goodnight Blessing
May you remember, little one, that the world makes beauty in every color — black as starlight, green as the deep, gold as the morning, and bright as your own true heart. You are not less for being different; you are special exactly because of it. Snuggle down now, soft and safe, like a turtle in warm sand. The waves will hush, the stars will sparkle, and tomorrow the island will make something new just for you. Rest easy, brave and beautiful. Goodnight.
