# Story 15: The Lei of Many Flowers

**Main Character:** Malia the Malasada
**Guest Characters:** Dole Whip Dani; Poke Pua; Niu the Coconut
**Setting:** A lei-making stand and flower garden before a family celebration
**Theme:** Many different things, woven together, become beautiful.
**Hawaiian Spotlight:** **lei** (LAY) = flower garland

---

On the morning the island smelled like a hug, Malia the Malasada woke upside down in plumeria petals.

"Mmf!" she giggled. Three sugar crystals popped off her cheeks. "Now the petals are sparkly. You're welcome."

Today was the **ʻohana** (oh-HAH-nah) = family reunion, and Malia had the most important job on the island.

A giant welcome **lei** (LAY) = flower garland, so every cousin could duck under and feel: you belong.

Malia wanted it perfect. She bounced to the stringing table. "Easy!" she told the sky.

It was not easy.

---

By mid-morning she had strung a whole rope of creamy plumeria, looped twice round the stand.

"Perfect," she said. "Identical. Tidy." She looked a moment longer. Her smile drooped at the edges.

Same, and same, and same. Like one very polite sentence that never told a joke.

"MALIA!" sang Dole Whip Dani, swirling up the path in a pineapple breeze. "Can I help? Can I?"

"It's boring," said Malia. "I made it all match, and now it's so matchy it dozed off."

Dani's eyes went huge. "It needs ME. I mean, color! Watch this!"

She threaded golden **ʻilima** (ee-LEE-mah) = papery orange flowers among the white. Little sunsets glowed through the cream.

"Boring," said Dani, "is just exciting that forgot to invite a friend."

---

"If one friend makes it better," gasped Malia, "we need EVERYONE." Off they went, bounce and swirl.

At the tide pools, Poke Pua was arranging shells so perfectly it looked rude to the other shells.

"A giant lei needs BALANCE," said Poke Pua, eyeing Dani's clumps. "No offense."

"Some offense received," said Dani cheerfully.

Poke Pua spaced deep purple **pōhuehue** (poh-hoo-EH-hoo-eh) = beach morning-glory blossoms just so, like waves coming in.

---

Under his palm tree sat Niu the Coconut, woven hat on, with an hour of advice saved up.

"Everybody forgets the greenery," said Niu. "Flowers without leaves are a parade that lost its road."

He wove tough fragrant maile vine down the whole length. "Tough shell, sweet heart. It holds everything together."

They stepped back. The lei was white and gold and violet and green, and no stretch matched.

"Oh," said Malia. "The matchy one looked dozy. This one looks AWAKE."

---

Then, far down the road, came the first happy hollers. The ʻohana was arriving. Early.

"To the gate!" cried Malia. "Everybody lift!" The lei was longer than four friends can carry.

A sea gust lifted the lei off their arms. It snaked down the path like a flower dragon.

It looped once round Niu. Twice. "I'm a coconut mummy," said Niu. "Tough shell, sweet, mmf."

Dani lunged, slipped on her own melt, and slid under it like a soft-serve on a waterslide.

Poke Pua grabbed the end. "Left! No, MY left! Just so! JUST SO!"

Malia bounced onto the dragon's head and held on, sugar flying, giggling the whole way.

The lei flopped at last across the garden gate and hung there, swinging, exactly where it belonged.

---

Niu unwound himself. "I meant to do that," he said. Nobody believed him.

Through the gate came the ʻohana: treat-folk of every size, big ones, tiny ones, dozy old ones.

One by one they ducked under, breathed in plumeria and maile, and smiled the very same smile.

"Different flowers," said Dani, melty-eyed.

"Different friends," said Niu, sloshing.

"Woven side by side," said Malia, and her custard heart glowed. "That's what a family looks like."

"Fresh, warm, and never alone!" she whispered, and bounced off to hug everybody.

She left a happy trail of sugar crystals all the way down the path.
