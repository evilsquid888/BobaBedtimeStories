# Story 15: The Lei of Many Flowers

**Main Character:** Malia the Malasada
**Guest Characters:** Dole Whip Dani, Poke Pua, Niu the Coconut
**Setting:** A lei-making stand and flower garden before a family celebration
**Theme:** Many different things, woven together, become beautiful.
**Hawaiian Spotlight:** lei (LAY) = flower garland

---

The morning the whole island smelled like a hug, Malia the Malasada woke up upside down in a basket of plumeria petals — which, if you have never done it, is a wonderful way to wake up, because everything is soft and sweet and faintly sneeze-y.

"Mmf!" said Malia, giggling so hard that three sugar crystals popped off her cheeks and bounced into the flowers. "Oops. Now the petals are sparkly. You're welcome, petals!"

It was the day of the big family party — the **ʻohana** (oh-HAH-nah) = family reunion — and Malia had been given the most important job on the whole island. She was to make the welcome **lei** (LAY) = flower garland. Not a little wrist one. Not a polite over-the-door one. A *giant* one. A lei so long and so grand that every cousin, auntie, uncle, and sleepy grand-elder could duck under it and feel, right away, *you belong here.*

The auntie who'd given her the job had said it the way aunties say important things — both hands on Malia's squishy shoulders, very serious, very soft. "You make the lei, sweet girl, and you make it *right.* When somebody walks under a welcome lei, they should feel the whole family wrapping around them at once. Can you do that?"

"Can I!" Malia had said, sugar crystals bouncing everywhere. "Watch me!"

Now she rolled out of the petal-basket, brushed herself off, and took one long, confident look at the empty stringing-table and the whole enormous garden glowing green and gold in the morning.

"Easy!" she announced, to nobody, to the flowers, to the sky.

It was not easy.

---

By the time the sun had climbed a hand's-width up the sky, Malia had a problem the exact shape of a giant lei: she did not have one.

She had picked plumeria, because plumeria are the friendliest flower, all creamy-white with sunny-yolk centers, and they smelled the way her custard heart felt. She had strung a whole long rope of them, looping it twice around the lei-stand, and stepped back to admire it.

"There," she said, dusting sugar off her squishy hands. "A whole lei of plumeria. Perfect. Identical. Tidy."

She looked at it a moment longer.

Then her smile drooped a little at the edges, the way she did on hot afternoons.

It *was* tidy. It was creamy and white and sweet — and it was the same, and the same, and the same, all the way down. It looked, Malia thought, like one very long, very polite sentence that never told a joke. It was missing something, though she couldn't say what. It was missing the way a party *felt.*

"Hmm," said Malia, which is the noise good ideas make right before they arrive.

Just then a swirl of cool came zipping up the garden path, golden and bright and slightly melting at the edges.

"MALIA!" sang Dole Whip Dani, skidding to a stop and sending up a tiny puff of pineapple-scented breeze. Her glossy curl wobbled with excitement. "I heard you're making the WELCOME lei! That's the best job! Stay cool and swirl on — can I help, can I, can I?"

Malia hugged her, very carefully, because hugging a soft-serve too hard is a sticky business. "Yes! But Dani — look. It's *boring.* I made it all plumeria so it would match, and now it's so matchy it's gone to sleep."

Dani tilted her swirl and studied the long white rope. Then her sparkly eyes went *huge.*

"Oh!" she gasped. "It needs ME. I mean — it needs *color!* Watch this!"

And before Malia could say *what are you*, Dani plucked a fistful of bright golden **ʻilima** (ee-LEE-mah) = a small papery orange flower from the sunny side of the garden — the same shade as Dani herself — and began threading them in among the white ones, fast and joyful, chattering the whole time.

When she stepped back, the lei had little bursts of sunset glowing through the cream, like someone had let the morning into it.

"Whoa," whispered Malia.

"Right?!" Dani beamed, doing a little melty wiggle of pride. "Different is *delicious!* You know what I always say — well, I don't always say it, I just thought of it, but I'm going to say it always *now* — boring is just exciting that forgot to invite a friend!"

Malia laughed so hard a fresh shower of sugar rained down onto the orange flowers, which made them glitter even more, which made Dani gasp all over again. The two of them stood there grinning at the half-finished lei like it was the cleverest thing either of them had ever helped make. And in a way, it was — because it had stopped being *just Malia's,* and started being *theirs.*

---

The trouble, of course, with one good idea is that it makes you greedy for more.

"If a little orange makes it that much better," Malia said slowly, her custard heart beginning to bubble, "then what if—" She gasped. "Dani! We need EVERYONE."

So they ran — well, Malia rolled and Dani swirled — through the garden and down to the shore path, collecting friends the way a lei collects flowers.

They found Poke Pua first, near the tide pools, arranging shells into a pattern so perfect it almost looked rude to the other shells. Her kelp-strand hair was tucked back, her ahi cubes glistening ruby-fresh in the sun.

"A giant lei?" said Poke Pua, sitting up at once, eyes already narrowing in the way of someone who has Opinions About Arrangement. "Fresh from the sea, that's me — and let me tell you, Malia, that lei needs *balance.* You can't just jam things in willy-nilly." She glanced sideways at where Dani had jammed things in willy-nilly. "No offense."

"Some offense received," said Dani cheerfully.

Poke Pua chose deep purple **pōhuehue** (poh-hoo-EH-hoo-eh) = beach morning-glory blossoms, ocean-cool and cool-toned, and she didn't string them in a clump. She spaced them — *just so* — one violet star, then a stretch of white, then a glow of orange, then violet again, like the rhythm of waves coming in.

And the strangest, loveliest thing happened. The orange that Dani had scattered and the white Malia had strung suddenly looked like they'd been *waiting* for the purple to show up and tie the whole thing together.

"Okay," breathed Dani. "*Okay.* That's so good I'm a little melty about it."

"You're always a little melty," said Poke Pua, but kindly, and arranged one more flower.

---

There was only one friend left to find, and he was the trickiest, because he was a coconut and coconuts do not, generally, *run* anywhere.

Niu the Coconut was sitting under his own palm tree in his little woven hat, sloshing thoughtfully, looking very much like someone who had been about to give advice for the last hour and was relieved to finally have someone to give it to.

"A welcome lei," Niu said, nodding sagely before Malia had even finished asking. "Big responsibility. You'll want greenery, you know. Everybody forgets the greenery. Flowers are flashy, sure—" he glanced at Dani "—no offense—"

"WHY does everyone say that to her," said Dani.

"—but flowers without leaves just look like a parade that lost its road. You need **maile** (MY-leh) = a fragrant trailing leafy vine. Tough little vine. Holds everything together. Tough shell, sweet heart — like somebody else I could mention." He patted his own round brown self proudly.

Malia hadn't even thought of leaves. She'd been so busy thinking about petals.

So Niu — who turned out to be surprisingly bendy when it mattered, and who insisted on supervising every single loop — wove long green ropes of fragrant maile down the whole length of the lei. The vine slipped between every flower, soft and shining and faintly woodsy, and it did exactly what Niu said it would.

It *held.*

"See?" said Niu, sloshing happily and tipping his little woven hat back. "Tough on the outside, holds the soft stuff safe on the inside. That's the whole secret of a coconut. And, it turns out—" he looked along the great long shining lei, and his three little face-dots went all soft "—the whole secret of a family, too."

For a coconut who claimed never to run anywhere, he had certainly hurried to say something true.

All those different blooms — sweet white plumeria, bright orange ʻilima, deep ocean-purple pōhuehue — stopped looking like a pile of nice things and started looking like *one* thing. A thing with a shape. A thing with a song.

The four of them stepped back together and went quiet, which for this particular four of friends was a small miracle.

The lei stretched the entire length of the stand and pooled on the ground at both ends, soft as a sigh. It was white and gold and violet and green all at once, and not one stretch of it was the same as any other stretch, and somehow *that* was the very thing that made it beautiful. The white made the orange brighter. The purple gave the white somewhere to rest. The green held all of it close, the way arms do.

"It's funny," Malia said softly, her custard heart glowing warm. "I thought I had to make everything match. I thought the same was the same as pretty." She touched a plumeria, then an ʻilima, then a violet star. "But the matchy one looked asleep. This one looks *awake.* This one looks like the whole family showing up at once."

"Because it *is,*" said Poke Pua, and for once she wasn't fixing anything. She was just looking.

"Different flowers," said Dani, melty-eyed.

"Different friends," said Niu, sloshing.

"Woven side by side," said Malia.

And right then, as if the island itself had been waiting for the lei to be finished, the first car horns and the first happy hollers came drifting up the road. The ʻohana was arriving — dozens of them, big ones and tiny ones and slow sleepy elder ones, all of them as different from each other as the flowers were, every single one of them sweet.

---

It took all four of them to carry it. Malia held the middle, Dani swirled along under one looping end, Poke Pua balanced the other with great and serious care, and Niu rolled underneath shouting directions that nobody needed but everybody enjoyed. They hung the giant lei across the garden gate, where it swung gently in the warm breeze, and the whole long sweet-smelling rope of it caught the light like a smile stretched from one side of the gate to the other.

Then, one by one, the whole family ducked under it to come inside.

And every single auntie and uncle and cousin and grand-elder stopped, just for a heartbeat, beneath all those different flowers — and breathed in the plumeria-and-ʻilima-and-pōhuehue-and-maile smell of *home* — and smiled the exact same smile.

Which was, Malia decided, the most wonderful thing of all: that so many different people, walking under so many different flowers, could each feel the very same warm thing inside.

"Fresh, warm, and never alone," she whispered, and went to hug everybody, leaving a happy little trail of sugar crystals all the way down the path.

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, you are a flower in a very long and lovely lei, woven beside every person who loves you — and no two of you are the same, and that is exactly why it is beautiful. Tonight, let the white be your soft pillow, the orange your warm dreams, the purple the quiet of the dark, and the green vine the gentle arms that hold it all together while you sleep. You belong, just as you are, woven right where you should be. May you smell plumeria in your dreams and wake up sweet. Rest now, beloved bloom. Goodnight.
