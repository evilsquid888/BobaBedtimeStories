# Story 44: The Lost Little Malasada

**Main Character:** Malia the Malasada
**Guest Characters:** Musubi Manu; Manapua Mano; Auntie Kulolo, a lei-stall elder made of taro-coconut pudding
**Setting:** A crowded county fair and food-truck festival in Honolulu
**Theme:** When you're lost, kind helpers are everywhere.
**Hawaiian Spotlight:** **huli** (HOO-lee) = to turn / search / flip

---

The Honolulu county fair sparkled like somebody had sneezed glitter over the whole island.

A juggling coconut dropped three pineapples, plonk, plonk, plonk, and bowed anyway.

Malia the Malasada laughed so hard a shower of sugar crystals tumbled off her golden cheeks.

"Stay close, you two," called Musubi Manu. "I have a snack schedule and a backup snack schedule."

"I have a hungry," announced Manapua Mano, bouncing high. "I was BORN for the pie-eating contest."

"You were steamed for it," teased Malia, and a nearby seagull jumped at their laughing.

Then, boom-boom-BOOM, a marching band of tiny musubi high-stepped right between them like a river of rice.

Malia clapped until the last little drummer bounced past, then turned around. Manu and Mano were gone.

---

Everything at the fair was tall, and Malia was about as tall as a soup can.

"We're not lost," Malia whispered. "We're un-found. Time to **huli** (HOO-lee) = turn and search!"

She marched bravely for three whole steps and bonked into something warm, sticky, and square.

It was Auntie Kulolo, a lei-stall elder made of taro-coconut pudding, draped in ropes of white pikake.

"Aloha, little one," said Auntie Kulolo. "You look like you lost your **ʻohana** (oh-HAH-nah) = family."

"There was a band," said Malia. "And rice. And then poof. No friends."

"First rule of being lost: find a helper," said Kulolo, slipping a pikake bracelet onto Malia's wrist.

---

Kulolo lifted Malia onto a wooden crate. "Tall folk see far." No square friend anywhere.

Malia sniffed with her custard-button nose. "Soy sauce! Manu carries emergency packets. Follow the nose!"

---

The soy-sauce trail ended at a saimin truck stacked with noodle crates. No Manu.

"Higher," said Malia. "I need higher." She climbed the crates like a sugary mountain goat.

From the roof she could see everything. Then the truck honked and drove away with her on top.

"Excuse me, truck!" cried Malia, clinging on. "I'm a passenger now, apparently!"

Past the hula stage, past the whale-shaped poke truck, she spotted a square shape with a dark band.

"MANU!" Malia leaped off the roof, arms wide.

---

She landed in a pyramid of rainbow shave ice. The pyramid wobbled. The pyramid came down.

Malia sat up striped yellow, blue, and pink. The square shape turned around. It was a waffle.

"You're not Manu," said Malia. One last sugar crystal slid down her cheek like a tiny tear.

---

Then she saw a plain wooden booth with a giant conch shell on a stand. A finding booth!

Malia climbed up, took the biggest breath of her puffy life, and blew the **pū** (POO) = conch shell.

PWAAAAAH. The whole fair went quiet.

"Attention, ohana!" called Malia. "One lost malasada would like to be found, please!"

"Helpers are everywhere," said Malia, hugging the shell. "You just have to let them find you."

"MALIA?!" hollered a voice from far across the fair.

---

Musubi Manu came running so fast his nori seatbelt flapped like a flag.

Mano bounced behind him in a magnificent pie-crumb mustache, and they crashed into one rainbow-striped hug.

"I made a search grid," panted Manu. "I asked eleven lei stalls. I asked a tent!"

"I checked the pie tent," said Mano. "Thoroughly. I was worried-eating. It's a real thing."

"You worried-ate four," said Manu. "I counted."

---

The four of them rode the Ferris wheel, slow and creaky, while the fair sparkled below.

Malia held both her friends' arms so tight no marching band could ever pull them apart.

"Next time somebody's lost," said Malia, "I'm the helper. I'll huli every little snack home."

"From up here," said Mano dreamily, "I can see the pie tent."

---
