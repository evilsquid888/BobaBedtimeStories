# Story 3: Malia the Malasada and the Warm Morning Batch

**Main Character:** Malia the Malasada
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A bustling Portuguese bakery in Honolulu at dawn
**Theme:** Sharing what you have makes more, not less.
**Hawaiian Spotlight:** mahalo (mah-HAH-loh) = thank you

---

The very first thing Malia the Malasada did that morning was sneeze, and seven sugar crystals flew off her cheeks like tiny shooting stars.

"Excuse me!" she giggled, plump and golden-brown and freshly tumbled out of the fryer. She was the last malasada in the whole morning batch, rolled extra-thick in sparkly sugar, and she was warm in the way only a brand-new doughnut can be — warm clear through to her lilikoi-custard heart, which peeked out when she smiled.

The Honolulu bakery glowed pink and gold with dawn light. Pans clinked. The fryer hummed. The window steamed up like a sleepy yawn.

And Malia, all by herself on the cooling tray, decided something terribly silly.

"This sugar," she whispered, hugging her own sparkly self, "is MINE. Every single crystal. I am keeping all of it forever and ever and not one grain for anybody!"

A passing fly wobbled by. Malia clutched her sugar tighter.

"Mine," she squeaked.

The fly left. Probably to find a less greedy doughnut.

---

Now, you should know that in this bakery, the snacks did not simply sit and wait. The moment the baker stepped into the back room — clomp, clomp, clomp went his big floury shoes — the whole counter woke up and stretched.

And outside the steamy window, a line had formed.

Not a line of people. Oh no. A line of the sleepiest, hungriest little snacks you ever saw, each one waiting their turn at the bakery's back step, where the warm crumbs always rolled.

First in line was a small brown coconut with a green sprout for hair, tapping the glass with three little face-dots.

"Good morning, Malia!" he called, steady and grounded even at dawn. "I am Niu. I have walked all the way from the beach, and my insides are sloshing something terrible. Might you spare a crystal or two of that lovely sugar?"

Malia's eyes went wide. "My sugar? Oh, no-no-no. This sugar is MINE, Niu. I'm sorry, but if I give some away, I'll have LESS. And less is sad. So I'm keeping all of it." She lifted her sugary chin. "Tough shell, sweet heart — that's YOUR motto, isn't it? Well, mine is: soft on the outside, full of sugar I'M not sharing."

Niu blinked his three dots. "That motto," he said gently, "needs work."

But Malia just rolled herself a half-inch farther down the tray, very pleased with her brilliant plan.

---

Next in line came a bouncing, pillowy white steamed bun, hopping so hard the whole windowsill rattled.

"Sugarsugarsugar!" sang Manapua Mano, leaving little wisps of steam in the air. "Malia! Best friend! Light of the morning! I am SO hungry I could eat my own swirl-knot. Could I please, pretty please, have the teensiest tiniest sprinkle?"

He pressed his soft round face against the glass and made it foggy.

Malia's tummy did a little flip. Manapua looked so hopeful. So bouncy. So very, very hungry.

But then she thought about her sugar. Her beautiful, sparkly, all-hers sugar.

"I can't, Manapua," she said, turning away so she wouldn't see his face. "If I share, I'll have less. And I worked SO hard to be this sparkly."

"You fell out of a fryer," Manapua pointed out.

"It was very tiring," sniffed Malia.

Manapua sighed a soft, steamy sigh and bounced gently to the back of the line. But — and here is the important part — he did not stomp off mad. He simply waited. Because that is what good friends do, even when you are being a little bit silly.

---

The sun climbed higher. The line grew longer. And one by one, the sleepy snacks asked Malia for just a crumb of sugar, and one by one, Malia said no.

A wobbly coconut-pudding sea turtle named Honu Haupia glided up, slow as the tide, her snow-white shell jiggling. "Slow flippers, far journeys," Honu murmured. "I have come a long, long way, little one. Might you share?"

"Sorry!" chirped Malia. "All mine!"

A regal golden pineapple swept past, green crown held high. "I, Pua, do not usually ASK," she announced grandly. "But that sugar does look divine. A pinch, perhaps?"

"Nope!" sang Malia.

And then — clomp, clomp, clomp — the baker's shoes came back. Everyone froze. The line of snacks scattered into the bushes like polite little shadows. And Malia sat alone on her tray, hugging her sugar, feeling extremely proud and just the tiniest bit lonely.

That was when she noticed something strange.

She was sweating.

---

Honolulu mornings get warm FAST, and bakeries get warmest of all. Malia could feel it now — the heat creeping in, the sugar on her cheeks beginning to go shiny and soft and... slippery.

"Oh no," she whispered. "Oh no, oh no, oh no."

A crystal slid right off her shoulder and melted into a sad little smear on the tray.

"My sugar!" Malia gasped. "It's MELTING! All my beautiful sugar that I saved and saved and didn't share — it's just running away!"

Another crystal melted. Then three more. The harder she clutched, the warmer she got, and the warmer she got, the faster her sugar dissolved into syrupy puddles she could not catch no matter how she squished.

Malia's lilikoi heart wobbled. Not from fear — remember, dear reader, nothing scary happens in this kitchen — but from a sad sort of surprise. She had kept ALL her sugar, every grain, just so she'd never have less.

And now she had less anyway.

"I don't understand," she sniffled. "I didn't give any of it away. So why is it disappearing?"

From the bushes outside, a steady voice answered.

"Because sugar," said Niu the coconut, peeking up over the windowsill, "is not for holding. It is for sharing. Held too tight in a warm spot, it just melts. But shared around..."

He couldn't quite finish, because he didn't know how it finished. He only knew the first part. Niu was a bit of a know-it-all, but an honest one.

---

Malia looked down at her shrinking sparkle. She thought about Manapua's foggy hopeful face. About Honu's long, slow journey. About little hungry tummies sloshing in the dawn.

And she had a brand-new idea — the best and warmest idea she'd ever had.

"Come back!" she called out the window. "Everybody, come back! Quick, before it ALL melts!"

The snacks tumbled out of the bushes. Niu rolled. Manapua bounced. Honu glided. Pua swept in like a queen pretending she hadn't hurried.

"Take some!" Malia laughed, tipping herself sideways so the loose sugar tumbled off in a sparkling shower. "Take it all! I'd rather share it warm than melt it alone!"

Manapua caught a faceful of sugar and squealed. "MALIA! You're the sweetest!"

"**Mahalo** (mah-HAH-loh) = thank you!" cried Niu, catching crystals in his little woven hat. "Mahalo, mahalo, mahalo!"

And here is the magic — the real magic, the kind that only happens when you finally stop clutching:

Every time a snack said mahalo, Malia felt warmer. Not melty-warmer. Glow-warmer. Happy-warmer. The kind of warm that starts in your custard heart and spreads all the way to your sugar-dusted cheeks.

Honu took a crystal and bowed. "Mahalo, keiki."

Pua took a pinch, daintily. "Mahalo, darling. Truly."

And Manapua hugged her so hard a puff of his steam fogged her sugar all over again — which made BRAND NEW crystals form, sparkling and fresh.

Malia gasped. "I have MORE sugar than before!"

"Of course you do," rumbled a deep, jolly voice from the doorway. It was Kalua the Pig, smoky-golden and roly-poly, who had wandered in last of all. "Low and slow, that's how love grows, little one. Sugar shared is sugar that comes back to you, every single time. That's the oldest recipe on the island."

---

Just then the bakery door jingled, and a sleepy little girl in pajamas padded in, holding her tutu's hand.

"That one, please," the girl yawned, pointing at the plate. "The sparkly one in the middle."

And Malia — warm, fresh, sparklier than ever, surrounded by friends all whispering mahalo — felt her whole self light up like a sunrise.

Because here is the lovely truth about being a malasada: the very best, proudest, happiest moment of your whole doughnut life is when somebody finally takes a bite, closes their eyes, and goes "mmmmmm." It's like a firework getting to light the sky. It's the whole point of being delicious.

"Take me!" Malia laughed. "I'm fresh, I'm warm, and I am NEVER alone!"

The little girl took a bite. Her eyes went wide. Lilikoi custard, warm sugar, and the taste of a doughnut who had finally learned how to share.

"Mahalo," whispered the girl, sugar on her chin.

And it was, Malia decided, the sweetest word on the whole island.

Snuggle down now, warm and never alone. Goodnight.
