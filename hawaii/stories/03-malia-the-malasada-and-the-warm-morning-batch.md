# Story 3: Malia the Malasada and the Warm Morning Batch

**Main Character:** Malia the Malasada
**Guest Characters:** Niu the Coconut; Manapua Mano; Pua the Pineapple; a line of sleepy treat-folk
**Setting:** A bustling Portuguese bakery in Honolulu at dawn
**Theme:** Sharing what you have makes more, not less.
**Hawaiian Spotlight:** **mahalo** (mah-HAH-loh) = thank you

---

The very first thing Malia the Malasada did that morning was sneeze.

Seven sugar crystals flew off her cheeks like tiny shooting stars. "Excuse me!" she giggled.

She was the last malasada of the batch, plump, golden, and rolled extra-thick in sparkly sugar.

The Honolulu bakery glowed pink with dawn. The fryer hummed. The window steamed like a yawn.

Malia hugged her own sparkly self. "This sugar is MINE. Every crystal. Not one grain for anybody!"

A fly wobbled past. Malia clutched tighter. "Mine," she squeaked.

The fly left, probably to find a less greedy doughnut.

---

Outside the steamy window, a line of sleepy, hungry treat-folk waited on the back step.

First came Niu the Coconut, tapping the glass with his three little face-dots.

"Good morning, Malia! I walked all the way from the beach. Spare a crystal or two?"

"If I give some away, I'll have LESS," said Malia. "And less is sad. So, no."

"My motto is: soft on the outside, full of sugar I'm not sharing," she added, chin high.

Niu blinked his dots. "That motto," he said gently, "needs work."

---

Next bounced Manapua Mano, a pillowy steamed bun, so hard the whole windowsill rattled.

"Sugarsugarsugar! Malia! Light of the morning! The teensiest sprinkle?" He pressed his face to the glass.

Malia's tummy flipped. He looked so hopeful. "I can't. I worked SO hard to be this sparkly."

"You fell out of a fryer," Manapua pointed out.

"It was very tiring," sniffed Malia.

Behind him swept a regal golden pineapple, crown held high. "I, Pua, do not usually ASK."

"Nope!" sang Malia, and hopped a half-inch farther down the tray, very pleased with herself.

---

Then the sun hit the window, and the bakery got HOT.

A crystal slid off Malia's shoulder and melted into a sad little smear. "My SUGAR!"

She hopped under the ceiling fan to cool off. WHOOSH. Twenty crystals blew away like confetti.

She dove into the flour bin instead. She came out white as a snowball.

"I'm a SNOWBALL," she wailed, and sneezed, and lost twenty more.

The harder she squeezed, the warmer she got. The warmer she got, the faster it melted.

"I kept ALL of it," she sniffled, "and I have less anyway. Why?"

---

"Sugar is not for holding," called Niu through the glass. "Held tight, it melts. Shared around..."

He stopped, because he didn't know how it finished. Niu was a know-it-all, but an honest one.

Malia had one last idea. The open window. The cool breeze. She climbed the tower of bread baskets.

Basket by basket, up she went, sugar trickling behind her. The tower leaned.

It leaned more. "Uh-oh," said Malia.

CRASH. Baskets everywhere. Malia shot out the window in a glittering shower of sugar.

She landed splat in the middle of the line. Sugar rained down on everybody.

---

Manapua caught a faceful. "MALIA! You're the SWEETEST!"

Niu caught crystals in his woven hat and sang out **mahalo** (mah-HAH-loh) = thank you. "Mahalo, mahalo!"

And every mahalo made Malia warmer. Not melty-warm. Glow-warm. All the way to her custard heart.

She looked at her last little sparkle. Then she tipped sideways. "Take it ALL! Share it warm!"

"Mahalo, darling. Truly," said Pua, taking a pinch daintily.

Manapua hugged her so hard his steam fogged her all over. Brand-new crystals formed, fresh and sparkling.

Malia gasped. "I have MORE sugar than before!"

"That's how it finishes," said Niu. "Shared around, it comes back."

---

The line settled onto the warm back step, sugar on every chin.

"Fresh, warm, and never alone," sighed Malia, and offered everyone a bite.

Manapua took the biggest bite, eyes closed. "Mmmmm."

"Mahalo," whispered Malia. It was, she decided, the sweetest word on the whole island.

---
