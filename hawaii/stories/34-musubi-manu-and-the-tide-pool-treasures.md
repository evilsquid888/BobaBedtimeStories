# Story 34: Musubi Manu and the Tide Pool Treasures

**Main Character:** Musubi Manu
**Guest Characters:** Niu the Coconut; Mac the Macadamia; a grumpy little crab; a hermit crab in a borrowed house
**Setting:** Rocky tide pools at low tide along the Oahu coast
**Theme:** Look closely and the world is full of tiny wonders.
**Hawaiian Spotlight:** **opihi** (oh-PEE-hee) = limpet (a clinging shellfish)

---

Low tide on the Oahu coast. The sea had peeled back like a blanket sliding off a bed.

Underneath, the rocks held hundreds of glittering pools, each a tiny ocean of its own.

Musubi Manu stood so straight his nori nearly saluted. He had a clipboard.

"Wrapped up and ready!" he announced. "Treasure. Everywhere. And today I find the grandest one."

Niu the Coconut bounced down in his woven hat. "I read about tide pools. I know everything."

"You read one page," said a very small voice from inside a glossy marble-sized shell.

"One VERY informative page," said Niu.

---

Manu held up a polished seashell. "The Grand Treasure Trophy! Most amazing find wins it."

"What do I win for second?" asked Niu.

"My respect and admiration," said Manu.

"I'll take the shell," said Niu.

Click. Mac the Macadamia's shell half-shut. "What if I find nothing? Amazing things are too big for me."

Manu set his clipboard down, very serious. "Small hunters find what big ones step right over."

---

Off they splished, pool to pool. Niu bounced straight for the biggest, sparkliest one.

"AHA!" He scooped up a swirly turban shell. A tiny crab popped out, waggled its claws, scuttled off.

"The treasure's bodyguard," said Niu, calmly, pretending he hadn't yelped. "I read that. Page two, probably."

Manu lined up his finds on a flat rock: a striped pebble, a seaweed curl, a feather.

The wind knocked the feather over. He stood it up. It fell again. "An artistic choice," said Manu.

"Manu," called Niu, "you've spent ten minutes arranging three things."

---

Mac had wandered to the splashy little pools in the cracks, the right size for someone small.

Clinging to the wet rock were dozens of tiny cone shells, plain as pebbles, like the littlest mountains.

"Um," said Mac, just loud enough. "Manu? What are these?"

Manu peered so close his nori touched rock. "**Opihi** (oh-PEE-hee) = limpet! Shellfish that hold onto rocks. Famous!"

"Tiny. Bumpy. Not even shiny," sniffed Niu, holding up his turban shell. "THIS is treasure."

Manu reached out one careful rice arm and pulled.

He wiggled. He braced. He went NNNNGH until his spam blanket flapped. The opihi held tight.

---

"Let me," said Niu, secretly competitive. He pushed till his hat slid over his face-dots.

"It's cheating," announced Niu from under his hat. "It's holding on with magic."

"With muscle," said Mac quietly. "A teeny one. All on its own."

Then a friendly low-tide wave raced up the rocks. WHOOOSH. Right over everything.

Niu's turban shell went clink-clink-clink across the rocks. "MY TREASURE, COME BACK!" cried Niu, bouncing after.

Manu's tidy row scattered in one slosh. The feather sailed off to sea to start a new life.

The water slid back. Every single opihi was still there. Still clinging.

"They held on," whispered Mac.

---

Niu returned soggy, hugging his shell. "They're not weak for being small. They're strong for holding on."

"Holding tight to what you love," said Manu, eyes sparkly, "is its very own kind of strength."

Mac glowed pink all through his shell. "Small but mighty. Shell to heart."

---

Niu lectured a hermit crab about its borrowed house. It waved one claw. "Applause," decided Niu.

Mac found a sunset-colored sea slug, no bigger than a raisin, doing the slowest happy-dance in the universe.

Manu held up the trophy. "The winner is... the opihi. For Best Holding On."

"It can't hold a trophy," said Niu. "It's holding the rock."

"Then I'll leave it NEXT to it," said Manu, setting the shell gently beside the little opihi.

---

The tide whispered back in, gold and slow.

"Tough shell, sweet heart," said Niu, leaning on Mac, who leaned right back.

Manu packed his clipboard away and held on to his friends. The strongest thing there is.

---

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, the best treasures are easy to miss unless you look close and look kind. Some sparkle, and some just hold on quietly, the way the ones who love you hold on to you. Goodnight.
