# Story 34: Musubi Manu and the Tide Pool Treasures

**Main Character:** Musubi Manu
**Guest Characters:** Niu the Coconut, Mac the Macadamia
**Setting:** Rocky tide pools at low tide along the Oahu coast
**Theme:** Look closely and the world is full of tiny wonders.
**Hawaiian Spotlight:** opihi (oh-PEE-hee) = limpet (a clinging shellfish)

---

Musubi Manu had a clipboard.

Now, a clipboard may not sound like the most exciting thing in the world, but you have never seen Musubi Manu with a clipboard. He was a tidy little rice-block with a pink spam blanket and a crisp black nori seatbelt buckled snug around his middle, and when he held a clipboard he stood up so straight that his nori almost saluted. On the clipboard was a list. At the top of the list, in his neatest letters, it said:

**OFFICIAL TIDE-POOL TREASURE HUNT — DO NOT LOSE THIS LIST.**

"Wrapped up and ready!" he announced to the sleepy morning beach. Then he checked the time, checked the tide, checked his bento backpack, and — just to be safe — checked the list again to make sure it was still a list. It was. Excellent.

The tide had gone way, way out, the way it does at low tide, peeling back from the rocks like a blanket sliding off a bed. And underneath that blanket, the whole rocky shoreline had turned into hundreds of little glittering pools, each one a tiny ocean of its own.

"Treasure," whispered Musubi Manu, eyes wide. "Everywhere."

---

His two best treasure-hunters arrived right on schedule, because Musubi Manu had told them the schedule three times and also drawn them a map.

First came Niu the Coconut, rolling down the sand in his tiny woven hat, his green straw-sprout bouncing. "Reporting for adventure," Niu said, in his steady know-it-all voice. "I read about tide pools last night. I know everything about them now."

"You read one page," said a very small voice.

That was Mac the Macadamia, peeking out of his glossy shell like a turtle who wasn't sure he wanted to be at the party yet. Mac was about the size of a marble, smooth and cream-colored, and when he got nervous he pulled his shell shut with a soft *click* and went very quiet.

"One *very informative* page," Niu corrected.

Musubi Manu clapped his little rice hands. "Team! Here is the plan. We hunt for the most beautiful treasures in all the tide pools. Whoever finds the most amazing one wins the Grand Treasure Trophy." He held up the Grand Treasure Trophy, which was a polished seashell he had found that morning and was extremely proud of.

"What do I win if I find the *second* most amazing one?" asked Niu.

"My respect and admiration," said Musubi Manu.

"I'll take the shell," said Niu.

Mac's shell gave a tiny worried *click* — half-closing, the way it did. "What if I don't find anything amazing at all?" he asked in his smallest voice. "I'm... pretty small. The amazing things might be too big for me to reach."

Musubi Manu set down his clipboard, which for Musubi Manu was a very serious thing to do. He crouched down so he was eye to eye with the little nut. "Mac," he said gently, "small treasure-hunters find the things big ones step right over. That's not a problem. That's a superpower."

Mac's shell opened back up, just a crack. "...Okay," he peeped. And he didn't look quite so worried after that.

---

Off they went, splishing and splashing from pool to pool. And oh, the tide pools were full of wonders!

Niu rolled straight to the biggest, sparkliest pool. "I shall find the grandest treasure," he declared, "because I am grand." He spotted a swirly turban shell as shiny as a marble. "AHA!" He scooped it up — and a tiny crab popped out, waggled its claws crossly, and scuttled off with a *plip*.

"That's the treasure's bodyguard," Niu said, very calmly, pretending he had not just yelped. "Every grand treasure has one. I knew that. I read it. Page two, probably."

A wave hissed past his feet and he added, in a much smaller voice, "Anyway, the crab can keep the bodyguard job. I'll just take the shell."

Musubi Manu, meanwhile, was being extremely organized about it. He examined each pool, made a tidy note on his clipboard, and arranged his findings in a neat little row on a flat rock: one striped pebble, one curl of green seaweed, one feather that had blown in from somewhere, all lined up perfectly straight. "Catalogued," he said happily. "Sorted. Tidy." He drew a little box on his clipboard and ticked it. He loved ticking boxes nearly as much as he loved his friends, which is saying something.

"Manu," called Niu, "you've spent ten whole minutes arranging three things."

"I have arranged them *beautifully,*" said Musubi Manu, with great dignity. The wind promptly knocked the feather over. He stood it back up. The wind knocked it over again. He decided, for the sake of his pride, to call this "an artistic choice."

But here is the funny thing about being so busy looking for the *grandest* treasure.

You walk right past the small ones.

---

While Niu chased shiny shells and Musubi Manu sorted his row, little Mac the Macadamia had wandered to the edge of the rocks, where the smallest, splashiest pools hid in the cracks. He liked it there. It was quiet. It was the right size for someone small.

And that's where he saw them.

Clinging to the wet rock, just above the lapping water, were dozens of tiny cone-shaped shells, the color of pebbles, shaped like the littlest mountains you ever saw. They were so small and so plain that you'd never notice them unless you were exactly Mac's size and exactly Mac's kind of patient.

He almost didn't say anything. Mac was used to staying quiet — quiet was comfy, quiet was safe inside his shell. But these little shells were so curious, and they had picked the most ordinary rock in the whole shoreline to live on, as if they knew a secret nobody else did.

"Um," said Mac, just loud enough. "Manu? What are these?"

Musubi Manu hurried over, clipboard ready. Niu rolled up behind, still polishing his crab-guarded turban shell.

Musubi Manu peered close. Then closer. Then so close his nori almost touched the rock. "These," he breathed, "are **opihi** (oh-PEE-hee) = limpets. Little shellfish that hold onto the rocks. My grandpa-musubi told me about these. They're famous!"

Niu sniffed. "Those? They're tiny. They're bumpy. They're not even shiny. That is the *opposite* of treasure." He held up his turban shell. "*This* is treasure."

"Hold on," said Musubi Manu, and he reached out one careful rice hand to pick up an opihi.

It would not come.

He pulled. He wiggled. He braced his little feet and tugged with all his musubi might, going *NNNNGH* until his spam blanket flapped.

The opihi did not budge. Not one millimeter. It clung to that rock like it had been born there, like the rock and the shell had made a promise to never, ever let go.

Musubi Manu sat down with a soft *plop*, out of breath, his nori a little crooked. "Whoa," he said.

"Let me try," said Niu, who was secretly a tiny bit competitive about strength on account of having such a tough shell. He rolled up, set his round coconut self against the opihi, and pushed. He pushed so hard his straw-sprout quivered and his woven hat slid down over his three little face-dots. The opihi did not so much as wobble.

"It's cheating," Niu announced from under his hat. "It's holding on with magic."

"It's holding on with *muscle,*" said Mac quietly. "A teeny tiny one. All on its own. Underneath that little shell."

The three of them looked at the opihi with new respect. It was, after all, smaller than Mac. And it had just out-pulled a coconut and a musubi without even trying.

---

Then a wave came.

Not a big scary wave — just a friendly low-tide swoosh, the kind that races up the rocks and slides back down again. *Whoooosh.* It splashed right over the whole pool. Niu's turban shell, which he had set on a rock to admire, went tumbling — *clink-clink-clink* — and Niu had to roll after it crying "MY TREASURE, COME BACK!" Musubi Manu's neat little row of pebble-and-feather-and-seaweed scattered everywhere in one slosh. The feather sailed off to sea, probably to start a new life.

But when the water pulled back...

Every single opihi was still there. Still clinging. Not one had washed away.

The three friends stared.

"They held on," whispered Mac.

"They held on," agreed Musubi Manu, and something warm and amazed bloomed inside his rice-block heart. "The wave came, and everything else got tossed around — even Niu's shell, even my whole catalogue — but the opihi *held on.*"

Niu rolled back, soggy, clutching his rescued turban shell. He looked at the opihi differently now. "So... they're not weak for being small," he said slowly. "They're strong for holding on."

"That's *exactly* it," said Musubi Manu, and his eyes went sparkly. He thought about it the way he thought about everything — practically, carefully. "You know what? I think holding on tight to what you love is its very own kind of strength. The opihi don't need to be shiny. They just need to never let go of their rock."

Mac, the smallest one, the one who'd found them, glowed pink all the way through his shell. "Small but mighty," he said softly. "Shell to heart."

"Shell to heart," everyone agreed.

---

They spent the rest of the morning the *right* way — looking close. And once they started looking close, the tide pools turned out to be stuffed with tiny wonders they'd missed completely.

Niu found a hermit crab wearing a borrowed house and gave a long lecture about it that the hermit crab politely ignored. "He's not even listening," Niu huffed. "He's the rudest treasure I've ever found." The hermit crab waved one claw, which Niu decided to count as applause.

Mac found a sea slug the color of a sunset, no bigger than a raisin, doing the slowest, wiggliest happy-dance in the universe. He found a baby cowrie shell as smooth as a bead. He found a pool so still and clear it held a perfect upside-down sky, with three tiny clouds floating in it. "Look," he kept whispering. "Look, look, *look.*" And the more he looked, the more there was — a whole hidden world that had been waiting all along for somebody small enough and quiet enough to notice it.

And Musubi Manu — Musubi Manu just watched a single opihi for a very long time, the way you watch something you've decided to love. He even made a brand-new page on his clipboard and wrote, very carefully, **THINGS THAT ARE STRONGER THAN THEY LOOK,** and at the top of the list he wrote *opihi,* and right under it he wrote *Mac.*

When the tide began to whisper back in, Musubi Manu gathered his soggy, salty, sandy crew on the warm rocks.

"I have made a decision," he announced, holding up the Grand Treasure Trophy seashell. "The winner of today's treasure hunt is... the opihi. For Best Holding On."

"You can't give the trophy to a shellfish," said Niu. "It can't hold the trophy. It's holding the rock."

"Then I'll leave the trophy *next* to it," said Musubi Manu, and he gently set the polished shell on the rock beside the little opihi, where the sea could see it. "A treasure for a treasure. Hold tight, little friend. I'll come visit."

The opihi, of course, said nothing. It just held on, the way it always had and always would. But somehow that felt like the warmest answer in the world.

And the funny part? Nobody minded losing. Because they'd all figured out the real treasure was the looking — and the looking-together.

"Tough shell, sweet heart," said Niu, leaning his coconut self against Mac, who leaned right back.

"Wrapped up and ready," said Musubi Manu, packing his clipboard away. He looked at his two friends, small and round and dear, sitting beside him in the golden light. *Hold on tight to what you love,* he thought. *That's the strongest thing there is.*

And he held on.

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, the world is full of tiny wonders, and the very best of them are easy to miss unless you look close and look kind. Some treasures sparkle, and some just hold on quietly, strong and sure, the way the people who love you hold on to you. Tonight, let the tide of sleep slide all the way in, soft and warm around your toes. You are someone's brightest treasure, found in the very first pool they ever looked. Hold on to the day you had, then let it gently go, knowing tomorrow's full of wonders too. Goodnight.
