# Story 22: Kauai, the Garden Island Mystery

**Main Character:** Lilikoi Lani
**Guest Characters:** Pua the Pineapple, Mac the Macadamia, Poi Popo
**Setting:** The lush green valleys and red dirt trails of Kauai
**Theme:** Curiosity is wonderful when you bring friends along.
**Hawaiian Spotlight:** **nahele** (nah-HEH-leh) = forest

---

On Kauai, the Garden Island, a red dirt trail wound between walls of the greenest green ever seen.

Lilikoi Lani froze mid-hop, wrinkly purple skin quivering. "Do you SMELL that?"

"I smell red dirt," said Pua the Pineapple. "On my crown. Which is unacceptable."

"I smell lunch," said Mac the Macadamia, mostly inside his shell, as usual.

"Something sweet. Something WILD," said Lilikoi, pointing at the **nahele** (nah-HEH-leh) = forest. "Out THERE."

"Adventure!" she squealed, and zipped straight off the trail into the leaves.

"Lani, WAIT!" hollered Pua. Too late. The purple fruit had vanished into the green.

"She'll get stuck in a spiderweb or married to a banana," sighed Pua. "Come on, Mac. Crown up."

---

Inside, the world turned cool and shadowy, sunlight wobbling on the moss in gold coins.

Pua ducked under a low branch. It caught her crown. She dangled there, gently swinging, with great dignity.

Mac bounced up and butted the branch. Twang! Pua dropped free, crown first, into the ferns.

"Over here!" came a faraway zesty voice. "You have to SEE this!"

"This is the part," announced Pua, "where something jumps out and scares us."

"Please don't say that," said Mac, and shut his shell so he was just a round nut.

---

The trail tipped downhill. A little stream appeared, giggling along beside them like it knew the way.

Lilikoi waited on a huge floating leaf. "Boat's leaving! Sour first, sweet always!"

Pua and Mac tumbled aboard. The leaf wobbled, then whooshed off down the chuckling water.

Faster. Bumpier. Around a mossy bend the stream stopped, then dropped. "WATERFAAAALL," said everyone.

The leaf slid down a silver ribbon of water, SPLASH, and stopped in a hidden valley.

---

A secret bowl of green ringed by silver waterfalls, guavas like pink lanterns, mangoes blushing gold everywhere.

"It's a whole valley of ME," breathed Lilikoi, gazing at a tree swinging with ripe purple lilikoi.

"It's a whole valley of SNACKS," breathed Mac, popping his shell open in wonder.

"Mystery solved," said Pua. "Can we go home? My crown is getting humid."

But the sweetest smell still drifted from behind a curtain of hanging vines. Lilikoi's skin tingled.

---

Behind the vines sat something round and lavender-grey, like a little cloud that had come down to nap.

It wore a yellow lei and hummed a slow song. Then it opened two ancient eyes.

Mac shut his shell. Lilikoi zipped right up close. "Who ARE you? Why do you smell so AMAZING?"

"I am Poi Popo, a very old taro," said the cloud. "The smell is my baking."

She lifted one soft arm. In the moss glowed a stone oven, golden mochi cooling on a leaf.

---

"I let the smell wander out as an invitation," said Poi Popo. "Most are too shy to follow."

"That was ME," said Lilikoi proudly. "I'm not shy about ANYTHING."

"I noticed," said Poi Popo, and Mac's shell shook with a tiny chuckle.

"A nose can find the valley," said Poi Popo. "Only **'ohana** (oh-HAH-nah) = family can enjoy it."

Lilikoi looked at Pua, who had dangled for her, and Mac, scared the whole way and here anyway.

"Mahalo for coming after me," said Lilikoi quietly. "Even though I zipped off without you."

"Always," said Pua, and did not mention her crown at all.

Then came the mochi. Mango melted golden. Mac ate three and glued one to his shell.

---

The waterfalls turned rosy. Poi Popo tucked the leftovers into Mac's shell, the safest pocket in the islands.

"Come back anytime," called Poi Popo from her glowing oven. "Follow your nose. And bring your 'ohana."

---

Down the moonlit trail went Lilikoi, slowly, in the middle, crown on one side, shell on the other.

Mac's shell smelled of mochi. The fireflies blinked. Sour first, sweet always, and sweetest side by side.

---
