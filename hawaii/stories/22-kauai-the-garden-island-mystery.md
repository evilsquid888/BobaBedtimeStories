# Story 22: Kauai, the Garden Island Mystery

**Main Character:** Lilikoi Lani
**Guest Characters:** Pua the Pineapple, Mac the Macadamia
**Setting:** The lush green valleys and red dirt trails of Kauai
**Theme:** Curiosity is wonderful when you bring friends along.
**Hawaiian Spotlight:** **nahele** (nah-HEH-leh) = forest

---

Lilikoi Lani caught the smell before anyone else did, because Lilikoi Lani always caught the smell first. That was the whole point of being a small round passion fruit with a nose for trouble. One moment she was rolling along a red dirt trail on the Garden Island of Kauai, leaving her usual tangy-sweet scent trail behind her like a ribbon of perfume — and the next, she froze mid-roll, wrinkly purple skin quivering.

"Do you smell that?" she whispered.

"I smell red dirt," said Pua the Pineapple, brushing a fleck off her royal green crown. "On my crown. Which is unacceptable."

"I smell lunch," said Mac the Macadamia, who was mostly inside his shell, as usual.

"No, no, NO," Lilikoi bounced. "Something sweet. Something WILD. Something out THERE." She pointed one tiny seed-finger toward the wall of green at the edge of the trail. "It's coming from the nahele."

And just like that, the mystery began.

The **nahele** (nah-HEH-leh) = forest of Kauai was the greenest green you ever saw, so green it made other greens feel embarrassed. Ferns spilled over ferns. Vines hugged trees that hugged other vines. Somewhere deep inside, water trickled and birds whistled and a mysterious sweet smell drifted out like a secret saying, *follow me, follow me, if you dare.*

Lilikoi dared.

"Adventure!" she squealed, and rolled straight off the trail into the leaves.

"Lani, WAIT —" Pua hollered, but it was too late. The purple fruit had vanished into the green.

Pua looked at Mac. Mac looked at Pua, then quietly closed his shell with a soft *click*, which is what Mac did when he was nervous, which was often.

"We can't just let her go alone," Pua sighed dramatically, the way she did everything. "She'll get herself stuck in a spiderweb or married to a banana or who knows what. Come on, Mac. Crown up, sweetheart."

And so they plunged into the nahele after her.

Inside, the world turned soft and shadowy and cool. Big leaves filtered the sunlight into wobbling gold coins on the ground. Pua had to duck her crown under a low branch — a terrible indignity for a pineapple of her standing — and Mac rolled close behind, peeking out of his shell just enough to see.

"Lani?" Pua called. "LANI. If you've found something wonderful, you have to share it with the GROUP. That's the rule."

"Over here!" came a faraway zesty voice. "You have to SEE this!"

They followed the voice, and they followed the smell, which grew sweeter and sweeter — guava and mango and something flowery, like the forest itself had been baking all morning. The trail tilted downhill. The red dirt turned to soft moss. A little stream appeared and giggled along beside them like it knew the way.

"This is the part of the story," Pua announced, "where something jumps out and scares us."

"Please don't say that," said Mac, ducking fully back into his shell so that he was just a smooth round nut rolling along on his own.

But nothing jumped out. Instead, the trees opened up — and the three friends tumbled into a hidden valley so beautiful that even Pua forgot to mention her crown.

It was a secret bowl of green, ringed by waterfalls that came down the cliffs in skinny silver threads. And everywhere — *everywhere* — grew wild fruit. Fat guavas hung like pink lanterns. Mangoes blushed gold and orange. Bunches of tiny apple-bananas dangled from one tree, and another tree was absolutely *crowded* with lilikoi just like Lani, swinging on the vines, purple and ripe and happy.

"It's a whole valley of ME," Lilikoi breathed.

"It's a whole valley of SNACKS," Mac breathed, popping his shell open in wonder.

"It's the source of the smell," said Pua. "Mystery solved! Can we go home now? My crown is getting humid."

But it was not, in fact, the source of the smell. Because the sweetest scent of all was still coming from somewhere deeper, behind a curtain of hanging vines at the far end of the valley. Lilikoi's wrinkly skin tingled.

"Sour first," she whispered, "sweet always." And she rolled toward the vines.

"Here we go again," groaned Pua, but she followed. So did Mac, though he kept very close to Pua's spiky side.

Behind the vines was a small, mossy clearing. And in the middle of the clearing sat — well. It was hard to say at first. It was round and soft and grey-lavender, like a little cloud that had come down to nap, wearing a lei of yellow flowers and humming a low, sleepy song.

Lilikoi stopped so fast she nearly rolled into the air.

The cloud opened two gentle, ancient eyes.

"Aloha, little travelers," said the cloud, in a voice as warm and slow as honey poured in sunlight. "You found my valley. Not many do."

Mac immediately closed his shell. Pua puffed up her crown to look braver than she felt. But Lilikoi — zesty, curious Lilikoi — rolled right up close.

"Who ARE you?" she asked. "And why do you smell so AMAZING?"

The cloud chuckled, a sound like a slow purple river. "I am Poi Popo, child. An old, old taro from an old, old land. And the smell you followed all the way into the nahele is not me." She smiled. "It is my baking."

She lifted one soft arm — and there, tucked into the moss, was a little stone oven, warm and glowing, with rows of tiny golden things cooling on a leaf.

"Mochi," said Poi Popo. "Wild-fruit mochi. Guava, mango, lilikoi. I make them every morning and let the smell wander out into the forest. It is my way of saying *come visit an old grandmother. Come share. Come be 'ohana.*" She winked. "The smell does the inviting. Most are too shy to follow it. You three were not."

"That was ME," said Lilikoi proudly. "I'm not shy about ANYTHING."

"I noticed," said Poi Popo, and everyone laughed, even Mac, just a little, from inside his shell.

Now here is the thing about curiosity, and Poi Popo knew it well. Curiosity is a wonderful thing — it pulls you off the trail and into the green, toward the sweet smell and the secret valley. But curiosity all by itself can get a little fruit lost, or stuck, or married to a banana. Curiosity is *best* when you bring your friends along, so that when you find something wonderful, there's someone to gasp beside you, and someone to share the mochi with, and someone to find the way home in the dark.

"You followed the smell with your heart," Poi Popo told Lilikoi gently. "But you brought your friends with your aloha. That is why you made it all the way to me. A nose can find the valley. Only 'ohana can enjoy it."

Lilikoi's whole tangy face went warm. She looked back at Pua, who had ducked under a hundred branches for her, and at Mac, who had been scared the entire time and *come anyway*.

"Mahalo for coming after me," she said quietly. "Even though I rolled off without you."

"Always," said Pua, and for once she did not mention her crown at all.

"Small," said Mac, peeking out, "but mighty. Shell to heart." Which, for Mac, was practically a speech.

Then Poi Popo passed around the warm wild-fruit mochi, and oh, you should have seen them. The lilikoi mochi burst tangy-sweet on the tongue. The mango melted golden. The guava tasted pink. Pua said it was the finest thing she'd ever eaten and immediately asked for another. Mac ate three and got mochi stuck to his shell. Lilikoi rolled in a happy circle, leaving little loops of scent behind her, and the whole valley smelled like a hug.

They stayed until the silver waterfalls turned rosy with sunset. Poi Popo told old island stories — of the land being born, of seeds floating across the whole wide ocean to land on Kauai, of forests that remembered every traveler who'd ever followed their nose into the green. The little fireflies came out and blinked like Pele's tiny cousins. The stream giggled itself to sleep.

When at last it was time to go, Poi Popo wrapped the leftover mochi in a ti leaf and tucked it into Mac's shell, because his shell, she said, was the safest pocket in the islands. She pointed them toward the moonlit trail home.

"Come back anytime," she called, glowing softly in the dark. "Just follow your nose. And —" she winked one last ancient wink — "bring your 'ohana."

Lilikoi led the way back through the nahele, scent trail looping bright behind her. But this time she rolled slowly, right in the middle, with Pua's crown on one side and Mac's smooth shell on the other, all three of them full of warm mochi and full of each other.

And do you know what the curious little passion fruit thought, all the way home under the Kauai stars? She thought that the very best mysteries aren't the ones you solve alone. They're the ones you solve *together* — and then snack on afterward.

Sour first. Sweet always. And sweetest of all, side by side.

## Goodnight Blessing
May your nose lead you toward sweet things, and may your friends always follow close behind. May every dark forest open into a hidden valley of warm mochi and gentle grandmothers. The waterfalls are whispering and the fireflies are dimming and the island is tucking you in. Wherever your curious heart wanders tomorrow, it will never wander alone. Goodnight.
