# Story 4: Musubi Manu and the Bento Backpack Mix-Up

**Main Character:** Musubi Manu
**Guest Characters:** Lani the Loco Moco; Lilikoi Lani; Manapua Mano
**Setting:** A neighborhood plate-lunch shop and the streets of Kaimuki, Oahu
**Theme:** Being dependable is its own kind of brave.
**Hawaiian Spotlight:** **ohana** (oh-HAH-nah) = family

---

Musubi Manu had exactly one job that morning, and he intended to do it perfectly.

Six lunches, six friends, before the lunch bell. He stood squared-up in the plate-lunch shop doorway.

Pink spam blanket smoothed flat. Nori seatbelt snug. Bento backpack fat and warm on his back.

"Wrapped up and ready!" he announced to nobody. Nobody never argues, and never loses a backpack.

He counted the lunches twice. Then he checked that he had counted twice. Six. Still six.

Off he marched into sunny Kaimuki, a tidy fellow who had never, ever lost anything.

He was about to lose something.

---

The first delivery went beautifully, which is exactly how disasters like to begin.

Lani the Loco Moco sprawled on a bus bench, fried-egg hat over one eye. "Howzit, Manu."

"One plate, extra crispy." Manu unzipped the backpack and passed over a warm bundle. "Eat slow."

The bench was a heap: an umbrella, one slipper, a paper bag of mangoes. Manu's tidy soul shuddered.

He set his backpack on the heap for one tiny second to re-buckle his crooked seatbelt.

Then he grabbed a strap, slung it on, and marched off feeling wonderfully organized.

He had grabbed the bag of mangoes.

---

Three blocks later, Lilikoi Lani bounced on a banyan root, leaving tangy purple loops in the air.

"Manu! Lunch lunch lunch! Sour? Sweet? Surprise?"

"Today's surprise is no surprise," said Manu proudly, and unzipped the bag. Six mangoes smiled up at him.

He shook the bag. He flipped it over. The mangoes just sat there being mangoes.

"Bold choice," said Lilikoi. "Very orange. Honestly, I respect it."

"I lost the lunches," Manu whispered, going pale as steamed rice.

Then he squared his shoulders and re-buckled his seatbelt tight. "Lilikoi. I have to **wikiwiki** (VEE-kee-VEE-kee) = hurry."

"Sour first, sweet always. Let's GO!" Lilikoi did three backflips.

---

Back at the bench: no umbrella, no slipper, no backpack. Just Lani, snoring.

"Lani! The heap! Where did it go?" Manu shook him gently.

"Mmf. A steamy bun grabbed the backpack," mumbled Lani. "Said it smelled like lunch."

"Manapua has the lunches," groaned Manu, "and Manapua is the hungriest friend on the island."

---

They followed the teriyaki smell like bloodhounds. Lilikoi bounced off three mailboxes and a fence post.

She zipped straight through a cloud of bakery sugar and came out white as a snowball.

"I'm a GHOST fruit," she announced. "Wikiwiki, wikiwiki!"

Then Manu saw it: a fat bento backpack bouncing along the top of the hill.

"THERE!" Manu grabbed a fallen banana leaf, hopped on, and sledded down the hill after it.

He hit a bump. He flew. He landed in the park, square side down, perfectly upright.

---

There sat Manapua Mano, backpack open, one lunch half-unwrapped in his soft pillowy arms.

"MANAPUA, WAIT!" hollered Manu, the loudest he had ever been about anything.

Manapua jumped. Steam puffed out of his swirl-knot. "Manu! I found it! I kept it SAFE!"

"I was just about to deliver them all. After one tiny snack. Maybe two."

Manu looked at Manapua's big, hungry, completely honest face. "You weren't taking them. You were guarding them."

"Of course! It's everybody's lunch. **ohana** (oh-HAH-nah) = family. Nobody's lunch gets left behind!"

Manu laughed so hard his seatbelt popped crooked, and for once he let it stay.

---

They delivered the rest together, a tidy square and a bouncy bun, snowy Lilikoi looping ahead.

The lunch bell rang the exact second the last lunch landed. Zero complaints.

Back at the shop, Manu sat down, wrung out and warm. "I thought losing it meant I failed."

"Dependable doesn't mean never dropping anything," said Manapua, mouth full. "It means going back."

Manu re-buckled his seatbelt, snug and straight. "Wrapped up and ready. For next time."

---
