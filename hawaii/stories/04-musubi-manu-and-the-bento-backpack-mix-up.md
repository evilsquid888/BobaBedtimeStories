# Story 4: Musubi Manu and the Bento Backpack Mix-Up

**Main Character:** Musubi Manu
**Guest Characters:** Lani the Loco Moco; Lilikoi Lani; Manapua Mano
**Setting:** A neighborhood plate-lunch shop and the streets of Kaimuki, Oahu
**Theme:** Being dependable is its own kind of brave.
**Hawaiian Spotlight:** **ohana** (oh-HAH-nah) = family
**Cameo:** Stitch (Lilo & Stitch) — drops out of a palm tree, sniffs Manu for lunch, and piles four wrong backpacks on top of him; walk-on: Meowth (Pokémon)

---

Musubi Manu had one job that morning, and he meant to do it perfectly.

Six lunches for six friends, his whole **ohana** (oh-HAH-nah) = family, before the lunch bell.

He cinched his nori seatbelt so snug he squeaked. "Wrapped up and ready!" he wheezed to nobody.

He counted the lunches twice. Then he checked his counting. Six. Still six.

Off he marched into sunny Kaimuki, a tidy fellow who had never lost anything.

He was about to lose something.

---

Lani the Loco Moco sprawled on a bus bench, fried-egg hat over one eye. "Howzit, Manu."

"One plate, extra crispy." Manu unzipped the backpack and passed a warm bundle. "Eat slow."

The bench was a heap: an umbrella, one slipper, a bag of mangoes. Manu's tidy soul shuddered.

He set his backpack on the heap for one second to re-buckle his crooked seatbelt.

Then he grabbed a strap, slung it on, and marched off feeling wonderfully organized.

He had grabbed the bag of mangoes.

---

Three blocks on, Lilikoi Lani bounced on a banyan root, leaving tangy purple loops behind.

"Manu! Lunch lunch lunch!" sang Lilikoi. "Sour? Sweet? Surprise?"

"Today's surprise is no surprise," said Manu proudly, unzipping the bag. Six mangoes smiled up.

He shook the bag. He flipped it. The mangoes just sat there being mangoes.

"Bold choice," said Lilikoi. "Very orange. Honestly, I respect it."

"I lost the lunches," Manu whispered, pale as steamed rice. "We must **wikiwiki** (VEE-kee-VEE-kee) = hurry."

---

Back at the bench: no umbrella, no slipper, no backpack. Just Lani, dozing.

"Lani! The heap! Where did it go?" Manu shook him gently.

"Mmf. A steamy bun grabbed the backpack," mumbled Lani. "Said it smelled like lunch."

"The hungriest bun on the island has our lunches," groaned Manu. "WIKIWIKI!"

---

They followed the teriyaki smell like bloodhounds. Lilikoi bounced off three mailboxes.

Then Manu saw it: a fat bento backpack bouncing along the hilltop.

"THERE!" Manu grabbed a banana leaf, hopped on, and sledded down the hill after it.

He hit a bump. He flew. He landed in the park, square side down, upright.

---

A blue blur dropped out of a palm tree onto Manu. Big ears. Four arms. Too many teeth.

Stitch sniffed the bag. He sniffed Manu. "Lunch?" rasped Stitch, grabbing a mango in each arm.

Stitch bit a mango. Bit the bag. Bit the banana leaf. "Ohana?" he asked hopefully.

From the hedge Stitch dragged a backpack. Then another. Then two more, one in each spare arm.

He stacked all four on Manu: coconuts, a ukulele, wet sand, and one surprised crab.

"Not lunch," sighed Stitch. He scrambled up the palm and hung by his toes, waving four arms.

"Four wrong backpacks," said Manu from under the pile. "Now I'm losing things I never had."

---

Manu wobbled free. Across the grass sat Manapua Mano, backpack open, one lunch half-unwrapped.

"MANAPUA, WAIT!" hollered Manu, the loudest he had ever been about anything.

Manapua jumped. Steam puffed from his swirl-knot. "Manu! I found it! I kept it SAFE!"

"I was about to deliver them," said Manapua. "After one tiny snack. Maybe two."

Manu looked at that big, hungry, honest face. "You weren't taking them. You were guarding them."

"Of course!" said Manapua. "It's everybody's lunch. Ohana. Nobody's lunch gets left behind!"

Manu laughed so hard his seatbelt popped crooked, and for once he let it stay.

---

They delivered the rest together. The lunch bell rang the second the last lunch landed.

Back at the shop, Manu sat, wrung out and warm. "I thought losing it meant I failed."

"Dependable doesn't mean never dropping anything," said Manapua, mouth full. "It means going back."

Outside, Meowth stomped past hugging the bag of mangoes. "Not one coin," he grumbled.

Manu re-buckled his seatbelt, snug and straight. "Wrapped up and ready. For next time."

---

## Goodnight Blessing
May your backpack be a little fuller than you remember, and your friends a little kinder than you fear. When something feels lost, go back. Ohana means nobody gets left behind. Rest now, folded neat as nori. Goodnight.
