# Story 20: The Road to Hana and the Slow Honu Way

**Main Character:** Honu Haupia
**Guest Characters:** Mac the Macadamia, Lilikoi Lani
**Setting:** The winding, waterfall-lined Road to Hana on Maui
**Theme:** Slowing down lets you see the wonders you'd miss.
**Hawaiian Spotlight:** **hana** (HAH-nah) = work / craft (also the town Hana)

---

The trouble began, as trouble often does, with a wiggly purple fruit who could not sit still.

"WE'RE GOING TO HANA TODAY!" hollered Lilikoi Lani, bouncing so hard her tangy-orange seeds rattled like a maraca. "Buckle up, buttercups, because I am going to ZOOM us there before lunch!"

She was perched on the dashboard of a little open-top island car, wrinkly and purple and absolutely vibrating with excitement. Beside her sat Mac the Macadamia, peeking nervously out of his glossy shell, and behind the wheel — moving exactly as fast as a sleepy cloud — was Honu Haupia.

Honu was a wobbly, snow-white coconut-pudding sea turtle, her jiggly shell patterned in neat little **haupia** squares that quivered with every gentle bump in the road. She blinked her ancient eyes.

"Zoom?" Honu said slowly, the way she said most things. "On the Road to Hana? Oh, little one. This road does not like to be zoomed."

"Everything likes to be zoomed!" said Lani. "VROOM!"

---

The Road to Hana, you should know, is the twistiest road on all of Maui. It curls around the island like a noodle that fell asleep and dreamed of more noodles. Six hundred curves. Fifty-four one-lane bridges. Waterfalls hiding behind every leaf. It is not a road for hurrying. It is a road for *hana* — for craft, for care, for doing a thing properly and slowly, the way the old island folk built it by hand.

But Lani did not know about craft. Lani only knew about VROOM.

So when Honu stopped the car to let a chubby crab cross the road, Lani groaned dramatically and flopped backward into Mac's shell.

"We could've been THREE curves ahead by now!" she wailed.

"Or," rumbled Honu, "we could be exactly here. Look."

She nodded one slow flipper toward the roadside. There, tucked behind a curtain of ferns, was a tiny waterfall no bigger than a kitchen tap — but the morning sun was hitting its mist *just so*, and a teensy rainbow had stretched itself across the spray like a ribbon on a present.

Mac the Macadamia gasped. It was the first sound he'd made all morning.

"A rainbow," he whispered. "Small. But... mighty."

"Pfft," said Lani. "I've seen rainbows. Bigger ones. Faster ones." But the funny thing was, she didn't look away. Not for a whole, entire minute. Which, for Lilikoi Lani, was approximately forever.

---

They rolled on, slow as warm honey, and Honu hummed an old turtle tune that had no hurry in it at all.

"Slow flippers," she said, "far journeys."

"Slow flippers, NUMB BOTTOM," Lani shot back, and Mac made a tiny squeak that might have been a laugh trying to escape his shell.

Around the next bend, the car gave a wheeze and a hiccup and a sad little *putt-putt-putt*.

"Uh oh," said Mac.

"UH OH?" Lani's seeds rattled. "What do you mean uh oh? We can't break down! We have ZOOMING to do!"

Honu eased the car gently to the side of the road, beneath a giant mango tree, and peered at the smoking engine with the calm of someone who has watched ten thousand sunsets and is not worried about an eleventh.

"The engine is tired," Honu announced. "It has been pushed too fast on a road that asks to be loved slow. It needs to cool. It needs to rest." She settled back. "It needs about an hour."

"AN HOUR?!" Lani looked like she might pop her own seeds out from sheer impatience. "An hour is a *thousand years* in lilikoi time!"

"An hour," said Honu serenely, "is exactly enough."

---

Now, here is where most fruits would have a meltdown. And to be fair, Lani started one. She paced. She huffed. She did a little angry roll across the dashboard and announced she was going to walk to Hana herself, which lasted four steps before she remembered she had very tiny legs and the road was very long.

But Mac the Macadamia, quiet little Mac, had wandered off the road and down a mossy path. And from somewhere in the green, his small voice floated back:

"You guys... you HAVE to come see this."

Honu glided. Lani stomped. And what they found at the bottom of that path made even Lani forget, for a moment, all about VROOM.

A pool. A perfect, secret swimming pool of clear blue water, fed by a waterfall three turtles tall, with bamboo clacking softly overhead like nature's own wind chimes. Sunlight came down in golden ropes. Little silver fish stitched the water with sparkles.

"This isn't on any map," breathed Mac.

"That," said Honu, with the smallest proud smile, "is because you cannot find it at fifty miles an hour. The fast ones drive right past. This place belongs to the slow."

Lani opened her mouth to say something cheeky.

Nothing came out.

For the first time in her whole zesty life, Lilikoi Lani was speechless — and then, very quietly, she did the bravest thing of all. She climbed up onto a sun-warm rock, dipped her purple toes in the cool water, and went *aaaaahhhh*.

"Okay," she admitted. "This is... kind of nice."

"Sour first," said Honu gently. "Sweet always. Isn't that your saying, little one?"

Lani grinned, and her grin was sweeter than her seeds.

---

They spent the tired-engine hour exactly the way the island wanted them to. Mac discovered he could pop his shell open and use it as a tiny floating boat, paddling in happy circles while Lani directed him like a very bossy lighthouse. Honu floated on her back, her haupia shell jiggling with each ripple, and told them the old story of how the Road to Hana was built — not by machines in a rush, but by many hands over many years, each curve carved with care, each bridge laid stone by stone.

"That," Honu said, "is *hana*. The work. The craft. You cannot rush a thing made with love. And you cannot *enjoy* a thing if you rush past it, either."

Mac nodded so hard he nearly capsized his shell-boat. Lani, floating with her seeds making little ripples, said, "So... the slow part isn't the boring part. The slow part is the *good* part."

Honu's ancient eyes twinkled like sun on the sea. "Now you are thinking like a honu."

When the hour was up, the little engine had cooled and forgiven them, and it started on the very first try with a cheerful *putt-putt-PUTT* — as if it, too, had enjoyed the rest.

---

They drove the rest of the way the slow honu way.

And oh, what they saw! A waterfall shaped exactly like a wedding veil. A bridge so old and mossy it looked like a sleeping green dragon. A grove where the trees had rainbow bark — actual rainbow, painted by nature in stripes of orange and green and purple — and Lani declared it "the most stylish forest I have ever met."

They stopped for every single one. Lani, who that morning had wanted to zoom past it all, now shouted "STOP! STOP! Look at THAT one!" so often that Honu joked she was getting whiplash from all the slowing down.

At last, as the sky turned the soft pink of the inside of a guava, they rolled gently into the little town of Hana itself. Sleepy. Peaceful. Wrapped in evening mist. A town named for the very thing they'd learned all day: doing things with care, and craft, and love.

"We made it," whispered Mac.

"We made it *slow*," said Lani — and she said it like it was the best part. Because now, it was.

They sat together on a quiet bluff above the sea, three friends and one tired-but-happy engine, watching the first stars blink awake over the water. Honu's shell glowed faintly in the dusk, soft and white as moonlight made of pudding.

"Tomorrow," said Honu, "we drive home."

"The slow way?" asked Lani, already yawning.

"Is there any other?" said Honu.

And Lilikoi Lani — the wiggliest, zoomiest, most impatient fruit on all of Maui — snuggled down against Honu's warm wobbly shell, closed her sparkly eyes, and decided that slow was, perhaps, her very favorite speed of all.

## Goodnight Blessing

Slow your flippers now, little one, the way the honu does. There is no race to sleep and no prize for getting there first — only the soft, good journey of a quiet breath, and then another. May you remember that the best things are the ones you pause to see: a small rainbow, a secret pool, a friend close beside you in the dark. Let the waterfalls hush you and the stars keep watch. Drift slow, drift safe, drift far. Goodnight.
