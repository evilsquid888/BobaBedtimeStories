# Story 24: Molokai and the Friendly Quiet

**Main Character:** Poi Popo
**Guest Characters:** Manapua Mano, Lani the Loco Moco, Kolo the rooster (and the treat-folk of Molokai)
**Setting:** The peaceful, old-fashioned island of Molokai
**Theme:** Quiet places hold their own kind of richness.
**Hawaiian Spotlight:** **mālie** (mah-LEE-eh) = calm / peaceful

---

The little ferry chugged toward Molokai, putt-putt-putt, so slow the waves kept overtaking it.

"Are we THERE yet?" bounced Manapua Mano. "Is there a snack store the size of a VOLCANO?"

Lani the Loco Moco yawned, egg-hat sliding sideways. "I hope there's a hammock. A LONG one."

At the bow sat Poi Popo, lavender-grey and soft, smiling her slow thousand-crossings smile.

"Molokai is not a roller-coaster island, **keiki** (KAY-kee) = little one," said Poi Popo.

"Molokai is a **mālie** (mah-LEE-eh) = calm island. You don't see it. You FEEL it."

"A calm island," said Manapua, chewing a snack. "Is that like a boring island, but polite?"

---

The ferry bumped a dock. Ashore: one sleepy street, no honking, no blinking lights, and a rooster.

Kolo the rooster strolled down the middle of the road like he owned it. He sort of did.

"It's so... QUIET!" wailed Manapua, and the hills wailed it back: quiet... quiet... quiet...

"Buddy," said Lani. "You woke up the WHOLE island."

"There IS no whole island to wake up!" Manapua flopped on a bench. "Where's the ZOOM?"

"You are looking for the loud kind of rich," said Poi Popo. "Molokai keeps a different treasure."

---

Manapua decided to make his own zoom. "Rooster! I challenge you! Race to the end of the street!"

Kolo blinked once, slowly, and began to walk.

Manapua bounced like popcorn, boing, boing, past the poi shop, past the bench, past a mango tree.

Bounce too high, bun. He landed in the mango tree and stuck there like a dumpling in chopsticks.

Kolo pecked the trunk once. A mango bonked Manapua loose. Plop, onto the road.

"I lost," said Manapua, dazed, watching Kolo stroll across the finish, "to a chicken who was WALKING."

---

Down a red-dirt road, an old sweet potato on a porch wove lauhala and waved. They waved back.

A fisher-crab trotted up with two fish and simply GAVE one to a neighbor planting taro.

"He didn't even sell it," said Lani, egg-hat tilting. "He just... gave it."

"The quiet is an empty bowl, keiki," said Poi Popo. "Empty is what lets you fill it."

Manapua knew bowls. "So quiet isn't NOTHING," he said slowly. "Quiet is ROOM."

Then Manapua was quiet for the first time all day. That deserves its own little firework.

---

On top of a green hill, Poi Popo spread her soft arms wide. "Now. Listen."

Manapua scrunched his pillowy face and listened. At first, nothing. Then... everything.

Wind combing the grass. A wave folding, whoosh... shhh. Lani's tummy gurgling from the grass.

"It's not empty!" marveled Manapua. "It's FULL. It's just full of small things."

"Loud places shout one big thing," said Poi Popo. "Quiet places give you a thousand little gifts."

Lani lay back with his egg-hat over his eyes. "I'm opening a gift right now. It's a nap."

---

Eleven minutes later, Manapua's tummy rumbled like thunder. "Popo, calm is nice, but there's no FOOD COURT."

"Come," said Poi Popo, as the sky turned lilikoi-gold. "Now you learn Molokai's deepest secret."

Down by the shore, jar-lanterns blinked on, and treat-folk drifted in: the sweet potato, the fisher-crab, Kolo.

"How did everyone KNOW?" whispered Manapua. "Nobody announced it!"

"On Molokai the sunset is the invitation," said Poi Popo. "You only need to belong."

---

"Everybody brought a SMALL thing," said Manapua, eyeing poi and mango pyramids, "but together it's HUGE?"

"The island's oldest math," said Poi Popo. "A little shared by many becomes a **pā'ina** (PAH-ee-nah) = feast."

Manapua handed his mango to Kolo. Kolo accepted it as his due.

---

"I thought quiet meant nothing happening," yawned Manapua. "Quiet is when you finally HEAR the good things."

Poi Popo wrapped a soft arm around him. "From the land, with aloha. You learn fast, bouncy bun."

The lanterns glowed low, the waves whispered, and Kolo finally, FINALLY fell asleep. Mālie, all along.
