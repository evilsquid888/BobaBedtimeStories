# Story 29: The Penguins Who Came to Surf

**Main Character:** Dole Whip Dani
**Guest Characters:** Piper Paddlefoot, Barnaby Beaksworth, Willa Wobble
**Setting:** A lively surf break off Waikiki with a visiting penguin crew
**Theme:** New friends bring new ways to ride the same wave.
**Hawaiian Spotlight:** nalu (NAH-loo) = wave

---

The morning the penguins arrived, Dole Whip Dani was doing the bravest thing a swirl of pineapple soft-serve can do at the beach: standing in the shade so she wouldn't melt into a puddle of sunshine.

"Stay cool and swirl on!" she sang to nobody, wiggling her glossy golden curl.

And that was when three round black-and-white shapes came waddling up the sand, wearing tiny sunglasses, dragging surfboards far too big for them, and arguing loudly.

"Piper, I am NOT certain the ocean is supposed to be this *warm*," said the tallest one. He had a very serious beak and a very serious frown.

"Barnaby, that's the FUN part!" said the smallest, bounciest one, hopping in circles.

The third penguin simply tipped over into the sand with a soft *floomp*, then gave a cheerful flipper-wave from down there.

Dani's huge sparkly eyes went *enormous*. New friends. At the beach. With boards. This, she decided, was going to be the best day in the history of best days.

"ALOHA!" she cried, swirling right over. "I'm Dani! Are you here to SURF?"

The bouncy one straightened up so fast her sunglasses flew off. "We're here to surf! I'm Piper Paddlefoot! That grumpy one is Barnaby Beaksworth, and the tipped-over one is Willa Wobble. We're *snowboarders*. From very far away. Very cold places."

"We board on snow," Barnaby explained, brushing sand off himself with great dignity. "Down mountains. On powder. Where things are *frozen*, the way nature intended."

"And then somebody," said Willa from the sand, "had the brilliant idea to swap our snowboards for *surf*boards and ride waves instead."

"That somebody," Piper announced proudly, "was ME."

Dani clapped her little cup-hands together. "Then you came to the RIGHT swirl! Out here we ride the **nalu** (NAH-loo) = waves! Come on, I'll introduce you to everybody. We'll have you riding by lunch!"

Barnaby looked at the rolling blue water, then at his board, then back at the water. He gulped. "Yes. Wonderful. Riding. By lunch. Can't wait."

He did not look like he couldn't wait. He looked like he could wait approximately one hundred years.

By the shoreline, the whole gang was already gathered. Kai the Shave Ice lounged in his paper cone, rainbow stripes glistening in the sun, his little paper-umbrella hat tilted at a very relaxed angle. Musubi Manu had laid out a tidy row of surfboards, each one polished and squared away and lined up so straight you could have used them as a ruler. And bobbing in the gentle shallows was Honu Haupia, the wobbly coconut-pudding sea turtle, her shell jiggling softly with each little ripple, looking like a slow scoop of moonlight learning to swim.

"Penguins," Kai said slowly, sitting up so fast his spoon nearly fell out from behind his ear. "Whoa. *Cool* penguins. Hey, hey — black and white, that's like the surf foam on dark sand at night. That's a *gorgeous* color combo, brah. You ever try strawberry syrup? You'd look amazing in pink."

"Wrapped up and ready to teach!" said Musubi, handing each penguin a board with a tidy little bow. "Lesson one. Lie down. Belly on the board. Lesson two — and this is the important one — do not eat the board."

"Why on earth would we eat the board?" asked Barnaby, scandalized.

"You'd be surprised," said Musubi, glancing sideways at Manapua Mano, the pillowy pork bun, who had wandered up and was already eyeing a surfboard like it might be a very large, very crunchy rice cracker.

Piper flopped down instantly. Willa flopped down even faster, because she had already been lying down and simply scooted onto the board. Barnaby lowered himself one careful inch at a time, like he was getting into a bath that might be a shark.

"Now we paddle out," said Musubi, "and wait for a good **nalu**."

So out they went — Dani swirling along on her own little board, Kai gliding like a snowy mountain that had learned to float, and the three penguins paddling with their flippers, which, it turned out, penguins are *extremely* good at. Even Barnaby zipped through the water like a torpedo in a bad mood.

"See?" laughed Piper. "Barnaby's a natural!"

"I am a natural at the SWIMMING," Barnaby corrected. "The standing-up-on-a-moving-mountain-of-water part is where I have concerns. Many concerns. A whole bag of concerns."

They bobbed together past the breakers, where the water rose and fell in long, sleepy swells. Honu Haupia drifted up beside them, slow and serene.

"Little snow-riders," she said in her gentle, ancient voice. "May I share something? When you ride snow, you point your board *down* the mountain, yes? You and the snow go the same way?"

"Exactly!" said Piper.

"The ocean is different," said Honu. "A **nalu** does not run away from you. It *lifts* you. You do not chase it. You let it carry you, the way the sea carries me." She gave a wobble, and her whole pudding-shell shimmied. "Slow flippers, far journeys."

Willa nodded thoughtfully. "So a wave is just... a mountain that comes to *you*?"

"Now you're swirling!" cheered Dani. "A nalu is just a wave you ride with your *belly* instead of with snow! Same fun. Squishier landing!"

A swell began to gather behind them, smooth and round and glassy.

"Here it comes!" called Musubi. "Paddle, paddle — and ride!"

Piper paddled like a windmill in a hurricane. The wave caught her, lifted her up, up, up — and she popped to her feet, wobbling like a wind-up toy, arms out, beak grinning — and she *zoomed*, shrieking with joy, all the way to the beach, where she rolled off into the sand laughing so hard she got the hiccups.

"AGAIN!" she hiccuped. "*hic* — AGAIN!"

Willa caught the next one. But Willa, being Willa, did it the Willa way — which is to say she stayed *lying down* the entire time, flat as a pancake, sliding along on her tummy with a blissful smile and her flippers tucked behind her like a tiny black-and-white rocket.

"Willa, you're supposed to stand UP!" called Piper from the shore.

"Why?" Willa called back, gliding past. "This is delightful. I can see the fish from down here. Hello, fish!"

Kai nearly fell off his cone laughing. "Brah, she's *belly-boarding*. That's a real thing! She invented her own style!"

And that left Barnaby.

Barnaby was still floating out past the break, gripping his board with both flippers, his beak set in a determined line. A perfectly nice **nalu** rolled toward him. He squeezed his eyes shut.

"No thank you," he announced, and let it roll right under him.

Another came. "Also no thank you."

A third. "I am simply going to live out here now. Please send snacks."

Dani swirled up beside him, her golden curl bouncing. "Barnaby," she said softly — and for once she wasn't fast or loud at all. "What are you scared of? You can tell me. I'm basically a melty dessert, I'm not gonna judge."

Barnaby was quiet a moment. The sea rocked them gently. "Back home," he said, "I'm the *good* one. I know exactly how snow works. I know how to point my board and lean and carve. I look graceful." He sighed. "Out here I don't know *anything*. And everyone's watching. What if I fall and look silly?"

Dani's whole sun-bright face went warm and gooey — and not just from the heat.

"Oh, Barnaby," she said. "Falling in the ocean isn't silly. It's the BEST part! You just go *splash* and the water hugs you and you come up laughing. Watch — I fall ALL the time. Sometimes I melt a little. And you know what my friends do?" She grinned. "They cheer LOUDER for the falls than the rides. Because falling means you *tried*."

Barnaby blinked. "That's... a very strange way to think about failing."

"It's the only way to think about it!" Dani said. "You're not the snow-Barnaby out here. You get to be a brand-new ocean-Barnaby. And maybe ocean-Barnaby has a totally new way to ride that nobody's ever seen!"

Down the line, the others had stopped to watch. But they weren't laughing at him. Piper was bouncing. Kai had two thumbs up. Willa hollered, "Come ride with the fish, Barnaby! The fish are very supportive!"

A new swell rose behind them — round, glassy, and patient, as if it had been waiting just for him.

"Slow flippers," murmured Honu, drifting close. "Far journeys."

Barnaby took a deep breath. He pointed his board toward shore. And as the **nalu** lifted him, he did something nobody expected.

He did not stand up like a surfer. He did not lie flat like Willa.

He *crouched* — low and tucked and aerodynamic, exactly the way he carved down a snowy mountain — beak forward, flippers out like little wings, sunglasses gleaming. And he *flew*. Down the face of the wave he carved, left and right, throwing up two glittering fans of spray, swooping like the wave was the most beautiful mountain in the world.

The whole gang went absolutely bananas.

"WHAT IS THAT?!" Kai screamed with delight. "That's the smoothest ride I have EVER SEEN! He's *snow-surfing*!"

Barnaby surfed all the way to the sand, popped up, and — to everyone's enormous shock — pumped both flippers in the air. "I RODE THE NALU!" he bellowed. "AND I LOOKED MAGNIFICENT!"

"You looked SO magnificent!" wept Dani, swirling in joyful circles.

They surfed until the sun grew sleepy and turned the whole sky pineapple-gold. Piper rode standing, hooting and hollering and falling off about every third wave, each splash bigger and gigglier than the last. Willa rode flat on her belly, befriending every fish in the bay and learning all their names — there was a Gerald, and a Susan, and one very small fish she simply called "Tiny." And Barnaby carved his snowy mountain-style down every single wave, inventing a brand-new way to ride that the surf break had never seen, and beaming so wide his serious beak forgot how to be serious.

Even Manapua Mano gave it a go, bouncing onto a board with a happy "Soft on the outside, full of love inside!" — and promptly belly-flopped into a wave with a splash so enormous it soaked everyone on shore. He surfaced, dripping and delighted. "Did everyone SEE that? I did a SPLASH!"

"We not only saw it," called Kai, wiping syrup-flavored sea spray off his face, "we *felt* it, brah."

That evening they all flopped on the warm sand together — soft-serve and shave ice and pork-bun and turtle and three very happy, very salty penguins — watching the last nalu roll gold and pink into the dark.

"You know what I learned today?" said Piper sleepily, leaning against Dani's cool side. "There's no *one* right way to ride a wave."

"There's just *your* way," said Willa, already half-asleep with a contented fish-friend's fin-print on her cheek.

"And sometimes," rumbled Barnaby, smiling for the first time all day, "your old way works perfectly fine in a brand-new place. You just have to be brave enough to try it."

Dani snuggled into the middle of them all, warm and golden and *not melting one bit*.

"Stay cool," she whispered, "and swirl on, my new 'ohana."

And out past the shore, the ocean kept rocking, patient and kind, sending wave after gentle wave — each one ready for whoever was brave enough, or silly enough, or wobbly enough, to ride.

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, may you ride your own waves in your own way, lying down or standing up or crouched like a flying penguin — whatever feels like *you*. When you fall, may the world hug you soft as warm water and cheer you on for trying. May you always find new friends who show you new ways to ride the same old sea. And may your dreams roll in gentle and gold, one cozy nalu at a time. Goodnight.
