# Story 29: The Penguins Who Came to Surf

**Main Character:** Dole Whip Dani
**Guest Characters:** Piper Paddlefoot and Barnaby Beaksworth, two visiting snowboarding penguins
**Setting:** A lively surf break off Waikiki with a visiting penguin crew
**Theme:** New friends bring new ways to ride the same wave.
**Hawaiian Spotlight:** **nalu** (NAH-loo) = wave

---

Dole Whip Dani stood in palm shade, doing the bravest thing soft-serve can do: staying un-puddled.

Up the sand waddled two round penguins in tiny sunglasses, dragging surfboards far too big for them, arguing.

"Piper, I am NOT certain the ocean is supposed to be this warm," said the tall, frowny one.

"Barnaby, that's the FUN part!" said the small bouncy one, hopping in circles until her sunglasses flew off.

Dani's eyes went enormous. "ALOHA! I'm Dani! Are you here to SURF? I'll have you riding by LUNCH!"

"We're snowboarders from somewhere very cold," said Piper Paddlefoot. "Somebody said try waves. That was ME."

Barnaby Beaksworth eyed the water. "Riding. By lunch. Can't wait." He could wait a hundred years.

---

Dani lined up three boards. "Lesson one: belly on the board. Lesson two: do not eat the board."

"You'd be surprised," said Dani, remembering a steamed bun. Piper flopped down instantly.

Barnaby lowered himself one inch at a time, like into a bath that bites.

"Now we paddle out," said Dani, "and wait for a good **nalu** (NAH-loo) = wave!"

Out they went. Penguins paddle like torpedoes. Even Barnaby zipped along, a torpedo in a bad mood.

---

A swell rose behind them, smooth and glassy. "Paddle, paddle, RIDE!" called Dani.

Piper paddled like a windmill in a hurricane, popped up, wobbled like a wind-up toy, and ZOOMED.

She tumbled off at the beach laughing so hard she got hiccups. "AGAIN! Hic! AGAIN!"

Next wave she fell flat on her belly and slid along anyway, flippers tucked like a tiny rocket.

---

Barnaby floated past the break, gripping his board with both flippers. A perfectly nice nalu rolled in.

"No thank you," he announced, and let it roll right under him.

Another came. "Also no thank you." A third. "I shall simply live out here now. Please send snacks."

"Watch me, it's EASY!" cried Dani, popping up on her board just as the sun blazed out.

Her curl drooped. Her peak slumped. She slid off the board like a golden puddle. Plop.

She bobbed up wearing a tiny fish as a hat. "Still cool! Totally planned! Swirl on!"

Piper paddled over and grabbed Barnaby's flipper. "I'll TOW you!" Flap-flap-flap.

The wave caught them both. Double somersault. Double splash. Barnaby surfaced wearing a kelp wig.

---

Dani swirled up beside him, for once neither fast nor loud. "What are you scared of?"

"Back home I'm the good one," said Barnaby. "I look graceful. Out here I'll fall and look silly."

"Falling here isn't silly," said Dani. "My friends cheer LOUDER for the falls than the rides."

"Because falling means you tried," said Dani. "You're not snow-Barnaby here. You get to be ocean-Barnaby."

Down the line, Piper hollered: "Come ride with the fish, Barnaby! The fish are very supportive!"

A new swell rose, round and patient, as if it had been waiting just for him.

---

Barnaby took a deep breath and pointed his board at shore. The nalu lifted him.

He crouched, low and tucked, flippers out like wings, exactly the way he carved down a snowy mountain.

And he FLEW, left and right down the wave, throwing up two glittering fans of spray.

"WHAT IS THAT?" screamed Dani with delight. "He's SNOW-SURFING! That's the smoothest ride I've EVER seen!"

Barnaby skidded onto the sand and pumped both flippers. "I RODE THE NALU! AND I LOOKED MAGNIFICENT!"

Piper bounced a cheer and lost her sunglasses again.

---

They rode until the sky turned pineapple-gold, then flopped on the warm sand, salty and happy.

"There's no one right way to ride a wave," yawned Piper, leaning on Dani's cool side.

"Just your way," said Barnaby, smiling at last. "Sometimes your old way fits a brand-new place."

"Stay cool," whispered Dani, "and swirl on, my new 'ohana."

"Lovely," said Barnaby. "Now. About those snacks."

---
