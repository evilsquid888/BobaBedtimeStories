# Story 41: Pua the Pineapple and the Field of Gold

**Main Character:** Pua the Pineapple
**Guest Characters:** Niu the Coconut, Dole Whip Dani
**Setting:** A golden pineapple field at sunset on the Oahu plateau
**Theme:** We grow best when we grow together.
**Hawaiian Spotlight:** lokahi (loh-KAH-hee) = unity / harmony

---

Pua the Pineapple marched up the red-dirt road of the Oahu plateau with her green leaf-crown held so high it nearly poked a passing cloud. The cloud did not complain, because clouds are polite, but it did scoot over a little.

"Out of my sunbeam, please," Pua announced grandly to nobody. "Royalty coming through!"

She had been away a long time, learning to be kinder, and she truly *was* kinder now — but old habits cling like sugar on a malasada. So when she crested the hill and saw it, even Pua forgot to be regal.

The whole field glowed.

Thousands of pineapples sat in tidy rows, their crowns catching the sunset until the entire plateau looked like a sea of small green-tufted lanterns dipped in honey. It was the most golden thing she had ever seen.

"Oh," whispered Pua, and her crown drooped just a smidge with wonder. "Oh, my."

---

A round, shaggy shape rolled up beside her with a cheerful *thunk-thunk-thunk*. It was **Niu** (NEE-oo) the Coconut, his green straw-sprout bobbing and his little woven hat tipped back.

"Welcome home, your highness," Niu said, with a grin that meant he was teasing but also a little bit serious. "Tough shell, sweet heart — and *long* road. You walked all this way?"

"A queen does not take shortcuts," said Pua. Then she paused. "Also I got lost twice. But mostly the queen thing."

"Swirl on up here, you two!" called a sunny voice, and a golden curl of soft-serve came bouncing through the rows, melting just slightly at the edges in the warm evening light. It was Dole Whip Dani, her tiny pineapple-leaf tuft quivering with excitement, her huge sparkly eyes catching the sunset like two little suns.

"Pua! PUA! You're *back*!" Dani cheered, doing a happy little spin that nearly toppled her cup-body. "Okay okay okay, big plan: tonight is the **Mahalo** (mah-HAH-loh) = thank you Harvest, when the whole field says thank you to the sun and the rain and the soil before the sweetest pineapples get picked. And YOU, miss royal crown, get to be guest of honor!"

Pua's leaves fluffed up like a peacock's. "Guest of honor? Naturally. I *am* the most magnificent pineapple here. Just look at my crown. Diamond skin. Royal posture. Frankly, the others should thank *me* for letting them grow nearby."

Niu and Dani traded a look. It was the kind of look friends give each other when somebody they love is about to learn something the gentle way.

---

"Tell you what," said Niu, sloshing thoughtfully. "Before the ceremony, why don't you go say hello to the field? Meet the other pineapples. Then you'll *really* know who deserves the honor."

"An excellent idea," said Pua, already gliding off between the rows. "They'll be thrilled."

The first pineapple she met was a small lopsided one named Keoni, whose crown leaned permanently to the left like he was always listening to a secret.

"I'm the sweetest pineapple in this field," Pua declared. "Obviously."

"Probably!" Keoni agreed warmly, not bothered at all. "I'm only sweet 'cause of the soil, you know. **Aina** (EYE-nah) = land. The rich red dirt right under us — that's what fills me up with sugar. Best dirt on the plateau. You're standing in it!"

Pua looked down. She *was* standing in it. The same dirt as Keoni. The exact same dirt.

"Hmph," said Pua, and glided on.

The next pineapple was a tall proud one named Lehua, with a crown nearly as grand as Pua's own.

"Lovely crown," Lehua said generously. "Mine got this big from the rain, you know. We share the same clouds — when it rains on me, it rains on you. We drink together!"

"We do *not* drink together," said Pua. "I drink alone. Regally."

But she remembered the polite cloud that had scooted over her on the road. The very same cloud, she realized, that would later rain on this whole field. On her. On Lehua. On lopsided Keoni. All of them. The *same* rain.

"Hmph," said Pua, a smaller hmph this time, and glided on.

---

By the time the sun was a fat orange yolk sinking toward the sea, Pua had met a hundred pineapples. And every single one had said something that snagged in her leaves like a burr.

*The sun ripens all of us the same.*

*The bees that visit you visited me first.*

*Same soil, same rain, same sun — we're basically cousins.*

*A pineapple all alone in a pot never gets sweet, you know. Takes a whole field.*

Pua climbed to the very top of the little rise at the field's heart and sat down — actually *sat*, crown and all — which queens are not supposed to do, but she was tired and her thoughts were heavy.

Niu rolled up. Dani swirled close. They sat on either side of her, three friends watching the sky turn pink and gold and purple all at once.

"I thought I was sweet because I was *special*," Pua said quietly. "Because of my crown. Because I'm me."

"You ARE special," Dani said gently. "Every single pineapple here is special. But sweet?" She gestured at the glowing field with one soft golden arm. "Sweet is a team sport, sister."

"The sun didn't pick favorites," Niu added. "Shone on all of you. The rain didn't either. The dirt fed every root the same. You didn't grow your sugar alone, Pua. Nobody does."

Pua looked out at the thousands of crowns catching the last light, all leaning ever so slightly toward the setting sun like a crowd waving aloha. And something in her chest went warm and soft, sweeter than any syrup.

"There's a word for it," she said slowly, remembering one Poi Popo had taught her long ago. "**Lokahi** (loh-KAH-hee) = unity. Harmony. All the parts working together. The sun and the rain and the dirt and... and every pineapple."

"Now you're growing," said Niu, beaming.

---

That night, the **Mahalo** Harvest began.

Lanterns made of fireflies floated up between the rows. Every pineapple in the field turned to face the place where the sun had set, and one by one they each said *thank you*. Thank you to the **aina** for the sugar in their hearts. Thank you to the rain for filling them up. Thank you to the sun for the gold in their crowns. Thank you to each other — for the **lokahi** that made the whole field shine brighter than any one pineapple ever could.

When it came time for the guest of honor to speak, Dani gave Pua a little nudge. "Go on, your highness. Say something royal."

Pua stood. Her crown was high again — but differently now, not above the others, just *among* them. She looked out at all those waiting faces and felt, for the first time in her life, that she was not the brightest light in the field.

She was one light. In a field full of lights. And that was so much better.

"I came here thinking I was the sweetest pineapple," Pua began, and a few leaves giggled. "I *was* a little bit insufferable about it. Maybe a lot a bit." More giggles. "But you've all taught me something. No pineapple grows alone. I'm sweet because the sun is generous and the rain is kind and this red dirt is the most loving dirt in all the islands. I'm sweet because of *you*. So tonight, the guest of honor would like to give the honor right back — to the whole field. To **lokahi**."

The fireflies pulsed brighter. A warm cheer rose up, and even the polite cloud overhead let down a few happy raindrops, which on a night like this felt exactly like joy.

When the sweetest pineapples of the season were gently lifted to become juice and Dole Whip and the centerpiece of a hundred happy luaus, it was not scary at all. It was the proudest, most golden moment imaginable — like a firework finally getting to light up the sky. Each one went off to bring sweetness to somebody's table, carrying a little piece of the whole field inside them.

And Pua — guest of honor, gentler queen, one bright light among many — sat between Niu and Dani under the firefly lanterns, crown gleaming, heart full.

"Crown up, sweetheart," she whispered to the whole glowing field. "We did this together."
