# Story 23: The Waimea Canyon Echo

**Main Character:** Mac the Macadamia
**Guest Characters:** Kai the Shave Ice, a plump little cloud wearing its mist sideways
**Setting:** The vast red-and-green Waimea Canyon on Kauai
**Theme:** Your true voice is worth letting out.
**Hawaiian Spotlight:** **leo** (LEH-oh) = voice

---

Mac the Macadamia had a very big secret, and it lived in a very small shell.

Mac loved to sing. Big swooping songs about waterfalls, warm rocks, and clouds wearing their hats sideways.

But only on the inside, shell snapped shut like a teeny round helmet, where nobody could hear.

"You're up, little buddy!" called Kai the Shave Ice, sparkling in his paper cone at the canyon's edge.

Mac peeked out and squeaked something smaller than a sesame seed.

"Was that a hello?" Kai asked the shell. "A goodbye? A please-pass-the-poi?"

Click. The shell said nothing. The shell was very good at saying nothing.

---

"THIS," announced Kai, sweeping a drippy arm, "is Waimea Canyon. The Grand Canyon of the Pacific. LOOK."

The shell creaked open a crack. And, oh.

Striped rust-red, orange and a hundred greens, it looked like syrup poured over the world's biggest shave ice.

A plump little cloud floated by BELOW them, wearing its mist sideways like a hat.

"This canyon catches your **leo** (LEH-oh) = voice and throws it across," said Kai. "Yell something."

Click click CLICK. "No way," squeaked the shell. "Everyone will hear."

"That," said Kai gently, "is exactly the point."

---

Then the canyon took a big breath of warm wind and blew it straight up the cliff. WHOOSH.

The shut little shell tipped, teetered, and bounced off along the rim like a runaway marble.

"MAC!" Kai lunged, slipped on his own melt, and face-planted into his umbrella hat.

Boing, off a red rock. Boing, over a root. Boing, right off the edge.

Plop. Mac landed on the sideways-hatted cloud, which drifted on as if nuts fell on it daily.

"Hang on, brah!" called Kai, waving both drippy arms from the rim. "Don't... um... move!"

The cloud floated gently away from the rim, out over the great red-and-green nothing.

---

"You're in the safest, most scenic spot on Kauai!" hollered Kai. "Probably!"

"How do I get BACK?" squeaked Mac, hugging a tuft of mist.

"Clouds follow songs," called Kai. "Everybody knows that. Sing it home, little buddy."

"I don't sing," squeaked Mac. "Out loud. Where things can HEAR me."

"Mac," said Kai. "Nobody pours syrup on me, I'm just plain ice. Cold. Forgettable."

"Hiding the color doesn't keep it safe," said Kai. "It just keeps it from shining."

Two red birds wheeled past, calling proudly across the canyon. Birds, Mac decided, were braver than nuts.

---

Mac took the biggest breath a macadamia can take, which is not very big at all.

Out came the teeniest, crumb-sized "...hello?" you ever heard. Mac's face fell. Then: hello... hello...

His own small voice came swooping back off the red walls, growing as it flew, golden and HIS.

"It WORKED!" gasped Mac, and the canyon gasped it back, it WORKED, cheering for one tiny nut.

Then Mac sang. Waterfalls, warm rocks, sideways hats. The canyon sang it back, a hundred Macs at once.

The cloud blushed pink, pleased as anything, and drifted straight back up to the rim to listen.

Mac hopped off onto solid red rock. Kai scooped him up, syrup flinging in happy rainbow arcs.

---

"BLOOP!" tried Mac, and the canyon bloop-bloop-blooped so seriously that both of them collapsed into giggles.

"The canyon didn't make your leo bigger, brah," said Kai. "It carried it. The big sound was yours."

"Small but mighty," said Mac, out loud, to everyone, "shell to HEART!"

---

The walls turned copper and rose, and the cloud below went pink as a malasada.

Mac sang one slow lullaby over the edge, and the canyon hummed it back, gentle as a tide.

Kai dozed in his cone, melting happily. Mac left his shell wide open to the warm air.

He wasn't a secret anymore. He was a song.

---
