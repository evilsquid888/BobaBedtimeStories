# Story 49: Poi and the Volcano's Quiet Glow

**Main Character:** Poi — a smooth, soft, lavender-grey bowl of pounded taro, humble and calm with a gentle smile
**Guest Characters:** Tūtū Poi, his grandmother; Uncle Kalo, his sturdy mud-flecked uncle; the three Poi Keiki, his tiny bouncing cousins
**Setting:** A grassy slope on the Big Island at night, the volcano glowing softly far away
**Theme:** Wonder is sweetest snuggled close to the ones you love
**Hawaiian Spotlight:** **hōkū** (HOH-koo) = star

---

On the Big Island, where beaches throw the splashiest parties anywhere, there lived a small bowl of poi.

Poi didn't sparkle or fizz. He didn't backflip off the buffet like the malasadas. Showoffs.

He was smooth, lavender-grey, with a dimpled smile and a wobble slow as a cloud deciding to nap.

"Plain," sighed Poi, peering at himself in a spoon. "Smooth, soft, purple-ish, and plain. That's me."

Around him the luau BOOMED. Ukuleles strummed themselves silly. Pineapple slices did the hula.

Poi looked up at the dark quiet hill behind the beach. "I want one quiet thing. Just one."

A coconut juggled three coconuts and hollered WATCH THIS so often that nobody watched anything anymore.

---

He tucked his spoon under his arm and tiptoed toward the blanket's edge. Wobble. Wobble.

A conga line of pineapples swept past and scooped him up. "Cha-cha-CHA!" Poi bounced along, mortified.

He wobbled free at the punch bowl. SPLOOSH. A poke bowl cannonballed in. Poi dripped pink punch.

He tried the back way. BONK. A juggled coconut bounced off his head and into the punch.

So Poi did the bravest thing a small snack can do. He pretended to be a cushion.

Still as a cushion, he slid off the blanket inch by inch, into the cool dark grass.

---

Up the slope he went, hop, wobble, hop. The music faded to a faraway hum.

Halfway up, his spoon slipped and went clattering down the hill. Poi watched it go.

"Keep it," he told the hill. "I'm not hungry. I'm... looking." He turned around, and gasped.

---

The whole island lay below him like a sleeping creature, breathing slow.

Far across the dark land, the volcano glowed. Not roaring. Just glowing, rosy-orange, like embers under a blanket.

Poi sat right down in the cool grass. "Oh," he said softly. "Oh, my."

Then he looked up. The sky was CROWDED with stars. Thousands. Silver freckles on a purple cheek.

"A **hōkū** (HOH-koo) = star," he breathed, like whispering hello to a friend. "And another. And another."

He lay back, and the whole smooth bowl of him caught the starlight and held it.

For the first time all night, Poi felt perfectly happy. Then: huffing. Puffing. Giggling.

---

His **ʻohana** (oh-HAH-nah) = family! Tūtū Poi, softest and most ancient. Uncle Kalo, mud-flecked. Three tiny Poi Keiki.

"There you are, e Poi!" cried Tūtū, settling beside him with a contented wobble. "We looked everywhere."

"You came looking for me?" blinked Poi. "But the party. The juggling. The coconuts."

"A coconut bonked my head," said Uncle Kalo. "A party is no place for a thinking snack."

The Poi Keiki bounced into a giggling heap against Poi's side. "Cousin Poi! What are you LOOKING at?"

And Poi, small, plain, quiet Poi, got to be the one to show them. "Look up."

The gigglers went silent. Even Uncle Kalo, who had a comment for everything, had no comment at all.

---

"Most beautiful thing I have ever seen," murmured Tūtū, "and I am four hundred years old."

She was about two weeks old. But four hundred felt right, on a night like this.

"Tūtū," said Poi slowly, "at the party everyone sparkles and booms. I just... wobble."

"Fireworks flash and go," said Tūtū. "The volcano glows all night. Some wonders shout. The best ones stay."

"Cousin Poi," whispered the smallest Keiki, eyes huge. "You look like a hōkū that fell into a bowl."

Poi looked down. He was shimmering, silver-purple, soft and steady, exactly like the volcano. Exactly like the stars.

"Positively GLOWING," said Tūtū, dabbing her eye with grass. "Quick, one spoonful each, before he gets more delicious."

---

Down at the beach the music hushed, and every snack looked up at the stars, really looked.

A soft purple pile of ʻohana snuggled close on the hill, and the volcano breathed slow and warm.
