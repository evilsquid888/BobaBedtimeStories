# Story 49: Poi and the Volcano's Quiet Glow

**Main Character:** Poi — a smooth, soft, lavender-grey bowl of pounded taro, humble and calm with a gentle smile
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A grassy slope on the Big Island at night, the volcano glowing softly far away
**Theme:** Wonder is sweetest snuggled close to the ones you love
**Hawaiian Spotlight:** hōkū (HOH-koo) = star

---

On the Big Island, where the beaches throw the loudest, splashiest, most ridiculous parties in all the Pacific, there lived a small bowl of pounded taro named Poi — and Poi was, by his own honest admission, the most boring snack on the entire menu.

He didn't sparkle. He didn't fizz. He didn't do backflips off the buffet table like the malasadas did (showoffs, every last one of them). Poi was simply smooth. He was a soft, gentle swirl of lavender-grey, the exact color of a sleepy evening sky, with a dimple of a smile and a wobble so slow and dignified that watching him jiggle was a bit like watching a cloud decide whether to nap.

"Plain," Poi sighed, peering into his own reflection in a spoon. "Smooth, soft, purple-ish, and plain. That's me."

And tonight, of all nights, the beach was throwing the biggest luau of the whole summer.

---

You could hear the party from a mile away, which, to be fair, was the entire problem.

Down at Kona Beach, the ukuleles were strumming themselves silly. The pineapple slices were doing the hula. A coconut named Big Kai was juggling three smaller coconuts and shouting "WATCH THIS, EVERYBODY!" so often that nobody actually watched anything anymore. Shave ice towers spun in dizzy rainbow stacks. A bowl of poke did a cannonball into the punch and soaked seventeen guests, who all cheered as though this were a tremendous achievement.

Poi sat at the very edge of the picnic blanket, holding his little spoon, trying to think of one single exciting thing to say.

"I... am also here," he announced.

Nobody heard him. A firework of papaya seeds went off somewhere. Big Kai shouted "WATCH THIS!" again.

Poi's gentle smile drooped just a smidge. Everyone at the party glittered and boomed and dazzled. And here he was, the color of a faded twilight, wobbling quietly in his bowl like a pudding that had given up on being interesting.

"Maybe," Poi whispered to himself, "I'm just not a party kind of snack."

So — and this is the brave part, because slipping away from a party feels enormous when you're small — Poi tucked his spoon under his arm and tiptoed up the dark grassy slope behind the beach, away from the lights, away from the noise, away from Big Kai (who was now juggling FOUR coconuts and one very alarmed crab).

He climbed and climbed. The music faded into a faraway hum. The grass grew cool and soft. And when Poi finally stopped to catch his breath and turned around —

He gasped.

---

The whole island lay spread below him like a sleeping creature, breathing slow.

And far, far across the dark land, the volcano was glowing.

Not exploding. Not roaring. Not doing anything you'd shout "WATCH THIS!" about. The volcano simply *glowed* — a warm, soft, rosy-orange light, the gentle color of embers tucked under a blanket of ash. It pulsed ever so slightly, in, out, in, out, like the steady breathing of something enormous and very, very calm.

Poi sat right down in the cool grass and stared.

"Oh," he said softly. "Oh, my."

He had never in his life seen anything so quietly, completely beautiful.

And then — he looked up.

The sky above him was *crowded* with stars. Thousands. Millions. Each one a tiny silver freckle on the great purple cheek of the night. Poi did not even know there were that many. Down at the beach, the party lights had been so bright he'd never once thought to look up.

"A **hōkū** (HOH-koo) = star," he breathed, naming the very first one he saw, the way you'd whisper hello to a new friend. "And another. And another. And — oh, I shall never count them all."

He didn't try. He just lay back in the grass, his smooth lavender-grey surface reflecting the starlight so gently that — and Poi did not notice this himself — he had begun, very faintly, to *shimmer*. The whole bowl of him caught the light of the sky and held it, soft and silver, like a little pond of caught moonshine.

For the first time all night, Poi felt entirely, perfectly happy.

And that, of course, is exactly when he heard the footsteps.

---

It was his **ʻohana** (oh-HAH-nah) = family — his whole big taro family, huffing and puffing their way up the slope. There was Tūtū Poi, his grandmother, the softest and most ancient swirl of them all. There were his little cousins, the Poi Keiki, three tiny purple dollops who rolled rather than walked. And there was Kalo, his sturdy uncle, still flecked with a bit of mud from the **loʻi** (LOH-ee) = taro field where the whole family grew.

"There you are, e Poi!" cried Tūtū, settling herself into the grass beside him with a contented wobble. "We looked all over that noisy beach for you."

"You — you came looking for me?" Poi blinked. "But the party. The juggling. Big Kai's coconuts."

"Pfft," said Uncle Kalo, flopping down on Poi's other side. "One of those coconuts bonked me right on the head. A party is no place for a thinking snack."

The three Poi Keiki rolled into a heap against Poi's side, warm and soft and giggling. "Cousin Poi! Cousin Poi! What are you LOOKING at?"

And Poi — small, plain, smooth, quiet Poi — got to be the one to show them.

"Look up," he whispered. "Each one is a **hōkū**. And look there, across the island. Do you see the volcano glowing?"

The little ones went silent. Even the gigglers. Even Uncle Kalo, who had a comment ready for everything, found he had no comment at all. The whole taro family lay back in the cool grass in a soft purple pile and looked up at the spilling, glittering, impossible sky, and across at the warm and breathing glow of the faraway mountain.

"Ohhhh," said all three Poi Keiki at once.

"It is the most beautiful thing I have ever seen," murmured Tūtū, "and I am four hundred years old."

(She was actually about two weeks old. Poi was a bowl of taro. But four hundred felt right, on a night like this.)

They stayed that way a long while. Nobody shouted. Nobody juggled. The volcano glowed its patient glow, and the stars wheeled their slow silver wheel, and Poi felt his family pressed warm and snug all around him, and a most surprising thought floated up inside him like a bubble.

*This,* he realized, *this quiet wonder, snuggled up close to the ones I love — this is the very best thing in the whole entire world. And it is mine. And I almost missed it being sad about coconuts.*

"Tūtū," Poi said slowly. "I always thought I was too plain to matter. At the party everyone sparkles and booms. I just... wobble."

Tūtū Poi laughed, a soft pudding-y laugh like a spoon stirring honey. "Oh, my little one. The fireworks flash and they're gone. But the volcano glows all night long, soft and steady, and warms the whole island while it sleeps." She nudged him gently. "Some wonders shout. The best ones simply *stay*. Like you. Like us."

And here is the funny part — the part that proves the universe has a sense of humor.

Because just then, the three Poi Keiki noticed how *shimmery* their cousin had become, catching all that starlight, glowing faintly silver-purple in the dark.

"Cousin Poi," whispered the smallest one, eyes huge, "you look like a hōkū that fell out of the sky and landed in a bowl."

Poi looked down at himself. He *was* shimmering. The whole gentle, smooth, lavender-grey swirl of him had drunk in the starlight and given it back soft and glowing — not loud, not flashy, just warm and quiet and lovely, exactly like the volcano, exactly like the stars.

He was not plain at all. He never had been. He'd simply needed the dark and the quiet and the people he loved to see his own light shining back.

And — well. You know how this goes by now.

A bowl of poi this gorgeous, this glowing, this snuggled-up cozy under a sky full of stars, surrounded by a family that adored him? There was only one possible ending.

"He's positively GLOWING," said Tūtū, dabbing her eye with a blade of grass. "Quick, my darlings, before he gets even more delicious."

And the whole giggling family scooped Poi up with their little spoons and shared him, bite by happy bite, right there in the starlit grass — each spoonful soft and sweet and faintly shimmering. And Poi, being eaten by the people he loved most under the glow of the gentle volcano, felt prouder and warmer and more *himself* than he ever had in all his (two weeks) of life.

The party down at the beach finally noticed the quiet hill above them. The music hushed. The pineapples stopped hula-ing. Even Big Kai set down his coconuts.

And every single snack on Kona Beach turned to look up at the stars — really look, for the very first time — and found that the night had been holding a quiet kind of wonder for them all along, just waiting for someone small and plain and lovely to point it out.

## Goodnight Blessing

May you find a quiet hill, little one, far from all the noise.
May the people you love climb up to find you, and lie down warm at your side.
May the stars spill across your ceiling like sugar across the dark,
and may the soft glow inside you shine — not loud, not flashy, just yours.
The fireworks have all gone home. The volcano breathes slow and warm.
Snuggle close. Wonder gently. You were never plain at all.
Goodnight.
