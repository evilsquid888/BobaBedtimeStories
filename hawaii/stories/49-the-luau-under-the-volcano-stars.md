# Story 49: The Luau Under the Volcano Stars

**Main Character:** Kalua the Pig (Kalua roast pig — luau pit-roasted pork)
**Guest Characters:** Poi Popo, Pele's Glow (Lava the gentle ember), Honu Haupia
**Setting:** A grand sunset luau on the Big Island beneath Kilauea's distant glow
**Theme:** Gratitude turns a meal into a celebration.
**Hawaiian Spotlight:** hoʻomaikaʻi (hoh-oh-mah-ee-KAH-ee) = to bless / give thanks

---

The very best smell on the whole Big Island was floating across the beach, and it was coming straight out of the ground.

"Mmmm," said Kalua the Pig, peeking up through the warm sand of the **imu** (EE-moo) = underground oven where he had been cozily roasting all day long. A curl of fragrant smoke rose off his back like a tiny gray scarf. His ti-leaf blanket had gone soft and smoky and snuggly. "Low and slow, that's how love grows!"

He stretched one tender little leg, then the other, and gave the happiest, sleepiest, rumbliest yawn you ever heard — a yawn so big and so warm it made three seagulls stop mid-flight and decide to stay for dinner.

Because tonight was no ordinary night.

Tonight was the Grand Sunset Luau, the biggest gathering the island had seen in a hundred moons, and Kalua the smoky-golden, roly-poly, pulled-pork pig was the guest of honor. Or, to be exact — he was the *meal* of honor.

And he could not have been prouder.

---

By the time Kalua wiggled all the way out of the imu, the whole beach had turned the color of warm honey. The sun was sliding down toward the sea, and far, far away — across the dark folds of the island — Kilauea glowed a soft sleepy orange, like a night-light left on for the whole world.

Tables stretched along the sand, and friends were arriving from every direction.

"WHOA. KALUA. You look DELICIOUS," boomed Manapua Mano, bouncing up with a wisp of steam trailing off his soft white head. "I mean that as the highest compliment. I have never meant anything more. My tummy is writing you a love letter as we speak."

Kalua laughed his enormous belly-laugh, which sounded like a friendly thunderstorm having a nice nap. "Mahalo, little bun. There is plenty for everyone tonight. Plenty and plenty."

Honu Haupia glided up next, slow and snow-white, her coconut-pudding shell jiggling gently with every flipper-step. "Slow flippers, far journeys," she said warmly. "And what a journey it has been to get here, old friend. We have come from every corner of the island."

"We sure have!" piped a small zesty voice, and Lilikoi Lani came cartwheeling across the sand, leaving a tangy-sweet scent trail behind her like ribbon. "I came over the cliffs! I came through the jungle! I came around a sleeping wild boar and tickled his nose — sour first, sweet always!"

"You did *what*," said Niu the Coconut, adjusting his tiny woven hat.

"I dared myself," Lilikoi shrugged. "Somebody had to."

---

The tables filled up. There was poi and pineapple and shave ice and macadamia and musubi, all glistening in the golden light. Everyone found their spot, everyone settled in, everyone got ready to eat —

And then Kalua, climbing slowly up onto the great wooden serving platter at the center of it all, lifted one little trotter for quiet.

The whole beach hushed. Even the waves leaned in.

"My dear ʻohana," Kalua rumbled, his sleepy eyes shining. "Before we feast — before a single bite — I want to do something my grandfather taught me. Something we must never, ever forget."

He looked out at all the faces glowing in the sunset.

"I want us to **hoʻomaikaʻi** (hoh-oh-mah-ee-KAH-ee) = to give thanks. To bless this meal. Because a feast without gratitude," he said softly, "is only food. But a feast *with* gratitude — ahhh. That is a *celebration*."

And from the far edge of the beach, where the warm sand met the rocks, a tiny light flickered to life.

It glowed orange-gold. It crackled cozily, like a campfire wrapped in a hug. It drifted closer on the evening breeze, twinkling like a piece of the sunset that had broken off and decided to come to the party.

It was Pele's Glow — Lava the gentle ember — the oldest, warmest spirit on the whole island.

"I felt your gratitude all the way from the mountain," the little ember said, in a voice like a bedtime fire. "And gratitude is my favorite thing to come down for. New land, new light, every night."

Honu Haupia bowed her ancient turtle head. Even Lilikoi Lani went still and round-eyed.

---

"But," said Manapua Mano, raising one nervous little hand, "what if I'm not very *good* at giving thanks? What if I just say 'thanks for snacks' and sit down? Because honestly, that's most of my thoughts."

Kalua chuckled. "There is no wrong way to hoʻomaikaʻi, little bun. Thanks is thanks. Try."

Manapua took a deep, steamy breath. "Okay. Um. I'm thankful for... soft things. And warm things. And friends who don't mind when I bounce too much." He paused. His rosy steam-blush deepened. "And I'm thankful for *you*, Kalua. Because you always make sure my plate is full before yours."

The little bun's voice wobbled. "Soft on the outside, full of love inside, right? You taught me that."

A warm hush rolled across the tables.

"That," said Pele's Glow, twinkling brighter, "was a *very* good thanks."

---

Then it was Poi Popo's turn.

The old lavender-grey elder rose slowly, like a soft purple cloud, her flower lei swaying. She smelled of earth and rain and a thousand quiet mornings.

"I am thankful," she said, in her gentle ancient voice, "for **aloha ʻāina** (ah-LOH-hah AH-ee-nah) = love of the land. For this island that grew the taro that became me. For the rain that fed it, and the sun that warmed it, and the hands that pounded it with patience and song." She smiled at Kalua. "And I am thankful for *you*, keiki. From the land, with aloha — you understand that better than anyone I know."

One by one, around the whole long table, the thanks went on.

Honu Haupia gave thanks for the slow tides that had carried her safely home. Niu the Coconut gave thanks for his tough shell and his sweet heart, and admitted (a little gruffly) that he was thankful for friends who put up with his know-it-all-ness. Lilikoi Lani gave thanks for every dare she'd ever survived, which made everyone laugh. And tiny Mac the Macadamia, half-hidden in his shell, peeked out and whispered, "Thankful. To be here. Small but mighty, shell to heart." — and somehow his four little words were the biggest ones of all.

By the time the circle of thanks came all the way around, the sun had dipped into the sea, and the whole beach glowed gold and rose and purple. Up on the mountain, Kilauea pulsed its warm sleepy orange, as if the island itself were nodding along.

---

And then, at last, it was Kalua's turn.

He stood in the very center of his platter, the smoke curling off his back, his ti-leaf blanket glowing in the firelight. Every friend turned to look at him. Every spoon and chopstick waited.

For just one heartbeat, somebody might have worried. Because Kalua was about to take *center plate*. He was about to be the feast. And a feast gets eaten.

But Kalua's sleepy eyes were brimming over with the happiest tears you ever saw.

"My whole life," he rumbled, "I have been roasting low and slow, getting tender and warm, getting ready for *this*. For the moment I get to gather everyone I love at one table and fill every belly and warm every heart. Do you know what an honor that is?"

He laughed, big and bright as a sunrise.

"To be the center of an ʻohana's celebration — to *bless* you all with this feast — that is the proudest a pig can ever be! This is not goodbye, little ones. This is *thank you*. This is me getting to hoʻomaikaʻi *you*, all of you, with everything I am!"

And Pele's Glow flared up gentle and golden, and Poi Popo wept happy purple tears into her lei, and the whole beach erupted in the warmest cheer the island had ever heard.

"PILE IT HIGH!" hollered Lani the Loco Moco from the back, finally waking up.

"FRESH, WARM, AND NEVER ALONE!" giggled Malia the Malasada, sugar crystals raining off her.

So they feasted. Oh, how they feasted, under the volcano stars. There was pork and poi and pineapple and laughter and stories and second helpings and third helpings and a fourth helping that Manapua swore he didn't take.

And Kalua the Pig, smoky-golden and grinning, watched every friend he loved grow full and happy and warm — and he glowed prouder than the mountain itself.

"Low and slow," he whispered, as the firelight danced. "That's how love grows."

---

Long after the plates were clean, the ʻohana lay back on the warm sand together and watched Kilauea's soft glow flicker against the night. Pele's Glow nestled in the middle of them all, twinkling like a little campfire made of pure aloha.

"Same time next moon?" yawned Manapua.

"Same time *every* moon," rumbled Kalua's warm voice on the breeze. "Because a feast with thanks in it never really ends. It just keeps everyone warm."

And nobody — not one single friend — ever forgot to hoʻomaikaʻi again.
