# Story 16: The Hula Everyone Forgot

**Main Character:** Honu Haupia
**Guest Characters:** Manapua Mano; Niu the Coconut
**Setting:** A grassy hula halau (school) near the beach at dusk
**Theme:** Stories can be told with your whole body, not just words.
**Hawaiian Spotlight:** **hula** (HOO-lah) = Hawaiian story-dance

---

It started when Manapua Mano tried dancing with his eyes closed and bounced into a coconut tree.

"OW! I mean, TA-DA!" he squeaked, steam puffing. "Did everybody see my graceful tree hug?"

"The whole beach saw it," said Niu the Coconut, since the tree was, in fact, his cousin.

The lawn of the **hula** (HOO-lah) = Hawaiian story-dance school glowed mango-orange as the sun slid down.

"I want to learn a dance," announced Manapua, "that is mostly snacks."

Across the grass, slow as a moonrise, glided Honu Haupia, pudding shell jiggling.

---

"Tonight," said Honu, "I will teach you a hula almost no one remembers. Slow flippers, far journeys. Watch."

She swept one flipper in a long slow curve. She rocked her shell like a little white boat.

She raised both flippers high and let them flutter down like falling rain. The gang stared.

"What did it SAY?" whispered Niu.

"That," said Honu, "is the part I have forgotten."

"The **kupuna** (koo-POO-nah) = elders danced it to remember how our home began," said Honu.

"I kept the steps. The story drifted away, like a canoe with no paddle."

"Then WE'LL figure it out!" said Manapua. "We're great at figuring things out!" This was not strictly true.

---

The curve. Niu tipped, wobbled in a circle, and ended up facing a hibiscus. "Am I doing it?"

"You are doing A thing," said Honu kindly.

The rocking. Manapua did not rock. Steamed buns bounce. Boing. Boing. BOING.

He bounced higher and higher and landed inside Niu's woven hat. "I FOUND A SEAT!"

"That is not part of the dance," said Honu.

"It should be," said Manapua, from the hat.

---

The flutter. Everyone reached up high, oh so graceful, ready to float down like rain. And froze.

Three dancers stood arms up, like confused snacks reaching for cookies on a high shelf. Somebody's tummy growled.

"Bigger!" said Manapua. "We do ALL of it at once!" Curve, rock, flutter, bounce, wobble.

They rocked down the lawn, across the sand, into the shallows. A wave swept them right back.

They landed in a soggy heap on the grass: turtle, bun, coconut, one hat.

Honu laughed until her shell wobbled. "We are getting it all wrong. And that is wonderful."

---

"When I dance it perfectly, I dance it alone, copying a memory," said Honu. "This felt ALIVE."

"Forget the old words," said Honu. "Tell it with your whole bodies, and let the meaning find us."

"The long curve," said Honu, sweeping her flipper. "What does it feel like?"

"The ocean!" said Manapua. "Going whoooosh to where the sky sits down!"

Honu rocked her shell. "A canoe!" said Niu, hat back on. "Brave voyagers paddling to find a home."

Honu fluttered her flippers down. Everyone went quiet, even the waves.

"The first rain," said Manapua softly, bounce gone gentle. "Falling on a brand-new island. Making it home."

---

And the dance found them. They whooshed the ocean together. They paddled the canoe across the dusk.

They raised their arms high, and down came the soft rain: flippers, bun-arms, stubby coconut nubs.

And then, oh, real rain. A warm soft shower fell from the plum sky onto all of them.

"We're telling a whole story!" whispered Manapua, mid-sway. "My mouth isn't even moving! Saves it for snacks!"

They danced through the laughing, wobbly and bouncing and absolutely together, as the sky went velvet blue.

---

The first star blinked open over the water. The sea whispered shhh, shhh.

"Tutu Honu," said Niu quietly, "is that how the old kupuna felt?"

"Tired in the legs," said Honu, gazing at the dark sea. "Full in the heart. Not alone."

Manapua yawned a steamy yawn and leaned on Niu, who leaned on Honu, who didn't mind.

---
