# Story 37: How the Island Was Born

**Main Character:** Pele's Glow (Lava the gentle ember)
**Guest Characters:** Poi Popo, Mac the Macadamia
**Setting:** A cooling lava field stretching to the sea on the Big Island
**Theme:** New beginnings can rise from anything.
**Hawaiian Spotlight:** **aina** (EYE-nah) = land

---

On the Big Island, on a night so soft it felt like being tucked inside a warm marshmallow, a tiny glowing ember went *pip-pip-pip* across a brand-new field of cooling rock. The ember was Pele's Glow, and he was about the size of a teacup, flickery orange-gold, cozy as a campfire that had decided to go for a walk.

"Whee!" he chirped, hopping from one warm stone to the next. "Watch this one! It's still toasty!"

Behind him bounced a small, smooth, cream-colored nut who kept ducking halfway into his shiny shell like a turtle who'd forgotten he had legs. That was Mac the Macadamia, and right now only his eyes and the top of his head were poking out.

"It's... it's all so *empty*," Mac whispered, peeking around. "There's nothing here. Just rock. And more rock. And — oop — more rock."

Pele's Glow spun a happy little circle. "Empty? Oh, keiki, this isn't empty! This is the *beginning* of everything!"

And that, my sleepy friend, is exactly where our story begins, too.

---

Following slowly behind them, gliding like a soft lavender cloud that had nowhere in particular to be, came Poi Popo. She was a smooth, gentle mound of pounded taro, lilac-grey and wobbly, wearing her favorite flower lei. She smelled of earth and aloha, and she moved at the speed of a grandmother who has all the time in the world and intends to use every bit of it.

"Slow down, little spark," Poi Popo chuckled, her voice as warm as bread. "These old taro-feet weren't built for racing."

"Sorry, Popo!" Pele's Glow zipped back to her and twinkled apologetically. "I just get *excited*. New land does that to me!"

"I can tell," said Poi Popo, fanning herself with a corner of her lei. "You've zigged and zagged so much I've watched you trip over the same warm pebble three times."

"Four," said Pele's Glow proudly. "I tripped over it twice on the way back. But that's the wonderful thing about a fresh field of aina! Everything is *brand new*, so even the pebbles haven't decided where they want to live yet. Whoops — there's another one!" He bonked gently against a little stone and bounced off, giggling sparks.

Mac the Macadamia popped a little higher out of his shell. "New land? But it's just... here. It's already land."

"Ahhh." Pele's Glow's flickery body glowed a gentle, happy gold. "That's the secret, Mac. This **aina** (EYE-nah) — this *land* — wasn't here at all just a little while ago. Not yesterday. Not last week. This whole field, every single rock you're standing on, used to be *me*."

Mac looked down at his feet. Then up at the tiny ember. Then down at his feet again.

"You?" he squeaked. "*All* of this was you?"

"Well, not just me," said Pele's Glow modestly, doing a little bow that made him wobble. "Lots of glow. Rivers of it! It came pouring down warm and bright and slow, all the way from the mountain, like the world's coziest blanket being unrolled across the sea. And then..." He paused dramatically. "...then it did the most wonderful thing of all."

"What?" Mac leaned in. He was so curious he forgot to hide in his shell, which, for Mac, was practically shouting from a rooftop.

"It got *sleepy*," whispered Pele's Glow.

Poi Popo laughed her slow, rolling laugh. "Now *that* I understand. I get sleepy too. Usually right after lunch."

"Me too!" Pele's Glow bounced. "But when *I* get sleepy, I turn into a whole island. When *you* get sleepy, Popo, you turn into a nap on the porch."

"And a fine nap it is," said Poi Popo, entirely unbothered. "Some of us cook the land. Some of us simply enjoy lying on it. Both are noble callings."

Mac let out a tiny snort of a laugh — the first laugh he'd made all night — and quickly ducked back into his shell as if he'd surprised himself by enjoying anything.

---

Pele's Glow floated up onto a wide, dark stone and spread his warm little light across it so the whole rock seemed to breathe orange for a moment.

"When the glow stops moving," he explained, "it starts to cool. And when it cools, it goes still and quiet and hard — like warm honey turning into candy. The bright orange softens to a sleepy red, and the sleepy red settles into cozy black-and-grey, and *poof* — what was once a roaring river becomes solid, brand-new ground. Aina that never existed before. Land that is being *born*, right under your toes, on a Tuesday, while everyone else is sleeping."

Mac touched the rock very, very carefully with one tiny hand. It was barely warm now — just cozy, like a pillow someone had been lying on.

"On a *Tuesday*?" Mac repeated, amazed. "I thought important things only happened on, you know, holidays. Or birthdays."

"Oh, keiki," chuckled Pele's Glow, flickering brighter, "the land doesn't check a calendar! It is being born on Tuesdays and Wednesdays and rainy Sunday mornings and any old afternoon you can think of. Right this second, all up and down the mountain, fresh aina is cooling and settling and getting comfortable. Why, the spot you're standing on might be having its very first night ever. You could be its first visitor! Say something nice. Make a good impression."

Mac looked down at the rock, suddenly bashful. "Um. Hello, land," he whispered. "You're very... flat. And solid. Good job."

"It feels... alive," Mac said softly.

"It *is*," said Poi Popo, and her ancient eyes shimmered. "This is the oldest story the islands know, little nut. From the fire comes the land. From the land comes the green. And from the green comes — everyone. You. Me. Every taro root, every macadamia tree, every single one of us came from a night exactly like this one."

Mac's eyes went very wide. "Even... me?"

"*Especially* you," rumbled Poi Popo warmly.

Just then, a tiny breeze fluttered across the lava field — and it carried something. A speck. A wisp. A teeny floaty thread, drifting and bobbing on the wind like a dandelion seed that had gotten lost on its way to a wish.

"Ooh!" Pele's Glow flickered with delight. "Mac, Mac, look! Quick — catch it! Gently, gently!"

Mac cupped his small smooth hands together and — *plip* — the little floaty thing landed right in his palms. He opened them slowly.

It was a seed. A teensy, brave, hopeful little seed, no bigger than a freckle.

"What's it *doing* out here?" Mac whispered. "There's nothing for it. No dirt. No garden. Just rock."

"It's *exploring*," said Pele's Glow, hovering close to peer at it. "Seeds are the bravest little travelers in the world, you know. This one might have floated all the way from a forest on the other side of the island — riding the wind, hopping the breezes, going wherever the sky decided to take it. And it ended up *here*. With *us*. On the newest aina in all of Hawaii."

"That's a long way to come," Mac said, "just to land on a rock."

"Ahhh," said Poi Popo gently, "but is it a rock? Or is it the very first day of a forest that hasn't happened yet?" She tilted her wobbly head. "Depends on whether somebody brave decides to help it along."

The seed sat in Mac's palm, tiny and trusting, and Mac felt his small heart go *thump*.

---

Now here is where Pele's Glow did something rather marvelous, and I want you to picture it, because it is one of the gentlest things in all the islands.

He floated down low — careful, oh so careful, because Pele's Glow never *ever* burns a friend, only warms them — and he breathed the softest, smallest puff of warmth onto a little crack in the cooling rock. A crack where, over many quiet nights, the wind had tucked away the tiniest pinch of dust and crumbled stone. Almost-soil. Baby-dirt. The very first whisper of a garden.

"Right there," he said. "Tuck it right there, Mac. In the crack. Where the new aina has already started making a cozy little pocket for it."

Mac hesitated. "But what if it doesn't grow? What if I do it wrong? I'm so small, and the field is so big, and —"

Poi Popo glided over and rested gently beside him, soft as a lavender pillow. "Mac," she said, "do you know what was here before this rock?"

"...The glow?"

"And before the glow?"

Mac thought. "...The mountain?"

"And before the mountain, the deep dark sea. And before the sea, just stars. Everything that has ever been beautiful," said Poi Popo, "started out as something that looked like *nothing at all*. New beginnings, little nut, can rise from anything. From fire. From rock. From a speck of dust in a crack. Even from one small, shy macadamia who isn't sure he's big enough." She smiled. "And he always is."

Mac took a deep breath. He held the seed over the warm little crack. And — small but mighty, shell to heart — he tucked it gently in, patting the tiniest pinch of almost-soil over the top like a blanket.

"There," he whispered. "Sleep tight, little seed."

"Beautifully done," said Pele's Glow, glowing so warm and proud that the whole crack lit up gold for a heartbeat. "Now we just have to add the most important ingredient of all."

Mac peeked up nervously. "Which is...?"

"*Patience*," rumbled Poi Popo, settling down with a contented wobble. "The land's favorite recipe. A pinch of fire, a sprinkle of dust, one brave seed — and then years and years of nothing-much, which is when all the best things quietly happen."

"Years?" squeaked Mac. "But I won't get to see it!"

Pele's Glow drifted close and warmed him gently, like a small sun saying goodnight. "You don't have to see the whole forest, keiki, to plant the first tree. That's the secret of aina. Nobody who builds an island ever sees it finished. They just add their warm little piece — and trust the next night to add the next."

---

For a moment, nothing happened, the way nothing happens right before everything does.

Then Pele's Glow gave the softest, warmest hum, like a lullaby made of firelight, and the three friends sat together on the brand-new aina and watched the stars wheel slowly overhead. The rock cooled gently beneath them. The sea sighed and hushed against the edge of the world. Somewhere far below, deep inside the island, the glow was still flowing, still cooling, still making *more* — more land, more aina, more places for more brave little seeds to someday sleep and someday wake.

"It won't grow tonight," said Pele's Glow drowsily. "Maybe not for many nights. Seeds are patient. Land is patient. But one morning — long after we've all had a good nap — there will be a tiny green leaf right here. And then a little fern. And then a tree. And birds. And keiki laughing. All because of one warm river, one cozy crack, and one small nut who was brave enough to try."

Mac yawned, the smallest, sleepiest yawn. He had stopped hiding in his shell entirely. He didn't need to. "Pele's Glow?" he murmured.

"Mm?"

"I think... I think I'm part of the story now."

"Oh, keiki," whispered the little ember, glowing the softest, happiest gold. "You always were."

And Poi Popo wrapped her gentle taro-warmth around them both, and the three of them stayed there together on the newest land in all the world — fire and earth and one tiny seed — while the island, as it does every single night, quietly went on being born.

New land, new light, every night.

## Goodnight Blessing
May you remember, little one, that everything wonderful began as something small and quiet and new — even you. May the warm not burn you and the dark not worry you, for the same gentle fire that makes the islands is the same soft glow that watches over your sleep. Somewhere out there, a brand-new piece of land is cooling beneath the stars, waiting patiently for a seed and a someday. And so are you, dreaming of all the lovely things you'll grow into. Rest now, brave little seed. The morning will come, and so will the green. Goodnight.
