# Story 10: Mac the Macadamia and the Little Big Rescue

**Main Character:** Mac the Macadamia
**Guest Characters:** Momi the Mango, Mac's best friend; Pua the Pineapple; Pepe the big Papaya
**Setting:** A macadamia farm and rocky cliff trail above Waikiki
**Theme:** The smallest one can make the biggest difference.
**Hawaiian Spotlight:** **wikiwiki** (wee-kee-wee-kee) = quickly / fast

---

High on a green hill above Waikiki, the trade winds smelled like roasted nuts and sunshine.

There lived the smallest snack on the whole farm: Mac, a nut the size of a thumbtip.

"Mac, you're TINY," boomed Pepe the big papaya, and bounced clean over him.

"Mac, I nearly sat on you!" gasped Pua the Pineapple, leaping sideways, crown wobbling.

Clik-clak. Mac snapped his glossy shell shut like a helmet. The world was loud.

"Small but mighty, shell to heart," he whispered inside. He mostly believed it. Mostly.

---

Today the whole farm buzzed about the **pā'ina** (PAH-ee-nah) = feast on the cliff lookout.

Pepe practiced his fanciest bounce. Pua polished her crown until it dazzled a gecko.

Mac peeked out through one crack. He wanted one thing: to be invited. Just once.

"Hop in my basket pocket, little buddy!" sang Momi the mango, golden and soft as sunrise.

"We go **wikiwiki** (wee-kee-wee-kee) = quickly," said Momi. "Sunset waits for nobody!"

Mac glowed so warm his shell steamed. Clik. Open, just a peek. "Really?"

---

The cliff trail wound along a green bluff. Far below, the sea sparkled turquoise and gold.

They spread the picnic on a wide flat rock. Haupia jiggled. Coconut slices gleamed.

"A view worthy of my crown," declared Pua, leaning nowhere, because queens do not lean.

Mac sat on a smooth stone and felt, for once, exactly the right size.

"I can see a sea turtle from here!" called Momi, leaning out a teensy bit.

CRUMBLE. The edge gave way. Momi yelped, slid, and dropped out of sight.

---

She landed on a ledge no wider than a chopstick, high above the long blue drop.

"I'm okay!" called Momi, in a voice that wobbled like haupia in an earthquake.

"The rock's too crumbly to climb," said Momi. "I'll just live here now. Forever. It's fine."

"I'll save her!" cried Pua, leaning over. Pebbles skittered. Her crown snagged a bush.

Pepe bounced to the edge. The edge said CRACK. Pepe bounced away very, very quickly.

The only way down was a twisty crack, thin as a curly straw. Nobody big could fit.

---

"I can do it," said a very small voice. Everyone turned. There stood Mac.

"You? But Mac, you're—" began Pua.

"Small," said Mac. For the first time he said it like a plan. "Small, smooth, and round."

Clik-clak. Helmet shut. Mac hopped to the crack, took one breath, and let go.

Down he went, PING-ping-DOINK, bouncing through the twisty tunnel like the wildest marble slide.

He was screaming and laughing at the same time. Don't tell anyone.

POP. He shot out the bottom and landed, clik-clak, right beside Momi.

"Told you," he puffed, shell popping open. "Small but mighty."

---

A tough vine of **pōhuehue** (POH-hoo-eh-hoo-eh) = beach morning-glory dangled just past Momi's reach.

Not past Mac's. He gripped it in his shell-jaws and zipped up, wikiwiki, like a bead.

"Loop it round the big rock!" he hollered from the top. "Everybody! WIKIWIKI!"

Pepe looped. Pua cinched with her crown. They tied it tight as a birthday bow.

"PULL!" Mac stood at the edge, tiny and loud. "Almost there, Momi! You're amazing!"

WHUMP. Momi tumbled over the edge onto the grass, golden, whole, and giggling.

She wrapped Mac in the squishiest mango hug in the history of Waikiki.

---

The sky melted pink, then peach, then mango-orange over the sea.

"You SAVED her!" sobbed Pua, mango juice everywhere. "And you said you were small!"

"He is small," laughed Momi. "That's why he fit right where it mattered."

The pā'ina happened after all, on the wide safe rock. Nobody called him teeny. They called him hero.

Mac popped his shell open to the warm wind and said it loud, for everyone.

"Small but mighty, shell to heart."

---

## Goodnight Blessing
