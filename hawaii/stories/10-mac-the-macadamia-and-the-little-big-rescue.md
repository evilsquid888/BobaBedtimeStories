# Story 10: Mac the Macadamia and the Little Big Rescue

**Main Character:** Mac the Macadamia
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A macadamia farm and rocky cliff trail above Waikiki
**Theme:** The smallest one can make the biggest difference.
**Hawaiian Spotlight:** **wikiwiki** (wee-kee-wee-kee) = quickly / fast

---

High on a green hill above Waikiki, where the trade winds smelled like roasted nuts and warm sunshine, there lived the very smallest snack on the whole macadamia farm.

His name was Mac.

Mac was a little round nut in a glossy brown shell that he could pop open and snap shut like a helmet — *clik-clak!* — whenever the world got too big and loud. Which, for a nut the size of a thumbtip, was pretty often.

"Mac, you're TINY," boomed the big papaya at the fruit stand.

"Mac, you're TEENY," sang the tall stalks of sugarcane.

"Mac, I almost sat on you!" gasped a passing pineapple, leaping sideways with a dramatic *gasp* and nearly losing her crown.

Mac sighed and pulled his shell shut. *Clik-clak.*

"Small but mighty, shell to heart," he whispered to himself, very quietly, in case nobody believed it.

Not even Mac was totally sure he did.

---

But that morning, something wonderful was happening. The whole farm was buzzing — quite literally, because the bees were buzzing too — about the **pā'ina** (PAH-ee-nah) = feast happening that night down by the cliff trail.

"A sunset picnic!" the fruits cheered. "On the lookout ledge above Waikiki! Best view on the island!"

Everyone wanted to go. The papaya practiced his fanciest bounce. The pineapple polished her crown until it sparkled like a chandelier. The sugarcane stretched extra tall to see the ocean.

And little Mac?

Mac just hoped someone would remember to invite him.

"Of course we'll bring you, Mac!" laughed a kindly mango named Momi, scooping him up gently. Momi was Mac's best friend in the whole world — soft, golden, and sweet, with a smile like a sunrise. "What's a picnic without my favorite little nut?"

Mac glowed so warm his shell nearly steamed. *Clik!* — open, just a peek. "Really?"

"Really-really," said Momi. "Now hop in my basket pocket. We'll roll out wikiwiki — that means quick-quick! Sunset waits for nobody!"

So off they all went, a parade of giggling snacks bouncing down the winding trail, with Mac tucked snug in Momi's pocket, feeling — for once — like he belonged.

---

The cliff trail above Waikiki was the most beautiful place Mac had ever seen.

The path wound along a tall green bluff. Far below, the ocean sparkled turquoise and gold, and tiny surfers rode the waves like sprinkles riding a wave of frosting. The sunset was just starting to pour itself across the sky — pink, then peach, then a melty mango-orange that made everyone *ooh* and *aah*.

"Now THAT," declared the pineapple, "is a view worthy of my crown."

They spread out their picnic on a wide flat rock. There was haupia jiggling on a leaf-plate, slices of cool coconut, and a whole pile of sweet things glistening in the golden light.

Momi the mango set Mac gently on a smooth stone where he could see everything.

"Best seat in the house, little buddy," she winked.

Mac watched the waves. He watched the sky. He watched his friends laughing, and for the first time in a long time, he didn't feel teeny at all.

He felt *just right.*

But the thing about sunsets on cliffs is that they make everyone want a better look.

"I'll just lean out a *teensy* bit," said Momi, peering over the edge toward the surfers. "Ooooh, I can see a sea turtle from here! Mac, you have to —"

*CRUMBLE.*

The edge of the trail gave way beneath her.

---

Momi slid.

Not far — but far enough. With a little *yelp* and a flurry of mango-tumble, she slipped right off the path and landed on a narrow rocky ledge below, a ledge no wider than a chopstick, jutting out over the long, long drop to the sea.

"Momi!" everyone screamed.

The mango clung to the ledge, golden and trembling. "I'm okay! I'm okay!" she called up, though her voice wobbled like haupia in an earthquake. "I just — I can't climb back up. The rock's too crumbly. If I move, I'll —"

She didn't finish that sentence. She didn't need to.

The friends crowded at the edge, all in a panic.

"I'll save her!" cried the pineapple — but she was far too round and prickly, and the moment she leaned over, pebbles skittered down and she had to leap back. "Oh dear. Oh DEAR. My crown is NOT built for rescues."

"I'll do it!" boomed the papaya — but he was too big and too soft, and one bounce too many would crumble the whole ledge.

The sugarcane was too tall and too stiff. The coconut couldn't bend. The haupia just jiggled helplessly, wibbling, "Oh no, oh no, oh no."

The narrow ledge sat at the bottom of a thin, twisting crack in the rock — a tunnel so small and so steep that nobody, not one of the big snacks, could possibly squeeze down it.

Nobody big, anyway.

---

From his smooth stone, a very small voice spoke up.

"I can do it."

Everyone turned.

There stood Mac, his glossy shell catching the last of the sunset. He looked, if anything, even tinier than usual against the great wide sky.

The pineapple blinked. "You? But Mac, you're —"

"Small," said Mac. And for the first time, he didn't say it like an apology. He said it like a *plan.* "I'm small. I'm smooth. And I'm round." He took a deep breath. "I can fit down that crack. And I can roll wikiwiki."

*Clik-clak* went his shell — snapping shut into a perfect little helmet.

"Mac, no, it's too dangerous!" Momi called up from the ledge, her voice tight with worry. "You could get hurt!"

But Mac was already moving.

---

He rolled to the edge of the crack and peered down. It was steep. It was dark. It twisted and turned like a curly straw. His little nut-heart hammered inside his shell.

*Small but mighty,* he told himself. *Small but mighty, shell to heart.*

And this time — he believed it.

"Hang on, Momi!" he shouted. "Here I come!"

Mac tucked himself tight, sealed his shell, and *let go.*

Down he went — *whirrrr!* — bouncing and pinging and ricocheting through the twisty stone tunnel like a marble in the world's wildest slide. *PingpingPING! Doinkdoink! Wheeeee — OOF — wheeee!*

It was, Mac would admit later, kind of the most fun he'd ever had. (Don't tell anyone he was screaming and laughing at the same time. But he was.)

He shot out the bottom of the crack — *POP!* — and landed neatly, *clik-clak*, right beside Momi on the narrow ledge.

"Mac!" she gasped. "You actually made it!"

"Told you," he puffed, a little dizzy, his shell popping open to reveal his proud cream-colored face. "Small but mighty."

---

But now came the hard part. How could one tiny nut help a whole big mango climb to safety?

Mac looked around — *wikiwiki, think wikiwiki* — and that's when he saw it. A long, tough vine of beach morning-glory dangling down the cliff, the kind that island kids called **pōhuehue** (POH-hoo-eh-hoo-eh) = beach morning-glory vine, strong as rope and twice as stubborn.

It hung just out of Momi's reach.

But not out of Mac's.

"Momi," he said, "I'm going to roll up that vine and tie it tight to the big rock up top. Then you grab it and climb. The vine'll hold you. You're strong — you just need something to hold onto!"

"You can't carry a whole vine, you're too —" Momi started.

"I'm exactly the right size," said Mac, grinning. "Watch me."

And — *clik-clak!* — he gripped the end of the pōhuehue in his little shell-jaws and went *zipping* up the vine, hand over hand, smooth and quick, the way only something small and round and brave could climb. Up and up, wikiwiki, until he reached the top.

"Pull the vine over here!" he called to the friends. "Loop it round that big rock! HURRY — I mean, wikiwiki!"

The big snacks finally had a job they could do. The papaya looped, the pineapple cinched, the sugarcane braced. They tied that vine to the great stone tight as a bow on a birthday present, with Mac scampering across the knot to check every loop.

"PULL!" Mac cried.

Momi grabbed the vine. She climbed. The whole crowd heaved and hauled, and Mac — tiny Mac — stood right at the cliff's edge, calling out, "You've got it! A little more! Almost there! Wikiwiki, Momi, you're amazing!"

And then —

*WHUMP.*

Momi the mango tumbled safely over the edge and onto the grass, golden and whole and giggling with relief, with one very small macadamia nut wrapped up in the biggest, squishiest, sweetest mango hug in the entire history of Waikiki.

---

The friends cheered until the stars came out.

"Mac, you SAVED her!" the pineapple wept, mascara of mango juice running everywhere. "And you said you were small!"

"He *is* small," laughed Momi, squeezing him tight. "That's exactly why he could do it. The biggest one couldn't fit. The tallest one couldn't bend. But our little Mac?" She booped his shell. "Our little Mac fit *right where it mattered.*"

That night, the pā'ina happened after all — right there on the safe wide rock, under a sky full of stars, with the whole island twinkling below. And the guest of honor, sitting on the softest leaf, in the very best seat, was the smallest snack on the whole farm.

Nobody called him teeny.

They called him *hero.*

And Mac, snug and proud, popped his shell open to the warm night air and whispered — loud enough for everyone to hear this time:

"Small but mighty, shell to heart."
