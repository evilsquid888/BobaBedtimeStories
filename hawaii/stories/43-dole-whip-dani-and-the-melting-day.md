# Story 43: Dole Whip Dani and the Melting Day

**Main Character:** Dole Whip Dani
**Guest Characters:** Kai the Shave Ice, Niu the Coconut
**Setting:** The hottest sunny afternoon of the year in Waikiki
**Theme:** Asking for help is brave, not weak.
**Hawaiian Spotlight:** **lā** (LAH) = sun / day

---

The hottest **lā** (LAH) = sun of the whole entire year arrived in Waikiki at exactly lunchtime, and it arrived like a giant golden bully who'd forgotten his manners. The sky turned the color of a fried egg. The sand began to hiss. A seagull tried to land on the beach, yelped "Hot-hot-HOT!", and flapped straight back up to hover in the cooler air like a feathery helicopter.

And there, on the very sunniest corner of the very sunniest sidewalk, stood Dole Whip Dani — a swirly golden-yellow soft-serve peak with a glossy curl on top, a tiny pineapple-leaf tuft of hair, and huge sparkly eyes that were, at this exact moment, sparkling a little *too* much.

"Aloha, beautiful **lā**!" Dani sang, spinning her curl. "What a gorgeous day to be the SUNNIEST treat in all of Waikiki! Stay cool and swirl on, everybody!"

A drip of golden pineapple slid down her side and went *plip* on the hot pavement.

Dani pretended she did not see it.

---

You see, Dani had a teeny tiny secret, and the secret was this: soft-serve does not love the sun. Soft-serve, in fact, is *terrified* of the sun, the way a snowman is terrified of a hairdryer. But Dani was so sunny on the inside that she had decided — very firmly, very stubbornly — that the sun was her best friend and would never, ever do her wrong.

(Between you and me, sweet dreamer, this is a little bit like deciding the bathtub is your best friend right before you climb in fully dressed. Lovely idea. Slightly soggy results.)

"I am a Dole Whip," she told herself, standing extra tall on her little cup-body. "I am made of sunshine. The hot lā cannot melt me, because I am basically RELATED to it."

*Plip. Plip-plip.*

Two more golden drops.

Across the street, Niu the Coconut was rolling by under his tiny woven hat, sloshing pleasantly with cool water inside. He was a round, shaggy brown coconut with three little face-dots and a green drinking-straw sprout, and he took one look at Dani and stopped rolling so fast that his hat slid over one eye.

"Dani," said Niu, in his steady, know-it-all-but-means-well voice. "You're dripping."

"I'm GLOWING!" Dani corrected, with a dazzling smile. "Tough shell, sweet heart, right? Well, I'm all sweet heart! Off I go to my favorite sunny bench!"

She wobbled. Just a tiny wobble. The glossy curl on top of her head drooped sideways like a question mark.

Niu narrowed all three of his face-dots. "That's not a glow. That's a *puddle starting*. Come sit in the shade with me, yeah?"

"Shade?" Dani gasped, as if he'd offered her a spider sandwich. "I don't need shade! Shade is for treats who can't handle a little **lā**! I'm fine! I'm GREAT! I'm—"

*SPLORP.*

Her left side slid off and landed on the sidewalk with a sad little splat.

There was a small, surprised silence.

"...I meant to do that," said Dani.

"You did not mean to do that," said Niu.

"I absolutely meant to do that. It's a new dance move." Dani wobbled the rest of herself in a way that was supposed to look graceful and looked, instead, like a flag caught in a sneeze. "It's called the Sunshine Shimmy. Very popular. Very cool."

Niu sighed the long, patient sigh of a coconut who has watched a friend be silly many, many times. "Dani. Sweetheart. The Sunshine Shimmy is just *melting* with extra steps."

---

Now here is the thing about being too proud to ask for help: it makes you do very silly things. Instead of sitting down in the shade like a sensible dessert, Dani scooped her melty side back up, plopped it on, and announced she was going for a "refreshing walk in the wonderful warm sunshine." Then she set off down the sidewalk at top wobble.

It did not go well.

She melted a little going past the surfboard rental. A tourist with a giant camera saw her golden trail and cried, "Honey, look! Free pineapple paint!" She melted a little more going past the shave-ice stand, where a hungry myna bird hopped over, gave her one sniff, and announced, "Ooh! Smoothie!" — which Dani did NOT find flattering, thank you very much.

"I'm not a smoothie," she huffed, wobbling onward. "I'm a *masterpiece*."

"You're a *puddle with eyebrows*," the myna chirped helpfully, and flew off before she could argue.

By the time she reached the big banyan tree, she had left a long, glistening, golden trail behind her like a snail made entirely of pineapple, and her proud peak had drooped into something closer to a pancake. Her little cup-body sloshed. Her leaf-tuft flopped over one eye. Even her sparkle had gone soft and runny, like sunshine that had stayed up too late.

"Stay cool," Dani panted, "and swirl... swirl... on..." Her huge sparkly eyes were starting to slide toward her chin.

That's when a cool blue shadow fell over her like a soft umbrella.

"Whoa. Whoa whoa whoa, sister." The voice was mellow and calm and very, very chill. "You're, like, leaking your whole self onto Kalākaua Avenue."

It was Kai the Shave Ice — a tall fluffy dome of snow-white ice drenched in rainbow stripes of lilikoi-gold, blue-coconut, and strawberry-red syrup, sitting in a paper cone with a little paper-umbrella hat and a tiny spoon tucked behind one ear. He glistened like fresh snow, and standing near him felt like opening a freezer door on a hot day. Pure bliss.

"Kaiii," Dani slurred happily, sagging toward him. "You look... so... cold... and lovely..."

"And you look like a melted crayon, no offense," said Kai. "Come into the shade. Right now. I got you."

"No, no, no." Dani peeled herself upright with a tremendous, sticky effort. "I do NOT need help. I am a strong, independent soft-serve and I can handle one teeny little sunny **lā** all by my— oooh." Her eyes wobbled in two different directions. "Why are there three of you, Kai?"

"There's one of me," said Kai gently. "You're melting, Dani. It's okay. It happens to the best of us. Once I melted into pure syrup at a beach party and Niu had to carry me home in a cup. True story."

"You DID?" Dani squinted. Even half-melted, she was curious. "And you... let him help you?"

"'Course I did." Kai shrugged, sending a little shiver of rainbow down his fluffy dome. "Chill out and rainbow on, yeah? You can't be a rainbow all by yourself. You need a whole sky." He tilted his paper umbrella thoughtfully. "Think about it. The lilikoi stripe can't make a rainbow alone. Neither can the blue coconut, or the strawberry. It takes all three, leaning together, to make something beautiful. That's not weakness, sister. That's the whole *point*."

Dani blinked her slidey eyes. Somewhere in her melty middle, a tiny door opened just a crack.

---

But Dani — stubborn, sunny, proud Dani — shook her drippy head. "I just need to find a sunnier spot. The sun MAKES me. The sun is my friend. If I ask for help, that means I'm not strong, and I AM strong, I'm—"

She stopped.

She couldn't finish the sentence. Because her mouth had melted into a wobbly little frown, and a great big golden glob of her had just slid right off her body and gone *splosh* into the gutter.

And for the first time all day, Dani felt something in her sunny heart that wasn't sunny at all.

She felt small. And scared. And so, so hot.

A tiny voice — much tinier than her usual bubbly shout — squeaked out: "...Kai? Niu? I... I think I really do need help. Please. I don't want to be a puddle."

And here is the magical part, sweet dreamer. The *instant* she asked — the very second the word "please" left her melty lips — her friends did not laugh. They did not say *I told you so*. They did not love her even one drop less.

They MOVED.

"Niu, the hat!" Kai called. Niu flung his tiny woven hat into the air, and Kai caught it and held it over Dani like a parasol. "Shade detail, activated!"

"I'm the wheels!" Niu announced, rolling close so Dani could lean her wobbly self against his hard, cool shell. "Tough shell, sweet heart — and right now my tough shell is YOUR shade. Hop on, sister!"

"And I'm the AC," said Kai, pressing his frosty, snow-cold side gently against Dani's overheated back. A delicious chilly *aaaaah* shivered all through her. "Banyan tree's just there. Big shade. Cool grass. Let's roll."

Together — one coconut, one shave ice, and one very melty Dole Whip — they bundled down the sidewalk, Niu rolling, Kai chilling, Dani leaning. And do you know what? Leaning on her friends did not feel weak at all. It felt like floating. It felt like the most wonderful, restful thing she'd ever done.

---

Under the big banyan tree, the world went cool and green and dappled, like stepping inside a giant leafy refrigerator. The roots curled up into comfy little seats. The breeze smelled of plumeria and salt. Niu sloshed open his sprout and dribbled cold coconut water over Dani's swirl, going "There you go, there you go" in his steady grandpa-voice. Kai fanned her with his paper umbrella and hummed a slow, cool, surfer-y tune. And slowly — *gloriously* — Dani stopped dripping. Her peak rose. Her curl curled back into its proud little glossy question mark. Her sparkly eyes pointed in the same direction again, and the sparkle went crisp and bright instead of runny.

"Better?" asked Niu.

"Better," sighed Dani, and it was the happiest, coolest sigh in the history of sighs.

"Oh," she breathed, feeling firm and swirly and herself once more. "Oh, that's so much better. I can FEEL my swirl again!"

"Welcome back, sunshine," grinned Kai.

Dani looked at her two friends — the shaggy coconut who'd given up his hat, the rainbow mountain who'd given up his cool — and her golden cheeks went rosy.

"I'm sorry I was so stubborn," she said. "I thought asking for help meant I wasn't strong. But that's not it at all, is it?"

Niu wobbled his hat back onto his head. "Nope. Asking for help when you need it? That's the bravest thing a little treat can do."

"It takes guts to say *please*," Kai agreed, settling into the cool grass. "Anyone can pretend they're fine. Only the strong ones admit when they're melting."

Dani giggled — a real, bubbly, sugary giggle. "Then I am the STRONGEST, swirliest, askiest-for-help Dole Whip in all of Waikiki!" She struck a proud pose under the shade. "And from now on, when the **lā** gets too big and bright, I'm not going to fight it all alone. I'm going to find my **ʻohana** (oh-HAH-nah) = family. Because that's its own kind of strength!"

"Now THAT," said Kai, raising his little spoon like a tiny toast, "is the coolest thing you've said all day."

"Speaking of cool," said Niu, sloshing happily, "I've still got plenty of water left. Who wants a sip?"

"ME," said Dani and Kai at exactly the same time, and the three of them burst out laughing — a coconut chuckle, a frosty giggle, and a bubbly pineapple hoot all tangled together under the leaves.

And the three friends sat in the green shade as the great golden **lā** softened into a warm, sleepy afternoon — not a bully anymore, just a friend who'd needed reminding to be gentle.

"Stay cool," said Dani softly, leaning her happy swirl against Niu's shell, "and swirl on. Together."

---

## Goodnight Blessing
Little dreamer, the **lā** can shine ever so bright, but you don't have to hold up the whole sky by yourself. When you feel like you're melting — too hot, too tired, too much — there is no shame in leaning on the ones who love you. Asking for help isn't being weak; it's letting your ʻohana be strong *with* you. So snuggle deep into your cool, soft blankets, and let someone else hold the hat for a while. Goodnight.
