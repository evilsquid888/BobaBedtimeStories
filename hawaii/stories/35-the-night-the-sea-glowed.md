# Story 35: The Night the Sea Glowed

**Main Character:** Lilikoi Lani
**Guest Characters:** Kai the Shave Ice, Dole Whip Dani
**Setting:** A dark beach at night where the waves sparkle with bioluminescence
**Theme:** The dark can hold the most beautiful surprises.
**Hawaiian Spotlight:** **pō** (POH) = night / darkness

---

Lilikoi Lani had pranked everything that could be pranked.

She had tucked a tickly seaweed ribbon into Kai the Shave Ice's paper-umbrella hat. She had drawn a teeny mustache on a sleeping sand crab (who woke up, looked at his reflection in a tide pool, and immediately decided he loved it). She had even hidden behind a coconut and shouted "BOO!" at a hermit crab, who had simply blinked and said, "Boo to you too," because hermit crabs have seen a lot and are very hard to startle.

Now Lilikoi sat in the sand — a small, round, purple-wrinkly fruit with a mischievous sparkle — and groaned the biggest groan a passion fruit can groan.

"It's so DARK," she complained. "There's nothing to DO at night! No colors. No tricks. No fun. The **pō** (POH) — that's night, or darkness — is the most boring time of the whole entire day, and I have decided this OFFICIALLY."

She crossed her tiny arms. "Sour first," she muttered, "and sour second, too."

---

Kai the Shave Ice flopped down beside her in the cool sand, his rainbow stripes glowing faintly under the starlight — lilikoi gold, blue coconut, strawberry pink. He stretched out long and slow, the way he did everything.

"Bummed out, little Lani?" he asked, tucking his tiny spoon behind one ear. "The pō's not boring, brah. It's just... quieter. You gotta listen for the fun instead of looking for it."

"Pfft." Lilikoi flicked a grain of sand at him. "Listen for fun? You can't *hear* a prank, Kai."

"You can hear my tummy," said a bright, bubbly voice, and Dole Whip Dani came swirling down the beach, her glossy golden curl bouncing with every hop, her tiny pineapple-leaf tuft of hair wiggling like it was excited just to exist. "And right now my tummy is saying, 'Daaani, why are we sitting on a dark beach at midnight?' And I am saying, 'Tummy, that is an EXCELLENT question!'"

She plopped down between her two friends, slightly melty at the edges, sun-bright even in the middle of the night. "So! What's the plan? There's always a plan! Stay cool and swirl on, I always say!"

"The plan," said Lilikoi grumpily, "is that there is no plan, because the pō is boring, and we should all just go to bed where at least I can dream about pranks."

Dani gasped like Lilikoi had said the most shocking thing in the world. "Bored? On the beach? At NIGHT? Oh, Lani-Lani-Lilikoi! Boredom is just a fun-shaped hole waiting to be filled with FUN! Watch — I'll prove it!"

She squinted into the darkness. The moon was hiding tonight, tucked behind the hills like it had its own bedtime. There was no light at all except the soft pinprick stars and the faint glimmer of Kai's syrup stripes.

"Hmm," said Dani. "Okay. The dark is a LITTLE dark."

"A little?" said Lilikoi. "I can't even see my own wrinkles, and I have a LOT of wrinkles, and I'm very proud of every single one of them."

---

Now, here is the thing about Lilikoi Lani that you should know, dear reader. (Lean in. I'll whisper it.) When Lilikoi got bored, she got *dangerous* — not bad-dangerous, but prank-dangerous, the kind of dangerous where if you leave your slippers unattended, you will later find them full of poi. So when she announced, "Fine. If the night won't be fun, I'll MAKE it fun," her two friends exchanged a worried little look.

"Lani," said Kai slowly, "what are you—"

But Lilikoi was already zipping down the beach toward the water, leaving her famous tangy-sweet scent trail behind her like a ribbon of perfume. "I'm going to splash you BOTH!" she called. "The old wet-surprise! A classic! Nobody expects the wet-surprise!"

"We literally always expect the wet-surprise," sighed Kai.

Lilikoi reached the edge of the dark sea, scooped back one little arm, and SMACKED the water with all her zesty might.

And that —

— that —

— is when the night turned magic.

Where her hand hit the water, the sea *lit up*. Not a little. A LOT. A great splash of glowing blue sparkles burst into the dark like someone had spilled a jar of liquid stars. The droplets hung in the air, twinkling electric-blue, then pattered back down into the waves and glowed there too, swirling and shimmering like the ocean had swallowed the whole Milky Way.

Lilikoi froze. Her mischievous sparkle went very, very wide.

"...Whoa," she breathed.

---

"OHHHH!" squealed Dole Whip Dani, swirling so fast her curl nearly came unswirled. "It GLOWS! The water GLOWS! Lani, do it AGAIN, do it again, DO IT AGAIN!"

Lilikoi smacked the water again — *FWOOSH!* — and a thousand blue sparkles leaped up and rained back down, glittering across the foam.

Kai the Shave Ice came gliding to the water's edge, and for once the mellow surfer was not mellow at all. His eyes were as round as full moons. "Oh, brah," he whispered, awed. "It's *kai hālāwai* — the meeting tide. My tūtū told me about this. It only happens on the darkest nights, the deepest **pō**, when there's no moon at all."

"But what IS it?" said Lilikoi, splashing again, giggling now as the light danced up her wrinkly purple arms. "Are there tiny lanterns in there? Did someone drop a flashlight? Is the ocean having a PARTY without inviting me, because frankly I am the BEST at parties—"

"They're little glowing sea-sparks," said Kai softly. "Itty-bitty ocean creatures, so small you can barely see one. But when the water moves them, they light up — all together, all at once. Millions of them. Saying hello."

"Saying hello to the dark," said Dani dreamily, wading in until the blue light swirled around her cup-body. "Hi, dark! We see you now! You're GORGEOUS!"

"But here's the funny part," Kai went on, his surfer-calm voice dropping low and secret. "They only shine in the **pō**. In the daytime — when the sun's all loud and bright and showing off — you'd swim right past 'em and never know they were there. They're invisible. Plain little nobodies. It takes the dark to make 'em magic."

Lilikoi stared at the glowing water, and something tickly happened inside her tangy little heart. "So... the dark doesn't *hide* the pretty stuff," she said slowly. "The dark is the only thing that *shows* it."

"Now you're surfing, little Lani," said Kai, and he smiled the biggest smile a shave ice can smile without melting.

"Ooooh!" gasped Dani, doing a delighted little hop that sent up a fountain of blue. "It's like a SECRET! A secret the night keeps just for the friends who are brave enough to stay up and look! Lani, we are in the secret CLUB now! We're founding members! I'll make us hats!"

"You always want to make us hats," said Kai fondly.

"Because hats are FUN," said Dani, "and so is the night, apparently, and I would like the record to show that I have been saying that this WHOLE time."

---

For the next hour — and oh, what an hour it was — the three friends played in the glowing sea.

Lilikoi, who only an hour ago had declared the night officially boring, now darted along the shoreline drawing pictures in the water with her hands, and everywhere she touched, glowing blue lines trailed after her like she was painting with light. She drew a wobbly turtle. She drew a lopsided pineapple ("For Pua! She'd love a glowing crown!"). She drew a face making a silly expression, and the sea held it, shimmering, for three whole seconds before the waves smoothed it away.

"It's the best prank canvas in the WORLD," she gasped, "except it's not a prank, it's just... beautiful, and I didn't even know the night could DO this!"

Then her sparkly eyes went extra-wide with a NEW idea — the kind of idea that, on Lilikoi's face, usually means somebody's slippers are about to get poi'd. But this idea was different. This idea was *kind*.

"Wait wait wait," she said. "Dani — float right HERE. And hold still."

Dani floated. Lilikoi zipped in big looping circles all around her, slapping and swirling the water as fast as her little arms could go, until a glowing blue ring spun out around her friend — round and bright and shimmering, hovering on the surface of the dark sea like a crown made of starlight.

"A crown!" Dani squealed, looking down at the glowing ring. "You made me a CROWN! Oh, Pua would be SO jealous, this is even better than her leaf one, don't tell her I said that, actually DO tell her, no DON'T—"

"Crown up, sweetheart," giggled Lilikoi, doing her very best impression of Pua the Pineapple's dramatic flair, and all three of them laughed so hard that the sea sparkled brighter, as if the ocean itself were giggling along.

Kai floated on his back, letting the glowing waves rock him, his rainbow stripes mixing with the electric blue until he looked like a sunrise that had wandered into a starry night by mistake. "Told ya," he said, grinning up at the stars. "Chill out and rainbow on. The pō's got its own kine rainbow. You just gotta wait in the dark long enough to see it."

Dani spun in slow happy circles, sending up sprays of blue light with every twirl. "You know what this means?" she announced. "It means the dark isn't the END of the fun. It's a whole NEW kind of fun! A secret fun! A fun you can only find when you stop being scared of the quiet and the dark and you just... look closer!"

Lilikoi flopped backward into the shallows, and the whole sea bloomed blue around her, hugging her in light. Tiny glowing sparkles caught in every one of her wrinkles, so that for one shining moment, the grumpy little prankster looked like a fruit made entirely of stars.

"I'm sorry I said the pō was boring," she said quietly. "I was just... looking for the same old kind of fun. The loud kind. The trick kind. And I forgot that some surprises are *quiet*." She smiled, soft and sweet. "Sour first," she whispered, "but sweet — oh, *always* sweet."

---

They stayed until the sky began to soften at its edges, the faraway promise of morning yawning over the hills. The blue sparkles grew fainter as the dark grew thinner, until at last the sea was just the sea again, gentle and grey and ordinary — but none of them thought it was ordinary anymore. Not even a little.

"Same time tomorrow night?" asked Dani, hopping out and shaking the last sparkles off her curl.

"Only if there's no moon," said Kai. "Gotta be the darkest pō. That's the secret."

Lilikoi climbed out last, leaving one final glowing handprint on the surface of the water, watching it shimmer and fade. She tucked the whole memory deep into her zesty little heart, right next to all her favorite pranks.

"The darkest night," she said, "holds the brightest surprise." Then she grinned her old cheeky grin. "Also, Kai, just so you know — I put a glowing sea-sparkle in your umbrella hat, and you'll find it in about three seconds."

"...Aw, *Lani*," said Kai — but he was laughing, and so was the glowing tide, and so were the stars.

---

## Goodnight Blessing

Little one, when your room goes dark and the night wraps soft around you, remember the glowing sea. The **pō** is not a thing to fear — it is a velvet blanket full of quiet wonders, twinkling just for those who wait and look closely. Somewhere out there, a sleepy ocean is glowing blue, and somewhere closer, you are glowing too. Let the dark hold you gently, like the sea holds the light. Goodnight.
