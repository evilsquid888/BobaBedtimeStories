# Story 48: The Great Island-Hopping Race

**Main Character:** Poke Pua
**Guest Characters:** Musubi Manu, Niu the Coconut, Kai the Shave Ice, Mac the Macadamia
**Setting:** A friendly canoe-and-surf relay race spanning several islands
**Theme:** Winning together beats winning alone.
**Hawaiian Spotlight:** **holo** (HOH-loh) = to go / run / sail

---

The morning the Great Island-Hopping Race began, Poke Pua woke up before the sun and rearranged her sesame stars four times.

"Symmetry wins races," she announced to the empty beach, lining up her ruby ahi cubes in a perfectly straight row. She fluffed her kelp-strand hair, polished her edamame freckles until they gleamed, and struck a pose so confident that a passing crab applauded with both claws.

"Fresh from the sea, that's me!" she sang. "And today? Fresh in FIRST PLACE."

The race was the biggest event of the whole year. Teams would **holo** — go, run, and sail — across three islands by canoe, by surfboard, and on foot, passing a single glowing kukui-nut torch from teammate to teammate until somebody crossed the great finish-line arch of woven ti leaves.

Poke Pua had assembled her crew the way she arranged everything: just so. There was Musubi Manu, square and sturdy with his crisp nori seatbelt. Niu the Coconut, round and brave in his little woven hat. Kai the Shave Ice, a drippy rainbow mountain under a paper-umbrella. And tiny Mac the Macadamia, half-hidden in his glossy shell.

"Listen up, team," Poke Pua said, pacing. "I have a plan. I have eleven plans, actually, but plan one is: we WIN."

---

Mac peeked out of his shell. "What's... plan two?"

"Win MORE," said Poke Pua.

The starting torch flared. **"Holo!"** cried the race-master, an ancient sea-turtle, and they were off.

The first leg was the paddle leg, and Niu the Coconut was their canoe-captain. He sloshed happily, his cool water gurgling inside as he steered. "Tough shell, sweet heart!" he called, digging his paddle into the waves.

But Poke Pua could not, would not, sit still. She leaned over Niu's shoulder. "Faster. Left. No — RIGHT. Niu, you're going too slow, I can see Team Mango pulling ahead, paddle like you MEAN it—"

"Poke," Niu grunted, "a coconut paddles best when nobody's yelling in his sprout."

"I am not YELLING, I am COACHING at volume," said Poke Pua, which is exactly the sort of thing someone yelling would say.

She grabbed a second paddle to help, and dug it in so hard — so very, very, determined-to-win hard — that she splashed an entire wave straight up Niu's three little face-dots. He sneezed. The canoe wobbled. Two of her perfect sesame stars went flying into the sea, where a delighted fish wore them like a tiny hat for the rest of the day.

By the time they reached the first island, Team Mango was way ahead, and Niu was very wet and very quiet.

"We lost the lead!" Poke Pua wailed. "Mac, your leg, GO — holo, holo, HOLO!"

---

The second leg was the running leg, across the warm sand of the middle island, and it belonged to Mac.

Now, Mac was the smallest of all of them. His legs were tiny. His shell was heavy. And when he tried to run very fast, he had a habit of tucking into his shell out of nervousness and simply... rolling.

"Don't tuck!" Poke Pua shouted, sprinting alongside him. "Whatever you do, do NOT tuck, you'll roll the wrong way, you'll—"

Mac, who had been doing perfectly fine, heard "tuck" so many times that he got flustered and tucked.

*Thunk-rolla-rolla-rolla.*

He rolled left. He rolled into a coconut palm. He bounced off it like a marble off a wall, *boing*, and rolled the opposite direction — backward, toward the start.

"MAC!" everyone yelled.

A passing gecko watched him zoom by and remarked, "Bold strategy, finishing the race in reverse."

By the time they untucked him, dusted him off, and pointed him the right way, Team Mango had finished their running leg too. The other teams were laughing — not meanly, just the way you laugh when a friend rolls into a tree. But Poke Pua's grin had gone tight and crinkly at the edges.

---

The third and final leg was the surf leg, and that was Kai the Shave Ice's moment to shine.

Nobody — *nobody* — surfed like Kai. He balanced his tall fluffy dome on the board, rainbow syrup streaming behind him like a comet's tail, and rode the swells with his surfer-calm smile. "Chill out and rainbow on!" he hummed.

But Poke Pua paddled out beside him on her own little board, refusing to wait at the finish.

"Catch the big one, Kai, the BIG one, no not that one, the BIGGER one, lean, LEAN—"

And Kai, who had been gliding as smooth as moonlight on milk, glanced back at her for just one worried second.

That one second was all it took. His board tipped. His dome wobbled. And Kai the Shave Ice — fluffy, drippy, beautiful Kai — went tumbling off his board in a glorious explosion of lilikoi and blue coconut and strawberry, *sploosh*, into the warm lagoon.

He came up sputtering, half his rainbow melting into the water, looking like a sunset that had fallen in the bath.

Team Mango sailed past them, hooting cheerfully, and slid under the woven ti-leaf arch.

The race was over. And Poke Pua's team had finished dead last.

---

For a long moment, nobody said anything. The waves lapped. A bird laughed somewhere, but quietly, like it felt a little bad about it.

Poke Pua floated in the lagoon next to soggy Kai, and her confident grin finally, finally crumpled.

"I'm sorry," she whispered. "I made Niu sneeze. I made Mac roll backward. I made you fall. I wanted us to win so badly that I... I squeezed all the fun right out of it." She sniffled, and an edamame freckle slid sadly off her face. "Some captain I am. I arranged everything just so, and it all fell apart."

That was when Musubi Manu paddled up on the board, calm and square and steady, with his bento backpack still neatly buckled.

"Poke," he said gently. "Can I tell you something I noticed?"

She nodded miserably.

"When Niu got splashed, Mac laughed so hard he nearly cracked his own shell. When Mac rolled into the tree, Niu caught him before he hit the water. And when Kai fell in the lagoon just now — did you see what he did?"

Poke Pua looked. Kai, dripping and rainbow-streaked, was floating on his back making syrup-clouds in the water and giggling.

"This is the BEST," Kai sighed dreamily. "I'm a floating sunset. Why don't I do this every day?"

"We lost the race," said Musubi Manu, "but I have never laughed so much in my whole tidy life. We were terrible. We were terrible *together*." He smiled. "That's worth more than a torch, yeah?"

---

And right then, Poke Pua understood something she'd been arranging too hard to see.

The prize had never been the finish line.

The prize was Niu sloshing, Mac rolling, Kai floating, Manu buckling his little backpack — the prize was the whole silly, soggy, splendid bunch of them.

"Okay," she said, sitting up straight in the water. "New plan. Plan twelve. The best plan I've got." Her sparkle came rushing back, but it was a warmer sparkle now. "We finish this race. Not to win. Just to **holo** across that line *together*. All five of us, all at once."

So they did.

Niu carried Mac on his round brown head. Kai pulled them all on his board, rainbow trailing. Manu kept everybody balanced and laughing with a perfectly-timed bad joke about "going against the *grain* of rice." And Poke Pua — for the first time all day — didn't coach a single soul. She just paddled beside her friends and cheered for *them* instead of for the win.

When the five of them slid under the woven arch in a wobbly, giggling, rainbow-soaked heap — dead last, dripping, and completely covered in sand — every single team on every single island stood up and cheered the loudest cheer of the whole day. Because everybody knows the most fun crew always crosses the line last, and the most-cheered finish is never the first one.

The sea-turtle race-master gave them a special torch anyway. A small one, glowing soft and golden.

"For the team," she said slowly, "that understood what the racing was *for*."

Poke Pua held it up, and her ahi cubes glistened like little rubies in its light. She didn't arrange a single one of them straight.

"Fresh from the sea," she said, looking around at her ohana — her sneezing, rolling, floating, joking, perfectly imperfect ohana — "*all* of me."

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, you can swim the fastest, climb the highest, and finish first all by yourself — and you will still have crossed only half a finish line. The other half is the friends who **holo** beside you, sneezing and rolling and laughing all the way. So tonight, let go of going first. Drift instead, like a sleepy shave-ice sunset floating in a warm lagoon, knowing the people who love you are floating right there too. May you wake tomorrow ready to paddle, ready to laugh, and ready to win — together. Goodnight.
