# Story 9: Lilikoi Lani and the Sour Surprise

**Main Character:** Lilikoi Lani
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A backyard fruit garden in Manoa Valley, Oahu
**Theme:** A good joke lifts people up, never puts them down.
**Hawaiian Spotlight:** akamai (ah-kah-MY) = smart / clever

---

In a backyard fruit garden tucked deep in green, misty Manoa Valley, where the rain came soft as a kitten's sneeze and rainbows arrived three at a time, there lived the cheekiest little fruit on the whole island of Oahu. Her name was Lilikoi Lani, and she was a small round passion fruit with purple-wrinkly skin like a tiny crumpled grape — except when she cracked herself open, *pop!*, out burst a sunset of tangy-orange seeds so zippy they made your nose wiggle.

Lani loved one thing more than sunshine, more than rain, more than the sweet-sour scent trail she left wherever she rolled.

Lani loved a good prank.

"Sour first," she'd whisper, sneaking up behind a sleepy snail, "sweet always!" And then — *boo!* — she'd squirt a teeny tangy mist and giggle so hard she rolled clean off the garden wall.

---

Now, the garden was a cozy 'ohana of fruits and friends. There was Pua the Pineapple, tall and regal under her green leaf-crown. There was Niu the Coconut, shaggy and steady, dispensing advice nobody asked for. And there was Lani's very dearest friend — a small, smooth, shy macadamia nut named Mac.

Mac was a quiet little fellow who lived half-tucked in his glossy shell like a turtle who'd misplaced his confidence. He didn't say much. But he laughed at every single one of Lani's jokes, even the bad ones, and *especially* the good ones, and that, dear reader, is the truest kind of friend there is.

The two of them were the funniest pair in the whole valley. Lani would zip up with a wild plan, and Mac would peek out of his shell, blink twice, and say one perfect quiet sentence that made the joke ten times funnier. "Lani," he'd murmur, "I do not think the gecko wants to wear your flower hat." And Lani would howl, and the gecko would scowl, and somehow it was the best thing that had ever happened to anyone. Where Lani was a bright fizzy splash, Mac was the warm little chuckle that kept her from rolling too far away.

One bright Manoa morning, Lani woke up positively *fizzing* with mischief.

"Today," she announced to a passing ladybug, "is going to be the greatest prank day in the history of pranks!"

She started small. She balanced a dewdrop on a leaf above Pua and tipped it — *plip!* — right onto the pineapple's crown.

"My CROWN!" gasped Pua, who treated her leafy crown like the queen of England treats hers. "It's *wilting!* It's — oh." She touched it. Just water. "Lani!"

"Crown up, sweetheart!" Lani sang, zooming away in a streak of purple.

Everyone laughed. Even Pua, after she'd fluffed her leaves back up, gave a royal little chuckle. That was a *good* joke — the kind where everybody, even the one who got got, ended up grinning.

But here's the thing about prank days. When they go well, a prankster gets a little too sure of herself. A little too *fast*. And she forgets to be **akamai** (ah-kah-MY) = smart, which is a thing you have to be *especially* careful about when you are very, very clever and feeling very, very pleased with yourself.

Lani rolled up to Niu next.

"Niu!" she cried. "There's a centipede on your hat!"

Niu didn't even blink his three little face-dots. "Lani," he said, in his steady know-it-all rumble, "a wise coconut checks before he panics. Tough shell, sweet heart." He tilted his woven hat. No centipede. "You'll have to be cleverer than that."

*Cleverer.* The word stuck to Lani like syrup. *I'll show him clever,* she thought. *I'll pull the biggest, most surprising, most akamai prank anybody has ever seen!*

And that's when she spotted Mac, snoozing in the warm sun beside the lilikoi vine, his shell open just a crack, the way he only ever opened it when he felt completely, perfectly safe.

---

Lani should have stopped to think. But thinking is slow, and Lani was fast, fast, fast.

She crept up. She took a great big breath. And right beside Mac's open shell she shouted in her zippiest, most thunderous voice —

"**A HUNGRY GIANT IS COMING TO EAT THE WHOLE GARDEN!**"

Mac shrieked. He slammed his shell shut so hard he bounced — *boing, boing, boing* — three times across the dirt and rolled under a fern, where he stayed, curled up tight as a fist, trembling.

"Ha!" laughed Lani. "Got you good! Sour first, sweet always! Come on out, Mac, it was just — Mac?"

Silence.

"Mac, it was a joke. There's no giant." She rolled to the fern. "Mac?"

A tiny, wobbly voice came from inside the shell. "I really thought... I really thought we were all in trouble. I really thought I couldn't help. I'm so small, Lani. I can never do anything when something's actually wrong. And you *knew* that. And you made it your joke."

Lani's tangy-bright seeds went very still.

She had pranked a hundred friends a hundred times. Pua. Niu. Snails, ladybugs, one extremely grumpy gecko. And they all laughed in the end, because the joke had bounced *up*, like a happy ball.

But this joke hadn't bounced up. It had landed right on the softest, most secret worry in Mac's whole little heart — *I'm too small to matter* — and it had pushed him *down*.

"Mac," she said quietly, "I didn't mean —"

But the shell stayed shut. And for the first time in her whole zesty life, Lilikoi Lani didn't feel one bit funny.

---

She rolled away slowly, her scent trail gone droopy and dull.

"Niu," she mumbled. "I think I made a real mess. Mac won't come out of his shell. I scared him about being small, and... I don't know how to fix it."

Niu was quiet a long moment. "Hm," he said, and for once he didn't lecture. "You're the cleverest fruit in this garden, Lani. The question is — clever *at* people, or clever *for* them? Anyone can be sour. Being akamai means using your cleverness to *lift somebody up*." He nudged his hat. "A good joke is like the rain in Manoa. It falls on everybody and makes everything grow. A mean one's just a puddle you slip in."

Pua drifted over, crown gleaming. "When *you* dripped water on my crown," she said, "I laughed because I knew, deep down, you think my crown is *worth* teasing. You made me feel *seen*, silly girl. That's the secret. The best jokes say *I know you, and I love you anyway.*"

Lani looked at her own bright seeds. *Sour first,* she always said. *Sweet always.* But lately she'd been all sour and forgetting the sweet.

She needed a new prank. The most akamai prank of her whole life. One that took the very thing Mac was afraid of — being small, being useless — and flipped it, *pop!*, into something wonderful.

She thought. And thought. And then her seeds began to glow.

"Niu," she whispered, "I'm going to need your help. And Pua's. And about a hundred ladybugs. And one *very* big surprise."

---

It took all afternoon, and it was the hardest Lani had ever worked in her zippy little life. She had to convince Pua to lend her royal crown for "structural support" (Pua agreed, but only after three compliments). She had to teach a hundred ladybugs how to line up in rows, which is roughly as easy as teaching raindrops to march. She had to bribe the grumpy gecko with a guava. And she had to think — really, truly think — about every single thing that made Mac wonderful, until she had a whole long list, far too long to fit in one little sign.

"That's the funny part," she muttered to herself, rolling back and forth in the dirt. "He thinks he's small. And the *truth* is the biggest, sweetest joke of all." For the first time, she wasn't planning a prank to catch somebody out. She was planning one to let somebody *in*.

As the Manoa mist turned gold, then pink, then sleepy purple, Lani finally rolled to the fern where Mac was still hiding.

"Mac," she said softly. "I'm sorry. Truly. I scared you about the realest thing, and I turned it into a joke, and that wasn't funny — that was just sour. Will you come out? Not for a prank. For an apology. I made you something."

Slowly — *creeeak* — Mac's shell opened one tiny crack. One shy eye peeked out.

"...You made me something?"

"Open your shell," Lani said, "and look up."

Mac opened his shell.

He looked up.

And there, strung across the whole garden in the violet evening, was a sign — made of fruit and flowers and a hundred ladybugs all holding still in a row, their little red backs spelling out words in the dusk:

**MAC THE MIGHTY — SMALL BUT THE BIGGEST HEART IN MANOA.**

Pua held up one corner with her crown. Niu balanced the other on his hat. And every snail, every gecko, every fruit in the 'ohana had gathered round.

"It's not a trick," Lani said, her voice all sweet now, no sour left. "It's a *true* surprise. Here's the joke, Mac, and it's the best one I ever made: you spent all day thinking you were too small to matter. *That's* the silly part. Because you're the macadamia who laughs at all my dumb jokes. You're the one who makes the whole garden warmer just by being in it. You crack open the *toughest* shell on this island to share your sweet heart. *That's* not small, Mac." She grinned. "That's the mightiest thing I ever saw. Get it? The joke's on *you* — you've been amazing the whole time!"

Mac stared at the ladybug sign. At all his friends. At the words that called him *mighty.*

And then — slowly at first, then all at once — Mac the Macadamia laughed. A real laugh, a deep-down belly laugh that shook the dewdrops off the ferns, the kind of laugh he only laughed for Lani's *very best* jokes.

"Small but mighty," he hiccuped happily, "shell to heart!"

"Sour first," Lani cried, doing three joyful loops in the air, "*sweet always!*"

The whole garden cheered. The ladybugs flew up in a glittering swirl. And Niu, who never admitted anything, mumbled, "Now *that* — that was akamai."

---

That night, snug in the warm Manoa dark, Lani and Mac sat shell-to-seed under the lilikoi vine, watching the stars wink on one by one.

"I'll still prank you," Lani warned sleepily.

"I know," said Mac, smiling. "Just... lift me up with it?"

"Always," she promised. "That's the whole point."

And the gentlest rain in all of Hawaii pattered down, soft as a kitten's sneeze, making everything in the garden grow.

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, the cleverest thing you will ever learn is how to make somebody laugh and feel *bigger*, never smaller. A good joke is a gift you hand to a friend, wrapped in giggles, that says *I see you, and you are wonderful.* So be akamai with your kindness, sweet first and sour never-too-much, and tuck your worries away like Mac tucking into his cozy shell. You are not too small. You never were. Goodnight.
