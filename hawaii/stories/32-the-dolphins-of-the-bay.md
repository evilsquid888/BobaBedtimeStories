# Story 32: The Dolphins of the Bay

**Main Character:** Kai the Shave Ice
**Guest Characters:** Dole Whip Dani, Poke Pua
**Setting:** A calm, sparkling dolphin bay at sunrise on the Big Island
**Theme:** Joy is best when it's shared and given space.
**Hawaiian Spotlight:** nai'a (nye-AH) = dolphin

---

Kai the Shave Ice woke up so early that the sun hadn't even finished combing its hair. The sky over the Big Island was peachy-pink, the color of strawberry syrup left in the bottom of a cup, and the whole bay lay still as a sleeping cat.

"Whoa," Kai whispered, his fluffy white dome tilting so far that his paper-umbrella hat almost slid off. "The water looks like blueberry shave ice that nobody's eaten yet."

He had dragged two friends out of bed for this. Dole Whip Dani bounced beside him, her glossy golden curl still squished flat from her pillow. Poke Pua stood a step behind, calmly arranging her sesame stars so they sat *just so* across her rice.

"Kai," Dani yawned, "the sun isn't even all the way up. What's the big sparkly emergency?"

Kai pointed one drippy mitten at the bay. "Somebody told me," he said, voice low and tingly, "that the *nai'a* come here to play."

And right then—as if the bay had been waiting for its cue—the water broke open in a silver splash.

---

A dolphin leaped straight up out of the sea, spun three full spins in the air like a wet, happy top, and slipped back under without so much as a splash of complaint.

Kai screamed. Quietly. (It is possible to scream quietly when you are a polite mountain of ice, and Kai had a lot of practice.)

"DID YOU SEE THAT?" he whisper-shrieked, hopping in his paper cone so hard that a stripe of lilikoi syrup sloshed down his side. "It SPUN! In the AIR! Like a cinnamon roll with FINS!"

"**Nai'a**," Poke Pua breathed, her kelp-strand hair lifting in the sea breeze. "Spinner dolphins. They rest in calm bays like this all morning, then go out to the deep water to hunt at night. Right now they're just—being happy."

More of them rose. Two, then five, then a whole shimmering pod, their grey backs curving up out of the water like the tops of smooth round stones. Some spun. Some leaped in pairs. One baby dolphin, no bigger than a loaf of bread, flopped sideways with a *splat* that made all three friends giggle.

"That little one belly-flopped," Dani gasped, melting a little at the edges from pure joy. "That's the best move I have ever seen and I am going to learn it."

"Look how they breathe," Poke Pua said softly, pointing as a dolphin rolled up, took a quick *pfft* of air through the little hole on its head, and slipped back under. "Each one comes up just enough to catch a breath, then back down to rest. They take turns keeping watch, too. Half the pod naps while the other half stays alert. It's the most patient, peaceful thing in the whole ocean." She smiled. "And then, every so often, one of them just—can't help it—and *boings* into the sky."

As if to prove her right, a dolphin shot up and corkscrewed so fast it looked like a silver ribbon caught in the wind. Kai's spoon nearly fell out from behind his ear.

"Stay cool and swirl on," Kai murmured dreamily, watching the pod twirl. "I've never seen anything so—so—" He searched for the right word, the way he'd search for the perfect syrup. "So *free*."

The three of them sat on the warm morning sand, side by side, just watching. The bay sparkled. The dolphins played. And for one whole golden minute, nobody said anything at all, because some things are too beautiful to interrupt.

Then Kai had an idea.

Now, Kai's ideas were usually wonderful. But every now and then, in his bubbling excitement, one came out a little *too* big.

"You know what would make this even MORE amazing?" he said, scrambling up. "If we got CLOSER. If we swam RIGHT OUT THERE. We could spin with them! We could be in the pod! Imagine it—Kai the Shave Ice, official spinning **nai'a**!"

"Uh," said Poke Pua.

But Kai was already wobbling toward the water, his whole snowy self quivering with delight. Dani bounced after him, because Dani could never resist a fun plan, and a fun plan with dolphins in it was the funnest plan of all.

"Wait for me!" Dani called. "Cannonball formation!"

"CANNONBALL!" Kai agreed.

They charged into the shallows together, splashing and hooting—*flomp, flomp, FLOMP*—straight toward the playing pod.

And the bay went quiet.

---

The dolphins stopped spinning.

The pod, which had been so loose and easy and full of leaps, suddenly pulled together into a tight little knot. They turned away. The baby dolphin tucked close to its mother. Where there had been a hundred happy splashes, now there was just the slap of two embarrassed treats standing knee-deep in cold water, watching the **nai'a** swim quietly toward the far side of the bay.

Kai's umbrella hat drooped.

"They... left," he said in a small voice. The kind of small voice that's hard for a big fluffy mountain to make. "Did I scare them?"

Poke Pua waded out and put a cool, gentle ribbon of seaweed-arm around his shoulder. "You didn't scare them on purpose, Kai. But the bay is *their* resting place. When we crowd them—when we get too close, too loud, too excited—they don't feel safe enough to play. They have to put their joy away and keep it small."

Kai felt a sad little drip slide down his side, and this time it wasn't syrup.

"I didn't want to make their joy small," he whispered. "I wanted MORE of it. I wanted to be IN it."

Dani sank down beside him in the shallows, her usual sunshine dimmed to a soft glow. "Me too," she admitted. "I think I wanted it so much I forgot it wasn't mine to grab."

For a moment, the three friends just sat there, feeling about as low as the tide.

"When I was little," Poke Pua said at last, gazing out at the far-off pod, "my Popo took me to a tide pool. I saw the most beautiful little fish you ever did see—gold and blue, quick as a wink. I scooped it up so fast, to keep it close, to *have* it." She paused. "And the second it was in my hands, it stopped shining. It just wanted to be back in its water. So I let it go. And do you know what? It swam in a happy little circle right by my flipper for the longest time, sparkling like a coin. It only stayed *because* I let it leave." She nodded toward the bay. "That's the same thing, I think. Some joys you can only keep by not keeping them."

Kai sniffled. "That is the most beautiful thing I have ever heard, and also I feel like a knucklehead."

"You're a kind knucklehead," Dani said, patting his snowy side. "Those are the best kind."

Then Poke Pua, who was fresh and confident and always quick with a clever idea, lifted her head.

"It's not mine to grab," she said slowly. "But it *is* ours to share. There's a difference, you know. Grabbing means taking it close. Sharing means giving it space." She tilted her bowl-body toward the shore. "Come on. I have an idea, and it involves sitting *very* still and being *very* patient. Two things, I'll be honest, that none of us are great at."

"I am EXCELLENT at sitting still," said Kai.

"You are vibrating right now," said Dani.

"...I am excited about sitting still," Kai corrected.

---

So they tiptoed back—*flomp, flomp, flomp*, but gentler this time—and settled on the warm sand far up the beach. Far enough that they were just three little shapes on the shore. Far enough to give the whole bay back to its dolphins.

And then they waited.

It was hard. Oh, it was hard. Kai wiggled. Dani hummed. Poke Pua had to rearrange her edamame freckles four times just to keep her flippers busy. A crab walked past and judged them.

"How long do we wait?" Kai whispered, after about eleven whole seconds.

"As long as it takes," Poke Pua whispered back.

"That's not a number," Kai whispered.

"It's not supposed to be," Poke Pua whispered.

To pass the time, they invented a quiet game. Whenever a dolphin surfaced far away, you could whisper one word about it, but only one. "Graceful," breathed Poke Pua. "Bouncy," breathed Dani. Kai thought very, very hard, scrunching up his fluffy face, and finally whispered the most magnificent word he knew: "Cold." Then, "—in a *good* way!" he added, which was two words, so Dani made him sit out a turn, which he did with enormous, melty dignity.

But slowly—so slowly, like syrup spreading—the bay began to relax.

First one grey back curved up out of the water. Then another. The knot of dolphins loosened. The baby peeked out. And after a while, when the friends had stayed small and quiet and far for long enough that the **nai'a** forgot to worry about them, the spinning began again.

A dolphin shot up into the morning light and twirled four whole times.

Kai didn't scream this time. He didn't charge. He just clapped his soft mitten-hands together, very gently, and whispered, "Bravo," like he was watching the most beautiful show in the world.

Because he was.

"This is better," he realized, his sparkly eyes wide. "From here, I can see the WHOLE pod. Every leap. Every spin. The baby one belly-flopping. Up close, I only would've seen splashes and grumpy fins." He let out a long, happy sigh that smelled like blue coconut. "Sometimes the best seat is the one a little farther back."

"And look," Dani breathed. "They're having more fun than ever. Because we *let* them."

Poke Pua nodded, sunrise glinting in her ruby ahi. "Joy's a funny thing," she said. "When you chase it down and squeeze it, it shrinks. But when you give it room? It grows big enough for everybody. The dolphins get to play. And we get to watch them play. Everybody's cup is full." She winked. "And no one's cup got knocked over."

The sun climbed higher. The pod spun and leaped and slapped the silver water, and the three friends watched from their cozy spot on the sand, laughing softly at every flip, gasping at every twirl, full to the brim with a quiet, shared, easy kind of happy.

And the funny thing was—Kai realized—he *did* feel like part of the pod after all. Not by swimming in the middle of it. But by loving it from exactly the right distance, the way you love a falling star: with your whole heart and your hands kept still.

"Chill out and rainbow on," Kai whispered to the bay, to the dolphins, to the morning. "You beautiful, spinning **nai'a**. Chill out and rainbow on."

The bay sparkled back like it had heard him.

And somewhere out in the bright blue water, the littlest dolphin did one more belly-flop—*splat!*—just, Kai was absolutely certain, to make them laugh.

It worked.

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, may your joys be the kind that grow when you share them, and never shrink when you hold them close. May you learn, like Kai, that the best way to keep something wonderful is to give it room to dance. Tonight, picture a calm silver bay at sunrise, with friendly **nai'a** spinning slow circles in the warm light, happy and free and watched over with love. Let their gentle splashing rock you soft and slow, the way the tide rocks the whole sleepy island. You are loved, you are safe, and there is room enough in this big bright world for all your dreams to leap. Goodnight.
