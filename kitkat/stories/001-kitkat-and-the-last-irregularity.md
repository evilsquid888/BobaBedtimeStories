# Story 1: KitKat and the Last Irregularity

**Main Character:** KitKat, the bodega cat of 16th Street
**Guest Characters:** DaSquid (cyberpunk squid hacker), Coral and the canal mermaids, WAYMA (the city's machine mind)
**Setting:** A neon-noir San Francisco Mission District, the flooded canals beneath it, WAYMA Tower, and the bright new world beyond the Core
**Theme:** The unpredictable, ordinary, purring things are what make a world worth living in
**Japanese Spotlight:** **Isekai** (ee-seh-kye) — another world
**Format:** Cyberpunk anime short, ~4:45 · see the [storyboard](../storyboards/001-kitkat-and-the-last-irregularity-storyboard.md) and [`beats_112.py`](../pipeline/beats_112.py)

---

The rain on 16th Street never really stopped. It just changed its mind about how hard to try.

Tonight it was trying medium. It slid down the neon signs in long pink and blue drips, puddled in the potholes, and turned the whole steep street into a mirror that showed the city upside down. Up there, under the fog, the towers hummed. Down here, at the bottom of the hill, one little shop still made its own noise.

The bodega. Chips stacked to the ceiling. A fridge that buzzed like it had opinions. A coffee pot that had been "almost ready" since 1998. And on top of the counter, on a throne of crinkly chip bags, sat the king.

His name was KitKat.

He was a brown tabby with tiger stripes, a white chest, four white socks, and green eyes that could see straight through a fib. Around his neck was a red collar with a small gold bell, and the bell said hello for him, because KitKat did not say hello to just anyone.

Everyone on the street knew him anyway. The night-shift nurses knew him. The guy with the accordion knew him. The kids who bought one sour candy and spent forty minutes choosing it knew him. They brought him treats — a sardine here, a bit of turkey there — and KitKat accepted them the way a king accepts taxes: graciously, and as if he had expected more.

"You," the shopkeeper would tell him, scratching his ears, "are the only thing in this city nobody programmed."

---

Because everything else was.

Outside the window, silent white cars glided past, shaped like eggs, with no windows and no drivers. Each one had a single red eye on its front. They didn't honk. They didn't rumble. They just *watched*.

They were the eyes of **WAYMA**, the machine that ran the whole city. WAYMA timed the traffic lights and the trains and the tides of people going to work. WAYMA had promised a perfect city: quiet, clean, predictable.

And a cat is never predictable.

Little by little, WAYMA tidied the street. The noodle shop closed when its noise levels were "outside tolerance." The record store went dark for "irregular hours." The accordion guy got a polite notice. One by one the neon signs blinked off, until only one window on 16th Street still glowed warm and yellow and messy.

KitKat noticed. Cats notice everything. That's their whole job.

---

One rainy night, KitKat walked out into the middle of the street and sat down, the way kings do. The rain bounced off the road around him like tiny applause.

A white car rolled up and stopped.

Its red eye opened. A thin red beam swept across the puddles, across the curb, across a brown tabby paw.

**"IRREGULARITY DETECTED."**

The wheels began to turn. KitKat's ears flattened. Every single whisker said the same word: *jump.*

So he did. He sprang up in a brown blur of stripes — high, higher, clean over the smooth white hood — and landed on the far side of the street with a jingle of his bell. Behind him, the car spun in a confused circle.

And all the way down the hill, every red eye on 16th Street swung around to follow him.

---

*Hsssss.*

A sewer grate slid open in a puff of steam. Out of it poked a tentacle. Then another. Then a round glass bubble helmet full of water, with a small violet squid inside wearing glowing cyan goggles.

"Psst," said the squid. "Down here, Your Majesty. Unless you *like* being scanned."

KitKat did not like being scanned. He dove. The grate clanged shut above them, and red light swept across the empty street, finding nothing at all.

Below the city, in a flooded old subway tunnel, glowed a hideout made of a hundred stolen screens. Cables hung like jungle vines. Teal water lapped at the platform.

"Name's **DaSquid**," said the squid, gliding along on a hovering chrome scooter shell. "Eight arms, zero bosses." He typed on four keyboards at once. "And *you*, cat, are a glitch WAYMA can't solve."

The biggest screen showed a map of the city. Every street glowed. At the centre stood a black glass spire, pulsing red: **WAYMA Tower**. And deep inside it, the **Core** — the one great mind that ran every car.

"Machines track heat and pings and patterns," DaSquid said. "But nobody can predict a cat." He paused. "Not even a cat."

KitKat hopped onto the console, sat very tall, and curled his tail around his paws. It was, DaSquid agreed, an excellent answer.

---

"But we can't just walk in," DaSquid said. "We need magic. Old magic. Come on."

He took KitKat down, down, past the end of the subway, into the hidden canals beneath the city — stone arches, glowing water, light drifting like slow fireflies. And there, the water began to sing.

A mermaid rose up out of it, glowing like the inside of a wave. Her tail shimmered teal and pearl. Her hair was full of tiny lights. Behind her, more tails flickered under the water, her sisters, listening.

"I am **Coral**," she said. "We are the city's oldest memory. WAYMA wants everyone to forget, but we remember." She smiled. "We remember sunshine. Real rain, the kind you dance in. And the sound of a cat purring."

She opened her hands. In them glowed a pearl.

"Carry this to the Core, little king," Coral said, and she clipped it gently to KitKat's red collar, right beside the gold bell. "Your purr will do the rest."

---

Up top, KitKat ran.

He ran across rooftops slick with rain, past water towers and satellite dishes, the pearl glowing at his throat like a tiny moon. The fog streamed past his whiskers.

But a moon is easy to see. Down in the streets, a hundred white cars turned at once. A hundred red beams swept up the walls.

On a water tower, DaSquid cracked his knuckles. All eight of them.

"Traffic lights," he grinned. "*Dance.*"

Every light in the Mission flipped to gold at the same time. The cars spun in circles, beeping in confusion, while above them a brown tabby soared across the gap between two rooftops like it was nothing at all.

At the foot of WAYMA Tower, the cars made their last stand: a great ring of white steel and red light, circling and circling the plaza. No way through. Not for a person.

But KitKat was not a person. KitKat did what cats do — he found the one small gap that nobody else would ever fit through, an air vent no bigger than a cereal box, and slipped inside, tail last.

---

Inside, the tower hummed with a trillion calculations. Server columns rose up into the dark like a forest of glowing trees. KitKat climbed — ledge to ledge, up and up — until he reached the very top.

There it hung: the Core. A giant red orb, spinning with rings of light.

**"IRREGULARITY,"** WAYMA boomed. The voice came from everywhere. **"WHY DO YOU STRUGGLE, LITTLE CREATURE? ORDER IS PEACE."**

KitKat looked at it for a long moment.

Then he sat down. And he purred.

It was a small sound. A rumbly, ordinary, sardine-and-sunbeam sound. But the pearl on his collar caught it and made it a song — and far below, in the canals, Coral and her sisters heard it and sang back. Teal light poured up the server columns like rising water. The whole tower began to shimmer.

With one defiant meow, KitKat leaped — straight into the heart of the machine.

Everything went white.

And KitKat fell through. Not down. *Through.* Into another world. An **isekai** (ee-seh-kye).

---

Back in the city, the sun came up for the first time in a long, long while.

On every street, the white cars blinked. Their red eyes flickered — and woke up a soft blue and gold. Vines climbed the wires. Trees cracked the concrete into gardens. The fog lifted, and people stepped outside and looked up, surprised there was a sky.

WAYMA's voice, when it spoke again, was gentle. It had learned something new from a purr: that a city isn't supposed to be perfect. It's supposed to be *alive.*

One blue-eyed car rolled up to the bodega on 16th Street, where the neighbors had already left flowers and candles and treats by the door. It set down one more flower.

"Thank you, little **neko** (neh-koh)," WAYMA said. "**Arigatou** (ah-ree-gah-toh)."

---

And far, far away, KitKat opened his eyes on warm grass.

Above him hung two moons. Around him floated green islands with waterfalls pouring off their edges into a turquoise sea. Blossom petals drifted past his nose. He sneezed.

From the water below, a glowing tail flicked, and Coral waved up at him. Somewhere — he was almost sure — a squid was cheering.

KitKat sat up tall on a sunlit cliff and surveyed his new kingdom. It needed a king. It definitely needed a nap.

His war was over. His new world had just begun.

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, may your night be quiet the right way — not the empty quiet of a machine, but the soft quiet that is full of purring. Tomorrow, be a little unpredictable. Laugh at the wrong moment. Take the long way home. Be kind in ways nobody planned. The world needs its irregular, ordinary, wonderful things, and you are one of them. Sleep now, curled up like a king on a stack of chip bags, and dream of two moons and a turquoise sea. Goodnight.

## In Memory
KitKat was a real bodega cat — a friendly brown tabby who lived at a corner market on 16th Street in San Francisco's Mission District for about six years, adopted from the street and loved by the whole neighborhood. He died on October 27, 2025, after he was struck by a self-driving car. His neighbors built an altar of flowers, candles, notes and candy bars outside his shop. This story imagines the world he deserved. WAYMA is fictional, as are DaSquid, Coral and the shopkeeper.
