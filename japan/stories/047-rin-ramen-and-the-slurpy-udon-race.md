# Story 47: Rin Ramen and the Slurpy Udon Race

**Main Character:** Rin Ramen
**Guest Characters:** Grandmother Koshi, an ancient udon noodle; Mero Melonpan; Kei the kei-truck
**Setting:** A countryside udon noodle shop famous for thick chewy noodles
**Theme:** Slowing down lets you taste the good stuff
**Japanese Spotlight:** **udon** (oo-dohn) — thick wheat noodles

---

Rin Ramen came bouncing down the misty mountain road so fast his broth sloshed over the rim.

Sploosh! Sploosh! Little soup-shaped clouds puffed up behind him. His corn freckle nearly popped off.

"FASTER!" hollered Rin, steam shooting from both ears like a happy kettle.

Behind him puttered Kei the kei-truck, a butter-yellow mini-truck, springs going boing-boing.

"Putt-putt! Rin, you're spilling your whole self everywhere!" beeped Kei.

Rin wanted one thing: to win the Great Udon Slurping Race, faster than anyone ever.

---

Up the green hill sat a little wooden shop, steam curling from its roof like a sleepy dragon.

Inside, long ropes of dough hung drying. On a cushion, Mero Melonpan was already snoring crumby snores.

Out shuffled the oldest, plumpest noodle anyone had ever seen, coiled up like a tiny round grandmother.

"I am Grandmother Koshi," she said, flour dusting her shoulders like the first gentle snow.

"I have made **udon** (oo-dohn) — thick wheat noodles — here for one hundred and twelve years."

"A hundred and TWELVE?" gasped Mero, waking up. "I can't stay awake for a hundred seconds."

"Then a race it is!" said Rin, stubby legs in the air. "Fastest slurper wins! GO fast!"

---

Three bowls arrived, slowly, noodles fat as garden hoses in broth like melted afternoon sunshine.

"Ready... set... GO!" cried Rin, and attacked his bowl like a tiny delicious tornado.

SLOOORP! Noodles flew. Broth splashed up his nose. A noodle wrapped around his ear like a scarf.

Then, HIC. Rin got the hiccups. Each hiccup launched a noodle straight up into the air.

One noodle on his head. One on his nose. Rin looked like a mop that fell in soup.

HIC! The biggest hiccup yet lassoed a noodle right around the handle of the great noodle pot.

The pot tipped. And out slithered the longest udon noodle in all of Japan, heading for the door.

---

The noodle slid out the door and down the green hill like a long white river.

"MY RACE!" yelled Rin, and bounded after it, broth sloshing, going faster than he'd ever gone.

The noodle went faster. Rin hopped onto Kei's flatbed. "Putt-putt, let's GO!" beeped Kei.

Kei zoomed. Rin reached. The noodle slipped through his stubby arms every single time.

Faster! Kei hit a bump. Rin flew off the flatbed and landed, splat, in a cabbage patch.

"Hic," said Rin, wearing a cabbage leaf like a hat. "Still winning."

---

Mero, meanwhile, had wandered outside for a nap on a sunny rock at the bottom of the hill.

The noodle slid gently up and curled around his crackly crust like a warm scarf. It stopped.

Mero opened one sleepy eye. "Oh. Hello, noodle." He did not move. The noodle did not move either.

Rin arrived last, puffing, cabbage on his head. "You CAUGHT it? You were NAPPING!"

"Crackle on the outside," yawned Mero. "I just held still. It came to me."

Grandmother Koshi shuffled down, slowly, slowly. "A noodle that is loved does not run. It waits."

---

Back inside, she lowered one fresh noodle into Rin's bowl. Plip. Just one, thick and shining.

"Not fast," she said. "Slow. Feel its bounce. Let the broth say hello. Then listen."

Rin's hiccups settled. He lifted the one noodle and gave it the tiniest, gentlest slurp.

Oh. It was chewy. It bounced back like a little hug. It tasted of wheat fields and patience.

"I couldn't taste anything when I was racing," said Rin, eyes shiny. "I forgot to enjoy."

"**Yukkuri** (yook-koo-ree) — slowly, taking it easy," said Grandmother Koshi. "That is how you taste the good stuff."

---

So they slurped again, slowly, together, steam rising soft and lazy through the warm shop.

Mero dozed off mid-slurp, noodle still in his mouth. The best race, Rin decided, he'd ever lost.
