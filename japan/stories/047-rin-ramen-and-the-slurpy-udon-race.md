# Story 47: Rin Ramen and the Slurpy Udon Race

**Main Character:** Rin Ramen
**Guest Characters:** Grandmother Koshi, an ancient udon noodle; Mero Melonpan; Kei the kei-truck
**Setting:** A countryside udon noodle shop famous for thick chewy noodles
**Theme:** Slowing down lets you taste the good stuff
**Japanese Spotlight:** **udon** (oo-dohn) — thick wheat noodles
**Cameo:** Saitama and Genos (One-Punch Man) — the bald hero wanders in, finishes a race bowl in one bored slurp, his cyborg disciple analyses the noodle technique out loud, and the udon granny is unimpressed by both; walk-on: Zoro (One Piece)

---

Rin Ramen bounced down the misty mountain road so fast his broth sloshed over the rim. Sploosh!

"FASTER!" hollered Rin, steam shooting from both ears like a happy kettle.

Behind him puttered Kei the kei-truck, butter-yellow, springs going boing-boing.

"Putt-putt! Rin, you're spilling your whole self everywhere!" beeped Kei.

Rin wanted one thing: to win the Great Udon Slurping Race, faster than anyone ever.

---

Up the green hill sat a little wooden shop, steam curling from its roof like a sleepy dragon.

Inside, ropes of dough hung drying. On a cushion, Mero Melonpan snored crumby snores.

Out shuffled the oldest, plumpest noodle ever seen, coiled like a tiny flour-dusted grandmother.

"I'm Grandmother Koshi," she said. "I've made **udon** (oo-dohn) — thick wheat noodles — for a hundred and twelve years."

"A hundred and TWELVE?" gasped Mero, waking up. "I can't stay awake for a hundred seconds."

"Then a race it is!" said Rin, stubby legs in the air. "Fastest slurper wins! GO fast!"

---

Three bowls arrived, slowly, noodles fat as garden hoses in broth like melted sunshine.

"Ready... set... GO!" cried Rin, and attacked his bowl like a tiny delicious tornado.

SLOOORP! Noodles flew. Broth splashed up his nose. A noodle wrapped around his ear like a scarf.

---

The door slid open on a bald hero in a yellow jumpsuit, white cape and shopping bag.

Saitama sat, lifted a race bowl, and SHLOORP. Empty. One slurp. "Okay," said Saitama, bored.

Behind him, a blond cyborg with glowing orange arm-joints whirred. "Master!" cried Genos. "Intake angle: forty degrees. Flawless."

Grandmother Koshi tapped the empty bowl. "A hundred and twelve years," sighed Koshi. "Nobody chews."

"Was there flavor?" asked Saitama, sitting down on the window bench beside Genos. "I think I missed it."

Rin's steam whistled. "ONE slurp? Then I'll do it in HALF a slurp!"

---

SLOOOOORP! Then, HIC. Rin got the hiccups. Each hiccup launched a noodle straight up.

One noodle on his head. One on his nose. Rin looked like a mop that fell in soup.

HIC! The biggest hiccup lassoed a noodle right around the handle of the great noodle pot.

The pot tipped, and out slithered the longest udon noodle in all of Japan, heading for the door.

---

The noodle slid out the door and down the green hill. "MY RACE!" yelled Rin, bounding after it.

Rin hopped onto Kei's flatbed. "Putt-putt, let's GO!" beeped Kei, and the noodle slipped away faster.

Kei hit a bump. Rin flew off the flatbed and landed, splat, in a cabbage patch.

Beside him, a green-haired swordsman frowned at a map held upside down. "Which way's the shop?" growled Zoro.

"Hic," said Rin, wearing a cabbage leaf like a hat, and pointed up the hill. "Still winning."

---

Mero, meanwhile, was dozing on a sunny rock at the bottom of the hill.

The noodle slid gently up and curled around his crackly crust like a warm scarf. It stopped.

Mero opened one eye. "Oh. Hello, noodle." He held perfectly still. So did the noodle.

Rin arrived last, puffing. "You CAUGHT it? You were NAPPING!"

"Crackle on the outside," yawned Mero. "I just held still. It came to me."

---

Back inside, Koshi lowered one fresh noodle into Rin's bowl. Plip. Just one, thick and shining.

"Not fast," she said. "Slow. Feel its bounce. Let the broth say hello. Then listen."

Rin's hiccups settled. He lifted the one noodle and gave it the tiniest, gentlest slurp.

Oh. It was chewy. It bounced back like a hug. It tasted of wheat fields and patience.

"**Yukkuri** (yook-koo-ree) — slowly, taking it easy," said Koshi. "That's how you taste the good stuff."

---

So they slurped again, slowly, together, steam rising lazy through the warm shop.

Mero dozed off mid-slurp, noodle still in his mouth. The best race, Rin decided, he'd ever lost.
