# Story 47: Rin Ramen and the Slurpy Udon Race

**Main Character:** Rin Ramen
**Guest Characters:** Grandmother Koshi, Mero Melonpan, Maru Matcha, Tama Onigiri, Kei the kei-truck
**Setting:** A countryside udon noodle shop famous for thick chewy noodles
**Theme:** Slowing down lets you taste the good stuff
**Japanese Spotlight:** **udon** (oo-dohn) — thick wheat noodles

---

Rin Ramen came bouncing down the misty mountain road so fast that his golden broth sloshed right over the rim and made little soup-shaped clouds behind him. *Sploosh! Sploosh!* His noodle-curl hair flapped. His soft-boiled-egg cheek wobbled. His corn freckle nearly popped clean off.

"FASTER!" Rin hollered, steam puffing out of his ears like a happy kettle. "We're going to the most famous noodle shop in the whole countryside, and I, the great Rin Ramen, am going to SLURP everyone under the table!"

Behind him puttered a teeny butter-yellow truck, her springs going *boing-boing*, her headlight eyes wide with worry.

"Putt-putt — Rin, you're spilling your whole self everywhere!" beeped Kei the kei-truck.

"That's just my EXTRA enthusiasm leaking out!" Rin slurped a stray noodle back up with a triumphant *slooorp*. "Everything's better warm and bubbly — and FAST!"

And up the green hill they raced, toward a little wooden shop with steam curling from its roof like a sleepy dragon stretching awake.

---

The shop was called **Nonbiri-an** (nohn-bee-ree-ahn) — the Take-It-Easy Cottage — and it was the coziest place Rin had ever seen — which, frankly, annoyed him a little. He didn't have *time* for cozy. He had a race to win.

Inside, the air was warm and white with steam, smelling of wheat and gentle soy. Long ropes of dough hung drying like sleepy snakes. And at the center of it all sat the friends Rin had invited: Tama Onigiri, snug as a little rice-pillow; Maru Matcha, calm half-moon eyes already half-closed; and Mero Melonpan, who had fallen asleep before they'd even sat down and was snoring soft crumby snores.

"FRIENDS!" Rin announced, climbing onto a cushion and nearly tipping his whole bowl over. "Welcome to the FIRST EVER Great Countryside **udon** (oo-dohn) — thick wheat noodles — Slurping Race! Rules: the one who slurps their bowl fastest WINS. And I plan to win SO fast that the noodles won't even know they've been eaten!"

"I packed a little extra napkins," said Tama gently, "just in case somebody splashes."

"I never splash," said Rin, who was, at that exact moment, dripping broth onto the table.

Maru Matcha raised her tiny bamboo whisk like a teacher about to make a very small, very wise point. "Rin," she said, in her cozy proverb-voice, "whisk slow. Feel calm. Why race the noodles? The noodles are not running anywhere."

"Because winning is the warmest, bubbliest feeling of ALL!" Rin declared, steam shooting up like a little firework. "Now — where's the chef? BRING ON THE NOODLES!"

---

From the back of the shop came a sound like slippers shuffling across a warm floor. *Shff. Shff. Shff.*

And out came the oldest, plumpest, most marvelously wrinkled noodle anyone had ever seen.

She was a single, enormous strand of udon coiled into the shape of a tiny round grandmother, soft and pale and pillowy, with a dusting of flour on her shoulders like the first gentle snow. Her eyes were two warm sesame seeds. She wore a little apron tied in a bow, and she moved as slowly as honey dripping off a spoon.

"Well, well," she said, her voice as soft as steam. "Welcome, little ones, to my shop. I am Grandmother Koshi — and *koshi*, you know, is the springy, chewy bounce a good noodle has when you take your time with it." She smiled. "I have been making udon here for one hundred and twelve years."

"One hundred and TWELVE?" gasped Mero Melonpan, waking up just long enough to be amazed before nearly dozing off again. "I can't even stay awake for one hundred and twelve *seconds*."

"That's because the best dreams are slow," Grandmother Koshi chuckled. "Just like the best noodles." She set a great steaming pot bubbling on the stove. *Bloop. Bloop. Blooop.* "Now. Who is hungry?"

"ME!" Rin shot his stubby legs in the air. "And I'd like mine FAST, please, because there's a race, and I'm going to win it!"

Grandmother Koshi tilted her flour-dusted head. "A *race*?" she said, as if Rin had said something delightfully silly, like *square soup* or *upside-down rain*. "With my udon? Oh, my dear little bubbling bowl. Nobody has ever raced my udon. My udon is for *slowing down*."

"Well, somebody's about to try!" said Rin, and steam shot out of both ears at once.

---

So Grandmother Koshi made them each a bowl, slowly, slowly, lifting the thick white noodles with two wooden chopsticks the size of broomsticks. The udon glistened. They were as fat as garden hoses and as soft as clouds, swimming in a broth so golden it looked like melted afternoon sunshine.

"Ready..." said Rin, vibrating so hard his egg-cheek jiggled. "Set..."

"Oh dear," murmured Tama.

"GO!"

Rin attacked his bowl like a tiny, delicious tornado. *SLOOOORP! SLUUUURP! SHLOOORP!* He sucked up noodle after noodle, faster and faster, broth flying everywhere, corn freckle bouncing, not tasting a single thing — just *go go GO!*

"I'M WINNING!" he tried to shout, except his whole mouth was a traffic jam of fat slippery noodles, so it came out as "MM HMMING!" Broth splashed up his nose. A noodle wrapped around his ear like a cozy scarf. Another one slapped him gently across the egg-cheek as if to say *slow down, friend.*

And then — *hic!* — Rin got the hiccups.

A great big bubbly hiccup, the kind that makes your whole broth ripple. *HIC!* And another. *HIC!* Each hiccup launched a single noodle straight up into the air, where it hung for a moment like a question mark before flopping back down with a wet *splat*.

"*HIC* — I'm — *HIC* — fine — *HIC* —" Rin insisted, now thoroughly tangled in his own dinner, a noodle on his head, a noodle on his nose, looking less like a champion and more like a soup-monster made of yarn.

Tama, Maru, and a freshly-woken Mero all stared.

Then they burst out laughing.

"You look like a mop that fell in a soup!" giggled Tama, who almost never giggled.

"A *very enthusiastic* mop," agreed Maru, deadpan, which somehow made it ten times funnier.

Rin tried to bow grandly, the way a champion should — and a noodle slid off his head and *plopped* directly into Tama's bowl with a polite little splash, as if it had simply decided it liked Tama's company better.

"Oh!" said Tama, blinking down at it. "I... think your noodle just moved in with me."

"It can stay," sighed Rin, who was now wearing broth like a hat. "*Hic.* It's clearly happier there."

Even Mero laughed so hard a sparkle of crumbs puffed off his crackly crust, and the whole table dissolved into warm, steamy giggles, the kind that fog up a window and make a cold night feel like a hug.

---

Grandmother Koshi shuffled over and, with one gentle wooden chopstick, lifted the noodle-scarf off Rin's ear. "There, there, little kettle," she said warmly. "You went so fast you forgot the most important part."

"*HIC* — winning?" said Rin.

"*Tasting*," said Grandmother Koshi.

She shuffled to her great pot and lifted, with her broomstick chopsticks, a single fresh noodle, letting it dangle and swing gently in the lantern light, glistening like a strand of warm pearl. The whole table went quiet to watch it. Even the steam seemed to slow down to look.

"Watch how it falls," she said softly, lowering it into Rin's bowl. *Plip.* "A noodle that has been loved does not splash. It settles. It sighs. It waits for you."

Just one. Thick and white and shining.

"Try this," she said. "But not fast. Slow. Take just this one noodle. Feel its *koshi* — its bounce. Let the broth say hello. And then..." she winked a sesame-seed eye, "...listen to it."

Rin's hiccups settled. *Hic... hic... hh.* He looked at the one fat noodle. He looked at his friends, who had stopped racing entirely and were simply sitting, warm and steamy and together. And slowly — very slowly, which felt strange and wobbly and new — Rin lifted the noodle and gave it the tiniest, gentlest slurp.

*Mmmmmm.*

Oh.

*Oh.*

It was *chewy*. It bounced back against him like a little hug. The broth was deep and golden and tasted of patience and wheat fields and a hundred and twelve years of someone caring. It tasted, Rin realized, like the warmest, bubbliest feeling of all — and he had nearly swallowed it whole without ever knowing it was there.

"I..." Rin's eyes went shiny. A happy bit of steam curled up, slow and soft this time. "I couldn't taste anything when I was racing. I was so busy *winning* I forgot to *enjoy*."

"That," said Grandmother Koshi, patting his bowl, "is the secret of udon. The chewiest, warmest noodle is best when you take your sweet, slow time. **Yukkuri** (yook-koo-ree) — slowly, taking it easy — that is how you taste the good stuff."

---

So they started again — but this time there was no race at all.

This time they slurped slowly, together, passing the steam around, *yukkuri yukkuri*. Tama shared her extra napkins, which nobody ended up needing, because nobody splashed. Maru whisked the broth in tiny calm circles and announced, between sips, that "a noodle, like a friend, is best when you give it your whole attention." Mero stayed awake for a whole, wondrous bowl, marveling at how each noodle was "crackle on the outside of a *dream*."

And Rin — Rin slurped one noodle at a time, savoring every bouncy bite, his steam puffing soft and content instead of frantic and fast. He found, to his great surprise, that slow slurping had its own little music: a gentle *mmm* on each bite, a happy sigh between, the soft clink of chopsticks. It was, he decided, the very best song he'd ever heard, and it had been hiding inside his hurry the whole time.

"You know what?" Rin said, settling back, his bowl warm in his stubby hands. "I think I just lost the race. And I think it's the best thing I've ever lost."

"Then you win the *real* prize," said Grandmother Koshi, ladling him a little more broth. "A second bowl, enjoyed slow."

And nobody — not one of them — argued with that.

Outside, Kei the kei-truck honked softly through the window. "Putt-putt... room for one more bowl?"

"Always," said Rin, scooting over. "But you have to promise to eat it *yukkuri*."

"Slowly," beeped Kei happily, springs going *boing*. "I can do slow."

And in the warm steamy cottage on the green misty hill, four friends and one little truck and one hundred-and-twelve-year-old grandmother noodle slurped their udon as slowly as falling snow, laughing every time Rin's hiccups launched one more noodle into the air — *splat!* — just for the joy of it.

---

## Goodnight Blessing
May your day, little one, never move so fast that you forget to taste the good stuff. May you find a warm bowl, a slow moment, and a friend to share it with. And when the steam rises soft and the noodles bounce gentle, may you remember: the best things are best taken *yukkuri* — slowly, sweetly, one happy bite at a time. Snuggle down now, warm as broth and cozy as a cottage in the mist. Goodnight.
