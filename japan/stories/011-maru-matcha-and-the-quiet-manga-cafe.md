# Story 11: Maru Matcha and the Quiet Manga Café

**Main Character:** Maru Matcha
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A hushed, cozy manga café with shelves to the ceiling
**Theme:** Quiet places hold loud-bright imaginations
**Japanese Spotlight:** **manga** (mahn-gah) — comic book

---

The bell above the door went *ting* — and then, very politely, hushed itself, as if even the bell understood the rules here. Maru Matcha glided inside on her tiny bowl-feet, the frothy cream swirl on top of her head wobbling like soft-serve hair, her little bamboo whisk tucked behind one ear like a wand. She breathed in. The air smelled of warm paper, old ink, and somebody's cocoa.

"Ahhh," she whispered to nobody, in the way calm friends do. "This place is the *coziest*."

It was the Quiet Manga Café — shelves and shelves of **manga** (mahn-gah) — comic books — stacked all the way up to a ceiling so high you'd need a ladder, a balloon, and a deep breath to reach the top. There were beanbags shaped like dumplings. There were lamps that glowed butter-gold. And there, in the corner, half-hidden behind a tower of books that wobbled like a very nervous tower, was a brand-new friend with the biggest worried eyes Maru had ever seen.

"Hello," Maru said softly. "You look like you're trying very hard to disappear."

---

The new friend squeaked and nearly fell off the dumpling beanbag.

It was a little steamed bun, fluffy and pale, with rosy cheeks and trembly paws. "I — I'm Yomi," the bun whispered. "I came to read my first manga. But there are SO many. There are nine hundred and eleventy-thousand of them. And what if I pick wrong? What if everyone else already knows the *right* one and I pick a silly one and the whole café turns around and *looks* at me?"

Maru blinked her calm half-moon eyes. Then she sat down beside Yomi on a beanbag shaped like a fat little daifuku.

"Whisk slow," she said gently. "Feel calm. There is no wrong shelf, only your shelf. Let's find it together."

"T-together?" Yomi's cheeks went pinker than a pickled plum. "But — but you must already know which one is the BEST one. You're so calm. Calm people always know."

Maru gave a small, secret smile — the kind that crinkles at the corners. "Oh, Yomi. Calm doesn't mean I know everything. Calm just means I'm not in a hurry to be wrong." She tapped her whisk thoughtfully against her bowl-head, which made a soft *bonk*. "Now. Let's go on a tiny, quiet adventure. The kind where the only loud thing is your imagination."

And so the two of them set off down the first aisle, walking on tip-toe because the café asked for **shizuka** (shee-zoo-kah) — quiet — and they both rather liked obeying.

---

The first shelf was tall and red and full of adventure manga — heroes leaping over volcanoes, ninjas hiding in noodle bowls, dragons who were secretly just very large, very polite cats. Maru pulled one down with a soft grunt and held it open.

"WHOOSH!" she whispered, which is a very hard thing to do, whispering a whoosh, but she managed. "Look. A hero who can punch a mountain. Does your heart go *bonk-bonk-bonk* fast and excited?"

Yomi peeked. The hero on the page had hair shaped like a flame and a grin shaped like a banana. "It's very... loud," Yomi admitted. "My heart went *bonk* once and then hid under the blanket."

"Then this is not your shelf," Maru said cheerfully, and slid it back. "And that's wonderful! Now we know one more thing about you." She made a tiny mark in the air with her whisk, as if ticking a box. "Whisk slow. We're just getting started."

Yomi felt something loosen in his chest, like the first knot in a too-tight scarf. *Nobody had said* that's wonderful *about a wrong answer before.*

"Wait," he whispered, hurrying after her. "You're not... cross? I made you take the book down and put it back and it was a no?"

Maru paused beside a lamp that glowed like a small captured sunset. "Oh, Yomi," she said, and her voice was as soft as steam off a teacup. "A 'no' is not a mistake. A 'no' is just a 'no thank you' wearing a shy coat. Every time you say it, you walk one step closer to your 'yes.' That's not a wrong turn — that's the whole map." She tapped his fluffy cheek with the flat of her whisk, very gently. "Now. Aisle two. Mind the wobbly tower."

They tip-toed past the wobbly tower, which wobbled at them in a friendly way but, thankfully, stayed standing.

---

The second shelf was green and curly and smelled faintly of green tea — which made Maru feel quite at home — and it was stuffed with funny manga. The covers were full of round little animals falling into puddings and sneezing so hard their hats flew off.

Maru opened one. On the very first page, a tiny cat tried to eat a dumpling that was bigger than its entire head, and the dumpling bonked it gently onto its bottom.

Yomi made a sound.

It was a small sound. A squeaky, surprised, *hic* of a sound. And then it grew — into a giggle, a real one, the kind that sneaks out before you can be embarrassed about it. He slapped both paws over his mouth.

"Sorry!" he gasped. "I laughed! In the quiet place! Everyone will—"

But the café did not turn and stare. In fact, from two aisles over, somebody *else* giggled. And from the cozy corner, a sleepy melon-bread person murmured, "Mmm, that's a good one," without even opening his eyes.

Maru's half-moon eyes curved up like two happy little hammocks. "You see?" she whispered. "A quiet café isn't a place with no feelings. It's a place where everyone's feelings get to be *big* on the inside, where it's safe. That giggle you just made? That was your imagination turning on its little light."

Yomi looked at the funny manga in his paws. He didn't want to put it down. But —

"...But what if funny isn't *enough*?" he whispered. "What if a first manga is supposed to be *important*?"

---

Before Maru could answer, there came a small sound from above — a *creak*, a *teeter*, and then a soft avalanche of paper. The wobbly tower from aisle one had finally, gently, decided to lie down. Books slid off it in a slow, lazy waterfall and went *flump, flump, flump* onto the dumpling beanbags, where they landed without hurting a soul.

Yomi gasped and froze, certain that NOW, surely now, the whole café would turn and frown at the mess.

Instead, Maru began — very quietly — to laugh. Not a mean laugh. A warm, fizzy one, like bubbles rising in green tea. "Even the shelf," she whispered, wiping a happy crinkle from the corner of her eye, "couldn't decide which book it loved best, so it brought you *all* of them."

And Yomi, despite everything, laughed too. Then, together, on tip-toe, they gathered the fallen manga into a tidy pile, and Yomi noticed something: picking the books *up* wasn't scary at all. It was rather nice, actually — holding each one, feeling its weight, peeking at its cover. By the time the pile was neat again, his paws weren't trembling one little bit.

"There," Maru murmured. "See? Sometimes the only way past being scared of all the choices is to simply *hold* a few of them. They're only paper and ink and somebody's daydream. They can't bite." She paused. "Well. The dragon-cat one might *pretend* to."

---

Maru thought about this seriously, the way she thought about everything — slowly, and with great kindness. She led Yomi to the very back of the café, where the tallest shelf of all stood like a quiet wooden mountain. At its foot was a single soft lamp and two cushions, as if the shelf had been waiting for exactly two friends.

"Sit," she said. "And I'll tell you a tiny secret about story-shelves."

Yomi sat. The cushion went *foomf*.

"Everybody," Maru began, "has a different shelf inside them. Rin Ramen — you'll meet him someday — his shelf is full of big steamy feelings and dramatic flavor. Kori the shaved ice? His shelf is all sparkle and sunglasses and *meltdowns*." She chuckled. "Mero Melonpan's shelf is mostly naps with a little dream tucked in. And mine—" she touched her cream swirl, "—mine is calm proverbs and warm tea and quiet rainy afternoons."

"What about a *right* shelf?" Yomi asked. "The one everybody's supposed to have?"

"There isn't one." Maru said it so simply that it landed like a feather. "That's the magic. If everyone liked the same manga, the shelves would only be one book tall. Look how *tall* these are." She gestured up, up, up, to where the books disappeared into the warm gold gloom. "All this height? That's because everyone's different. Your shelf doesn't have to match. It just has to be *yours*."

Yomi was quiet for a moment. Then, in a voice so small Maru had to lean her whole bowl-head in to hear it, he said: "I think... I think I like the funny one. The cat and the dumpling. I want to know if the cat ever eats it."

"Then," said Maru, with the deep satisfaction of a whisk that has whisked exactly the right amount, "we have found your shelf."

She rose and led him to a low table where the café kept a pot of warm **matcha** (mah-chah) — whisked green tea — just for readers. With a few easy flicks of her bamboo whisk — *shik, shik, shik* — she frothed two tiny cups until they wore little hats of foam. "A story is best with something warm to hold," she said. "That's the last rule of the Quiet Manga Café, and it's my favorite one." She handed him a cup. "Whisk slow. Read slower. There's no rush at all."

Yomi held the warm cup in both paws and felt, for the very first time all afternoon, completely and entirely *unhurried*. The worry that had been buzzing in his chest like a trapped firefly went quiet, settled, and finally tucked its little wings away.

---

They padded back to the green-and-curly funny aisle. But on the way, Yomi stopped. He looked up at the volcano-hero shelf, and the dragon-cat shelf, and the spooky-but-cute ghost shelf, and the one about a girl who fixed flying bicycles.

"Maru?" he said. "Now that I'm not so scared... I think I might like to *peek* at the others too. Someday. Just to see."

Maru's smile bloomed wide and warm. "*That's* the most wonderful thing you've said yet. A shelf isn't a cage, Yomi. It's a garden. You can plant new things in it your whole life long." She handed him the funny manga, then — after a wink — tucked a second one under his arm: the one about the flying-bicycle girl. "One for tonight. One for *someday soon*."

Yomi hugged them both to his fluffy chest. He didn't feel like disappearing anymore. He felt, instead, like the warm gold lamp in the corner — small, and steady, and glowing on the inside.

They curled up together on the dumpling beanbags. Outside, the evening went soft and blue. Inside, two friends read in the hush, and every now and then a giggle escaped — Yomi's, then Maru's, then a sleepy one from the melon-bread in the corner — little bursts of bright, quiet joy, popping up all over the café like fireflies who'd found exactly the right place to glow.

And if you'd peeked through the window just then, you'd have seen the truth Maru already knew: that the quietest room in the whole town was, on the inside, the loudest and most colorful place of all.

## Goodnight Blessing
May your evening be soft as a cream swirl and your imagination as tall as a shelf that touches the ceiling. May you never be afraid to pick the silly one, the funny one, the *your* one — for there is no wrong story, only the story that is yours. Whisk your worries slow, little one, until they go calm and warm and quiet. Tonight, let your dreams be a cozy café where every page turns to something kind. Snuggle in, breathe out, and let the lamps go gold. Goodnight.
