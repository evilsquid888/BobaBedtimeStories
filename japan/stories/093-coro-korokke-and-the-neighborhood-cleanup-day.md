# Story 93: Coro Korokke and the Neighborhood Clean-Up Day

**Main Character:** Coro the Korokke
**Guest Characters:** Kori Kakigori, Pock Ramune, Kei the kei-truck; a lane full of treat-folk neighbors
**Setting:** A neighborhood street on community clean-up morning
**Theme:** A clean, kind town is everyone's job
**Japanese Spotlight:** **kirei ni suru** (kee-RAY nee soo-roo) — to make clean / tidy

---

Coro the Korokke liked to doze until breakfast smelled like something. Not today.

He popped awake with a pfft of steam, which only happens when he's nervous.

He pressed his soft potato face to the window. Oh dear. Oh crumbs.

A paper cup sat on the lucky-cat statue's raised paw like a teeny hat.

The windy night had also tipped the leaf baskets and stuffed three napkins into a hedge.

"Somebody should **kirei ni suru** (kee-RAY nee soo-roo) — make it clean," whispered Coro. "Somebody big."

He looked at his broom. He looked at his tiny arms. He went out anyway.

---

Swish. Swish. Brooms are gossips. A window slid open above him.

"WHO is being COOL and BUSY at this UNCIVILIZED hour?" boomed Kori Kakigori, sunglasses already on.

"Just me," squeaked Coro. "Sorry. The lucky cat is wearing a hat."

Kori gasped so hard his milk swirl wobbled. "A fashion emergency AND a tidiness emergency. I'm IN."

He whooshed down the drainpipe and landed in a slushy splat. "I work fast, sweetie. Or I puddle."

Pock Ramune zipped past, pop-pop-pop. "Is it a party? I LOVE parties!"

"It's a clean-up," said Coro.

"Even better!" Pock zoomed up a lamppost and popped a napkin loose with one bubble.

---

Kei the kei-truck putt-putted around the corner, flatbed empty and hopeful. "What's MY job?"

"Hauler!" said Coro, and his steam settled into a steady simmer. "Pock, high places. Kori, the long sidewalk."

"PUTT-PUTT, LET'S GO!" beeped Kei, headlights flashing.

Kori swept so fast the sidewalk frosted. Leaves, napkins and petals rolled up before his broom.

The pile became a lump. The lump became a ball. The ball became a BOULDER.

"Darling," said Kori, admiring it, "I am the fastest cleaner in the WORLD."

The boulder wobbled. The street, you see, was ever so slightly a hill.

---

Off it went, rumbling downhill, gobbling every leaf pile on the way and growing fatter.

"MY BOULDER!" wailed Kori, and whooshed after it, melting a glittery trail.

Pock zipped ahead and fired bubbles at it. Pop! Pop! The bubbles bounced right off.

Kori planted himself in front, arms wide. The ball rolled him flat as a strawberry pancake.

"I'm fine," said Kori from the pavement. "Still cool. Flatter. But cool."

Kei raced alongside, beeping, but the ball swerved off her bumper and bounded toward the lucky cat.

Coro stood frozen in the road with his tiny broom. His steam whistled like a kettle.

---

Then Coro did a thing he had never done. He shouted.

"EVERYBODY! Brooms OUT!"

Windows flew open down the lane. A pomelo auntie. A tofu uncle. A family of tiny dumplings.

Treat-folk poured out with brooms and mops and one very long spoon.

They lined up across the street, bristles forward, like a very bristly fence.

The boulder hit the brooms, squashed, bounced, and rolled back UPHILL.

Straight up Kei's ramp, into her flatbed. FLUMP.

"Oh," said Coro softly. "It was never a job for somebody big. It's everybody's job."

---

The sun came up over a street that sparkled. The hedge looked freshly combed.

Pock popped the paper cup off the lucky cat's paw. The cat looked much more dignified.

Kori peeled himself off the pavement and reshaped his peak. "Still the fastest, sweeties."

Kei putt-putted off to the recycling with the boulder, beeping proudly the whole way.

Everyone flopped down in the clean street and shared warm rice balls and tea.

Coro sat in the middle, a soft puff of happy steam rising off his golden head.

"Crispy outside," he said, "soft heart inside." And the lucky cat waved.
