# Story 93: Coro Korokke and the Neighborhood Clean-Up Day

**Main Character:** Coro the Korokke
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A neighborhood street on community clean-up morning
**Theme:** A clean, kind town is everyone's job
**Japanese Spotlight:** きれいにする (kirei ni suru) = to make clean / tidy

---

Coro the Korokke woke up before the sun, which was very unusual, because Coro was the kind of crispy golden croquette who liked to sleep until the smell of breakfast tickled his breaded nose. But this morning he had popped awake all at once — *pfft!* — with a tiny puff of steam shooting out the top of his head, the way it always did when he was nervous.

"Oh dear," whispered Coro, looking out the window. "Oh dear, oh dear, oh *crumbs*."

The whole street was a mess.

Not a scary mess. A *silly* mess. A windy night had tipped over the leaf baskets, scattered cherry blossoms into soggy piles, blown three lonely napkins into a hedge, and somehow — *somehow* — parked a runaway paper cup right on top of the lucky-cat statue's raised paw, like a teeny hat.

Coro pressed his soft potato face to the glass. His tummy did a worried little flip.

"Somebody," he said quietly, "should *kirei ni suru*." That meant **きれいにする** (*kirei ni suru*) = to make clean and tidy. "Somebody should make it sparkle again."

Then he gulped. "But I'm only a small croquette. Crispy outside, soft heart inside. Who would listen to *me*?"

A bigger puff of steam went *poof* off his head.

Still — the lucky cat looked so *ridiculous* in its paper-cup hat that Coro couldn't leave it. So he grabbed his little broom, tiptoed outside into the gray-blue morning, and got to work all by himself.

*Swish. Swish. Swish.*

He swept one leaf. Then another. Then a whole tiny pile.

And here is the funny thing about sweeping, dear reader: it is almost impossible to do *quietly*. Brooms are gossips. They go *swish-swish* and the leaves go *crinkle-crinkle*, and pretty soon a window slid open above Coro's head.

"WHO is being COOL and BUSY at this UNCIVILIZED hour?" boomed a dramatic voice.

It was Kori Kakigori, the towering mountain of strawberry shaved ice, leaning out the window with her sunglasses already on even though the sun wasn't up yet. A bead of melt rolled off her ruby-syrup peak.

"It's just me," squeaked Coro, steam puffing. "Sorry to wake you. I was, um, making the street clean. *Kirei ni suru.* It got messy in the wind, and—" He held up the soggy paper cup. "—and the lucky cat is wearing a hat."

Kori gasped so hard her condensed-milk swirl wobbled. "The lucky cat is wearing a HAT? That is a fashion EMERGENCY, sweetie, and *also* a tidiness emergency, and I cannot allow either!" She slid down the drainpipe in one cool *whoosh*. "I'm IN. But I work *fast*, because if I stand still I melt, and a puddle does NOT help clean-up, darling."

"Two of us!" said Coro, and a happy little steam-puff replaced his nervous one.

Two became three in about thirty seconds, because Pock Ramune the soda bottle came zipping past leaving a trail of bubbles. "What's happening — *pop!* — is it a party? — *pop!* — I LOVE parties!"

"It's a clean-up," said Coro.

"Even better! — *pop!* — I'm fizzy, I'm fast, I'll do the spots nobody can reach!" And he zoomed up a lamppost to flick a stuck napkin loose with one bubbly *pop!*

Now, you might think one small worried croquette could never wake a whole street. But kindness, it turns out, is even gossipier than a broom.

By the time the sky turned the soft pink of Momo Mochi's cheeks, the whole cast had tumbled out of doors, still rubbing sleep from their eyes.

Momo Mochi came bouncing down the lane, stretchy arms already open. "A clean-up? Oh, I LOVE a clean-up! *Squish together, stay together!*" She gave Coro such an enormous squishy hug that a puff of potato-starch dust *poofed* off her and made him sneeze, which made *her* giggle, which made *everyone* giggle.

Rin Ramen rolled up on his stubby legs, steaming gently. "Everything's better warm and bubbly! I'll boil up tea for the workers — and snacks. Cleaning is hungry work!"

Tama Onigiri patted the little backpack tied to her nori sash. "I packed a little extra, just in case. Riceballs for everyone! You can't tidy on an empty tummy."

Maru Matcha drifted in last, calm as a cloud, her tiny bamboo whisk tucked behind one ear. She looked at the messy street, then at the crowd of excited friends about to all start shouting at once, and she lifted her whisk.

"Whisk slow," she said softly. "Feel calm. A messy street is just a tidy street that hasn't been hugged yet." She tapped her whisk on her palm. "Coro. You started this. *You* should say how we do it."

Every single face turned to Coro.

The steam shot *straight up* off his head like a kettle. "M-me? Oh, no, I'm not really a leader, I'm just a—"

"You're just the one who got up before the sun and started," said Maru gently. "That's exactly what a leader is."

Coro looked around at all his friends, blinking and ready and *waiting for him*. His soft potato heart went warm all the way through. He took a deep breath, and the trembly steam settled into a steady little simmer.

"Okay," he said. "Okay! Here's the plan. We split into teams."

And — oh, dear reader, you should have *seen* him. Humble little Coro, who an hour ago was too shy to ask anyone for help, now had a plan like a proper general of niceness.

"Pock, you do the high places — lampposts, hedges, the lucky cat's hat. Kori, you sweep the long sidewalk *fast*, before you melt. Momo, you hug all the loose leaves into one big pile so they don't blow away again."

"Hug the LEAVES?" said Momo, delighted. "That's the best job I've ever been given!"

"Rin, snack-and-tea station. Tama, riceball patrol. And Maru..." Coro hesitated. "...you keep us calm if we get in a muddle."

"I shall whisk away all muddles," said Maru, with the tiniest smile.

"And me?" asked a teeny eager voice. It was Kei, the butter-yellow kei-truck, bouncing on his springs at the corner. "Putt-putt, what's MY job? I have a whole flatbed! I can carry away the rubbish so it goes where rubbish goes!"

"PERFECT," said Coro. "Kei, you're our hauler. Putt-putt to the recycling and back."

"PUTT-PUTT, LET'S GO!" beeped Kei, so excited his headlights flashed.

And the clean-up *whooshed* into a glorious, giggly whirl.

Pock zipped up high — *pop! pop! pop!* — knocking the paper-cup hat clean off the lucky cat, who (if you ask Coro) looked *much* more dignified without it. Kori swept so fast she left a frosty shine on the sidewalk, calling "STAY COOL, sweeties — but not TOO cool!" while a little melt-trail followed her like glitter. Momo squished leaf-piles into neat fluffy hills and could not resist diving into one, which scattered it everywhere, which meant she had to squish it again, which she did not mind one bit.

There was, of course, one moment of muddle.

Tako and Yaki, the giggly takoyaki twins, had wobbled over to "help," and they decided the cleanest possible thing to do would be to roll *all* the rubbish into one ENORMOUS ball.

"Roll it once—" said Tako.

"—it comes as ONE!" finished Yaki.

And they made a rubbish-ball the size of a beach ball, which then — naturally — got away from them and went rumbling down the little slope of the street, right toward Rin's tea station.

"AAH! My BUBBLY TEA!" cried Rin, steam shooting out of his bowl in alarm.

Everyone froze. Coro's heart leapt into his crispy throat.

But Maru Matcha simply stepped sideways, lifted her bamboo whisk, and gave the runaway rubbish-ball one calm, perfectly-timed *tap*. It wobbled, slowed, tipped — and rolled gently, *gently*, straight up the ramp into Kei's open flatbed with a soft *flump*.

"Whisk slow," said Maru. "Feel calm."

There was a beat of silence.

Then the whole street *cheered* so loudly that windows opened up and down the lane, and even more neighbors poked their heads out — and seeing all the sparkly fun, they grabbed brooms too. An old auntie pomelo. A wobbly tofu grandpa. A whole family of tiny dango on a stick. *Everyone.*

Because that is the secret of a clean-up, and it is the secret Coro learned that morning: tidying isn't a chore you do *alone* and *grumbling*. It's a thing the whole town does *together*, laughing, until the work disappears into the fun.

The sun rose all the way up. And the street — oh, the street *sparkled*. The sidewalk shone. The leaf-piles sat in tidy bags. The lucky cat gleamed, hatless and proud. Even the hedge that had eaten the three napkins looked freshly combed.

Coro stood in the middle of it all, completely amazed, a soft puff of *happy* steam rising off his golden head.

"We did it," he whispered. "We made it *kirei*. We made it clean."

"*You* did it," said Momo, scooping him into a group squish so big it pulled in Kori and Rin and Tama and Maru and zippy Pock all at once. "You woke up before the sun and started — and starting is the bravest part!"

"Crispy outside," said Coro, his voice wobbly with joy, "soft heart inside."

And then — because all that cleaning really *was* hungry work — they sat down right in the sparkling street, and Tama passed out riceballs, and Rin poured warm bubbly tea, and they watched their gleaming neighborhood shine in the morning light, every single one of them proud as a polished little lucky-cat paw.

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, the world gets a tiny bit brighter every time someone bends to pick up one small thing, the way Coro did before the sun was even awake. You don't have to be big to start, and you never have to finish alone — kindness is wonderfully catching, just like a giggle. So rest your sleepy head, knowing that tomorrow there will be small good things you can do, and friends who will *whoosh* in to help. Tonight, let the world be tidy and calm and softly shining all around you. Snuggle in, breathe slow like Maru's whisk, and feel how cozy a clean, kind little corner of the world can be. Goodnight.
