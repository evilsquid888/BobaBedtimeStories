# Story 29: The Dango Three and the Portable-Shrine Parade

**Main Character:** The Dango Three (Pinku, Shiro, Cha)
**Guest Characters:** Daikon-san, a round old radish-lantern; the carved golden bird on the shrine
**Setting:** A festival street parade carrying a mikoshi portable shrine
**Theme:** Everyone carries a little of the load
**Japanese Spotlight:** **omikoshi** (oh-mee-koh-shee) — portable shrine
**Cameo:** Totoro (My Neighbor Totoro) — lifts the whole shrine like a teacup and roars a wasshoi made of wind, so nobody carries anything, which is the wrong amount; walk-on: Soot Sprites (My Neighbor Totoro)

---
The festival street glowed with egg-yolk lanterns and smelled of grilled corn.

Right in the middle wobbled three little dumplings on one stick.

"Today," announced Pinku, the bold pink one on top, "is the BIGGEST day of the year!"

"Are you SURE it's not the scariest?" squeaked Shiro, the shy white one in the middle.

"It's both," said Cha, the clever green one at the bottom. "Which makes it interesting."

They pivoted like a tiny carousel, faster and faster, and flopped dizzy into a snack stack.

And there, behind the snacks, it waited. The **omikoshi** (oh-mee-koh-shee) — portable shrine.

---

A tiny golden shrine with a curved roof, dangling bells, and a glittering carved bird.

"We HAVE to carry it!" gasped Pinku.

"Three pieces, four corners," whispered Shiro. "The math doesn't math!"

"The math," said Cha, "is rude."

A round old radish-lantern bobbed over. "Heavier than it looks. Strong friends have sat right down."

"We're not strong friends," said Pinku. "We're a STICK of friends. That's different."

"The secret," said Daikon-san, "is that it's never carried by lifting." And off he floated, humming.

---

Pinku grabbed front-left. Shiro hugged front-right. Cha wrapped round back-left. Back-right stood terribly empty.

"ONE, TWO, THREE!" They heaved. The omikoshi sat there like a golden rock.

"Lever!" said Cha. They wedged their stick under a pole, and Pinku jumped on the end.

The stick sprang. Three dango shot skyward and landed in a bucket of grilled corn.

"Push it!" said Pinku, corn on her head. They shoved. The shrine tipped. The bird teetered.

It tipped back with a tiny offended chirp. The dango flopped onto the cobbles, panting.

---

THUMP. Every lantern swung. THUMP. Into the lane plopped a huge round grey forest spirit, grinning.

Totoro, a leaf on his head, hooked one claw under the shrine and lifted it like a teacup.

The dango dangled from their pole, feet kicking. "WE'RE FLYING," squeaked Shiro. "I HATE IT."

Totoro took an enormous breath and roared. The roar was pure wind, and it sounded like WASSHOI.

Lanterns streamed sideways. "We're carrying zero," said Cha, hair flat. "Zero is the wrong amount."

Totoro set the shrine down gently, sat in the lane, and yawned a rumble that rattled the bells.

---

"Thank you, big fluffy sir," said Pinku, "but we want to carry our own piece."

Then a sticky, joyful sound floated down the street. "Wasshoi! Wasshoi! WASSHOI!"

Around the corner bounced a parade of little treats, one at each corner of a small shrine.

"They aren't lifting harder than us," said Cha, narrowing his eyes. "They lift at the same moment."

---

Behind the parade scurried a swarm of fuzzy black fluffballs with big white eyes, chittering.

Together the soot sprites carried one golden shrine bead, dropped in the fuss, and popped it back on.

"Mind if I take that corner?" said a warm, crackly voice.

Daikon-san settled over the empty pole. "I'm old and round. But I can carry a little."

Far down the street the **taiko** (tie-koh) — big drum — boomed. BOOM.

"Same moment," breathed Shiro, and his wobble went still. BOOM.

"WASSHOI!" they cried together, and lifted. The shrine rose like it had been waiting to be asked.

---

"IT'S UP AND I'M HELPING AND THE BIRD IS STILL ON!" shrieked Shiro happily.

Down the street they swayed, chanting. Pinku pushed harder, to show off. The shrine tipped and giggled.

"Showing off tips it," called Cha. "A little, plus a little—"

"—plus a little, equals a LOT," finished Shiro. "That's the only math I like!"

---

They set it down together, on the count of the chant. The carved bird gave one golden chirp.

"Three on a stick," said Pinku.

"Plus one kind lantern," said Shiro.

"Stuck together for good," finished Cha, and the whole street breathed one warm, sleepy sigh.
