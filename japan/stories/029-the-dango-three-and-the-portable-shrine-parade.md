# Story 29: The Dango Three and the Portable-Shrine Parade

**Main Character:** The Dango Three (Pinku, Shiro, Cha)
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A festival street parade carrying a mikoshi portable shrine
**Theme:** Everyone carries a little of the load
**Japanese Spotlight:** おみこし (omikoshi) = portable shrine

---

The festival street was lined with paper lanterns the color of warm egg yolks, and the whole town smelled like grilled corn, sweet syrup, and adventure. Right in the middle of it all sat three little dumplings on one stick, wobbling with excitement.

"Today," announced Pinku, the bold pink one on top, "is the BIGGEST day of the year!"

"Is it?" squeaked Shiro, the shy white one in the middle, who was already a little dizzy from all the noise. "Are you SURE it's not the scariest day of the year?"

"It's both," said Cha, the clever green one at the bottom, in his calm matcha voice. "Which, statistically, makes it interesting."

The three dango pivoted in a circle like a tiny carousel — Pinku, Shiro, Cha, Pinku, Shiro, Cha — until they were so dizzy they had to flop sideways against a stack of festival snacks. And that, dear reader, was when they spotted it.

The **おみこし** (omikoshi) = portable shrine.

---

It was the most beautiful thing they had ever seen. A tiny golden shrine no bigger than a lunchbox, with a curved roof, dangling bells, and a little carved bird on top that glittered when the lanterns flickered. It sat on four polished poles, waiting to be lifted and carried through the streets so that all the good luck inside could be shared with the whole town.

"We HAVE to carry it!" Pinku gasped.

"We absolutely cannot," Shiro whispered. "We have three pieces and four corners. The math doesn't math!"

"The math," said Cha thoughtfully, "is rude."

A round old radish-lantern bobbed over to them, glowing softly. "Little dango," it said in a crackly, kind voice, "the omikoshi needs carriers. But I must warn you — it is heavy. Heavier than it looks. Many strong friends have tried to lift it alone, and..." The lantern dimmed dramatically. "...they sat right back down."

Pinku puffed up. "We are not strong friends," she said. "We are a STICK of friends. That's different."

The lantern chuckled and dimmed its flame in a little bow. "Then take your places. The parade begins when the **太鼓** (taiko) = big drum sounds three times. And remember the secret of the omikoshi: it is never carried by lifting. It is carried by chanting."

"By WHAT?" said all three dango at once.

But the lantern had already floated away to light the next corner, humming to itself, leaving the three little dumplings staring up at a shrine that was, indeed, looking heavier by the second.

---

They scooted up to the omikoshi and took their places. Pinku grabbed the front-left pole. Shiro, trembling, hugged the front-right. Cha wrapped himself around the back-left.

And the back-right pole stood completely, terribly empty.

"See?" Shiro wailed softly. "Three on a stick, but FOUR on a shrine! We're going to tip it over and the little carved bird will fall off and the whole town's luck will roll into a drain and it'll be ALL OUR FAULT—"

"Shiro," said Cha gently. "Breathe like Maru taught us. Whisk slow."

Shiro took a wobbly breath. "Whisk... slow," he repeated. He felt one tiny bit better. Maybe one and a half.

"Okay, on three!" Pinku shouted. "We lift the whole thing together! ONE, TWO—"

"THREE!" they cried, and they HEAVED.

The omikoshi did not move.

Not one wiggle. Not one wobble. It just sat there, golden and serene, as if to say, *nice try, little snacks.*

Pinku strained until her pink cheeks went a deeper pink. Shiro pushed until tiny worried lines crinkled across his white forehead. Cha leaned in with all his clever green might. And still — nothing.

They flopped back against the cobblestones, panting.

"It's impossible," huffed Pinku, and for the first time all day, the bold pink one sounded almost quiet.

---

That was when a small, sticky, joyful sound floated down the street.

"Wasshoi! Wasshoi! WASSHOI!"

Around the corner came a parade of festival treats, each one carrying a corner of a different little shrine — a candy apple, a stick of mochi, a tiny grilled rice ball — and they weren't straining at all. They were bouncing. They were grinning. They were chanting that funny word, over and over, in perfect rhythm.

"**わっしょい** (wasshoi)!" they sang. "Heave-ho, all together-o!"

And here's the strange thing, the wink-at-you thing: those treats were no bigger than the dango. Some were smaller. Yet their shrines floated down the street as light as bubbles, swaying happily side to side.

"They're not lifting harder than us," Cha observed, narrowing his clever green eyes. "They're lifting... TOGETHER. At the exact same moment. With the chant."

"The chant!" Pinku gasped. "That's the secret! The lantern said it's carried by chanting!"

Shiro bit his lip. "But there are still only THREE of us. The fourth pole—"

And right then, a fourth voice answered.

"Mind if I take that corner?"

---

It was the radish-lantern, glowing warm and gold, settling itself gently over the empty back-right pole.

"I'm old and round and not very strong," it admitted, "but I can carry a *little.* That's all anyone needs to carry. A little."

Pinku looked at Shiro. Shiro looked at Cha. Cha looked at all of them and gave a slow, certain nod.

"Then let's do it the right way this time," said Cha. "Not by lifting hardest. By lifting *together.*"

They took their places again — Pinku front-left, Shiro front-right, Cha back-left, the warm little lantern back-right. And far down the street, the **太鼓** boomed.

*BOOM.*

"Get ready..." whispered Pinku.

*BOOM.*

"Whisk slow," breathed Shiro, and this time he meant it.

*BOOM.*

"WASSHOI!" they all cried together — and they lifted.

And the omikoshi rose into the air as if it had been waiting all along just to be asked nicely, by everybody, at once.

---

"It's UP!" Shiro shrieked, except this time it was a happy shriek, the very best kind. "IT'S UP AND I'M HELPING AND THE BIRD DIDN'T FALL OFF!"

"Don't stop!" laughed Cha. "Keep the rhythm — the rhythm IS the magic!"

So they marched. And oh, what a sight they made — three little dumplings on a stick and one wobbly glowing radish, carrying a golden shrine down a lantern-lit street, chanting at the top of their tiny lungs.

"Wasshoi! Wasshoi! WASSHOI!"

Every time they said it together, the shrine grew lighter. Every time someone tried to push a little harder than the others, it tipped, just slightly, and giggled at them. The omikoshi, it turned out, had a sense of humor. It refused to be carried by showing off. It only wanted to be carried by *us.*

"This is the secret!" Cha called out as they swayed past a stall of sizzling skewers. "It was never about being strong enough! No single one of us could lift it — not Pinku, not me, not the biggest treat in the whole festival! But each of us can lift a *little!*"

"A little, plus a little, plus a little, plus a little," sang Pinku, pivoting the carousel of dango in time with the chant.

"Equals a LOT," finished Shiro, beaming. "That's the only math I like!"

---

Down the festival street they went, and as they passed, something wonderful happened. The good luck inside the omikoshi — warm and golden and giggly — spilled out over everyone. A grumpy grilled-corn cob un-grumpled and started to hum. A pair of sleepy lanterns blinked awake and glowed brighter. A lost little candy found its way back to its friends. The whole street swayed along, and the chant grew and grew until the whole town was one big *wasshoi.*

When at last they set the shrine down — gently, all together, on the count of the chant — the little carved bird on top gave a tiny, golden chirp, as if to say *thank you.*

The radish-lantern glowed the warmest it had glowed all night. "You see?" it said. "You did not carry it because you were strong. You carried it because you carried it *together* — and you let an old round lantern carry his little piece too."

Pinku, Shiro, and Cha pivoted in their happy carousel one last time, dizzy with joy instead of worry.

"Three on a stick," said Pinku.

"Plus one kind lantern," said Shiro.

"Stuck together for good," finished Cha.

And somewhere above them, the little golden bird gave one more chirp, and the lanterns swayed, and the whole festival breathed out a warm, sweet, sleepy sigh — because the load had been shared, and a shared load is the lightest, happiest load of all.

---

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, whatever feels too heavy to lift tonight, you do not have to carry it all by yourself. You only have to carry your little piece, and let those who love you carry theirs. A worry shared is a worry made light, just like a shrine that floats when everyone lifts together. So breathe slow, snuggle close, and let the warm gold luck of the festival drift over your pillow. Goodnight.
