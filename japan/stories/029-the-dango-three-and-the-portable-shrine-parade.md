# Story 29: The Dango Three and the Portable-Shrine Parade

**Main Character:** The Dango Three (Pinku, Shiro, Cha)
**Guest Characters:** Daikon-san, a round old radish-lantern; the carved golden bird on the shrine
**Setting:** A festival street parade carrying a mikoshi portable shrine
**Theme:** Everyone carries a little of the load
**Japanese Spotlight:** **omikoshi** (oh-mee-koh-shee) — portable shrine

---

The festival street glowed with lanterns the color of egg yolks and smelled of grilled corn and adventure.

Right in the middle wobbled three little dumplings on one stick.

"Today," announced Pinku, the bold pink one on top, "is the BIGGEST day of the year!"

"Are you SURE it's not the scariest?" squeaked Shiro, the shy white one in the middle.

"It's both," said Cha, the clever green one at the bottom. "Which makes it interesting."

They pivoted like a tiny carousel, Pinku-Shiro-Cha, until they flopped dizzy into a stack of snacks.

And there, behind the snacks, it was. The **omikoshi** (oh-mee-koh-shee) — portable shrine.

---

A tiny golden shrine with a curved roof, dangling bells, and a carved bird on top that glittered.

"We HAVE to carry it!" gasped Pinku.

"Three pieces, four corners," whispered Shiro. "The math doesn't math!"

"The math," said Cha, "is rude."

A round old radish-lantern bobbed over. "It's heavier than it looks. Strong friends have sat right back down."

"We're not strong friends," said Pinku, puffing up. "We're a STICK of friends. That's different."

"The secret," said the lantern, "is that it's never carried by lifting." And off he floated, humming.

---

Pinku grabbed the front-left pole. Shiro hugged the front-right. Cha wrapped round the back-left.

The back-right pole stood completely, terribly empty.

"ONE, TWO, THREE!" They heaved. The omikoshi did not move one wiggle.

Pinku strained pinker. Shiro crinkled. Cha leaned with all his clever might. Nothing.

"Lever!" said Cha. They wedged their stick under a pole and Pinku jumped on the end.

The stick sprang. Three dango shot into the air and landed in a bucket of grilled corn.

"Push it!" said Pinku, corn on her head. They shoved. The shrine tipped. The bird teetered.

It tipped back with a tiny offended chirp. The dango flopped onto the cobbles, panting.

---

"It's impossible," huffed Pinku, and for the first time all day she sounded almost quiet.

Then a sticky, joyful sound floated down the street. "Wasshoi! Wasshoi! WASSHOI!"

Around the corner bounced a parade of little treats, each carrying one corner of a shrine.

A candy apple. A mochi. A grilled rice ball. None of them straining. All of them bouncing.

"**Wasshoi** (wah-shoy)!" they sang. "Heave-ho, all together-o!"

"They aren't lifting harder than us," said Cha, narrowing his eyes. "They lift at the same moment."

"But there are still only three of us," said Shiro. "The fourth pole—"

"Mind if I take that corner?" said a warm, crackly voice.

---

The radish-lantern settled over the empty pole. "I'm old and round. But I can carry a little."

Far down the street the **taiko** (tie-koh) — big drum — boomed. BOOM.

"Whisk slow," breathed Shiro, and this time he meant it. BOOM.

BOOM. "WASSHOI!" they all cried together, and lifted.

The shrine rose like it had been waiting to be asked nicely, by everybody, at once.

---

"IT'S UP AND I'M HELPING AND THE BIRD DIDN'T FALL OFF!" shrieked Shiro, happily.

Down the lantern street they swayed, chanting. Every wasshoi made the shrine lighter.

Pinku pushed a little harder, to show off. The shrine tipped sideways and giggled at her.

"It won't be carried by showing off," called Cha. "Only by us. A little, plus a little—"

"—plus a little, equals a LOT," finished Shiro. "That's the only math I like!"

---

They set it down together, on the count of the chant. The carved bird gave one golden chirp.

"You carried it because you carried it together," said the lantern, glowing his warmest.

"Three on a stick," said Pinku.

"Plus one kind lantern," said Shiro.

"Stuck together for good," finished Cha.

The lanterns swayed, and the whole street breathed out one warm, sweet, sleepy sigh.
