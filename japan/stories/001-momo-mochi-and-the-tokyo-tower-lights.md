# Story 1: Momo Mochi and the Tokyo Tower Lights

**Main Character:** Momo Mochi
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** Tokyo, beneath the glowing orange Tokyo Tower at dusk
**Theme:** A new friend can light up a whole city for you
**Japanese Spotlight:** **Konnichiwa** (koh-nee-chee-wah) — hello

---

On her very first evening in Tokyo, Momo Mochi bounced off the train so excitedly that she bounced right *over* the platform sign, *past* a startled pigeon, and *into* a stranger's shopping bag full of radishes.

"Oh! Excuse me, radishes," she said, squishing herself politely back out. "You smell wonderful."

The radishes did not answer, because radishes are shy.

Momo Mochi was a soft, pillowy snow-white rice cake, plump as a pillow you'd want to nap on, dusted all over in a fine powder of potato starch so she left tiny snowy footprints wherever she squished. Her cheeks were rosy peach-pink — *momo* means peach, you know — and her arms could stretch like warm taffy when she really, truly wanted a hug. Right now they were stretching quite a lot, because she wanted a hug very badly, and she did not know a single soul in the whole enormous city.

"Tokyo is *big*," she whispered, gazing up. "Bigger than a thousand mochi stacked into a tower."

That was when she got, very gently, lost.

---

It happened the way getting lost usually happens — one wrong turn that *felt* like a right turn, then another, then a third that was so confident it practically wore a hat. Momo squished down one glowy street and up another, past noodle shops puffing steam and lantern stalls glowing like fireflies, until she didn't know north from noodle.

Her bottom lip wobbled. A wobble is dangerous in a mochi, because mochi are extremely squishy, and a wobble can become a whole-body jiggle in about two seconds.

"No, no," she told herself, patting her own cheeks. "Squish together, stay together!"

She took a deep, soft breath. And when she looked up — *there it was.*

Far above the rooftops, glowing the warm toasty-orange of a campfire marshmallow, stood the tallest, friendliest light Momo had ever seen. It rose into the violet dusk like a candle somebody had lit just for lonely travelers. A tower. A beautiful, twinkly, orange tower.

"You look like you'd give good hugs," Momo decided. "I'm coming to say hello!"

And she bounced toward the light.

---

She had not bounced three blocks when she rolled — *boing* — straight into something tall, cold, and dramatically delicious.

"WATCH the *swirl*, sweetie!" gasped a towering mountain of fluffy shaved ice, drizzled in ruby-red strawberry syrup with a curl of condensed milk on top and tiny sunglasses perched at a jaunty angle. "Do you KNOW how long it takes to look this cool? One degree warmer and I'm a *puddle*."

"I'm so sorry!" Momo squished apologetically. Then, remembering the very first thing she'd learned about big cities — that you should always be brave enough to say hello — she stretched up tall and beamed. "**Konnichiwa** (koh-nee-chee-wah) — hello! I'm Momo Mochi. I'm new, and a little bit lost, and I'm trying to reach that warm orange light."

The shaved-ice mountain lowered his sunglasses. Under all the drama, his heart was as big as his swirl.

"*Konnichiwa* back at you, snowball. I'm Kori Kakigori. Lost on your first night? That's a meltdown waiting to happen." He tilted his syrupy peak toward the glow. "That, darling, is **Tokyo Tawaa** (toh-kyoh tah-wah) — Tokyo Tower. Half the city steers by it. Stay cool, sweeties — I'll point you partway, but I do NOT do warm streets. Follow the lanterns to the bridge, then ask the bun. He naps there."

"The bun?"

"You'll know him," said Kori, already drifting back toward an air-conditioned doorway. "He's the dreamiest crumb in Tokyo."

---

The bun was, in fact, fast asleep on the railing of the little bridge, snoring softly with a smile. He was a puffy domed roll with a crackly golden-green cross-hatched crust, and every time he breathed out, a few sparkling crumbs drifted off him like glittery snow.

"Um. *Konnichiwa?*" Momo whispered.

One sleepy eye opened. "Mmm... is it morning? Is it cookies?" He yawned enormously. "Crackle on the outside, dreams on the inside. I'm Mero Melonpan. Were you in my dream? You're shaped like a cloud I was napping on."

"I'm Momo! I'm trying to find Tokyo Tower." She pointed. "The orange light."

"Ohhh." Mero gazed up at it with soft, happy eyes, as if he'd never grow tired of looking. "Isn't it lovely? It looks exactly like the inside of a toasted marshmallow." He tipped his crackly head. "You're going the wrong way, little cloud. The river curves, see? You have to follow it like a sleepy snake. I'd take you myself, but..." He yawned again, mid-sentence, and his eyes drifted shut. "...I'm in the middle of such a good dream..."

"That's all right," Momo said warmly, tucking a stray crumb back onto his crust. "Sweet dreams, Mero."

He was already snoring. But just before she bounced off, he murmured, "Find the truck with the bell... it sings..."

---

Momo followed the curving river, and the warm orange tower grew taller and brighter with every block, until she could see it twinkling like ten thousand tiny lanterns stacked to the sky. She was nearly there. She could *feel* it.

And then the street split into five.

Five streets! Each one curling off in its own direction like noodles dropped from a great height. The tower glowed straight ahead — but a tall glass building had swung in front of it, swallowing the light, and suddenly Momo couldn't tell which noodle-street led to her glow.

Her lip wobbled. The jiggle began.

That was the precise moment a bell went *ding-ding-DING!* — bright and warm — and around the corner rolled a gleaming white-and-indigo food truck with a little curtain fluttering at its window, paper-lantern headlights glowing gold, and a tiny fish-flag flapping happily on its roof.

"Fresh sushi, anywhere the road goes!" it announced, ringing its bell. Then, spotting one small jiggling mochi all alone, it slowed, and its voice went gentle as a tucked-in blanket. "Well, *konnichiwa*, little traveler. You look like you've lost your light."

"I have!" Momo's words tumbled out. "I'm new, and I got lost, and I found the most beautiful orange tower, and a shaved-ice and a sleepy bun pointed the way, and now there are FIVE streets and a building ate my glow and—" She stopped, breathing hard, snow-dust puffing off her shoulders.

A neat little oval of pearly rice in a glossy salmon cape leaned out from the truck's window, standing very straight and proud. "Five streets, one tower. We can solve this *neatly.*" He tapped his nori belt thoughtfully. "I'm Niko Nigiri, and this is the Sushi-ya Express. Hop on the counter — best view in Tokyo. We'll have you to the tower before your starch settles."

---

Momo squished gratefully onto the warm fold-down counter, and the Sushi-ya Express rolled off down the middle street, bell singing softly. The lantern-headlights painted the road gold. And as they rounded the last corner —

*there it was, all of it.*

Tokyo Tower, lit up against the deep blue night, glowing the warm tangerine-orange of a campfire, of toasted marshmallows, of a nightlight left on just for you. It soared so high that Momo had to lean all the way back to see the top, and the light spilled down over the whole city, turning every rooftop gold.

"Oh," she breathed. "*Ohhh.*"

"Lovely, isn't she?" Niko said warmly. "On your very first night, too. That's lucky."

Momo gazed and gazed. And a funny thing happened to the lonely, lost feeling she'd carried all evening: it simply melted, the way Kori always worried she would, except this melting felt *wonderful*. Because she wasn't lost anymore. She was here, under the warmest light in Tokyo — and she hadn't gotten here alone at all.

"I thought the tower was the thing lighting up the city for me," she said softly. "But it wasn't, was it? It was Kori. And Mero. And you." She stretched out her warm taffy arms and wrapped the whole truck-counter in the squishiest hug a mochi can give. "*Konnichiwa* turned out to be a magic word. Every time I said it, somebody helped me find my way."

Niko's straight little posture went all soft and crinkly at the edges. "That," he said, "is the nicest thing a customer has ever squished about my truck."

Far off, a bell across the river rang in answer — Kori, drifting cool and dramatic, calling, "Did she make it, truck?! Tell her she looked FABULOUS getting lost!" And nearer by, half-asleep on his bridge, Mero murmured into a dream, "Told you... the truck sings..."

Momo Mochi laughed, a soft bouncy laugh, and looked up at the great orange light that had brought her, hello by hello, all the way home to a city full of friends she hadn't even met that morning.

A whole city, lit up — just for her.

## Goodnight Blessing
Wherever you are tonight, little one, may there be a warm orange light to steer by, and kind voices to point the way. May every *konnichiwa* you whisper turn into a friend, and every wrong turn turn out to be the road to someone wonderful. Squish your blanket close, the way Momo squishes a hug, and remember: you are never truly lost when there are friends ahead, waiting to say hello. Let the city glow soft and gold behind your eyes. Sleep snug as a mochi in starch. Goodnight.
