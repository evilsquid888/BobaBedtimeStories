# Story 100: The Grand Rising-Sun Reunion

**Main Character:** Momo Mochi
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A spectacular finale festival beneath Tokyo Tower and Skytree, with fireworks over all of Japan
**Theme:** Every good story circles back to friends and home
**Japanese Spotlight:** おやすみ (oyasumi) = goodnight

---

The biggest, bounciest, most ridiculously squishable evening in the history of evenings began, as all the best evenings do, with a hug that got a little out of hand.

Momo Mochi had hugged exactly forty-one friends in a row, and her stretchy taffy arms were now so tangled around so many shoulders that she had accidentally connected the entire night market into one giant pillowy chain. Tako and Yaki bounced at one end. Rin Ramen steamed happily at the other. And right in the middle, glowing pink with joy and a faint dusting of potato starch, was Momo herself.

"Squish together, stay together!" she sang, her rosy peach cheeks bunching up. "Oops — Maru, was that your whisk in my elbow?"

"It was," said Maru Matcha calmly, the matcha-cream swirl on her head wobbling. "Whisk slow. Feel calm. Also, please un-squish my whisk."

Tonight was the night. The Grand Rising-Sun Reunion.

---

It had taken weeks of planning, and a truly enormous amount of glitter. Every friend the whole gang had ever made — from snowy mountaintops to bubbling hot springs to a hundred glowing night markets — had been invited to one tremendous festival, held right in the heart of Tokyo, where the great lit towers stood like two friendly giants keeping watch over the city.

To the west glittered **東京タワー** (Tōkyō Tawā) = Tokyo Tower, orange and tall and warm as a campfire. To the east rose Skytree, silver-blue and so high it seemed to tickle the clouds. And strung between them, across rooftops and rivers and rows of paper lanterns, were the friends.

Momo had organized the whole thing with Niko Nigiri, who stood very straight and proud beside a clipboard nearly as big as he was.

"The schedule is perfect," Niko announced, adjusting his glossy salmon cape. "First, the lantern lighting. Then the hot-spring soak. Then the feast. Then — at precisely the right moment — the **花火** (hanabi) = fireworks. Let's do this neatly... or deliciously!"

"Or both!" Momo cheered, and stretched an arm clear across the plaza to squish him. "Definitely both."

But, as Niko was about to learn, the night had its own ideas about *neatly*.

---

The trouble — though it was a soft, silly, marshmallowy sort of trouble — began at the lanterns.

The plan was simple. Every friend would light one paper lantern and let it rise into the sky, until all of Tokyo glowed like a jar of fireflies. Tama Onigiri had brought extra lanterns, of course. "I packed a little extra, just in case!" she said, handing them out from a basket nearly bursting.

Pock Ramune lit his lantern first — *POP!* — and it shot up beautifully, trailing happy bubbles. Tai Taiyaki swam his lantern in a loop-de-loop. Sora the Soft-Cream released hers with a dreamy sigh. "Soft swirls, slow smiles," he murmured, watching it drift.

Then Kori Kakigori arrived.

Now, you must understand that Kori was made entirely of shaved ice, sunglasses perched on top, and the plaza beneath the towers was very, *very* warm with all those glowing lights. The moment Kori swept in, arms thrown wide, crying, "Sweeties! Your COOLEST friend has ARRIVED!" — she felt herself begin to do the one thing a mountain of ice must never do at a party.

She began to melt.

"Oh no. Oh NO. This is a meltdown! A LITERAL meltdown!" Kori gasped, ruby syrup sliding dramatically down her side. "I'm dripping! Darlings, I'm a puddle of my former fabulous self!"

The friends gathered round in a flutter. Dai Daruma wobbled forward, eyebrows fierce with encouragement. "Fall down seven, bounce up eight! We don't melt on my watch!"

But Dai, being round and roly-poly, had no idea how to *un*-melt a friend.

And here is where the night threatened to tip from joyful into just-a-little-sad — which, as you and I both know, is simply not allowed at a reunion. So Momo Mochi did what Momo always did. She thought with her heart first and her head second.

"Everyone!" she called. "We need somewhere cool and steamy and snug all at once. We need — "

"The **温泉** (onsen) = hot spring!" finished the Dango Three all together, pivoting like a tiny carousel. Pinku, Shiro, and Cha had set up a pop-up onsen on the festival's edge, fed by gentle, drifting steam.

"But the onsen is *hot*," fretted Shiro. "Won't that melt her faster?"

Cha, the clever green one, tapped his little round chin. "Not the soaking pool. The *steam*. Steam is warm but it doesn't pour. We wrap Kori in a cloud of it — cozy, but not drippy."

"Genius," said Niko, who had thrown his perfect schedule entirely out the window and discovered, to his surprise, that he didn't mind one bit.

---

So the whole reunion became a rescue, and the rescue became the best part of the party.

Yuki the Snow Monkey-Manju, who adored a good soak more than anyone alive, took charge of the steam. "Warm cheeks, warm heart, warm friends," he said, herding gentle wisps of onsen mist into a swirling, snug little cloud. The steam curled around Kori like a soft blanket — warm enough to be cozy, cool enough that she stopped dripping.

"Ahhh," Kori sighed, her sunglasses fogging up adorably. "Sweeties... I am still fabulous. Slightly smaller. But STILL fabulous."

"Smaller and still the coolest," agreed Momo, squishing her gently. "That's just more snuggle per scoop."

Meanwhile, Pepper-kun fired up his sizzling hot-plate at a *safe* distance, counting down like a showman. "Stir it, sizzle it, share it! Hot food this way — but keep our ice-friend in the SHADE!" Rin Ramen ladled warm broth into bowls. Coro the Korokke handed out crispy golden croquettes, a shy puff of steam rising whenever someone thanked him. "Crispy outside, soft heart inside," he mumbled, turning pink with pleasure.

Mero Melonpan, who had fallen asleep approximately four times already, woke up just long enough to murmur, "Crackle on the outside... dreams on the inside..." before drifting off against Kuma the Kuri's cozy autumn scarf. Kuma, brave and quiet, simply held still so his sleepy friend wouldn't tumble. "Small and roasty," he whispered. "Ready and toasty."

Neko-Pan waved his lucky paw at everyone who passed. "Wave for luck, share the luck!" And Gacha the Gachapon rolled through the crowd, popping open every few feet to spill tiny mochi prizes that made the littlest guests squeal.

Dora the Dorayaki sat on a lantern-lit step, scribbling everything down in a notebook. "A good story is the best filling," he told Momo, beret tilted thoughtfully. "And THIS, my friend, is the best story we've ever filled."

Then — because no reunion is complete without one tiny, harmless disaster — Tako and Yaki discovered the rice crackers.

"There's a WHOLE pyramid of them!" gasped Tako.

"A pyramid!" echoed Yaki. "Roll us once — we come as two!"

And roll they did, bouncing in perfect unison straight into the bottom of the cracker tower, which teetered, tottered, and tipped over in a glorious, crunchy, slow-motion avalanche. Crackers slid everywhere. They landed in Rin Ramen's broth (*sploosh!*), bonked gently off Dai Daruma's round red head (*boing!*), and one came to rest, perfectly balanced, on Mero Melonpan's sleeping nose.

For one long second, everyone froze.

Then Momo Mochi laughed — a big, bubbly, whole-body laugh that wobbled her from cheek to cheek — and suddenly everyone was laughing, even Niko, who had finally, completely, made peace with the idea that the best parties are never on schedule.

"I packed extra crackers too," said Tama Onigiri, lifting her basket with a wink. "Just in case."

"Of COURSE you did," everyone said at once.

Pock Ramune fizzed himself silly. Tai Taiyaki did a victory loop through the air. And Tako and Yaki high-fived from the bottom of the cracker pile, entirely unbothered, grinning their twin grins. "Worth it," they said together.

---

By the time everyone was warm and fed and slightly sticky, the towers above had begun to glow even brighter, and a hush fell over the plaza like a soft quilt being pulled up to a chin.

Niko climbed onto the bed of Kei the kei-truck, who beeped proudly — *putt-putt!* — to be part of the big moment. The Sushi-ya Express rolled up beside them, paper-lantern headlights aglow, and rang its little welcome bell so the whole festival turned to look.

"Friends," Niko began, and his polite voice wobbled with feeling. "We started so many adventures apart. Tonight, we are all together. So I'd like to do this part... not neatly at all. Just happily." He took a breath. "Momo? Would you light the first firework?"

Momo's peach cheeks glowed brighter than any lantern. She stretched her taffy arm up, up, up — past Tokyo Tower, nearly to Skytree — and with the gentlest little *boop*, she touched the first firework awake.

And oh, how it loved being lit.

It rushed into the dark like a treat that had waited its whole life for this one proud, sparkling moment — bursting open in a thousand colors, joyful and grand, lighting the upturned faces of every friend below. More followed: golden chrysanthemums, fizzing blue spirals (Pock Ramune insisted those were named after him), and one enormous pink heart that everyone agreed looked *exactly* like Momo.

The fireworks bloomed over rivers and mountains and quiet little towns, over snowy peaks and steaming springs, over every place the friends had ever wandered — until it seemed all of Japan was tucked beneath one shimmering, sparkling sky.

---

When the last firework had faded into a soft drift of glittering smoke, the friends did not cheer loudly. They were far too cozy for that. Instead they leaned together — pillowy and warm, mochi against manju against melonpan — in one enormous, gentle, breathing heap.

Momo looked around at every single face: the planners and the dreamers, the bouncers and the nappers, the ones who steamed and the ones who fizzed and the one fabulous mountain of ice who was, by now, mostly a very happy puddle wearing sunglasses.

"You know," Momo said softly, "we traveled to the snowiest mountains and the bubbliest springs and a hundred glowing markets. And every single time, the very best part was the part where we came home — to each other."

"Squish together," whispered Tama.

"Stay together," finished everyone, in one warm, sleepy breath.

And then Maru Matcha, calm as a still green pond, raised her tiny whisk like a tiny wand toward all the sleeping cities below.

"**おやすみ** (oyasumi) = goodnight," she said, to her friends, to Tokyo, to the towers, to the fireworks-smoke and the floating lanterns and the whole gentle island curled up beneath the stars.

"Oyasumi," they all murmured back, one by one, until the word itself seemed to glow.

And the towers dimmed their lights, just a little, the way you turn down a lamp for someone you love who is finally, happily, ready to sleep.

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, the lanterns are floating low now, and the fireworks have settled into a soft silver hush. Every friend you met along the way is tucked in close, warm cheek to warm cheek, breathing slow beneath a sky full of quiet stars. Wherever you wandered today, you get to come home — to your pillow, to your blanket, to the people who love your squishable heart. Let the steam curl gentle and the night hold you snug. Oyasumi, dear traveler. Goodnight.
