# Story 10: Dora Dorayaki and the Cozy Comic Convention

**Main Character:** Dora the Dorayaki
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A friendly anime fan convention hall full of costumes and booths
**Theme:** Loving something is more fun when you find your people
**Japanese Spotlight:** オタク (otaku) = a passionate fan / hobbyist

---

Dora the Dorayaki had a secret tucked inside his beret, right next to his red-bean smile.

The secret was this: Dora *loved* a gentle little comic called *Slow Tea, Sleepy Cat*. It was the coziest, sweetest, most absolutely nothing-happens-y comic in all the world. In every chapter, a fluffy cat poured tea, blinked twice, and took a nap. That was it. That was the whole story.

Dora thought it was the greatest thing ever made.

But here was the trouble. Whenever someone asked Dora what he liked to read, his two fluffy pancake layers squeezed shut around his red-bean heart and he mumbled, "Oh, you know. Normal stuff. Exciting stuff. Dragons, probably."

Because what if nobody else loved a comic where a cat just... napped? What if everyone laughed?

So Dora kept his beret pulled low, and his secret tucked tight, and his red-bean heart a little bit lonely.

Until the morning the flyer fluttered down from the sky and landed — *flump* — right on his soft round head.

---

**コミコン** (komikon) = comic convention, said the flyer in big bouncy letters. **A COZY ONE! Costumes! Booths! Friends! THIS WEEKEND!**

Dora picked it up with a trembling pancake-hand. A whole hall full of people who loved comics and stories? It was terrifying. It was wonderful. It was *exactly* the most frightening wonderful thing he could imagine.

"A good story is the best filling," Dora whispered to himself, which was his catchphrase and also the only brave thing he could think to say. He took a deep breath that fluffed both his pancakes, and he went.

The convention hall was the size of a hundred night markets stacked together, and *oh*, it was a marvel. There were booths draped in glittering cloth. There were friends dressed as their favorite heroes — a melon-pan in a cape, three little dango on a stick all wearing matching wizard hats, a soft-serve swirl carrying a foam sword nearly as tall as itself. Everywhere Dora looked, somebody was laughing, hugging, or pointing excitedly at a drawing.

"Welcome, welcome!" beeped a tiny butter-yellow voice. It was Kei the kei-truck, putt-putting along as the convention shuttle, his knitted seat-cover today knitted into the shape of a superhero's chest emblem. "Putt-putt — what are you here to celebrate?"

And Dora — brave, bold, here-on-purpose Dora — pulled his beret down over his eyes and squeaked, "Dragons. Probably."

Kei tilted his headlights kindly but didn't push, and putt-putted on.

---

Dora wandered the aisles for a long, careful while. He admired the action comics with their big *KA-POW* lettering. He admired the adventure comics with their maps and monsters. Everyone around those booths was an **オタク** (otaku) = a passionate fan — bursting with love for their favorite thing, talking a mile a minute, fingers flying as they described every plot twist.

It was a beautiful thing to watch. It was also, Dora thought sadly, clearly *not for him*. These otaku loved exciting things. Loud things. Things where the cat did more than nap.

He drifted toward the very back corner of the hall, the quiet corner, the corner where the air was soft and a little dusty and nobody seemed to go. He thought he might sit there a moment, and miss his cozy comic, and then quietly putt-putt home.

But in the quiet corner, there was one small booth.

It had a wobbly hand-painted sign. The sign had a drawing of a fluffy cat. The cat was holding a teacup. The cat's eyes were closed.

Dora's red-bean heart did a tiny somersault inside his pancakes.

He crept closer, the way you creep toward something you can't quite believe is real — slowly, on tip-pancake, holding your breath in case it turns out to be a daydream. But it didn't turn out to be a daydream. With every step, the little booth got realer and realer, until Dora was close enough to read the smaller words painted under the sleepy cat.

*Come sit a while,* the sign said. *Tea optional. Naps encouraged.*

And Dora thought his heart might just melt right out through his red-bean smile.

---

Behind the booth sat a snow-white steamed bun shaped like a baby snow monkey, with rosy onsen-pink cheeks, curled up half-asleep beside a neat little stack of comics. It was Yuki the Snow Monkey-Manju, and the comics in the stack were — Dora could hardly breathe — *Slow Tea, Sleepy Cat*. Every single volume. All of them.

"Oh," said Yuki softly, blinking awake. "A visitor. Warm cheeks, warm heart, warm friends. Hello." She yawned a snuggly little yawn. "Hardly anyone comes to this corner. They all want the dragons." She didn't say it sadly. She said it the way you'd say *some folks like their tea piping hot, and I like mine just warm.*

Dora opened his mouth to say *dragons, probably.*

But he looked at the stack of comics. And he looked at the painted sleepy cat. And the secret tucked inside his beret got so big and so warm it simply could not stay tucked one second longer.

"I LOVE *Slow Tea, Sleepy Cat*," Dora blurted, all in a rush, his whole face going red-bean pink. "I love it I love it I LOVE it! It's my favorite! Chapter Forty when the cat almost knocks over the teapot but *doesn't*? I read it eleven times! I cried! It's the coziest thing that's ever — " He clapped both pancakes over his mouth, mortified. He'd said it. The secret was out, flapping around the dusty corner like a startled bird.

There was a silence.

And then Yuki the Snow Monkey-Manju gasped the biggest, happiest, most wide-awake gasp Dora had ever heard.

"*CHAPTER FORTY?!*" she squealed, leaping right off her cushion. "The teapot! The almost-spill! I have read it *thirty-one* times! Do you — do you want to see the limited edition where you can see the steam rising from the cup if you tilt it in the light?"

"There's a STEAM edition?" Dora whispered, like she'd told him there was a second moon.

"Sit, sit, sit!" Yuki patted the cushion beside her. "Warm cheeks, warm heart — *sit!*"

And Dora sat.

---

What happened next was the best afternoon of Dora the Dorayaki's entire fluffy life.

He and Yuki talked and talked and *talked*. They talked about the cat's favorite teacup (the chipped blue one, obviously the best one). They debated whether the nap in Chapter Twelve was a *good* nap or merely a *fine* nap (Dora said good; Yuki said it was the finest nap in literature; they agreed to disagree, warmly). They each confessed their most embarrassing secret: that they sometimes read the comic out loud to themselves, doing a tiny squeaky voice for the cat — and when they discovered they did *the exact same squeaky voice*, they laughed so hard that Yuki rolled right off her cushion and Dora's beret popped clean off his head and landed neatly on the sleepy-cat sign, like the cat had decided to wear a hat.

Yuki showed Dora a tiny drawing she'd made of the sleepy cat curled inside a teacup, and Dora gasped so hard a little puff of red-bean steam came out of his smile.

"You can *draw* it?" he said. "You can draw a thing you love?"

"That's half of being an **オタク** (otaku)," Yuki said cozily. "Loving the thing. The other half — " she gestured around the dusty quiet corner, which suddenly didn't feel dusty or quiet or sad at all — "the other half is finding the people who love it too. It's not nearly so fun alone, is it?"

And Dora, who had spent so long keeping his favorite thing tucked away where nobody could laugh at it, realized something so true it made his pancakes go all soft and warm:

He'd never once laughed *alone* this whole afternoon. He'd laughed *with* somebody. And it had been the best laughing of all.

---

That might have been the whole story. But conventions have a funny magic to them, and the magic was this:

Other friends, drifting past the quiet corner, heard the laughter. And laughter, you know, is the most welcoming sound in the world. It travels.

A little soft-serve swirl peeked around the booth. "Is that... is that *Slow Tea, Sleepy Cat*? I thought I was the only one who liked the boring comic!"

"It's not boring, it's *cozy*," said Dora and Yuki at exactly the same time, and then they all three laughed.

Then came a melon-pan, rubbing his sleepy crackly eyes. "Did somebody say sleepy cat? I love the sleepy cat. I nap *with* the comic open. Crackle on the outside, dreams on the inside." He flopped down onto the cushion pile at once, as if he had simply been waiting his whole life for an excuse.

Then the three little dango in their wizard hats came pivoting around the corner like a tiny carousel, one piece of them gasping, the next piece pointing, and the third piece already clambering onto the cushions. "We have *opinions*," announced the pink one, "about the teapot." "Big opinions," whispered the white one. "And a *theory*," finished the green one, "about the chipped blue cup."

Then Kei the kei-truck, putt-putting up with his headlights bright, beeping, "I knew it! I knew you weren't really a dragons person! Putt-putt!"

Before long, the quiet dusty corner of the convention had become the *loudest, coziest, happiest* corner of all — a great soft pile of friends passing comics back and forth, gasping at the steam edition, arguing affectionately about teacups, and discovering, one by one, that the thing they'd each kept secretly tucked away was a thing they all *shared*.

"You know," said Yuki, snuggled in the middle of the happy heap, "I think we just became a club."

"The Cozy Comic Club!" cheered the soft-serve.

"The Sleepy Cat Society!" yawned the melon-pan.

"The We-Like-Boring-Things-And-They're-Actually-The-Best Brigade!" chorused the dango three, one word each.

"A good story," said Dora, beaming so wide his red-bean heart practically glowed, his beret finally, finally pushed all the way back, "is the best filling. But sharing one with friends? *That's* the whole snack."

---

When the convention closed that evening and the paper lanterns dimmed to a soft glow, Dora and Yuki walked out together, comics tucked under their arms, already planning next weekend.

Dora had come in with a secret hidden inside his beret and a lonely little heart.

He left with a beret pushed proudly back, a head full of new friends, and a heart so full of red-bean warmth it could've fed the whole hall. He didn't have a secret anymore. He had a *club*. And somehow that was so much cozier.

Loving something, Dora had learned, was lovely on its own.

But loving something *with your people* — well. That was the steam edition of feelings. The one where, if you tilt it just right in the light, you can see all the warmth rising up.

## Goodnight Blessing
Sleepy one, somewhere out there is a whole warm crowd who loves the very same gentle things you do — the cozy stories, the quiet hobbies, the favorite-est favorites you keep tucked close. You never have to hide what makes your heart go soft and glowy. Tomorrow you might find your people, the way Dora found his, in the quietest corner of all. Tonight, hug your favorite thing close and know it's wonderful, and so are you. Let your eyes go gentle as a sleepy cat beside a warm cup of tea. Goodnight.
