# Story 42: Tai Taiyaki and the Riverside Stall

**Main Character:** Tai Taiyaki
**Guest Characters:** Pip, a small new taiyaki; Bun, a slow extra-stuffed taiyaki; Taisho, the oldest taiyaki on the stall; a small silver canal fish
**Setting:** A canal-side taiyaki stall where the little fish cakes are made
**Theme:** Be brave enough to swim toward your dream
**Japanese Spotlight:** **taiyaki** (tie-yah-kee) — fish-shaped cake

---

A little taiyaki stall leaned over the edge of a canal, so close the water giggled below it.

On the rack sat Tai Taiyaki, a **taiyaki** (tie-yah-kee) — fish-shaped cake, smiling so wide his heart glowed.

"Someday," said Tai, pressing his nose over the edge, "I'm going to swim in a REAL river."

"Friends!" he announced, wiggling his fins at the rack. "We are fish! And what do fish DO?"

"Get eaten with great joy?" offered Pip, a new little taiyaki, crumbly at the edges.

"Sit on a rack?" offered Bun, who was extra full of red bean and took everything slowly.

"They SWIM!" cried Tai, flopping onto the soy bottle. Squirt. A fish-shaped splat. "Even my SHADOW swims."

---

"I once met a real fish," said Bun slowly. "Down in the canal."

"Wet," said Bun, after a long pause. "Very wet. It wiggled at me. A magnificent wiggle."

Tai's eyes shone like syrup. He waddled to the edge and looked all the way down.

The canal slid by, black and silver, very far below. His red-bean heart did a little flip.

Not the excited kind. The other kind. The kind that lives next door and borrows its socks.

"I'm an adventure fish," Tai whispered. "Or maybe just an adventure-SHAPED fish."

---

He leaned forward. Flip. He leaned back. Forward. Flip, somersault. Back.

"We'll help," said Pip. The rack nudged a long wooden paddle out over the water, sloping down.

"A ramp!" said Pip. "And a noodle on your tail, so we can pull you back."

Tai slid. Wheeee! Then Bun stepped onto the paddle's other end to watch.

The paddle see-sawed. Tai shot UP, sailed over the rail, and landed inside the lantern.

The lantern swung out over the canal with a golden fish glowing inside.

"New plan," called Pip. "Don't look down." Tai looked down.

---

Below on the rail sat the stall's oldest fish: Taisho, toasty brown, fin chipped, eyes like honey.

"Hello, swimmer," said Taisho. "Sixty years I've sat at this edge. I know that flip."

"Brave isn't the fish with no flip," said Taisho. "Brave looks down, flips, and dips a fin anyway."

"Just a fin," said Taisho. "The river will still be a river tomorrow."

The lantern swayed. Tai looked at the dark, beautiful, terrifying water. His heart flipped.

And this time, Tai dipped a fin anyway. He let go.

---

Down he sailed, fins out, the most cautious belly-flop in history. His tail touched water. Blip.

It was cold. It was wet. It tickled. It was the GREATEST FEELING IN THE WORLD.

"It's WATER!" shrieked Tai. "Real, wet, splashy WATER, and it's tickling my TAIL!"

Then a small silver fish, a REAL one, drifted up from the dark to see about the blip.

Fish looked at fish. The silver fish blinked sideways. And then it wiggled.

Tai wiggled back with everything he had. The silver fish flicked, which might have been a giggle.

"**Oyogu** (oh-yoh-goo) — to swim!" cheered Pip from the rail, and the whole rack cheered.

---

Then, a surprise. Old Taisho slid down the paddle, slow as syrup, and dipped his chipped fin.

"Sixty years," said Taisho, eyes crinkling. "Cold. Wet. Tickly. What was I waiting for?"

"**Daijoubu** (dye-joh-boo) — it's okay," called Pip, tugging the noodle. "Soggy fish are sad fish. Reeling in!"

---

Wrapped in paper sleeves by the warm iron mold, two fish steamed and dripped and grinned.

"You didn't swim very far," said Pip kindly, snuggling in to share the warmth.

"I dipped a fin," said Tai, glowing. "Tomorrow, maybe two. That's how you swim toward the sweet stuff."

Bun arrived at last, having taken the long way. "Did I miss the wiggle?"

The lanterns dimmed to sleepy amber. The canal giggled below. Tai smiled so wide his heart glowed.
