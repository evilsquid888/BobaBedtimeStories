# Story 42: Tai Taiyaki and the Riverside Stall

**Main Character:** Tai Taiyaki
**Guest Characters:** Pip, a small new taiyaki; Bun, a slow extra-stuffed taiyaki; Taisho, the oldest taiyaki on the stall; a small silver canal fish
**Setting:** A canal-side taiyaki stall where the little fish cakes are made
**Theme:** Be brave enough to swim toward your dream
**Japanese Spotlight:** **taiyaki** (tie-yah-kee) — fish-shaped cake
**Cameo:** Alto's VF-25 Valkyrie, Ranka Lee and Sheryl Nome (Macross Frontier) — a white-and-red jet screams up the canal, unfolds into a hovering robot, and two idols land on its shoulders for a sing-off whose high notes float every taiyaki, and Tai's lantern, up over the roof; walk-on: Mudkip (Pokémon)

---

A little taiyaki stall leaned over the edge of a canal, so close the water giggled below it.

On the rack sat Tai Taiyaki, a **taiyaki** (tie-yah-kee) — fish-shaped cake, grinning so wide his heart glowed.

"Friends!" he cried, wiggling his fins at the rack. "We are fish! What do fish DO?"

"Get eaten with great joy?" offered Pip, a new little taiyaki, crumbly at the edges.

"They SWIM!" cried Tai, and flopped onto the soy bottle. Squirt. A fish-shaped splat.

"Someday," said Tai, nose over the edge, "I'm swimming in that REAL river. Tonight, maybe."

---

"I met a real fish once," said Bun, the roundest, slowest taiyaki. A long pause.

"Wet," said Bun at last. "Very wet. It wiggled at me. A magnificent wiggle."

Tai waddled to the edge and looked down. The canal slid by, black and silver, far below.

His red-bean heart flipped. Not the excited kind. The kind that lives next door and borrows its socks.

"I'm an adventure fish," Tai whispered. "Or maybe just an adventure-SHAPED fish."

---

He leaned forward. Flip. Back. Forward. Flip, somersault. Back. His tail smacked the rack.

"We'll help," said Pip, nudging a long paddle out over the water. "A ramp! Noodle on your tail!"

Tai slid. Wheeee! Then Bun stepped onto the paddle's other end to watch.

The paddle see-sawed. Tai shot UP, sailed over the rail, and landed inside the lantern.

"New plan," called Pip, as the lantern swung out over the canal. "Don't look down." Tai looked down.

---

Then the canal ROARED. A white-and-red jet screamed up the water and unfolded, CLANK, into a hovering robot.

A green-haired idol in a yellow dress landed on its left shoulder, arm raised. "Kira!" sang Ranka.

A strawberry-blond idol in pink struck a pose on the right shoulder. "MY stage, darling," belted Sheryl.

Ranka sang a high note. Every taiyaki on the rack floated up a fin's width. Tai's lantern rose.

Sheryl sang higher. The rack rose, Bun rose, Pip rose, and Tai's lantern floated above the roof.

The robot drifted away down the canal, arms folded, both idols waving from its shoulders, still singing.

---

The song faded. Everything settled softly back. Tai's lantern swung out over the dark water again.

"Even a singing robot couldn't get me in," Tai whispered to the lantern.

Below on the rail sat Taisho, the oldest fish, toasty brown, fin chipped, eyes like honey.

"Hello, swimmer," said Taisho. "Sixty years on this edge. I know that flip."

"Brave isn't the fish with no flip," said Taisho. "Brave looks down, flips, and dips a fin anyway."

Tai looked at the dark, beautiful, terrifying water. His heart flipped. He let go anyway.

---

Down he sailed, fins out, the most cautious belly-flop in history. His tail touched water. Blip.

"It's WATER!" shrieked Tai. "Real, wet, splashy, tickly WATER, and it's tickling my TAIL!"

A small silver fish drifted up to see about the blip. It blinked sideways. It wiggled.

Tai wiggled back with everything he had. The silver fish flicked, which might have been a giggle.

A small blue creature with an orange belly bobbed up. "Mud!" said Mudkip, and squirted Tai's nose.

"**Oyogu** (oh-yoh-goo) — to swim!" cheered Pip from the rail, and the whole rack cheered.

---

Then, a surprise. Old Taisho slid down the paddle, slow as syrup, and dipped his chipped fin.

"Sixty years," said Taisho, eyes crinkling. "Cold. Wet. Tickly. What was I waiting for?"

"**Daijoubu** (dye-joh-boo) — it's okay," called Pip, tugging the noodle. "Soggy fish are sad fish. Reeling in!"

---

Wrapped in paper sleeves by the warm iron mold, two fish steamed and dripped and grinned.

"I dipped a fin," said Tai, glowing. "Tomorrow, maybe two. That's how you swim toward the sweet stuff."

Bun arrived at last, having taken the long way. "Did I miss the wiggle?"

The lanterns dimmed to sleepy amber. The canal giggled below. Tai smiled so wide his heart glowed.
