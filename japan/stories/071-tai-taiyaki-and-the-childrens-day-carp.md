# Story 71: Tai Taiyaki and the Children's Day Carp

**Main Character:** Tai Taiyaki
**Guest Characters:** Tama Onigiri; Old Hata, a creaky paper-lantern festival keeper
**Setting:** A breezy hillside on Children's Day with giant carp streamers
**Theme:** The smallest flag matters most to the smallest watchers
**Japanese Spotlight:** **kodomo no hi** (koh-doh-moh noh hee) — Children's Day

---

At sunrise on **kodomo no hi** (koh-doh-moh noh hee) — the day for little ones, Tai Taiyaki waddled uphill.

And oh, the sky was fishing: giant carp streamers gulped the breeze from tall poles, scarlet and gold.

"Swim toward the sweet stuff!" whooped Tai, grinning so wide a sparrow mistook him for sunrise.

Papa carp, mama carp, big-sister carp, and then a snapped string, flicking like a sad noodle.

"The storm took the littlest carp," sighed Old Hata, the creaky lantern-keeper. "The little ones come at noon."

Tai's red-bean heart went thump. "Hata," he said, fins on hips, "this fish is going fishing."

---

Tai followed the storm downhill, checking everything. A hedge: one bent umbrella. The pond: three unhelpful ducks.

Under a bench: one pickle, mysteriously. "Not a carp," said Tai. "But noted."

Then he heard crows laughing at the scarecrow in the vegetable patch.

The scarecrow appeared to be getting swallowed headfirst by a silver fish.

"Hang on, friend!" called Tai, scrambling up the post. FWIP. The crows applauded, which sounds like more laughing.

On the grass, the carp had a ragged tail-tear big enough to put his head through.

Which he did, just to check. Then wished he hadn't, because now he was wearing it.

---

Tama Onigiri was spreading a picnic blanket at the bottom of the hill. "EMERGENCY—" panted Tai.

"Needle and thread?" said Tama, already digging. "I packed a little extra, just in case."

Out came a sewing kit, two spoons, a whistle, a backup whistle, and one emergency pickled plum.

"You're magic," said Tai.

"I'm prepared," said Tama. "It looks the same from far away."

The hole needed a patch. Tai unfolded his own wax-paper wrapper, tiny blue waves, his cold-morning cape.

"He's a water fish," said Tai. "He should have a little water on him."

Tama stitched fast and neat. She sewed Tai's fin to the carp only once, a very good average.

---

The streamer clips on at the very top of the pole, and the storm had jammed the pulley.

"I'm a fish," said Tai. "Climbing is just swimming with extra holding on."

"**Ganbatte** (gahn-baht-teh) — do your best!" said Tama, and stuffed the emergency plum in his mouth for luck.

Hug, scoot. Hug, scoot. Up the slick bamboo, his tail-fin catching each gust like a little sail.

"A fish is climbing the pole," said one tiny mochi below, in the tone of reporting normal weather.

At the top, Tai reached for the clip. The rude noon wind snatched the carp from his fins.

---

Tai let go. A flat fish in a dive is FAST. He dropped like a golden arrow.

He hooked the streamer with his tail-fin, spread flat, and glided, whoooosh, straight into Tama's picnic.

He bounced twice and came to rest in the potato salad. The little ones cheered wildly.

"Nobody would blame you," said Tama gently. "It's a long climb twice."

Tai stood up, dripping potato salad, and faced the littlest watchers. "Little fish stick together," said Tai.

Halfway up, his fins shook. From below came small voices: "Go, fish! Go, fish!"

At the top he held the streamer in his teeth and clipped it on. The noon bell sang.

---

The wind poured in. The patched tail held. The smallest carp filled out silver, blue waves flashing.

"The baby carp flies for US!" squealed the little ones. One tried to bow and sat down instead.

"Not one of those little ones is looking at the big carp," said Tama, dusting him off.

"The littlest watchers need a fish their own size," said Tai softly.

---

The evening wind went soft, and up the hill the carp swam slow, nose to tail, like breathing.

"He swims fine with a patched tail," murmured Tama, in the clover.

"Better than fine," yawned Tai. "He swims loved."

---
