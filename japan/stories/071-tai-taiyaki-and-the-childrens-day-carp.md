# Story 71: Tai Taiyaki and the Children's Day Carp

**Main Character:** Tai Taiyaki
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A breezy hillside on Children's Day with giant carp streamers
**Theme:** Grow strong by swimming your own brave way
**Japanese Spotlight:** こどものひ (kodomo-no-hi) = Children's Day

---

On the very best breezy morning of the whole spring, Tai Taiyaki waddled up the hillside flapping his crispy fin-tail so hard that three nearby crumbs got blown clean off a picnic blanket. He was a cheerful golden fish-shaped cake, you see, with a warm red-bean heart you could spot whenever he grinned wide — and today he was grinning EXTRA wide.

"It's **こどものひ** (kodomo-no-hi) = Children's Day!" he announced to a confused beetle. "The one day a year when the whole sky goes FISHING!"

He pointed his little waffle-fin upward. And oh — what a sight! Strung between tall bamboo poles, dozens of giant carp streamers rippled in the wind: scarlet ones, midnight-blue ones, gold ones the size of canoes, all gulping the breeze and swimming through the air with their mouths wide open.

"Swim toward the sweet stuff!" Tai whooped. "Today, I'm going to swim with the BIG fish!"

The beetle, wisely, took cover.

---

Now, you should know that Tai Taiyaki had never met a puddle he didn't want to splash through or a breeze he didn't want to ride. So when he saw those magnificent carp streamers — called **こいのぼり** (koi-nobori) = carp streamers — swimming so grandly up the hill, his red-bean heart went *thump-thump-FLUTTER*.

"If I race them to the top," he decided, "I'll prove I'm a real, strong, grown-up swimmer!"

He found the lowest carp streamer, a great crimson fellow named Beni-san, snapping and billowing in the wind.

"Excuse me, Beni-san!" Tai called up, waddling fast. "RACE you to the top of the hill! Last one there is a soggy senbei!"

Beni-san's enormous fabric mouth rippled in something that *might* have been a chuckle. "Little cake," boomed the wind through him, "we carp do not race. We swim *up*. We climb the sky. It is the way we grow strong. Can you climb the sky?"

"Climb the sky? Easy-peasy red-bean-squeezy!" said Tai, who had absolutely no idea how to climb the sky but had never let *not knowing how* slow him down.

So up the hill he went, waddle-waddle-flap, trying to do exactly what the carp did.

This, it turned out, was the trouble.

---

The carp streamers swam by going *straight up*, mouths open, letting the wind pour through them and lift their long bodies high and proud. So Tai opened his mouth as wide as he could — *gulp!* — and waited to be lifted high and proud too.

He was not lifted high and proud.

He was lifted exactly four inches, spun in a dizzy circle, and plopped — *flump!* — into a clump of clover.

"Hmph," said Tai, spitting out a petal. "Try, try again. **がんばって** (ganbatte) = do your best!"

He scrambled out, climbed onto a flat rock, and tried once more to swim *straight up* like Beni-san. He puffed out his red-bean heart. He held his fin-tail stiff and tall like a streamer. He pointed his nose at the clouds and made himself as long and grand as a taiyaki could possibly be.

He held very, very still.

He did not move at ALL.

"Maybe I'm not holding still ENOUGH," he reasoned, which is the sort of thing you reason when you are determined and a little bit dizzy. So he held even stiller. He held so still that a curious dragonfly mistook his fin-tail for a fence post and landed on it for a nice long rest.

"Shoo," whispered Tai out of the corner of his grin, trying not to ruin his very grand and streamer-like pose. "I'm SWIMMING here."

The dragonfly yawned, settled in more comfortably, and did not shoo.

A whole minute passed. Then two. A picnicking family farther down the hill unwrapped their **おにぎり** (onigiri) = rice balls and finished their entire lunch, and STILL Tai Taiyaki had not swum so much as an inch into the sky.

"Why isn't the sky lifting me?" he huffed, so frustrated that the dragonfly finally took the hint and buzzed off.

From high above, Beni-san's deep voice rolled down on the breeze. "Because, little cake — you are not made of cloth and air. You are made of warm batter and bravery. You cannot swim *up* the way I swim up. You will tire yourself flat as a pancake trying."

Tai's fins drooped. A tiny puff of steam — the kind he made when he was discouraged — escaped from his red-bean heart.

"But I want to grow strong," he said quietly. "I want to swim with the big fish on Children's Day. Everyone says that's what Children's Day is *for* — getting bigger and braver. And the carp are the biggest, bravest swimmers there are."

---

That was when a small, calm wind curled around the hillside, and with it came the rustly, kindly voice of the very TOP carp — old Kogane-san, the great golden streamer at the highest pole of all, so high she nearly tickled the clouds.

"Come up here, little taiyaki," she whispered, "and watch how my friends swim."

Tai waddled to a high mossy ledge and looked out over the whole breezy hillside. And for the first time, he stopped trying to swim like a carp — and simply *watched* the carp swim.

And he noticed something.

Beni-san the crimson carp swam by going straight up, yes — but with great heaving billows, all power and push.

The little blue carp beside him swam by *wiggling*, tail-tip to nose, a shimmery ripple-dance.

A round, fat orange carp barely moved at all — he just opened wide and let the wind float him like a happy balloon.

And way up high, golden Kogane-san swam in slow, looping spirals, calm as a leaf in a stream.

There was a tiny silver carp, too — the smallest one of all, no bigger than Tai himself — and HE swam in funny little hops, *up-pause-up-pause*, like he was climbing invisible stairs. And a chubby spotted carp who swam by going almost sideways, drifting and gliding like a paper kite. Not one of them swam the same as the fish beside them. And yet every single one of them was climbing the sky just fine, billowing and proud and strong.

"Every carp," said Kogane-san softly, "swims a *different* way. The wind is the same for all of us. But the swimming? That, each fish finds for itself. That is the secret of こどものひ, little one. We do not grow strong by swimming the same. We grow strong by swimming our *own* brave way."

Tai's eyes went very wide. His red-bean heart did a slow, warm somersault.

"My own... brave way?"

"Tell me," said Kogane-san, with a twinkly billow. "When YOU swim — even on dry land, even waddling up a hill — how do *you* like to do it?"

And Tai didn't even have to think about it.

"I like to SPLASH!" he burst out. "I like to waddle-wiggle and flap my crispy tail and zoom and bonk into things and pop right back up! I like to go *sideways* and *loop-de-loop* and shout 'swim toward the sweet stuff!' I don't like going slow and straight and still. That's not how *I* swim. That's how a STREAMER swims."

"There it is," said Kogane-san warmly. "Now. Swim up this hill *your* way. Not my way. Not Beni-san's way. *Yours*."

---

Well.

Tai Taiyaki took the deepest breath a fish-cake has ever taken. He set his fin-tail loose and wiggly. He pointed his nose NOT at the clouds, but at the wide-open hillside — and he *splashed off*.

He waddle-zoomed up the slope in big silly loops, flapping and flopping, riding the gusts sideways like a leaf on a river, bonking off a dandelion (*boing!*), spinning through a patch of clover (*wheee!*), and popping right back up every single time he tumbled — because popping back up was the most Tai thing in the whole world.

"Swim toward the sweet stuff!" he hollered, zigging when the wind zagged, wiggling where a streamer would have held still.

A gust came along — a big one, the kind that would have knocked a held-still taiyaki flat as a coaster. But Tai didn't fight it and he didn't freeze. He *leaned into it*, the way you lean into a wave at the seaside, and let it whoosh him up a whole stretch of hill in one glorious spinning whoop.

"WHEEEEE — okay that one was a LOT," he laughed, dizzy and delighted, "but I LIKED it!"

His crispy fin-tail flapped so happily it sounded like applause. Down below, the picnicking family pointed up at the little golden cake doing loop-de-loops among the giant carp, and they clapped too, and somebody's little sister shouted "GO, fish-cake, GO!" — which made Tai's red-bean heart swell up so warm and proud he very nearly forgot to flap at all.

And here is the funny part — the part that made even grand old Beni-san billow with laughter:

By swimming his own bouncy, loopy, splashy, sideways way... Tai Taiyaki was *faster up the hill than any of them*.

Because the carp could only go straight up. But Tai could go *around* the boulder, *over* the log, *through* the gap in the fence, and *under* the low branch where the wind funneled strongest. He flap-zoomed past the blue carp. He looped past the orange one. He bonked merrily off the bamboo pole (*ow — boing!*) and corkscrewed all the way up, up, UP to the very top of the hill, right beneath golden Kogane-san herself.

"I DID IT!" he cried, breathless and glowing and dizzy and SO happy his red-bean heart practically sparkled through his grin. "I swam the sky — MY way!"

"You certainly did," laughed Kogane-san, her golden body looping a proud little victory spiral. "And do you feel it, little cake? Do you feel how much braver you are than when you started?"

Tai pressed a fin to his warm, full heart.

"I do," he whispered. "I really do."

---

They spent the rest of that breezy こどものひ together on the sunny hilltop — Tai Taiyaki and all the great carp streamers, swimming the wind side by side. The carp went straight and grand and slow. Tai went loopy and splashy and silly. And nobody — not one single fish — tried to swim like anybody else.

When the sun melted gold across the hills and the streamers grew sleepy and slow, Beni-san dipped his crimson nose down to Tai.

"You are a true swimmer, little taiyaki," he rumbled. "The bravest kind. The kind who swims *himself*."

And Tai Taiyaki, full of red-bean warmth and wind and wonder, did the happiest, sleepiest little waddle all the way back down the hill — bonking off exactly two dandelions on the way, and popping right back up both times, just because it felt so good.

---

## Goodnight Blessing
Sleepy one, the wind tonight is soft and the carp are folding up their wings. Wherever your own brave way takes you tomorrow — loopy or straight, fast or slow, splashy or still — know that it is exactly the right way, because it is *yours*. You don't have to swim like anybody else to grow up strong; you only have to swim like *you*, and pop right back up each time you tumble. Close your eyes now, little swimmer, and let the breeze carry you gently up into your dreams. Goodnight.
