# Story 71: Tai Taiyaki and the Children's Day Carp

**Main Character:** Tai Taiyaki
**Guest Characters:** Tama Onigiri; Old Hata, a creaky paper-lantern festival keeper
**Setting:** A breezy hillside on Children's Day with giant carp streamers
**Theme:** The smallest flag matters most to the smallest watchers
**Japanese Spotlight:** **kodomo no hi** (koh-doh-moh noh hee) — Children's Day
**Cameo:** All Might (My Hero Academia) — lands with "I AM HERE!", plucks the whole carp pole out of the hill with Tai still hugging it, flexes until the bamboo bows and flings fish and carp skyward, then plants it back crooked; walk-on: Deku (My Hero Academia)

---

At sunrise on **kodomo no hi** (koh-doh-moh noh hee) — the day for little ones, Tai Taiyaki waddled uphill.

The sky was fishing: giant carp streamers gulped the breeze from tall poles, scarlet and gold.

"Swim toward the sweet stuff!" whooped Tai, and swam straight into papa carp's open mouth.

Papa carp, mama carp, big-sister carp, and then a snapped string, flicking like a sad noodle.

"The storm took the littlest carp," creaked Old Hata the lantern-keeper. "Little ones arrive at noon."

Tai's red-bean heart went thump. "Hata," he said, fins on hips, "this fish is going fishing."

---

Tai followed the storm downhill. Under a bench: one pickle, mysteriously. "Not a carp," said Tai. "But noted."

Then crows, laughing. In the vegetable patch, a silver fish was swallowing the scarecrow headfirst.

"Hang on, friend!" called Tai, scrambling up. FWIP. The crows applauded, which sounds like laughing.

The carp's tail had a hole big enough for his head. Which he checked. Now he wore it.

---

At the bottom of the hill, Tama Onigiri was spreading a picnic blanket. "EMERGENCY," panted Tai.

"Needle and thread?" said Tama, already digging. "I packed a little extra, just in case."

Out came a sewing kit, two spoons, a whistle, a backup whistle, one emergency plum.

"You're magic," said Tai, from inside the carp.

"I'm prepared," said Tama. "It looks the same from far away."

For the patch, Tai unfolded his own wax-paper wrapper: tiny blue waves, his cold-morning cape.

Tama stitched fast and neat, sewing Tai's fin to the carp only once. A very good average.

---

The clip waited at the top of the tallest pole. The storm had jammed the pulley.

"I'm a fish," said Tai. "Climbing is just swimming with extra holding on."

Hug, scoot. Hug, scoot. Up the slick bamboo, tail-fin catching each gust like a sail.

"A fish is climbing the pole," said one tiny mochi, like reporting normal weather.

---

Halfway up, BOOM. A towering hero with two tall blond hair-spikes landed below, grinning like sunrise.

"I AM HERE!" boomed All Might. "Fear not, small fish! I shall bring the top down to you!"

He plucked the pole from the hill, Tai still hugging it, and hoisted it like a flag.

He flexed. The bamboo bent into a bow, and Tai whipped back and forth like a fishing lure.

"HA HA HA! Too much might!" laughed All Might. The pole sprang straight and flung Tai skyward.

He planted the pole back, crooked as a bent straw, then froze mid-flex, grin shining, and stayed.

From behind mama carp, a freckled hero in green saluted All Might so hard his arm blurred.

---

High over the hill, Tai spread flat and dove. A flat fish in a dive is FAST.

He hooked the tumbling carp with his tail-fin and glided, whoooosh, into Tama's picnic.

He bounced twice and sat up in the potato salad. The little ones cheered.

"Nobody would blame you," said Tama. "It's a long climb twice, and the pole leans."

Tai stood, dripping potato salad, looked at the littlest watchers, and marched back.

---

Halfway up the leaning pole, his fins shook. Small voices below: "Go, fish! Go, fish!"

At the top he held the streamer in his teeth and clipped it on. The noon bell sang.

The wind poured in. The patch held. The smallest carp filled out silver, blue waves flashing.

"The baby carp flies for US!" squealed the little ones. One tried to bow and sat down instead.

"Not one little one is looking at the big carp," said Tama, dusting him off.

"The littlest watchers need a fish their own size," said Tai softly.

---

The evening wind went soft, and the carp swam slow, nose to tail, like breathing.

"The leaning pole stays," creaked Old Hata. "It is bowing to the baby."

"He swims loved," yawned Tai, in the clover.

---
