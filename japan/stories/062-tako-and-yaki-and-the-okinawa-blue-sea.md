# Story 62: Tako and Yaki and the Okinawa Blue Sea

**Main Character:** Tako & Yaki (takoyaki twins)
**Guest Characters:** Kame, a very old sea turtle; a hermit crab wearing a rice bowl
**Setting:** A turquoise tropical beach in sunny Okinawa
**Theme:** Adventure is sweeter shared with your other half
**Japanese Spotlight:** **umi** (oo-mee) — sea
**Cameo:** Porco Rosso (Porco Rosso) — lands his scarlet seaplane in the bay, orders one takoyaki to go, nets two attached ones, and the net rips them straight back into the whirlpool; walk-on: Oshawott (Pokémon)

---

The first thing Tako and Yaki did on the Okinawa beach was bonk into each other.

They always did. Two golden batter-balls joined by one wobbly bonito-flake wave.

"Look look LOOK!" said Tako, bouncing so hard the sauce zig-zags wiggled.

"I'm attached to you," laughed Yaki. "I CAN'T not look!"

Before them lay the **umi** (oo-mee) — sea — turquoise as a spilled bottle of sky-flavored soda.

"It's the size of a million takoyaki trays," breathed Tako. "We're swimming ALL of it. Today."

"Last one in is a soggy FLAKE!" yelled Yaki, and the twins hopped down the sand. SPLOOSH.

---

The warm water took them to the bonito-wave. Like floating in warm honey.

"Tako," gasped Yaki, "we are FLOATING."

"We are floating!" cried Tako, paddling the other way, so the twins turned a slow silly circle.

"We've come awfully far," said Yaki. "I can't see our bounce-marks anymore."

"Pfft, we're fine," said Tako, braver by exactly one sauce-freckle. "What could possibly—"

---

The water lifted them on a long slow swell and set them down turning.

Gently at first. Then faster. A sneaky current had found them and wanted a ride.

"I'd like to go back to the sand now," said Yaki carefully.

Tako paddled left. Yaki paddled right. Round they went, faster, bonito wave flapping.

A seaweed oar snapped. The bonito wave flopped. The beach shrank to a thumbnail.

---

Then, a roar from the sky. A scarlet seaplane with a round nose swooped down. KA-SPLOOSH.

Its wave rocked the whole bay. The current, thrilled, whirled the twins twice as fast.

A stout goggled pig in a brown flight jacket leaned out. "One takoyaki," said Porco Rosso. "To go."

He scooped them up in a long-handled net and squinted. "I ordered ONE," grumbled Porco.

"We come as TWO!" shouted the twins together. The net ripped. PLOP. Straight back into the whirlpool.

"Two for the price of one," grumbled Porco, arms folded on his wing. "And I still got nothing."

---

Then from below came a sound. Fwoomp. Fwoomp. Something big, gentle, and in no hurry.

Up rose a shell patterned like green and gold tiles, two slow flippers, the calmest face ever.

"Well, hello there," said the sea turtle. "You two seem to be going in circles."

"We're STUCK that way!" shouted both twins together.

"I'm a **kame** (kah-meh) — turtle," she said. "Are you two paddling in different directions?"

Their sauce zig-zags went pink. "...Maybe," they mumbled.

"Then it's not a current problem," said Kame. "It's a teamwork problem. Easiest kind."

---

"One calls it," said Kame. "The other follows. Push at the very same moment."

"I'll call it," said Tako.

"You ALWAYS call it," said Yaki. "I have a freckle too, right here—"

The current swung them round twice more. Dizzy. Extremely dizzy.

"Okay," said Yaki. "Tako calls it. I trust you."

"Paddle on THREE," called Tako. "One, two, THREE!" Both halves pushed on the same heartbeat.

WHOOSH. They shot straight out of the loop. "WE DID IT!" they screamed, bonking heads on purpose.

---

"Now," said Kame, "want to see the umi the way a turtle sees it?"

They rode the warm wash behind her flippers past a coral cave glowing orange.

An Oshawott paddled past on its back, polishing its belly shell with a proud squeak.

Past a sunken rice bowl, now the hat of a hermit crab, extremely pleased with himself.

---

Kame set them in the warm shallows as the sun turned the umi to melted gold.

"The sea always loops back around," she said. "So do friends." One last flipper wave. Fwoomp.

The twins flopped onto the sand, dripping, just the right amount of tired.

"Roll us once—" sighed Tako.

"—and we come as two," finished Yaki, and the twins squished closer.
