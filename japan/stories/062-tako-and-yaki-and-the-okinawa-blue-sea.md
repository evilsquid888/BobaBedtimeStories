# Story 62: Tako and Yaki and the Okinawa Blue Sea

**Main Character:** Tako & Yaki (takoyaki twins)
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A turquoise tropical beach in sunny Okinawa
**Theme:** Adventure is sweeter shared with your other half
**Japanese Spotlight:** うみ (umi) = sea

---

The very first thing Tako and Yaki did when they reached the Okinawa beach was bonk into each other. They always did. They were two golden batter-balls connected by a wobbly little wave of bonito flakes on top, drizzled with zig-zags of sauce, and when one of them rolled, the OTHER one came along whether it wanted to or not.

"Look look LOOK!" said Tako, bouncing so hard the sauce zig-zags wiggled.

"I'm looking, I'm attached to you, I CAN'T not look!" laughed Yaki.

In front of them stretched the bluest blue they had ever seen — water so clear and turquoise it looked like someone had spilled a giant bottle of melted sky-flavored soda across the sand.

"It's the **うみ** (umi) = sea," whispered Yaki, awed.

"The umi," repeated Tako. "It's the size of a million takoyaki trays!"

And then, because they were takoyaki and could not help it, they rolled straight down the warm sand toward the water — bonking, giggling, and finishing each other's sentence the whole way.

"Last one in is a soggy—"

"—FLAKE!"

*Sploosh.*

The warm Okinawa water swallowed them up to their bonito-wave with a happy gurgle. It was the most wonderful feeling in the world — like floating in a giant cup of warm honey, except the honey was the entire shining sea and it tasted faintly of salt and sunshine.

"Tako," gasped Yaki, paddling their stubby half. "Tako, we are FLOATING."

"We are floating!" cried Tako, paddling their stubby half the OTHER direction so the two of them spun in a slow, silly circle. "Stop swimming that way, you noodle!"

"YOU stop swimming that way!"

They spun. And spun. And then they laughed so hard they sank a little, blew a string of bubbles, and popped back up grinning. That was the thing about being two balls stuck together on one wobbly wave — you could never quite go where you wanted, but you always went there *together*.

They bobbed out past the gentle little waves to where the water turned deep and dreamy. Tiny silver fish flickered beneath them like spilled glitter. A pink coral fan waved hello. Far below, the sand glowed pale gold, lit by sunbeams that wiggled down through the water like ribbons.

"This is the COOLEST place we've ever rolled," said Tako.

"It's even better than the festival griddle," agreed Yaki. Then a tiny worry-bubble floated up in their batter. "Tako… we've come awfully far out. I can't see our footprints anymore. Or — well. We don't have feet. I can't see our roll-marks."

"Pfft. We're fine," said Tako, who was always the brave half by exactly one freckle of sauce. "We've got each other. What could possibly—"

The water lifted them up on a long, slow swell.

When it set them back down, they were spinning. Gently at first. Then a little faster. A sneaky-cozy current had found the twins and decided to take them for a ride, swirling them out toward a darker patch of deeper blue.

"Tako," said Yaki very carefully, "I would like to go back to the sand now."

"Me too," admitted Tako, paddling. "But every time I paddle, you paddle the wrong way, and we just spin!"

"That's because every time YOU paddle—"

"We BOTH paddle wrong, I KNOW," wailed Tako. They spun in another helpless little circle, faster now, the warm water tugging them along like a friendly puppy who didn't understand it was pulling. The beach with its leaning palm trees and umbrellas grew smaller. The bonito-flake wave on top of them drooped just a tiny bit.

For the first time all day, the twins went quiet.

That, my sleepy friend, is the loudest thing in the whole world — a pair of giggly twins going quiet at the very same moment.

Then, from below, came a sound. Not a scary sound. A friendly *fwoomp, fwoomp* — the sound of something big and gentle moving through water with no hurry at all.

Up rose an enormous, beautiful shape. A shell patterned like a mosaic of green and gold tiles. Two soft flippers fanning the water like slow wings. And a calm, ancient, kindly face with the sleepiest eyes the twins had ever seen.

"Well, hello there," said the sea turtle, in a voice as slow and warm as the tide. "You two seem to be going in circles."

"We're STUCK that way!" the twins shouted together, which made the turtle's eyes crinkle.

"I'm **かめ** (kame) = turtle," said the turtle, drifting closer. "And out here we call this little spin the merry-go-current. It carries things in big lazy loops. Most who fall into it panic and paddle every-which-way and only spin faster." A slow, knowing smile. "Tell me. Are you two, perhaps, paddling in different directions?"

Tako and Yaki looked at each other. Their sauce zig-zags went pink.

"…Maybe," they mumbled.

"Ahh." The turtle gave a soft, bubbly chuckle. "Then you don't have a current problem. You have a *teamwork* problem. And THAT, little golden ones, is the easiest kind to fix."

Kame the turtle taught them the trick. "Pick one of you to call it," she said. "The other follows. Push at the very same moment, and you'll cut clean across the loop instead of riding it round and round."

"I'll call it," said Tako, puffing up bravely.

"You always call it," said Yaki.

"Because I'm braver by one sauce-freckle!"

"You are NOT, I have a freckle right here—"

"Children," said the turtle, with the eternal patience of someone three hundred years old. "The umi is very large and very patient. It will wait. But the longer you argue, the dizzier you'll get."

They were, in fact, getting quite dizzy.

So Yaki took a breath. "Okay. Tako calls it. I trust you, Tako."

And Tako, hearing that, felt something braver than any sauce-freckle bloom right in the middle of their batter. "Ready," they called. "Paddle on THREE. One — two — *THREE!*"

Both halves of the takoyaki twins pushed at the exact same heartbeat, in the exact same direction.

They shot forward — *whoosh!* — slicing right across the lazy loop, free of the merry-go-current at last, gliding in a perfectly straight, perfectly together line.

"WE DID IT!" they screamed, and this time the scream was pure joy.

"You did," said Kame, paddling easily alongside. "Now. As long as you've come all this way — would you like to see the umi the way a turtle sees it?"

Oh, they would. They WOULD.

Kame the turtle dipped low and let the twins ride the warm wash just behind her flippers, and she gave them the grandest tour Okinawa had ever offered. She showed them a cave of coral that glowed soft orange, where shy little clownfish peeked out and dared each other to say hello. She showed them a meadow of swaying sea-grass where baby sea slugs as small as sprinkles inched along, polka-dotted and proud. She showed them a sunken old rice bowl, lost from a fishing boat ages ago, now home to a hermit crab who wore it like a hat and was extremely pleased with himself.

"Everyone down here shares," Kame explained, as a parade of blue tang fish swirled around the twins in a living ribbon. "The reef is one big playground, and the umi is one big *うみ* full of friends. No one plays alone for long."

"That's just like us!" said Tako.

"We never play alone," agreed Yaki. "We literally can't. We're stuck together."

"Best stuck in the whole sea," they said at once, and bonked their golden heads together gently, the way they had a thousand times — except this time they meant it extra.

The sun began to slide low and turn the whole turquoise umi into a sheet of melted gold and pink. Kame carried them all the way back to the warm shallows, right up to where their roll-marks waited in the sand.

"Thank you, Kame," said the twins. "Will we see you again?"

"The umi is wide," said the turtle, with her slow, kindly smile, "but it always loops back around. That's the nice thing about a sea, and about a friend. You always come round to each other in the end." She gave one last wave of her great green flipper. "Roll safe, golden ones. And remember — paddle *together*."

Then, with a soft *fwoomp, fwoomp*, she slipped back into the deep blue and was gone, leaving only a trail of bubbles that caught the sunset light like tiny floating jewels.

Tako and Yaki rolled up onto the warm evening sand, dripping and happy and just the right amount of tired. They flopped down side by side — well, they were always side by side — and watched the first stars wink awake over the darkening sea.

"Best adventure ever," sighed Tako.

"Best adventure ever," sighed Yaki. "And you know what made it best?"

"That we did it as two?"

"That we did it as two," said Yaki. "Roll us once—"

"—and we come as two!" finished Tako.

And under the wide Okinawa sky, with the gentle umi whispering goodnight against the sand, the takoyaki twins squished a little closer and let their eyes go soft and sleepy.

Snuggle close, like two golden balls on one cozy wave. The sea is humming its bedtime song just for you. Goodnight.
