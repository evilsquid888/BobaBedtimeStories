# Story 62: Tako and Yaki and the Okinawa Blue Sea

**Main Character:** Tako & Yaki (takoyaki twins)
**Guest Characters:** Kame, a very old sea turtle; a hermit crab wearing a rice bowl
**Setting:** A turquoise tropical beach in sunny Okinawa
**Theme:** Adventure is sweeter shared with your other half
**Japanese Spotlight:** **umi** (oo-mee) — sea

---

The first thing Tako and Yaki did on the Okinawa beach was bonk into each other.

They always did. Two golden batter-balls joined by one wobbly wave of bonito flakes.

"Look look LOOK!" said Tako, bouncing so hard the sauce zig-zags wiggled.

"I'm attached to you," laughed Yaki. "I CAN'T not look!"

Before them lay the **umi** (oo-mee) — the sea — turquoise as a spilled bottle of sky-flavored soda.

"It's the size of a million takoyaki trays," breathed Tako. "We're going IN."

"Last one in is a soggy—" said Yaki, hopping down the sand.

"—FLAKE!" finished Tako. SPLOOSH.

---

The warm water swallowed them to the bonito-wave. It was like floating in a giant cup of honey.

"Tako," gasped Yaki, "we are FLOATING."

"We are floating!" cried Tako, paddling the other direction, so the twins turned a slow silly circle.

Silver fish flickered below like spilled glitter. A pink coral fan waved hello.

"We've come awfully far," said Yaki. "I can't see our bounce-marks anymore."

"Pfft, we're fine," said Tako, who was braver by exactly one sauce-freckle. "What could possibly—"

---

The water lifted them on a long slow swell and set them down turning.

Gently at first. Then faster. A sneaky current had found the twins and wanted a ride.

"I'd like to go back to the sand now," said Yaki carefully.

Tako paddled left. Yaki paddled right. Round they went, faster.

They tried a seaweed oar. It snapped. They tried flapping the bonito wave. It flopped.

The beach shrank to a thumbnail. The bonito flakes drooped.

For the first time all day, both twins went quiet, which is the loudest sound in the world.

---

Then from below came a sound. Fwoomp. Fwoomp. Something big, gentle, and in no hurry.

Up rose a shell patterned like green and gold tiles, two slow flippers, and the sleepiest face ever.

"Well, hello there," said the sea turtle. "You two seem to be going in circles."

"We're STUCK that way!" shouted both twins together.

"I'm a **kame** (kah-meh) — turtle," she said. "Tell me. Are you two paddling in different directions?"

Their sauce zig-zags went pink. "...Maybe," they mumbled.

"Then you don't have a current problem," said Kame. "You have a teamwork problem. Easiest kind."

---

"One of you calls it," said Kame. "The other follows. Push at the very same moment."

"I'll call it," said Tako.

"You ALWAYS call it," said Yaki. "I have a freckle too, right here—"

The current swung them round twice more. They were getting extremely dizzy.

"Okay," said Yaki. "Tako calls it. I trust you."

"Paddle on THREE," called Tako, feeling braver than any freckle. "One, two, THREE!"

Both halves pushed at the exact same heartbeat. WHOOSH. They shot straight across the loop and out.

"WE DID IT!" they screamed, and bonked heads on purpose.

---

"Now," said Kame, "want to see the umi the way a turtle sees it?"

They rode the warm wash behind her flippers past a coral cave glowing orange.

Past a sunken rice bowl, now the hat of a hermit crab, who was extremely pleased with himself.

"Everyone down here shares," said Kame. "No one plays alone for long."

"We can't," said Yaki. "We're stuck together. Best stuck in the whole sea."

---

Kame set them in the warm shallows as the sun turned the umi to melted gold.

"The sea always loops back around," she said. "So do friends." One last flipper wave. Fwoomp.

The twins flopped onto the sand, dripping, just the right amount of tired.

"Roll us once—" sighed Tako.

"—and we come as two," finished Yaki, and the twins squished closer.
