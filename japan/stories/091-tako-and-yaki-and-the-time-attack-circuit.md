# Story 91: Tako and Yaki and the Time-Attack Circuit

**Main Character:** Tako and Yaki (takoyaki twins)
**Guest Characters:** Kei the kei-truck; Old Gori, the shaved-ice lantern-keeper
**Setting:** A tidy little go-kart-style circuit under the evening lights
**Theme:** Finishing together beats finishing first
**Japanese Spotlight:** **hayai** (hah-YAH-ee) — fast
**Cameo:** Tanya the Evil (Saga of Tanya the Evil) — flies in on a glowing orb, salutes the clock, declares the lap times inefficient, demonstrates straight into the puddle and files a soggy complaint with Old Gori; walk-on: a Kamen Rider (Kamen Rider)

---

Behind the market curled the cutest little racetrack you ever saw, lined with orange lanterns.

One little hill, one swooshy S-bend, a checkered finish. Tako and Yaki gasped one double-gasp.

"Did you SEE that?" said Tako, bouncing so hard his bonito flakes fluttered like confetti.

"I saw it first," said Yaki, bouncing exactly as hard, one half-second later.

"Tonight," said Tako, "we become the FASTEST snack in the whole entire market."

At the gate bounced a butter-yellow truck in a knitted sweater, headlights blinking.

"Putt-putt! Hop on," beeped Kei. "One smooth lap, and the clock does the rest."

---

Above the finish line hung a big round clock with one red arrow.

"A short arrow trip means **hayai** (hah-YAH-ee) — fast," said Kei. "But smooth is the secret."

"Smooth," repeated Tako, in the voice of someone already ignoring the word.

"Smooth," repeated Yaki, in exactly the same voice, because of course he did.

---

Green light. Vroooom, politely. Off they zipped, lanterns blurring into gold ribbons.

"Lean left for the curve!" called Kei.

"LEFT!" yelled Tako, leaning left.

"RIGHT!" yelled Yaki, leaning right.

The twins wobbled off the flatbed, bounced over the hill, and boinged into the hay bales.

"I SAID left," said Tako, upside down.

"I HEARD right," said Yaki, also upside down. "We have the same EARS."

Kei scooped them up and finished the lap. The red arrow had crawled almost full circle.

---

"FASTER this time!" said Tako, bouncing on the flatbed.

"Here it comes," said Kei. "The puddle."

Green light. Kei hit the hill at top speed and boinged off like a grasshopper.

SPLOSH. A fan of water rose, glittering like a thousand tiny diamonds, and came down. Splat.

Two soggy takoyaki sat in stunned silence, bonito wave drooping like a wet noodle.

"Your FACE!" howled Tako.

"Your face is MY face!" howled Yaki, and they laughed until they hiccuped.

---

Then a glowing orb zipped over the lanterns, carrying a tiny officer in a greatcoat and cap.

"Lap times: inefficient," said Tanya, saluting the clock. "This circuit requires management."

She zoomed the S-bend herself to demonstrate, cap first, and plunged straight into the puddle. SPLOSH.

Dripping, she floated to Old Gori, the slushy shaved-ice lantern-keeper, and stamped a soggy blank form.

"A formal complaint," said Tanya. "Against the puddle." Old Gori blinked once, very slowly.

She saluted from high above the far hill, orb glowing, still grumbling about paperwork.

"Even the puddle got in trouble," whispered Yaki.

---

"Funnest racers I've ever carried," said Kei, shaking dry like a happy dog. "Not the smoothest."

"One of you yanks left, one yanks right," said Kei. "All that yanking goes somewhere."

"**Issho ni** (EE-shoh nee) — together," said Kei. "Four wheels, one direction."

"Roll us once, we come as two," whispered Tako, and they squished together like one warm egg.

---

Green light. Kei purred forward. The twins felt the curve coming the way you feel a yawn.

"Leeean left," murmured Tako, and they tipped as one, like an egg in a warm bowl.

The S-bend melted away beneath them, left-right-left, smooth as condensed milk over shaved ice.

Across the finish. Beep-boop. The red arrow had barely moved. Fastest lap of the evening.

"But we went SLOW!" said Tako.

"Together is the hayai you were chasing," said Kei, "you wobbly, wonderful weirdos."

---

Old Gori waddled over with a plate of hot, sizzling takoyaki, lantern swinging.

"Henshin!" shouted a Kamen Rider at the gate, posing on his motorcycle, red scarf flapping.

"To being eaten one day," said Tako, "as the fastest snack in town. Together."

Kei purred her sleepiest putt-putt, and the lanterns glowed soft along the empty track.

---

## Goodnight Blessing
The world may whisper that you must be first. The twins know better: the smoothest journeys are the ones you take side by side. Breathe slow, like a truck putt-putting home. Lean into the dark together. Goodnight.
