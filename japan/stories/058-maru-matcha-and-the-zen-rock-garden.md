# Story 58: Maru Matcha and the Zen Rock Garden

**Main Character:** Maru Matcha
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A raked-gravel Zen rock garden at a quiet Kyoto temple
**Theme:** Stillness can be a kind of play
**Japanese Spotlight:** いし (ishi) = stone

---

The trouble with bringing seven of your wiggliest friends to the quietest garden in all of Kyoto is that the garden does NOT wiggle back, and the friends absolutely refuse to believe that.

"Maru," whispered Pock Ramune, fizzing so hard his bubbles popped right off his frosty blue shoulders, "what is it? Where's the ride? Where's the buttons? POP — where's the FUN?"

Maru Matcha set down her tiny bamboo whisk and looked at the wooden gate. Beyond it spread a wide rectangle of pale gravel, raked into long curving lines like the ripples on a still pond, with five mossy stones rising up out of it like sleepy little islands. No buttons. No music. No churros. Just gravel, and stones, and the soft gray hush of an old temple at dusk.

Maru smiled her small half-moon smile.

"This," she said calmly, "is the most exciting place I know."

Seven faces stared at her. Somewhere, a single bird said *one* word and stopped.

"Oh no," whispered Kori Kakigori. "She's finally melted."

---

Maru had invited them all on purpose. She had noticed, lately, that her friends never stopped. Tako and Yaki bounced from breakfast to bedtime. Pock zipped in dizzy loops. Pepper-kun sizzled even in his sleep. And dear, dramatic Kori announced every five minutes that he was *positively expiring* from boredom, which is a hard thing to do when you are mostly ice.

So Maru had walked them up a hill of crooked stone steps, past lanterns and pines, to a little temple where a kind old monk-bun had waved them through and pressed one cool finger to his lips. *Shhh.*

"Sit," said Maru now, lowering herself onto the smooth wooden veranda at the garden's edge. The matcha-cream swirl on her head wobbled gently and went still. "Just sit. And watch."

"Watch WHAT," said Tako and Yaki together, plopping down with a double thump.

"The いし," said Maru. "**いし** (ishi) = stone."

There was a long pause.

"...They're not doing anything," said Yaki.

"They're not even ROLLING," said Tako, deeply offended on the stones' behalf.

"That's the game," said Maru. "Watch slow. Feel calm. The stones won't move toward you." Her eyes twinkled the tiniest bit. "So you'll have to move toward them."

---

Now, you and I both know that telling a takoyaki twin to *sit still* is like telling rain to stay in the sky. For about four whole seconds, everyone tried. Pepper-kun held his sizzle. Kori held his sunglasses. Dai Daruma held his breath and immediately fell over with a soft *bonk*, then bounced right back up, eyebrows fierce.

"Fall down seven, bounce up eight!" he announced. "But — er — what are we bouncing UP to?"

"Shhh," said Maru, so gently it felt like a blanket.

And then a funny thing happened. Because there was nothing loud to look at, the friends started noticing the quiet things instead.

Mero Melonpan noticed first, of course, because Mero notices tiny wonders the way other people notice loud ones. "Ohhh," he breathed, sleepy and slow. "Look. The gravel lines... they go *around* the big stone. Like the stone is a little island, and the gravel is the sea, frozen mid-splash." His crackly crust crinkled into a dreamy grin. "Crackle on the outside... ocean on the inside."

Niko Nigiri sat up very straight. He had been longing to fix something, to organize, to do it the *right way* — but there was nothing to tidy here. The garden was already, somehow, perfect. "Someone raked these lines," he murmured, half to himself. "So carefully. Curve by curve. They did it neatly..." He blinked. "...and it's beautiful, and then they just — let it be."

"That's the whole secret," said Maru softly. "Sometimes the doing IS the calm. And sometimes the *not*-doing is, too."

---

The light went golden, then rosy, then a soft sleepy gray. And one by one, the wiggliest dessert friends in Kyoto fell into the strangest adventure of all: the adventure of *paying attention.*

Pock Ramune, who had never once stopped moving, found himself watching a single leaf drift down, down, down. It landed on the gravel without a sound. Pock did not pop. He just... watched it land. "Whoa," he whispered, the smallest pop he had ever popped. "I didn't know things could be quiet AND amazing at the same time."

Kori Kakigori discovered that if he held very, very still, he stopped feeling like he was about to melt. The cool stone air settled over his shaved-ice peak like a soft hand. "Sweeties," he announced — but in a whisper, a *Kori* whisper, which is still louder than everyone else — "I think this is the COOLEST I have ever felt. And I mean that... on the inside." He sniffed, dramatically moved. A drip of strawberry syrup rolled down like a happy tear. "Don't tell anyone I said something heartfelt."

"We're all going to tell everyone," said Tako.

"Constantly," agreed Yaki.

But even the twins had gone gentle now. They had found a tiny ant marching across the gravel, hauling a crumb three times its size, and they were silently, breathlessly, *rooting for it.*

"Go, ant," mouthed Tako.

"You've got this, ant," mouthed Yaki.

The ant made it. Nobody cheered out loud. But seven hearts cheered on the inside, which Maru thought was the very best kind of cheer there is.

---

Then came the moment that nearly broke the spell.

Pepper-kun had been sizzling quietly — *too* quietly for a hot iron plate — and the holding-still had built up a great deal of bottled-up SHOWMANSHIP inside him. He simply could not contain it one second longer.

"OKAY but here's my IDEA," he burst out, sparks flying. "What if — hear me out — what if we RAKE the garden into a giant smiley face?! And then build a ramp! And then —"

Every leaf in Kyoto seemed to gasp.

But Maru did not scold him. She never scolds. She simply picked up her tiny bamboo whisk, dipped it ever so lightly, and drew one slow, curving line in the air. *Whisk... slow.* "Pepper," she said, "what if the most exciting thing you can do... is exactly nothing, beautifully?"

Pepper-kun's sizzle sputtered. He frowned. He thought. This was a brand-new flavor for him, and he wasn't sure he liked it. "But doing nothing is so... boring," he said.

"Try it," said Maru. "Just look at one stone. Pick your favorite いし. And wonder about it."

Grumbling a tiny *tss*, Pepper-kun looked at the smallest stone — a round, mossy little fellow tucked behind the big one, easy to miss. And the longer he looked, the more he wondered. *How long had it sat there? Had it watched a hundred years of sunsets? Was it lonely behind the big stone, or did it like the cozy shade?* Pepper-kun's sizzle slowed to a warm, steady hum, like a kettle deciding to be a friend instead of a noise.

"Huh," he said at last, very softly. "I think... I think that's my stone. The little one. The one nobody else picked."

"He's been there longer than any of us," said Maru. "Just being. Just being enough."

Pepper-kun was quiet for a long, long time. Which, for Pepper-kun, was the loudest love he had ever shown.

---

By now the sky had gone the deep velvet-blue of bedtime. The first star blinked open above the temple roof, and the garden looked silver, the gravel ripples glowing softly like a still moonlit sea. The five stones sat in the hush, exactly as they had for centuries, patient as grandparents.

Dai Daruma, who had been determined to be *good at* sitting still (and had fallen over and bounced back up at least eleven more times), finally went calm and round and steady. "You know," he whispered, "I always thought being brave meant DOING the big scary thing. Bouncing up, charging ahead." He looked at the stones. "But maybe being brave is also... staying. Sitting with the quiet, even when it itches. The stones never run. The stones just *stay.* And that's a kind of brave too."

Maru's whole soft face lit up. "Daruma," she said, "you just found the deepest stone of all."

And then — because the best calm always loops gently back to giggles — Mero Melonpan let out a tiny, sleepy snore. He had nodded off sitting up, his crackly crust drooping, a happy crumb-sparkle dribbling onto the veranda. The whole group dissolved into the *quietest* laughter you ever heard, shoulders shaking, hands over mouths, trying so hard not to wake him that they laughed even harder.

"Shhh," giggled Pock.

"You shhh," giggled Yaki.

"WE'LL shhh together," whispered Tako, "like Momo always says — squish together, stay together!"

And they did. They scooched close on the cool wood, a little pile of warm sweet friends, and watched the moon climb over five quiet stones and a thousand silver ripples, and not one of them wanted to be anywhere louder than right here.

"Maru," whispered Niko at last. "You were right. This IS the most exciting place you know."

Maru Matcha closed her calm half-moon eyes and smiled.

"No," she said. "The exciting place was always inside us. The garden just held very still... so we could finally hear it."

---

## Goodnight Blessing

Little one, the world will spin and fizz and sizzle and shout, and most days you'll spin right along with it — and that's lovely. But tonight, like Maru's friends, let yourself go soft and still. Let your busy thoughts settle down like gravel raked into gentle waves, and pick one quiet wonder to rest upon, the way Pepper-kun found his small forgotten stone. You do not have to *do* anything to be wonderful; like the いし in the garden, you are already enough, just by being here, just by being you. Breathe slow. Feel calm. Whisk your heart into a soft, sleepy swirl. Goodnight.
