# Story 9: Gacha and the Capsule-Toy Arcade

**Main Character:** Gacha the Gachapon
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A wall of gachapon capsule machines in a glowing Tokyo arcade
**Theme:** The surprise you get is sometimes better than the one you wanted
**Japanese Spotlight:** **Gachapon** (gah-chah-pohn) — capsule toy

---

Late at night, when the last human had wandered home and the arcade lights dimmed to a sleepy glow, the capsule machines came alive.

And nobody came more alive than Gacha.

Gacha was a glossy two-tone capsule — sunny yellow on top, bright cherry-red on the bottom — with a surprise-filled grin that split right across his middle. He was round and smooth and could roll faster than a startled marble, which is exactly what he did the moment the arcade went quiet.

"Wheeeee!" Gacha cried, rolling down the long polished shelf. "It's GACHA NIGHT!"

You should know that a **gachapon** (gah-chah-pohn) — a capsule toy — is the most exciting thing in any arcade, because nobody — not one single soul — knows what's inside until it pops open. Gacha loved that. Gacha *was* that. He bounced off a shelf, spun three happy circles, and announced to the whole sparkling wall of machines:

"Tonight, friends, I am going to win the RAREST prize in the entire arcade!"

---

The wall of capsule machines stretched up, up, up — a glowing cliff of glass domes packed with hundreds of capsules, all twinkling under the soft arcade lights like a galaxy you could spin with a coin. Each machine had a little paper picture taped to the front showing the treasures inside: tiny mochi figurines, mini food charms, teeny umbrellas, baby robots, and one machine — *one* — with a golden sticker that read **rea** (reh-ah) — rare.

That was the one.

"Behold," Gacha whispered, gazing up at it like a knight gazing at a castle. "The Legendary Golden Mochi Prince. Only one exists in the whole machine. They say he wears a tiny crown and a cape made of red-bean clouds."

A nearby capsule — a chubby pink one named Pippa — rolled over. "Oooh, that sounds amazing! How do you get him?"

"You don't *get* him," said Gacha grandly. "You *hope* for him. That, dear Pippa, is the whole point of being a gachapon. You give it a turn — surprise inside!"

"Can I come?" asked Pippa.

"Can WE come?" squeaked two skinny green capsules, tumbling over each other. They were twins named Mimi and Pipi, and they always arrived as a clattery, giggling pile.

Gacha hesitated. A hero, he felt, ought to go on his quest *alone*. It was more dramatic that way. But Pippa was already wobbling beside him, and the twins were already arguing about who had asked first.

"Fine, fine," Gacha sighed, secretly a little pleased. "But keep up. A rare prize waits for no capsule."

---

The first machine was called the Mighty Roller. To win, you had to roll yourself up a curvy ramp and tip into the chute at the very top — and *plonk!* — out would pop a fresh capsule with a prize inside.

Gacha squared up at the bottom of the ramp. He took a breath. He rolled.

He got exactly halfway up, wobbled like a confused egg, and rolled *aaaall* the way back down, bonking gently into Pippa.

"Oof," said Pippa.

"That was a practice roll," Gacha announced.

He tried again. And again. On the fourth try, Mimi and Pipi got behind him and *pushed*, giggling the whole way, and the three of them tumbled up the ramp in a clattering pile and tipped over the top together.

*Plonk! Plonk! Plonk!*

Out popped three capsules.

Gacha cracked his open, heart thumping. Inside was... a tiny rubber duck wearing sunglasses.

"A duck," said Gacha flatly. "I rolled up an entire mountain for a duck in sunglasses."

"He's SO cool, though," breathed Mimi.

"The coolest," agreed Pipi.

Gacha looked at the little duck. The little duck looked back, extremely cool. Despite himself, Gacha snorted a laugh. "Okay. He *is* pretty cool." He balanced the duck on top of his head like a tiny captain. "Onward! The Golden Mochi Prince still awaits!"

---

The second machine made them *spin*.

It was a great glass drum, and to win you had to climb inside and let it turn you round and round and round until — *whoosh* — it flung you out the bottom with a capsule.

"I'll go first," said Gacha bravely, "because I am the most experienced spinner."

He climbed in. The drum began to turn. Slow at first. Then faster. Then SO fast that Gacha's grin smeared sideways and his vision filled with stars and his voice came out all wobbly: "I REGRET THIS DECISIOOOOON—"

*Whoosh!* Out he flew, dizzy as a top, and rolled to a stop upside down against Pippa's side.

"Did I get the Prince?" he mumbled, world still spinning.

Pippa peeked at the capsule that had popped out with him. She cracked it open. Inside sat a teeny tiny **daruma** (dah-roo-mah) — a round good-luck doll — crimson and roly-poly, with one painted eye and big determined eyebrows.

"It's a daruma," said Pippa softly. "You make a wish on him and paint his other eye when it comes true. He's lucky!"

Gacha, still upside down, considered the little doll's brave eyebrows. "Hm. He looks like he never gives up."

"Fall down seven, bounce up eight," squeaked the tiny toy daruma in a tiny voice, doing its very best Dai Daruma impression, and wobbled, and righted itself.

"...I like him," Gacha admitted, flipping right-side up. "But he is STILL not the Golden Mochi Prince. To the next machine!"

The twins cheered. Pippa tucked the duck and the daruma somewhere safe. And on they rolled.

---

By the eighth machine, Gacha's grin was getting tired.

He had won a tiny umbrella that turned inside out. A mini robot that only walked backward. A capsule that, when opened, contained... a *smaller* capsule, which contained an even smaller one, which contained a single sesame seed wearing a hat. (The twins laughed so hard at the sesame seed that Mimi got the hiccups, and you cannot imagine how silly a hiccuping capsule sounds — *pop-HIC-pop-HIC* — until you have heard one.)

But no Golden Mochi Prince.

Gacha rolled up to the last machine — the rea one, glowing gold, the Prince's machine — and stared up at it. His little duck-captain wobbled on his head. His daruma rode along beside him, bouncing back up every time he tipped.

"This is it," Gacha said quietly. "The big one. If he's not in here..." He swallowed. "Then this whole night was a flop."

"It wasn't a flop!" said Pippa. "We climbed a mountain!"

"We got SPUN!" cheered the twins.

"And I got a duck in sunglasses," Gacha admitted. "But I *wanted* the Prince." He took a deep breath and rolled into the machine's chute. The crank turned with a heavy *ka-CHUNK*. A single golden capsule dropped down toward him, shining like a tiny sun.

Everyone went silent.

Gacha's grin trembled open.

Inside was... a sticker.

A single, small, slightly crooked sticker. It showed a cartoon mochi with a crown drawn in marker, and underneath, in wobbly letters, someone had written: **Mata asobou** (mah-tah ah-soh-boh) — let's play again.

That was all. No tiny crown. No cape of red-bean clouds. No Legendary Golden Mochi Prince.

Just a sticker that said *let's play again*.

---

Gacha was quiet for a long, long moment.

Pippa rolled close. "Are you okay?"

And then — to everyone's surprise — Gacha started to *laugh*. A big, round, rolling laugh that bounced him back and forth on the shelf.

"It's a sticker!" he wheezed. "I rolled up a MOUNTAIN, got spun into NEXT WEEK, opened a capsule inside a capsule inside a capsule, and the legendary rare prize is a STICKER that someone drew with a marker!"

The twins started laughing too. Then Pippa. Then even the tiny daruma, bouncing up and down going *fall-down-seven-laugh-up-eight*.

When Gacha finally caught his breath, he looked around at all of them — Pippa beaming, the twins in a giggling heap, the duck-captain on his head, the brave little daruma at his side.

"You know what's funny?" he said. "I spent all night hoping for one rare prize. But the rare thing wasn't in any of the machines." He looked at his friends. "*You* came along. You pushed me up the ramp. You laughed at the sesame seed. You waited while I got spun silly. *That's* the prize you can't get from a capsule."

He stuck the crooked sticker right on his front, next to his grin, where everyone could see it.

"Let's play again," he read aloud, and grinned wider than ever. "Yes. Let's."

"So... you're not sad about the Prince?" asked Pippa.

Gacha thought about it. "Nope. The surprise I got was better than the one I wanted." He gave a happy spin. "Give it a turn — surprise inside! Turns out the surprise was friends all along."

And as the arcade lights dimmed even softer, the five of them rolled in a sleepy circle — Gacha, Pippa, Mimi, Pipi, and one very brave little daruma — telling the story of the night over and over, each time making the sesame seed's hat a little sillier, until one by one they wobbled to a stop and went still and warm on the glowing shelf.

The duck in sunglasses stood watch.

And somewhere up in the rare machine, behind the golden glass, the *real* Golden Mochi Prince waited patiently for tomorrow — happy, in his quiet way, that the very best prize had already been won.

---

## Goodnight Blessing
May your night be full of happy surprises, little one — the duck in sunglasses, the sesame seed in a hat, the friends who push you up the ramp when your wheels get tired. May you learn, like Gacha, that the prize you hope for is sweet, but the prize that rolls up beside you is sweeter still. Snuggle down now, soft and round as a capsule waiting to be opened in the morning. Whatever surprise tomorrow brings, you will be ready, and you will not face it alone. Give it a turn — there is wonder inside. Goodnight.
