# Story 50: The Grand Half-Way Festival of Friends

**Main Character:** Momo Mochi (mochi rice cake / daifuku)
**Guest Characters:** The whole cast — Niko Nigiri, Tama Onigiri, Rin Ramen, Tako & Yaki, Tai Taiyaki, Maru Matcha, Dai Daruma, Mero Melonpan, the Dango Three (Pinku, Shiro, Cha), Kori Kakigori, Pock Ramune, Dora the Dorayaki, Neko-Pan, Yuki the Snow Monkey-Manju, Coro the Korokke, Pepper-kun, Gacha the Gachapon, Sora the Soft-Cream, Kei the kei-truck, the Sushi-ya Express
**Setting:** A giant summer matsuri where the whole cast reunites halfway through their journey across Japan
**Theme:** Friendship is the best kind of homecoming
**Japanese Spotlight:** **tomodachi** (toh-moh-dah-chee) — friend

---

Momo Mochi could not stop bouncing. Off the grass, off a lantern pole, off a drum. BOING.

Below the hill, a thousand lanterns glowed over the biggest **matsuri** (mah-tsoo-ree) — festival — anyone had ever seen.

"Fifty stories!" sang Momo. "Halfway across Japan! And tonight, EVERYONE is coming!"

And there came the whole gang, a colorful river of treat-friends pouring onto the field.

Momo wanted one thing: to ring the Half-Way Bell at nightfall and watch every lantern float up.

"Squish together," she announced, arms stretching wide enough to hug a field, "stay together!"

---

In the middle of the field stood the **yagura** (yah-goo-rah) — the festival tower — tall and wooden.

Kori Kakigori leaned against it, melting one dramatic degree at a time. "Sweetie, the view is COOL."

Momo bounced up the tower steps. On top, the hook was empty. Just a sad swinging rope.

"The Half-Way Bell is GONE," said Momo. Her bounce went flat as a pancake.

"No bell, no fireworks, no lanterns," gasped Kori. "Saddest festival in HISTORY. Magnificent, but TRAGIC."

"We don't panic at a party," said Momo. "**Tomodachi** (toh-moh-dah-chee) — friends — find things together. Search!"

---

First, the goldfish-scooping pool. Momo leaned over to look and bounced straight in. SPLOOSH.

She came up with three goldfish on her head and a paper paddle in her mouth. No bell.

Next, the snow-cone stand. "I have searched my SOUL," declared Kori, "and also the snow-cone stand."

"You searched the snow-cone stand," said Momo, "because it has your picture on it."

---

Then the candy-apple cart, where Momo got stuck. Mochi, it turns out, is very sticky.

She pulled one arm free. The cart tipped. Twenty candy apples rolled off across the grass.

Momo bounced after them, catching apples on her arms, her head, her sticky cheeks.

She returned looking like a candy-apple tree. Still no bell.

Over by the tower, Mero Melonpan had dozed off standing up, crumbs sparkling.

"Mmf," he mumbled dreamily. "The bell went... to find its own party. Little ding. Went THAT way."

Everyone laughed. Then Momo went very still. "Mero. Which way?"

Mero waved one sleepy paw toward the food stalls and started snoring.

---

Past the sizzling stalls they went, following a faint, shy little ding. Ding. Ding.

It led to a glossy two-tone capsule in the grass, rocking guiltily back and forth.

Gacha the Gachapon popped open with a sheepish click. Inside, among his tiny mochi prizes, sat the bell.

"It was SHINING," said Gacha, words tumbling. "I gave it a turn. Then it fell IN."

"I couldn't get it OUT, so I hid," he wobbled. "Worst surprise ever."

Momo never scolded. She gave Gacha the softest squish in the history of squishes.

"You didn't lose it," said Momo. "You kept it safe, right where the best surprises live."

---

Back at the tower, the last daylight was slipping behind the hills. The hook waited, high.

"Climb ME, sweetie," said Kori, straightening to his full frosty height. "I'm basically a mountain."

Momo climbed. Kori was slippery with syrup. Momo slid down and landed, floomp, on dozing Mero.

Try two: Gacha bounced up, popped open mid-air, and rained mochi prizes on everyone.

Try three: Momo stretched BOTH taffy arms from Kori's peak, longer than she had ever stretched.

Gacha, held tight, opened one last time. Momo plucked the bell and hooked it. Click.

She gave the rope one great joyful PULL. DONNNNG.

---

Fireworks bloomed gold and pink. A thousand lanterns lifted off the field at once, into the night.

Below, the whole gang squished together, a field of glowing little treat-friends, looking up.

"Home isn't the end of the road," said Momo, arms wide. "Home is who you're standing with."

"Surprise inside," whispered Gacha, happy at last. "The surprise was us."

And under the lanterns, under the fireworks, they squished together. And stayed together.
