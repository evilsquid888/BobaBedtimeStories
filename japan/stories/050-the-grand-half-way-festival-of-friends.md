# Story 50: The Grand Half-Way Festival of Friends

**Main Character:** Momo Mochi (mochi rice cake / daifuku)
**Guest Characters:** The whole cast — Niko Nigiri, Tama Onigiri, Rin Ramen, Tako & Yaki, Tai Taiyaki, Maru Matcha, Dai Daruma, Mero Melonpan, the Dango Three (Pinku, Shiro, Cha), Kori Kakigori, Pock Ramune, Dora the Dorayaki, Neko-Pan, Yuki the Snow Monkey-Manju, Coro the Korokke, Pepper-kun, Gacha the Gachapon, Sora the Soft-Cream, Kei the kei-truck, the Sushi-ya Express
**Setting:** A giant summer matsuri where the whole cast reunites halfway through their journey across Japan
**Theme:** Friendship is the best kind of homecoming
**Japanese Spotlight:** **tomodachi** (toh-moh-dah-chee) — friend

---

Momo Mochi could not stop bouncing. She bounced off the train seat, off the window, off the little folding tray, and once — *boing!* — clean off the ceiling, where she stuck for a happy second before peeling away like warm taffy.

"Momo," sighed Niko Nigiri, straightening his nori belt for the eleventh time, "we are doing this neatly. We arrive, we set up the banner, *then* we celebrate."

But Momo was already pressed against the glass, her snow-white cheeks fogging it up. Below the hill, in a wide green field strung between two rivers, glowed the biggest **matsuri** (mah-tsoo-ree) — festival — anyone had ever seen. A thousand paper lanterns swayed like sleepy fireflies. Food stalls puffed sweet steam into the dusk.

"Fifty stories," Momo whispered, her rosy peach cheeks glowing. "We're halfway across Japan, everybody. Halfway! And tonight — *everyone's coming.*"

She turned around, arms already stretching wide.

"Squish together," she announced, "stay together!"

---

The Sushi-ya Express rumbled into the festival field with a cheerful *ding-ding* of its little bell, noren curtain flapping. Behind it, putt-putting proudly, came Kei the kei-truck, bouncing on her springs like an excited puppy, her knitted sweater-cover slightly askew.

"Putt-putt, we MADE it!" Kei beeped, and her headlight-eyes went wide as dinner plates. "Look at all the *lanterns!*"

There were, in fact, exactly one thousand lanterns. Dai Daruma had counted them. Twice. Out loud. With great determination.

"Nine hundred and ninety-nine," he had bellowed earlier, wobbling along a string of them, "AND one for luck, because we never give up on a thousand!"

The festival grounds filled fast. From the north came Yuki the Snow Monkey-Manju, cheeks pink from a hot-spring soak, towing Kori Kakigori — who was, as always, exactly one degree from melting and absolutely loving the drama of it.

"Sweeties!" Kori cried, sunglasses sliding down his icy slope. "I am MELTING with joy! Possibly also with the weather! Both! It's the COOLEST tragedy!"

Tako and Yaki rolled up together, bouncing in unison, sauce zig-zags gleaming. "We brought games—" "—and snacks—" "—and MORE games—" "—roll us once, we come as two!"

Momo squished her way through the whole crowd, hugging everyone she could reach, which was everyone, because her arms were extremely stretchy and her heart was even stretchier.

"This," she sighed happily, "is the best night of all fifty nights."

That was when she noticed the problem.

---

In the very center of the field stood the **yagura** (yah-goo-rah) — the festival tower — a tall wooden stage where, at the stroke of nightfall, the great Half-Way Bell was supposed to ring. When it rang, the fireworks would light, and all thousand lanterns would float up into the sky at once.

But the Half-Way Bell was *gone.*

The little hook on top of the tower was empty. Just a sad, swinging bit of rope, waving in the breeze like it had lost a tooth.

"Oh no," whimpered Coro the Korokke, a nervous puff of steam rising off his crispy golden top. "No bell, no signal. No signal, no fireworks. No fireworks, no— oh dear, oh dear."

Momo's bounce went flat as a pancake. (Dora the Dorayaki took mild offense.)

"Okay," said Momo, taking a deep, squishy breath. "Okay okay okay. We do *not* panic at a party. We find the bell. **Tomodachi** (toh-moh-dah-chee) — friends — fix things together, right?"

"Right!" beamed Neko-Pan, raising his lucky paw. "Wave for luck, share the luck! Somebody's bound to find it!"

Maru Matcha closed her calm half-moon eyes and gave her bamboo whisk a gentle twirl. "Whisk slow," she murmured. "Feel calm. A lost thing is only a found thing that's running late."

It was, everyone agreed, a *very* Maru thing to say.

So Momo divided everyone into search teams, which mostly meant pointing in directions while being hugged. "Water-people that way! Sleepy-people stay here so you don't nap in a river! Bouncy-people up high!"

"What am I?" asked Dai Daruma, eyebrows fierce.

"You," said Momo warmly, "are the *never-give-up* team. All by yourself."

Dai Daruma swelled with pride and immediately wobbled over backwards into a row of lanterns. *Bonk-bonk-bonk.* He bounced right back up. "Fall down seven," he announced to the startled lanterns, "bounce up eight!"

---

So the search began — and oh, what a wonderfully *un*-neat search it was.

The Dango Three pivoted across the field like a tiny carousel, Pinku leading, Shiro fretting, Cha thinking. "It was here at sunset!" said Pinku. "What if it rolled into a river?!" worried Shiro. "Or," said Cha thoughtfully, "what if something *carried* it?"

Pock Ramune zipped overhead in a trail of fizzy bubbles. "I'll check from the air — *pop!* — bells are heavy — *pop!* — somebody strong took it — *POP!*"

Tai Taiyaki "swam" through the air in eager loops, his red-bean heart glowing warm. "I'll check the river-stalls! Swim toward the sweet stuff — er — swim toward the *clues!*"

Rin Ramen waddled along on his stubby legs, broth steaming with worry. "If we don't find it," he fretted, "the whole party goes cold! And nothing should EVER be cold — everything's better warm and bubbly!"

Tako and Yaki searched the games tent — slowly, because they kept getting distracted. "Goldfish scooping!" "Ring toss!" "Look, a stall that scoops *mochi* into the water!" Momo had to gently un-stick them from a candy-apple cart. "Friends first," she reminded them, "treats second."

"Friends first," they agreed solemnly. Then, in a whisper: "But the candy apples are *RIGHT THERE.*"

Kori Kakigori draped himself dramatically across a bench. "I have searched my SOUL," he declared, "and also the snow-cone stand, and the bell is in NEITHER. This is the saddest festival in all of history! Magnificent, but tragic!"

"You searched the snow-cone stand," said Cha flatly, "because it had your photo on it."

"...It's a very GOOD photo," sniffed Kori.

Mero Melonpan, meanwhile, had fallen asleep standing up against the tower leg, crackly crust crumbs sparkling. "Mmf," he mumbled dreamily. "The bell went... to find its own party..."

Everyone laughed — and then everyone stopped laughing, because Momo had gone very still.

"Mero," she said slowly. "Say that again."

"Mmf?" Mero blinked his sleepy eyes. "I said... the bell went to find its own party. I heard it. Little *ding* sound. Went *that* way." He waved a vague, sleepy paw toward the food stalls. "Crackle on the outside... dreams on the inside..."

---

They followed the sound of a faint, shy little *ding.*

It led them past the takoyaki stall, past Pepper-kun sizzling his beef and rice with theatrical sparks ("Stir it, sizzle it, share it!"), past Sora the Soft-Cream serving slow, dreamy swirls — and straight to a small, glossy two-tone capsule, sitting in the grass and rocking guiltily back and forth.

"Gacha?" said Momo.

Gacha the Gachapon popped open with a sheepish *click.* Inside, cradled among his tiny mochi prizes, was the Half-Way Bell.

"I'm SO sorry," Gacha said in a tumble of words. "I saw it shining on the tower and I thought — *ooh, what a prize!* — and I gave it a turn — surprise inside! — and it rolled right *into* me and I couldn't get it back OUT and I got so embarrassed I rolled away to hide and— surprise?" He gave a tiny, miserable wobble. "Worst surprise I've ever had."

Momo did not scold him. Momo *never* scolded.

Instead she stretched out her warm taffy arms and gave Gacha the biggest, softest squish in the history of squishes.

"You didn't lose the bell," she said gently. "You *kept* it safe. Right in the middle of you. Where the best surprises always are."

Gacha's lid wobbled — but this time, happily.

"Now," grinned Momo, "let's give it back to the sky."

---

Getting the bell up the tower was a team job, and *that* was the whole point.

Dai Daruma rolled to the base and braced himself. "I'll be the bottom! Fall down seven, bounce up eight!" Niko Nigiri stacked up next, very straight, very proud. ("Let's do this neatly... or deliciously!") Tama Onigiri climbed on top with a bag of riceball snacks for energy ("I packed a little extra, just in case!").

It wobbled. It teetered. Niko Nigiri made a small, dignified squeak. "We are doing this *neat-ly,*" he insisted, even as the whole tower swayed like seaweed.

"We are doing this *togeth-er,*" Momo corrected gently, and she stretched her taffy arms around the whole shaky stack like a warm rubber band. Suddenly nothing wobbled at all. That, it turned out, was the secret: a stack of friends holds steadier when somebody hugs it.

Then Tako and Yaki bounced the bell up, hand to hand, *boing-boing-boing,* all the way to the empty hook.

It clicked into place.

The whole field held its breath.

And just as the last sliver of daylight slipped behind the hills, Momo Mochi — bouncing higher than she ever had, so high she seemed to hang in the lantern-light like a tiny full moon — reached out one stretchy arm and gave the rope a great, joyful *PULL.*

***DONNNNG.***

The Half-Way Bell rang out across all of Japan.

---

What happened next was the most beautiful thing any of them had ever seen.

The fireworks bloomed — gold and pink and matcha-green, the exact colors of the Dango Three, who shrieked with delight. A thousand lanterns lifted off the ground all at once, drifting up like a river flowing the wrong way, into the stars.

And in the glow, the whole squishy crowd of friends stood squished together, looking up.

"Fifty stories," said Dora softly, beret tilted back. "A good story is the best filling. But *this* — this is the best story of all."

"You know what I figured out?" said Momo, her cheeks rosy with firework-light. "We've traveled so far. Snow mountains. Hot springs. Big cities. Bullet trains zooming so fast my filling jiggled. And I kept thinking *home* was a place we'd find at the end."

She stretched her arms wide enough to hug the whole crowd at once.

"But home isn't the end of the road, silly me. Home is *who you're standing with.* Home is tomodachi. And I'm standing with all of mine."

Kori Kakigori burst into happy, melty tears. "I'm not crying, I'm just THAWING beautifully!"

Pock Ramune popped a fountain of celebration bubbles. Rin Ramen steamed so proudly he fogged his own nori name-tag. Neko-Pan waved his lucky paw at every single friend, one at a time, so nobody felt unwaved-at. And Gacha — brave, embarrassed, forgiven Gacha — popped open one last time and showered the whole crowd with tiny mochi prizes that pattered down like the sweetest, softest rain.

"Surprise inside," he whispered, happy at last. "The surprise was *us.*"

And under the lanterns, under the fireworks, under the great warm *donnng* still humming in the air, the whole cast did the only thing that made any sense.

They squished together.

And stayed together.

## Goodnight Blessing

Little one, the road ahead is long and full of wonderful surprises, but you never have to walk it alone. Somewhere tonight a thousand lanterns are floating up to keep your dreams company, and every single one was lit by a friend. May your heart be squishy and your cheeks be warm, and may you always know that home is wherever your favorite people are. Whisk slow, breathe deep, and let the day drift gently up and away. The bell has rung, the fireworks have faded, and now it is time to rest. Goodnight.
