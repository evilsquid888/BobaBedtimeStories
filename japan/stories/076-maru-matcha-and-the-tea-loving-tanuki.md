# Story 76: Maru Matcha and the Tea-Loving Tanuki

**Main Character:** Maru Matcha
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A countryside teahouse tucked among misty hills and rustling bamboo
**Theme:** Sharing turns a sneaky guest into a true friend
**Japanese Spotlight:** たぬき (tanuki) = raccoon dog

---

On a hill where the bamboo whispered secrets to the breeze, there sat the coziest little teahouse you ever did imagine. It had a curvy roof like a cat stretching after a nap, paper windows that glowed honey-gold at dusk, and one very calm green tea-friend inside: Maru Matcha.

Maru was a small jade-green bowl-head with a frothy swirl of matcha-cream hair, half-moon eyes, and a tiny bamboo whisk she carried like a wand. Tonight she had brewed her finest cup, the steam curling up in lazy little ribbons.

"Whisk slow," she murmured, settling onto her cushion. "Feel calm."

She lifted her cup. She lowered her cup to admire it.

And when she lifted it again — *slurrrp!* — it was empty.

Maru blinked her half-moon eyes. A perfectly good cup of tea, gone. The steam was even still rising from the rim, looking just as confused as she was.

"How curious," said Maru, very calmly, because Maru was always very calm. "Tea does not usually drink itself."

---

She brewed a second cup, whisking the bright green froth until it stood up like soft little mountains. This time she watched it the whole way through, eyes wide, whisk ready.

A leaf drifted past the window. Maru glanced at it — just for a heartbeat —

*Slurrrp-slurp-GULP!*

Empty again. And this time, a single fuzzy hair floated down into the bottom of the cup, brown and soft as a pinecone's whisker.

"Aha," said Maru, lifting the hair on the tip of her whisk. "A thief with fluff. The plot, as Dora would say, thickens like good red-bean filling."

She tiptoed around the teahouse. She peeked behind the kettle. She peeked under the cushion, where she only found three lost senbei crackers and a button. She peeked into the big round shadow beside the tea shelf —

— and the big round shadow *giggled.*

Out tumbled a **たぬき** (tanuki) = raccoon dog: a roly-poly fellow with a pot belly so round and full it bounced when he laughed, a stripey raccoon-mask face, a bushy tail like a feather duster, and the guiltiest, twinkliest grin in all the misty hills. A tiny dribble of matcha foam clung to his whiskers.

"Hello!" said the tanuki, foam and all. "Lovely weather for tea, isn't it? Not that I would know. I haven't had any. Definitely not three cups."

"You said three," noted Maru.

"...Two cups," said the tanuki.

---

Now, Maru Matcha was the steady one. While other friends might have shrieked or chased the round little rascal in circles, Maru simply tilted her bowl-head and considered him, the way the moon considers a pond.

"You have been sneaking my tea," she said.

"I prefer the word *borrowing,*" said the tanuki, patting his belly with both paws. It made a sound like a happy little drum: *bom-bom-bom.* "I'm Pon, by the way. The finest belly-drummer in these hills. And the thirstiest. Mostly the thirstiest."

"Why not simply ask, Pon?"

Pon's twinkle dimmed, just a smidge. He scuffed one fuzzy foot against the floor. "Tanuki are tricksters, you see. Everybody knows that. We turn into teakettles and leaves and old straw sandals and we play pranks and bonk our bellies in the moonlight. Nobody *invites* a tanuki to tea." He puffed up bravely. "So we take it. With STYLE."

And to prove the style, Pon snapped his stubby fingers — *poof!* — and turned into a teapot. A very lumpy teapot. With a tail. And whiskers. And a belly.

"Ta-daaa," said the teapot, slightly muffled.

Maru looked at the lumpy whiskered teapot for a long, calm moment.

"That," she said gently, "is the least convincing teapot I have ever met."

The teapot sighed and *poofed* back into Pon, drooping like a wilted dumpling. "I know. My disguises are terrible. Last week I tried to be a garden rock and a snail crawled onto my nose and I sneezed and rolled all the way down the hill. Tanuki are *supposed* to be magnificent shape-shifters. I'm just... round."

---

Here is a thing about Maru Matcha that you should tuck away for your own cozy nights: she believed that nearly every grumpy, sneaky, prickly creature in the whole wide world was simply a lonely creature who had not yet been offered a proper cup of tea.

So she did not scold. She did not chase. She did the most surprising thing of all.

She fetched a second cushion.

"Sit, Pon," she said, patting it. "If you love my tea so much, then let us drink it the right way — together. **Dōzo** (doh-zo) = please, go ahead."

Pon's whiskers trembled. His twinkly eyes went wide and round as mochi. "You... you're not chasing me out with a broom?"

"I do not own a broom," said Maru. "And even if I did, a broom is a poor host. Sit."

Slowly, hardly daring to breathe, the roly-poly tanuki waddled over and plopped onto the cushion. His belly settled around him like a warm cushion of its own.

Maru lifted her whisk. "Watch closely. Good tea is not gulped from the shadows. It is made slowly, and shared." She measured the bright green powder. She poured the steaming water. And then — *swish-swish-swish-swish* — she whisked, faster and faster, until the matcha foamed up soft and proud, the color of a hillside in spring.

She turned the cup three times, the polite way, and held it out in both hands.

"For you," she said. "Made on purpose. For a guest."

---

Pon took the cup as though it were made of moonlight. He looked at the froth. He looked at Maru. He took the tiniest, most careful sip in tanuki history.

His ears wiggled. His tail fluffed. His twinkly eyes filled up shiny and bright.

"It's the same tea I've been stealing all week," he whispered, "but it tastes about a hundred times better."

"That," said Maru, with the smallest smile, "is the flavor of being welcome. Stolen tea always tastes a little of worry. Shared tea tastes of friends."

Pon drank it down — slowly this time, savoring — and when he set the cup down he gave a tremendous, satisfied *bom-bom-bom* on his belly that rattled the teacups on the shelf. "**Oishii!** (oh-ee-shee) = delicious! Oh, Maru, that was the best cup I've ever had, and I've had — well. Several of yours. From inside a cupboard."

"I noticed," said Maru.

"I'll pay you back," Pon said, sitting up tall and round. "Tanuki always repay a true kindness. It's in the very oldest stories! What can a fuzzy fellow do for the kindest tea-friend on the hill?"

---

Maru thought about it, whisk tapping thoughtfully against her bowl-head. "Friends do not pay each other back," she said. "Friends simply *do for* each other. So. Can you help me carry water from the spring? My legs are very short, and the bucket is very full."

Pon leaped up so fast his belly bounced him an extra inch. "Can I! I'm a champion roller! Watch this!"

And here, dear sleepy reader, is where the evening turned absolutely silly. Because Pon discovered that the very best way to carry water up a hill was to balance the bucket on his round belly and *roll* — which worked beautifully, except that a rolling tanuki cannot easily steer. He rolled into the bamboo (*sproing!*). He rolled over a sleeping frog, who hopped onto his head and rode along like a tiny green captain. He rolled in one full circle around the teahouse before bonking gently, *boomf,* into the front step — and somehow, miraculously, not a single drop of water spilled.

"Ta-daaa," wheezed Pon, dizzy and upside-down and very proud.

Maru laughed — a soft, rare, lovely laugh, like the *clink* of a spoon against a delicate cup. "You may be a terrible teapot," she said, "but you are a *magnificent* water-bucket."

Pon glowed. Nobody had ever called him magnificent at *anything* before.

---

After that, the teahouse on the misty hill was never quite so quiet again — and that, it turned out, was exactly right.

Every evening, when the paper windows glowed honey-gold, Pon would waddle up the path (rolling the last bit, for fun), and Maru would already have two cushions out and the kettle warming. She taught him to whisk, though his froth always came out lopsided and bubbly. He taught her belly-drum rhythms, and on the calmest nights they would sit together — *swish-swish* and *bom-bom-bom* — making the cozy sort of music that only true friends make.

And if a traveler ever wandered up that hill, footsore and lonely, Pon would be the very first to fetch a third cushion and say, in his warmest voice, "Sit, friend! **Dōzo!** There's always enough tea to share — I learned that from the best tea-friend in all the hills."

Maru would whisk a little faster then, hiding her smile in the steam. Because she knew something gentle and true, something worth carrying with you all the way down into sleep:

You cannot chase loneliness away with a broom. But you can *welcome* it away — one warm, shared, foamy-green cup at a time.

And every cup tastes a hundred times better when it's poured for a friend.

## Goodnight Blessing

May your evening be warm as fresh-whisked tea, little one, and your heart as round and happy as a tanuki's drum. May you always find a cushion waiting, and a friend who saves you the first sip. When the misty hills go quiet and the kettle hums low, may you remember that the sweetest things in the whole wide world are the ones we share. Snuggle close, breathe slow, and feel calm. Goodnight.
