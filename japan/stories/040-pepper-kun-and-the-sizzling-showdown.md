# Story 40: Pepper-kun and the Sizzling Showdown

**Main Character:** Pepper-kun (Pepper Lunch sizzle)
**Guest Characters:** Mero Melonpan, Rin Ramen
**Setting:** A sizzling Pepper Lunch counter in a buzzing food court
**Theme:** Showing off is fun, but sharing the plate is better
**Japanese Spotlight:** suteeki (soo-TEH-kee) — steak / sizzling beef

---

The food court was the loudest, brightest, most deliciously crowded place in the whole night market, and right in the middle of it all sat a tiny round black iron hot-plate friend named Pepper-kun — and Pepper-kun was about to make a LOT of noise.

He hopped onto the edge of his counter, tied his little chef's bandana tight, and struck a pose so dramatic that two passing dumplings actually gasped.

"Ladies and gentlemen, crumbs and noodles," Pepper-kun announced, "prepare yourselves... for the SIZZLE of the CENTURY!"

He flexed. A puff of steam shot off his shoulders. *Tsssss-POP!*

A baby bun in the front row clapped so hard it fell off its stool.

"Stir it, sizzle it—" Pepper-kun spun in a circle, scattering golden corn like confetti, "—and SHARE it! Well. Mostly the first two parts."

Because here is the thing you should know about Pepper-kun: he loved being loud. He loved being watched. And more than anything in the wide steamy world, he wanted to be the loudest plate in the entire food court.

The trouble was, he was not.

The trouble — the friendly, bubbly, impossible-to-be-mad-at kind of trouble — had a name, and the name was Rin Ramen.

Two counters down, Rin Ramen — a steaming ceramic bowl on stubby legs with noodle-curl hair and a soft-boiled egg cheek — let out a slurpy, bubbly laugh that rolled across the whole food court like warm thunder.

"Everything's better warm and bubbly!" Rin sang, and a crowd of hungry friends *whooped* and gathered round.

Rin wasn't showing off, mind you. He never did. He even waved a friendly noodle at Pepper-kun across the food court — which was, honestly, the most annoying part. You can't be properly grumpy at a rival who keeps being *nice* to you.

Pepper-kun's butter-melt swirl went a little flat anyway.

"Bubbly," he muttered. "BUBBLY. Anybody can bubble. But sizzle? Sizzle is *art*." He puffed himself back up. "Tonight, I will out-sizzle, out-dazzle, and out-LOUD every single plate in this place. They'll forget all about soup."

He grabbed his tiny spatula like a sword.

"It's showdown time."

---

Pepper-kun's big idea was a Sizzling Showdown — a one-plate spectacular so dramatic that the whole food court would have to stop and stare. He spent the whole afternoon practicing.

First, the **suteeki** (soo-TEH-kee) — sizzling beef. He arranged his beef strips in a perfect spiral, balanced his butter swirl in the exact center, and rehearsed his sizzle countdown until his iron edges glowed warm and proud.

"Five! Four! Three! Two! ONE—" *TSSSSSSSS!*

The steam went up so high it tickled the paper lanterns.

"PERFECT," he gasped. "Loud. Hot. Mine. All mine."

He polished his iron plate until he could see his own grinning reflection. He practiced his bows. He practiced his *deeper* bows. He even practiced the little wink he planned to give the crowd at the very end.

He drew a poster, too — a big one — with letters so curly and dramatic they practically sizzled on the page: *TONIGHT ONLY! PEPPER-KUN'S SIZZLING SHOWDOWN! THE LOUDEST PLATE IN THE FOOD COURT!* He hung it right over his counter, stood back, and admired it with both stubby arms on his hips.

"They're going to talk about this for WEEKS," he said. "Maybe months. Maybe forever and ever and *ever*."

A soft, sleepy voice drifted over from the napping spot by the lanterns.

"You're making an awful lot of noise for one plate," yawned Mero Melonpan, a puffy domed bun with a crackly golden-green crust and sleepy happy eyes. He cracked one eye open. "Who's all that suteeki for?"

"For showing OFF," Pepper-kun said proudly. "I'm going to be the loudest plate in the food court. Louder than the noodles. Louder than the gongs. Louder than the *fireworks*, maybe."

"Mm." Mero rolled over slowly, like a bun-shaped cloud. "That's an awful lot of beef to be loud all by yourself."

"It's not by myself," Pepper-kun huffed. "There'll be a whole crowd watching!"

"Watching," Mero repeated, with a sleepy little smile. "Not the same as *eating*, is it now?" He closed his eye again. "Crackle on the outside... dreams on the inside. Sometimes the inside part matters most." And he drifted back to his nap mid-sentence, crumbs sparkling on his blanket.

Pepper-kun did not really hear that last bit. He was too busy practicing his wink in the shine of his polished iron plate.

---

When evening came, the food court filled up the way it always did — warm and golden and packed wall to wall with hungry, happy friends. Pepper-kun climbed onto his counter, banged his spatula like a gong, and bellowed:

"GATHER 'ROUND, EVERYBODY! The Sizzling Showdown BEGINS!"

And, wonderfully, marvelously — they did.

Dumplings rolled over. Buns bounced closer. Even Rin Ramen wobbled across on stubby legs to watch, noodle-hair bobbing. A whole steamy semicircle of faces turned toward little Pepper-kun, and his iron heart nearly melted with joy.

"This is IT," he whispered. "This is everything I ever wanted."

He cranked up the heat. He flung the beef into the air — it landed in a perfect spiral. He drizzled the butter, scattered the corn, and threw both stubby arms wide.

"Behold... the GREATEST suteeki ever sizzled! FIVE! FOUR! THREE—"

The crowd leaned in. Eyes went wide. Tiny mouths watered.

"—TWO! ONE!"

*TSSSSSSSSSSSSS-POP!*

The steam shot sky-high. The beef sizzled like a hundred tiny fireworks. The butter swirled into a golden ribbon. It was, without any doubt, the loudest, hottest, most dazzling sizzle the food court had ever seen.

The crowd *roared*.

"AGAIN!" they cheered. "AGAIN, Pepper-kun! AGAIN!"

Pepper-kun beamed so wide his bandana nearly popped off. He did it again. *TSSSSS!* And again. *TSSSSS!* He sizzled and spun and winked and bowed his deeper bow, and the cheering got louder and louder and—

And then something strange happened.

The cheering... stopped.

Not all at once. It sort of trailed off, the way a song ends when nobody remembers the last line. One by one, the friends in the front row started to shuffle. A little dumpling tugged its friend's sleeve. A bun glanced sideways. Somebody's tummy grumbled, loud and lonesome, in the quiet.

Pepper-kun froze mid-bow. "What — what's wrong? Wasn't it loud enough? I can be LOUDER—"

"It was super loud," said the little dumpling, very kindly.

"It was the loudest *ever*," agreed the bun.

"It's just..." The dumpling shuffled its feet. "We're really, really hungry. And you've been sizzling that beautiful suteeki for ages, and... nobody's getting any."

Pepper-kun looked down at his iron plate.

It was piled high — gloriously, dramatically high — with steaming beef and golden corn and curls of melted butter. A perfect spiral. A perfect spectacle.

And not one single friend had been given a single bite.

His steam puffed small and embarrassed. "Oh," he said quietly. "Oh, no. I made all this noise about *sharing*... and then I went and forgot the sharing part entirely."

The food court had gone soft and still. Even Rin Ramen had stopped bubbling.

Pepper-kun's bandana drooped. Being the loudest plate didn't feel loud at all anymore. It felt kind of... lonely. Like clapping with only one hand.

---

That was when a sleepy voice yawned from the lanterns.

"Crackle on the outside," murmured Mero Melonpan, not even fully awake, "dreams on the *inside*. The good stuff's inside, Pepper-kun. Always was."

And all at once, Pepper-kun understood.

The sizzle wasn't the gift. The sizzle was just the *invitation*. The gift was what came after — the warm, shared, everybody-gets-a-bite part. He'd been so busy being the loudest plate that he'd forgotten he was supposed to be a *plate full of dinner*.

His iron heart flickered back to life, warmer than ever.

"Friends!" he called out. "I have been the silliest hot-plate in the whole night market. I sizzled you a feast and forgot to *feed* you. Let's fix that — TOGETHER!"

He grabbed his spatula. But this time, instead of striking a pose, he started serving.

"You — front row — first bite!" *Plip!* A juicy strip of beef onto a tiny plate. "And you! And YOU! Corn for the corn-lovers! Butter for the butter-fans! Everybody gets a piece of the suteeki!"

The food court came back to life — but a *different* kind of life. Not the staring kind. The together kind.

Pepper-kun sizzled and served, sizzled and served. He flipped a beef strip behind his back and *caught* it on a plate. The crowd gasped — not because it was loud, but because it landed right in front of a hungry little bun who squealed with delight.

"Stir it!" Pepper-kun called, scooping up a portion. "Sizzle it!" — *TSSSS!* — "and SHARE it!" — and he plopped a steaming bite into Rin Ramen's bowl.

Rin slurped it up and let out the biggest, bubbliest laugh of the night. "Everything's better warm and bubbly — and SHARED!" Then Rin tipped his whole bowl toward Pepper-kun, slow and steamy, the way one champion salutes another. And Pepper-kun tipped his spatula right back, grinning, because the only showdown worth having had just ended in the best possible tie.

He even invited the friends right up onto the counter to help. A pair of dumplings stirred the corn. A bun fanned the steam. Rin Ramen ladled out warm broth on the side so nobody's beef got lonely. The Sizzling Showdown had turned into something much better than a showdown — it had turned into a great big bubbling, sizzling, everybody-helping *party*.

Soon the whole food court was passing plates, trading bites, going *mmm* in cozy unison. The dumpling who'd been brave enough to speak up got the very best, most spiraly piece of all, and Pepper-kun gave it a wink — a real one this time, just for being kind.

"You know," the little dumpling said shyly, cheeks full and happy, "I liked the loud part. But I like *this* part even more."

"Me too," said Pepper-kun, and he was surprised to find it was true — truer than anything he'd practiced all afternoon. "Me too, little friend."

And here's the funny thing. With everyone laughing and eating and elbow-to-elbow happy, the food court got *louder* than it had been all night. Louder than the showdown. Louder than the loudest sizzle.

Pepper-kun stood in the middle of it, steam curling up gentle and glad, and realized he'd finally done it. He was the loudest plate in the food court.

He just had to fill it up with friends first.

"Pepper-kun!" called a tiny bun from somewhere in the warm, glowing crowd. "Do the countdown one more time — so we can ALL count!"

So he did. He climbed back up, raised his spatula high, and this time the whole food court counted with him, a hundred happy voices rolling together: "FIVE! FOUR! THREE! TWO! ONE—" *TSSSSSSSS-POP!* — and the steam went up and the laughter went up higher, and not a single belly in the place was left empty or alone.

---

By the end of the night, every belly was full and every plate was empty, and Pepper-kun was the happiest little hot-plate in all of Japan.

"Tomorrow," he yawned, his iron edges cooling soft and warm, "I'll do another showdown. But this time, the sizzle's just the doorbell. The feast is the whole point."

He pulled his bandana down like a cozy little blanket.

"Stir it, sizzle it... and *share* it," he murmured. "Mostly that last part."

And out by the lanterns, Mero Melonpan smiled in his sleep, crumbs sparkling, dreaming of warm things shared.

## Goodnight Blessing

Little one, may your day have just enough sizzle to make people smile — and may your plate always be big enough to share. Remember that the loudest, happiest sound in the whole world is friends laughing together over something warm. You don't have to dazzle anyone to be loved; you only have to be kind, and pass the corn. Snuggle down now, warm as butter melting slow, cozy as a counter full of friends. The lanterns are dimming and the night is soft and full. Goodnight.
