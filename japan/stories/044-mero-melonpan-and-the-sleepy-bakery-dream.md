# Story 44: Mero Melonpan and the Sleepy Bakery Dream

**Main Character:** Mero Melonpan
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A warm-smelling neighborhood bakery at closing time
**Theme:** Rest helps your imagination rise like dough
**Japanese Spotlight:** ねむい (nemui) = sleepy

---

The little bakery on Cinnamon Corner was doing the thing it did best at the end of every day: smelling absolutely delicious. The ovens had cooled to a snug, toasty hum. The last loaf had been sold to the last hungry person. And up on the warm shelf by the window, where the late sun came in gold and slow, sat Mero Melonpan — a puffy domed bun with a crackly green-gold crust like a tiny sweet planet — yawning so wide you could practically hear his crumbs sparkle.

"Crackle on the outside," Mero mumbled, his sleepy happy eyes drifting half-shut, "dreams on the inside."

"Mero!" squeaked a voice. "Don't you dare doze off! We're supposed to count the muffins!"

But it was too late. Mero's chin had already nestled into his own crust. A soft little snore puffed out — *poof* — and a single sparkly crumb floated up like a firefly going to bed.

The whole bakery held its breath. Mero Melonpan had fallen asleep. And when Mero fell asleep, *interesting things* tended to happen.

---

The voice that had squeaked belonged to Niko Nigiri, standing very straight on the counter beside a clipboard he had drawn himself out of a napkin. "This is most irregular," Niko said, smoothing his glossy salmon cape. "We have a job. Count the muffins. Tidy the trays. Then bed." He tapped his little nori belt for emphasis. "Let's do this neatly... or deliciously."

Beside him, Momo Mochi bounced on her pillowy white tummy, her peach cheeks glowing. "But look at his FACE," she cooed. "He's so cozy. Squish together, stay together! Maybe we should just... let him dream a teeny bit?"

"We do not have time for teeny dreams," Niko began — and then he noticed something extremely odd.

The air above Mero had begun to *shimmer*. Tiny golden crumbs were lifting off his crust, one by one, and spinning together in the warm bakery light. They twirled. They gathered. And slowly, softly, they built themselves into a little shining doorway right there on the shelf — a doorway made entirely of crackly sweet bread, glowing like morning toast.

"Oh my," whispered Maru Matcha, who had been quietly stirring nothing with her tiny bamboo whisk in the corner. Her calm half-moon eyes blinked once. "He's dreaming so warmly that the dream is leaking out."

"That's not a thing dreams do," said Niko.

"It is now," said Maru. "Whisk slow. Feel calm. And maybe... follow him?"

Through the little bread doorway, they could see it: a whole kingdom, soft and golden, waiting on the other side of sleep.

---

They went in together — because that is what friends do when one of them leaves a magic door open.

On the other side was the most wonderful place any of them had ever seen. The ground was springy and warm, like the top of fresh-baked bread you press with one careful finger. Rolling hills rose up in puffy domes, each one cross-hatched with crackly green-gold crust, exactly like Mero. Little crumb-rivers sparkled between them. And the sky was the soft amber color of an oven window at the exact perfect moment.

"Welcome," said Mero, drifting toward them on his own two crumbs, "to the Kingdom of Nemui." He yawned hugely. "**ねむい** (nemui) means *sleepy*. It's the coziest country there is. Everything here is allowed to be a little bit slow."

"It's beautiful," gasped Momo, hugging the nearest bread-hill. It hugged her back, gently. "Squish! Oh, it squishes!"

"Of course it does," said Mero dreamily. "In Nemui, even the mountains like a cuddle before bed."

A little path of warm crumbs unrolled itself in front of them as they walked, polite as a welcome mat, and where their feet touched the springy ground, tiny puffs of bread-smell rose up — vanilla and honey and that toasty just-out-of-the-oven warmth that makes everybody's tummy feel like a hug. Overhead, butter-colored birds made entirely of flaky pastry drifted in lazy loops, too sleepy to flap properly, gliding instead with soft little *pfff* sounds. One landed on Momo's head, sighed, and immediately fell asleep.

"I think I love it here," Momo whispered, holding very still so as not to wake her new pastry hat.

Niko Nigiri stood very straight, but his eyes were enormous. "There's so much to *organize* here," he breathed. "I could make a list. I could make seven lists." Then a curious thing happened — a fluffy bread-cloud floated by and bonked him softly on the head, and instead of fixing his cape, Niko... giggled. "Oh. Oh, that was nice."

"That's the trouble with Nemui," Mero said, smiling sleepily. "It makes you forget to worry."

---

But there *was* a small problem in the kingdom — because every good adventure has a gentle one.

At the very center of Nemui stood the Great Oven, a cozy golden dome where, Mero explained, all the kingdom's dreams rose up nice and fluffy. "When the Oven is warm," he said, "everyone's imagination puffs up big and round. But look."

The Oven had gone dim. Its glow was low and tired, and the bread-hills nearest it had begun to droop and go a little flat, like dough that didn't quite rise.

"Oh no," murmured Momo. "It's like the kingdom is too *tired* to dream."

"How do we fix it?" asked Niko, already reaching for an imaginary clipboard. "Do we stir it? Stoke it? I read a pamphlet once about ovens—"

"You can't *push* a dream to rise," said Maru Matcha softly, padding forward with her whisk. "That's the secret of bread, and the secret of imaginations too. You can't squeeze them to grow." She tapped the cool Oven gently. "They only puff up big when they're allowed to *rest*."

Mero's sleepy eyes brightened the tiniest bit. "Maru's right. The Oven isn't broken. It's just been *busy* too long. Everyone's been rushing — counting, tidying, hurrying, fixing. Nobody's let it simply... be warm and quiet." He looked, very gently, at Niko.

Niko's salmon cape wilted just a little. "Oh," he said. "Is that... is that me?"

"It's all of us, sometimes," said Mero kindly. "But here, in Nemui? Resting is the *brave* thing. Resting is how the magic rises."

---

So they tried it — and trying to rest, it turns out, is a delightfully silly activity when you're not used to it.

Niko Nigiri lay down in the warm bread-grass and stared at the amber sky. "I am resting," he announced. "I am resting very efficiently. Am I doing it right? Should I rest faster?"

"You can't rest *faster*," giggled Mero. "That's like trying to bake bread by shouting at it."

"I have never shouted at bread," said Niko with great dignity. Then he thought about it. "Well. Perhaps once. The bread won."

"Niko," laughed Momo, "that's not how—" and then a bread-cloud floated over and *boop*ed her too, and she dissolved into giggles and rolled down a fluffy hill, going *wheeee* all the way to the bottom.

Maru Matcha simply sat. She whisked the warm air in slow little circles, and a soft green steam curled up from her bowl-head. "Breathe in the bakery smell," she said. "Breathe out the busy. Whisk slow. Feel calm."

Mero curled up right against the cool Great Oven, his crackly crust rising and falling with each sleepy breath. "Just be cozy with me," he murmured. "That's the whole job. The comfiest job in the world."

And one by one, they did. Niko stopped counting. Momo stopped bouncing — well, *almost*. Maru kept gently whisking. And the whole Kingdom of Nemui grew quiet and warm and slow, full of nothing but soft breathing and the good smell of bread.

That's when it happened.

---

A tiny glow flickered to life inside the Great Oven. Then a bigger one. The warmth spread out in golden rings, and the drooping bread-hills began — ever so slowly — to *rise*. They puffed. They rounded. Their crusts crackled into beautiful green-gold cross-hatches, one little *tick-tick-tick* at a time, like the sweetest sound in the world.

"It's working!" whispered Momo. "We're not even *doing* anything!"

"That's exactly why it's working," said Mero, beaming. "The dreams are rising because we finally let them."

Higher and higher the kingdom puffed, fluffy and fragrant and golden, until all of Nemui glowed like a tray of perfect melon pan fresh from the oven. New hills rose. New crumb-rivers sparkled. A whole new corner of the kingdom — one nobody had ever dreamed before — unfolded itself in soft puffy folds, just because everyone had finally stopped to rest.

The sleepy pastry-birds woke up just enough to do a single happy loop before settling back down. The bread-clouds drifted by in slow, contented herds. And from somewhere deep in the warm golden hills came a sound like the whole kingdom breathing out at once — a soft, satisfied *ahhh*, the exact sound you make when you finally crawl under a warm blanket on a cold night.

"Listen," whispered Mero. "That's the sound of dreams rising. It only ever happens when nobody's hurrying it."

Niko Nigiri sat up, his eyes shining. "I made a list once," he said quietly, "of all the things I needed to do today. I forgot to put *this* on it." He smiled. "I think 'rest' goes at the top from now on. Neatly... at the top."

"Now you're learning," said Mero, with the biggest, sleepiest, crumbliest grin of all.

---

The amber sky began to soften toward evening, and the warm bread doorway shimmered gently behind them, ready to lead home.

"Time to wake up?" asked Momo, a little sad.

"For now," said Mero. "But Nemui's always here. Whenever you let yourself rest, the door opens again — that's the lovely thing about it." He gave the puffy kingdom one last cozy look. "Crackle on the outside. Dreams on the inside. Always."

They stepped back through together, crumb by golden crumb.

---

Back on the warm shelf at Cinnamon Corner, the bakery was dim and quiet and snug. Mero Melonpan blinked open his sleepy happy eyes. Momo was squished against his side, Maru was whisking slow circles of green steam, and Niko Nigiri — straight-backed, tidy Niko — was lying on his back on the counter, smiling up at nothing at all.

"The muffins," Niko said softly, "can wait until morning. They'll dream better overnight anyway."

Maru's half-moon eyes crinkled. "Now *that*," she said, "is the wisest thing you've said all day."

And as the last gold light slipped out of the window, the four friends curled together on the warm shelf, breathing in the cozy bread-smell, letting their imaginations rise quietly in the dark — big and round and fluffy — ready for whatever sweet kingdom tomorrow's nap might bring.

## Goodnight Blessing

Just breathe in slow and let your dreams puff up soft and golden, the way bread does when it's finally, cozily, beautifully **ねむい** (nemui). Your imagination grows biggest when you let it sleep. Crackle on the outside, sweet dreams on the inside. Goodnight.
