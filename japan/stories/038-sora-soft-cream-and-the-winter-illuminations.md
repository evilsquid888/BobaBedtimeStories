# Story 38: Sora Soft-Cream and the Winter Illuminations

**Main Character:** Sora the Soft-Cream
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A park glittering with millions of winter lights
**Theme:** Cold nights can hold the warmest glow
**Japanese Spotlight:** kirei (kee-RAY) — beautiful

---

Now, between you and me, there is exactly one creature in the whole wide world who has ever been *too cold* and *too happy* at the same time, and that creature is Sora the Soft-Cream.

You see, Sora was a tall, dreamy swirl of pale-gold Hokkaido milk soft-serve, balanced just so in a crisp little cone, with a curl on top like a question mark made of cream. Most of the year, Sora worried — just a teeny bit — about melting. But tonight? Tonight it was the coldest night of the whole winter, and Sora stood at the gates of the Light Park wearing the silliest, fluffiest scarf you have ever seen.

"Soft swirls, slow smiles," Sora hummed, breath puffing out in little frosty clouds. "Tonight I shall not drip even one drip."

A snowflake landed on Sora's curl and simply stayed there, sparkling, like a tiny crown.

"Oh," whispered Sora. "**Kirei** (kee-RAY) — *beautiful*."

Now, if you have ever stood outside on a truly freezing night — the kind where your nose goes pink and your breath turns to little ghosts — you might think ice cream would be miserable in the cold. But Sora was *thrilled.* All summer long, soft-serve lives in fear of the slow, sad slide of melting. All summer long, Sora had to scurry from shade to shade, and never, ever stand still. But winter! Winter was a soft-serve holiday. Winter meant Sora could stand as long and as still and as dreamy as she liked, gazing up at the world, perfectly solid, perfectly safe.

---

Here is the thing about the Light Park. Every winter, the whole place lit up with *millions* of tiny lights — blue ones strung through the bare trees like frozen rain, gold ones spiraling up the lampposts, pink and green ones blinking along the paths. From far away the whole park glowed like a dropped jewelry box. From up close it was even better, because up close you could feel how *quiet* a million lights could be.

Sora had wanted to see the illuminations for ages. But Sora had a small, soft worry, the kind that sits in your tummy like an ice cube.

The worry was this: *What if it's too cold to enjoy? What if I'm all alone, and beautiful things are only beautiful when somebody's beside you?*

Sora shook the worry off — *plip* went the snowflake — and waddled through the gate on careful cone-bottom, into the glow.

The first thing Sora saw was a tunnel of light. It curved overhead, thousands of blue bulbs woven into a long shimmering arch, so that walking through it felt like walking through the inside of a wave that had decided to freeze and twinkle instead of crash.

"Ohhh," breathed Sora, tipping the whole creamy swirl back to look up. "**Yuki** (YOO-kee) — *snow*... but made of stars."

A small voice giggled somewhere near Sora's cone.

Sora looked down. There, hopping along the frosty path, was a tiny something — round, fluffy, white, with rosy pink cheeks and a smell of warm steam. It was a baby snow-monkey-bun — the same kind of rosy-cheeked steamed bun as Sora's friend Yuki, but barely a handful tall, no bigger than Sora's curl — and it was *lost.*

"Hello there," said Sora gently, because Sora was the calmest, dreamiest creature in any park. "Are you all by yourself, little one?"

The fluffy bun nodded, lip wobbling. "I came with my whole family," it squeaked, "to see the lights. But I chased a glowy butterfly — it was *kirei!* — and now I can't find anybody, and it's so c-c-cold, and—" The little bun shivered so hard it nearly bounced off the path.

Now, Sora was made of *ice cream.* Sora was, technically, one of the coldest beings in the entire park. Hugging a shivering friend was not exactly Sora's specialty.

But Sora had a scarf. The silliest, fluffiest scarf you have ever seen.

"Here," said Sora, unwinding it with a slow, swirly motion. "I run cold, but my scarf runs warm. We can fix this together."

Sora wrapped the scarf around the little snow-bun until only two rosy cheeks and a relieved smile poked out. The bun gave a tiny, steamy sigh — "Aahh" — like a creature sinking into a hot spring.

"Now," said Sora, "let's go find your family. A park is big. But lights are made for finding your way home."

---

And off they went, the tall dreamy soft-serve and the tiny scarf-wrapped bun, two glows wobbling through a million glows.

They tried the carousel of light first — a big wheel of golden bulbs that spun slow and grand, with little glowing horses going up and down, up and down, like a music box that had wandered outdoors. "Is your family here?" Sora asked.

The little bun stood on tippy-toes, wobbling on Sora's cone like a tiny lookout in a crow's nest. "I don't see them... but ooh, look! The whole wheel is kirei!" It clapped its tiny paws, forgetting, just for a second, to be scared. And Sora noticed something rather lovely: the cold didn't feel quite so cold when somebody beside you was busy being delighted.

"You have," Sora said gravely, "the loudest delight of any small bun I have ever met. It's marvelous."

"I get it from my grandpa," the bun said proudly. "He claps at *everything.* He once clapped at a very good radish."

Next they wandered to the Sea of Lights — a wide field where bulbs rippled across the ground in blue and white waves, programmed to glide like a slow tide. Sora, who came from snowy Hokkaido and loved wide skies and slow days, felt all the worry-ice in her tummy begin to soften.

"My family loves this part," the bun whispered. "We always say it looks like the **onsen** (OHN-sen) — *hot spring* — I take baths in, except sparkly." It peered around hopefully. No family. Its little shoulders drooped.

"Slow swirls, slow smiles," Sora said softly. "We're not lost. We're just not done looking yet. Those are very different things."

The bun blinked up at Sora. "Are they?"

"Mm-hm. Lost means there's no path. We have a whole park full of paths, and every one of them is lit." Sora gave a dreamy little smile. "Besides, I packed patience. It never melts."

That made the little bun laugh — a real one, warm and bubbly — and the laugh puffed out as a cloud of steam that drifted up and caught the light and turned, for one twinkly moment, gold.

"How do you stay so *calm?*" the bun asked, amazed. "I would be running in circles."

"Practice," Sora said. "When you live in Hokkaido, you learn to love a slow day. The snow comes down slow. The milk pours slow. Even the sky takes its time turning pink. So I learned to take my time too." Sora gazed up at the swaying blue lights. "Hurrying never made anything more beautiful. But noticing — *noticing* makes everything more beautiful."

The little bun thought about this very hard, the way small creatures do when handed a big idea, and then announced: "I'm going to notice things now. I'm noticing that you're squishy."

"That's a start," said Sora, very kindly.

---

But the Sea of Lights was *very* big, and the night was *very* cold, and after a while even Sora's calm began to feel thin around the edges. The bun had gone quiet inside the scarf. The crowds had thinned. And — oh dear — a few of the path-lights ahead had flickered out, leaving a stretch of dark right when they needed it most.

Sora stopped. The worry-ice came back, bigger now: *What if I can't help? What if a small cold creature is depending on me and I drip and I fail?*

Sora looked down at the fluffy bun. And the fluffy bun looked up at Sora, and said the bravest thing a tiny lost thing can say:

"I'm glad it's you, though."

Sora's whole swirl wobbled. "Pardon?"

"If I had to be lost," said the bun, "I'm glad I got lost with *you.* You're warm. Not on the outside. On the inside." It snuggled deeper into the scarf. "That's the warmest kind."

And — well. Have you ever felt something melt the *good* way? Not drippy-melt, but glowy-melt, the way a worry melts when a friend hands you exactly the right words? That's what happened in Sora's tummy. The ice cube turned into a candle.

"You know what," Sora said, standing up straighter than soft-serve has ever stood, "we don't need the path-lights. *We* are lights."

Sora's pale-gold swirl caught what little glow there was and shone it back, soft and steady. And the bun's rosy steam-cheeks gave off their own faint, warm glimmer. Two small lights in a dark stretch of park — but two is so much more than one.

So they glowed, and they walked, and just as they came out the other side, a sound floated toward them.

"...Yumeee? Where are you? Yumeeee!"

The little bun's ears shot straight up. "That's my big sister!" it squeaked. "YUMEEE IS HERE! WITH A SOFT-CREAM! AND A SCARF!"

Out from beneath the brightest tree in the whole park — a towering pine wrapped in a hundred thousand white lights, so bright it looked like it was made of moonbeams — tumbled a whole bouncing family of snow-monkey-buns, cheeks rosy, arms wide, steam puffing everywhere with relief.

There were hugs. Oh, there were *so* many hugs. Sora stood back and watched the little bundle of buns squish together in a steaming, giggling pile, and the worry that beautiful things were only beautiful with somebody beside you?

It was gone. Because Sora *had* been beside somebody. And it had made all the difference.

---

The littlest bun wriggled free of the family pile and toddled back to Sora, holding up the silly fluffy scarf.

"Your scarf," it said. "Thank you for the warm."

Sora knelt down — slowly, swirlily — and tucked the scarf back around the little one's shoulders. "Keep it," Sora said. "I run cold, remember? But you... you keep somebody warm with it next time. That's how the warm keeps going."

The bun's eyes went huge and shiny. "Will you stay? For the finale? The biggest lights happen at the very end!"

And so Sora, who had come to the park afraid of being alone in the cold, sat down with a whole family of snow-monkey-buns beneath the moonbeam tree.

At the stroke of the hour, every single light in the entire park brightened all at once — *whoom* — a million tiny stars blazing up together, blue and gold and pink and white, until the whole world looked like the inside of a happy heart.

"Kirei," whispered the whole family at once.

"Kirei," agreed Sora, glowing softest of all.

And the coldest night of the whole winter turned out to hold the very warmest glow Sora had ever felt — not from the million lights at all, but from the small warm bun pressed sleepy against her cone, and the family humming nearby, and the simple, snowy, certain truth that nobody, nobody, was alone.

---

## Goodnight Blessing
May your blankets be as soft as a swirl of Hokkaido cream, and your room as cozy as a scarf wrapped twice around. When the night feels cold and wide, remember that the warmest glow comes not from the brightest light, but from someone dear curled up close. You are never lost, little one — only not yet done resting. So close your eyes, breathe a slow and dreamy breath, and let a million quiet stars keep watch while you sleep. Goodnight.
