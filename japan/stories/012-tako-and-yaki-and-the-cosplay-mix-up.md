# Story 12: Tako and Yaki and the Cosplay Mix-Up

**Main Character:** Tako & Yaki (takoyaki twins)
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** The cosplay dress-up corner at a bright, busy fan festival
**Theme:** Being yourself is the best costume of all
**Japanese Spotlight:** かわいい (kawaii) = cute

---

If you have ever tried to tell two perfectly round, perfectly golden takoyaki balls apart, then you already know the kind of trouble we are about to roll into. Tako and Yaki bounced into the fan festival side by side, connected by their wobbly little bonito-flake wave, giggling so hard their sauce zig-zags nearly slid off.

"Look, look, LOOK!" said Tako.

"We are LOOKING!" said Yaki.

And there it was: the **コスプレ** (kosupure) = cosplay dress-up corner, a glittering tent stuffed with capes, wigs, sparkly hats, fox masks, paper swords, and a mirror so shiny you could comb your nori in it. Strings of paper lanterns swayed overhead like sleepy jellyfish. The whole place smelled of warm batter and fresh dreams.

"It's SO kawaii," whispered Tako.

"Kawaii means cute," Yaki explained to a passing dumpling, who already knew, but smiled anyway.

And right then, bouncing in unison, the twins had A Plan. Oh dear. Oh deliciously dear.

You see, everybody at the festival could tell Tako and Yaki apart by exactly one thing. Tako always wore a tiny blue scarf. Yaki always wore a tiny red scarf. That was the whole secret. Take away the scarves and even their own grandmother would have to guess (and Grandmother Takoyaki guessed wrong constantly, which she found hilarious).

"What if," said Tako, hopping closer to the costume rack, "we swap costumes—"

"—and trick ALL our friends!" finished Yaki.

"Roll us once," they sang together, "we come as two!"

It was, they agreed, the most kawaii prank ever invented.

So into the dress-up corner they tumbled. Tako grabbed a swooshy purple wizard cape and a pointy star-hat. Yaki grabbed a shiny silver robot helmet with blinky button-eyes and a cardboard chest-plate that said BEEP. Then — this was the truly tricky part — they traded scarves. Blue went to Yaki. Red went to Tako.

They looked in the shiny mirror and laughed so hard they wobbled into a basket of fox masks.

"Now," said Tako-in-the-robot-helmet, "I am Yaki."

"And I," said Yaki-in-the-wizard-cape, "am Tako."

"This is going to be the BEST," they said, which is exactly the sort of thing characters say right before everything gets gloriously confusing.

The first friend they found was Maru Matcha, calm as a still pond, stirring her little bamboo whisk.

"Greetings, Tako," Maru said to the wizard.

"Wrong!" the wizard crowed. "I'm Yaki!"

Maru's half-moon eyes did not even blink. "Whisk slow," she said. "Feel calm. You are wearing a red scarf. Red is Tako."

The twins froze. They looked down. Somewhere in the giggling and the fox-mask tumbling, they had gotten their own swapped scarves swapped *again*. Now the wizard wore red. The robot wore blue. They had completely, totally, sauce-droppingly lost track of who was pretending to be whom.

"Wait," said the robot. "Am I Yaki being Tako, or Tako being Yaki?"

"I don't know!" said the wizard, delighted and horrified at once. "Who are YOU?"

"I asked YOU first!"

Maru took a slow sip of tea. "This," she observed, "is a very kawaii mess."

And it only got messier from there, in the warmest possible way.

They rolled on to find Rin Ramen, who was steaming gently by the festival noodle stall. The moment Rin saw a wizard and a robot bouncing toward him in unison, he burst into happy steam.

"Tako! Yaki!" he cried. "Everything's better warm and bubbly! But — wait — which of you is which?"

"THAT," said the twins together, "is the prank."

"Guess!" said the wizard.

Rin squinted. He leaned in. His soft-boiled-egg cheek jiggled with concentration. "The robot is Tako," he declared, "because Tako always laughs first."

"Ha!" said the robot — and then clapped a hand over its own mouth, because it *had* laughed first, which gave a clue, except nobody could remember anymore whether the laugher was supposed to be Tako or Yaki, including the laugher.

"I have confused myself," the robot admitted.

"You've confused EVERYONE," said Rin, laughing his big slurpy laugh, "and it's wonderful."

So you can imagine how it went after that. They bounced to Dora the Dorayaki, who adjusted his cozy beret and said, "A good story is the best filling — and THIS is a delicious story, but I have no idea how it ends." They bounced to Pock Ramune, who got so excited he popped his marble — *pop!* — and announced, "I'll guess by the bounce! Tako bounces left-first!" Both twins immediately bounced left. Pock fizzed in defeat.

The whole festival began to gather, the way friends do when something giggly is happening. There was Momo Mochi, peach-cheeked and stretching her taffy arms. There was Dai Daruma wobbling and righting himself. There was Mero Melonpan, half-asleep on a beanbag, murmuring, "Crackle on the outside... two robots? No... one robot... mmm." Everyone wanted to play the guessing game. Nobody could win it. The twins had hidden the one true clue — their colored scarves — under so many swaps and costumes that even *they* had forgotten the answer.

That is the funny thing about a really good prank. Sometimes it works so well it tricks the trickster.

"Um," said the wizard quietly, when the laughing settled for a moment. "Yaki?"

"Yes?" said the robot.

"...are you Yaki? Or am I Yaki?"

The robot's blinky button-eyes flickered. "I... I don't actually know anymore."

And here is the part where, in a scarier story, things might get sad. But this is a Boba Bedtime Story, so instead something cozy happened. The two twins, who had spent their whole round little lives finishing each other's sentences, suddenly couldn't tell where one of them ended and the other began. And it made them feel — wobbly. Not the fun kind of wobbly. The kind where you've dressed up so much you've sort of misplaced yourself under all the costume.

Momo Mochi noticed right away, because cozy hearts always do.

"Oh, my sweet little batter-balls," she said, gathering them both into a soft squish. "Squish together, stay together! Now — let's find you."

"But the SCARVES are mixed up," said the wizard.

"And we forgot," said the robot.

"We don't need the scarves," Momo said gently. "Tell me — when Yaki laughs, what does Tako do?"

"I laugh right after," said the robot, automatically. Then it gasped. "I... I always laugh right after Yaki. I'm the *after* laugh. I'm Tako!"

"YES!" said the wizard, throwing off the pointy hat. "And I start it! I'm the one who shouts 'look, look, LOOK' first! I'm Yaki!"

Maru Matcha gave the smallest, calmest smile. "There you are," she said. "You did not lose yourselves. You just dressed them up for a while."

The twins looked at each other — robot and wizard, red scarf and blue scarf all tangled — and started to laugh, the real kind, the kind only the two of them did, where one began and the other finished, perfectly, like always.

"It wasn't the scarf that made us US," said Yaki slowly.

"It was the way we go TOGETHER," said Tako.

"You could put me in a hundred costumes—"

"—and I'd still know my own twin by his laugh!"

The whole festival cheered. Pock Ramune popped three marbles in celebration (he had been saving them). Rin Ramen steamed so happily he fogged up the shiny mirror. And Dai Daruma wobbled to the front, eyebrows fierce with pep.

"Lesson learned!" Dai boomed. "Fall down seven, bounce up eight! You can dress up as a wizard, a robot, a fox, a king, a flying turnip — and you'll STILL be wonderfully, completely, deliciously YOU underneath!"

"That," said Dora, scribbling in his little notebook, "is the best filling of all."

So Tako and Yaki kept the costumes on — because they really were kawaii, and a good cape is a good cape. But they swapped their scarves back. Blue for Tako. Red for Yaki. Not because anyone needed to tell them apart, but because the scarves were *theirs*, the way your favorite blanket is yours.

For the rest of the festival, the twins led a grand parade of dress-up. Momo wore a paper crown. Maru wore a tiny wizard hat over her matcha swirl and looked extremely unbothered. Mero Melonpan wore a fox mask and immediately fell asleep inside it. Everybody dressed as somebody else, and laughed, and underneath every silly costume was the same warm friend you already loved.

And as the lanterns dimmed and the festival grew sleepy, Tako and Yaki bounced one last time, in perfect unison, exactly as twins do.

"Roll us once—" said Tako.

"—we come as two," said Yaki.

"And no costume in the whole wide world," they finished together, "could ever mix THAT up."

The lanterns swayed. The batter smelled warm. And two round golden friends, snug under their swooshy capes, leaned together and yawned a single sleepy yawn.
