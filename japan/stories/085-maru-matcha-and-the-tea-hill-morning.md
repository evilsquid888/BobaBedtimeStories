# Story 85: Maru Matcha and the Tea-Hill Morning

**Main Character:** Maru Matcha
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** Misty terraced green-tea hills at sunrise in Shizuoka
**Theme:** The earliest, quietest hour holds a gentle gift
**Japanese Spotlight:** midori (mee-doh-ree) — green

---

Long before the sun cracked its first sleepy yawn, Maru Matcha was already awake — which, if you knew Maru, will not surprise you one bit. While the whole tea hill snored under blankets of fog, this small jade-green friend tiptoed out her door, bamboo whisk tucked behind one ear like a tiny umbrella she'd forgotten to put down.

"Whisk slow," she whispered to nobody. "Feel calm."

The frothy matcha-cream swirl on top of her head wobbled gently, the way soft-serve does when you walk too fast, so Maru did not walk too fast. Maru never did. She padded between the long curving rows of tea bushes, each one heavy with dew, each leaf glowing a deep dreamy **midori** (mee-doh-ree) — green. The air smelled like wet grass and cool stone and the very first idea of morning.

And then — *plip* — a single drop of dew rolled off a leaf and landed *splat* right on her nose.

Maru blinked her calm half-moon eyes. "Good morning to you too," she said.

---

Now, here is a thing about Maru Matcha that her friends never quite understood: she *loved* getting up at the silliest, sleepiest, owl-iest hour, when the sky was still the color of cold tea and even the birds were hitting the snooze button. Her friends thought she was bonkers. Lovingly bonkers. Bonkers like a bamboo whisk in a soup pot.

"Why would anyone wake up *before* the sun?" Kori Kakigori had once gasped, sunglasses sliding down in pure horror. "The sun is the COOLEST thing in the sky, and even *it* sleeps in!"

But Maru only smiled her small calm smile, because she had a secret. The earliest hour held a gift. A quiet, dewy, hush-soft gift that you could not buy at any night market, could not unwrap from any **hoso** (hoh-soh) — wrapping. You could only *be there* for it. And this morning, Maru had decided, was finally the morning to share it.

She climbed to the very top terrace, where the rows of tea bushes stacked down the hillside like green steps for a giant who liked her tea. There she set out five little cups in the dew-wet grass, lined up neat as buttons. One for Mero Melonpan. One for Yuki the Snow Monkey-Manju. One for Sora the Soft-Cream. One for Pock Ramune, who she suspected would arrive whether invited or not, fizzing the whole way. And one extra, because Maru always whisked one extra — "for whoever the morning brings," she liked to say.

She had asked them all to come at dawn. She had walked all the way down the hill the evening before, knocking on doors with her tiny whisk, saying the same gentle thing to each yawning friend: "Come to the top terrace tomorrow, before the sun. I want to show you something. Bring nothing but yourself." Mero had nodded and immediately fallen back asleep mid-nod. Yuki had asked, very seriously, whether there would be snacks. Sora had drifted off into a soft cloud of "...mmm, maybe..." And Pock had popped three times and zoomed in a circle, which Maru took to mean *yes.*

She was fairly sure none of them would actually show up.

After all, the bed is a warm and convincing friend, and it has a very persuasive voice at four in the morning. It says, *stay,* and it says it so softly. Maru understood. She loved her bed too. But she loved the green hush even more, and some gifts, she had learned, are only given to the ones who climb out from under the blankets to meet them.

---

She was wrong.

The first to arrive was Pock Ramune, of course, zipping up the hill trailing a banner of happy bubbles and very nearly bonking into a tea bush. "*Pop!*" he wheezed, marble rattling. "I'm here! I'm UP! Do you know what time it is, Maru? It's *o'clock-in-the-dark*! There isn't even a number for it! *Pop!*"

"There is," said Maru gently. "It's called dawn. Sit. Breathe."

"I don't think I can sit," fizzed Pock. "I have too many bubbles in me. They're all going *go-go-go.*"

"Then sit," said Maru, "and let the bubbles do the going for you."

This was a very Maru thing to say, and it did not make sense to Pock at all, which is exactly why it worked. Pock plopped down. He fizzed. He vibrated. He hummed a tiny *zzzz* of leftover excitement. But the longer he sat there in the cool wet quiet, the more he noticed that the going-feeling inside him was already going on its own — bubble after bubble, rising up through him and breaking softly at the top, *fff, fff, fff* — and he didn't have to chase a single one. A single dewdrop landed on his frosty blue glass, and he watched it slide all the way down, slow as honey, and — for one whole second — Pock Ramune did not say *pop.* He was so surprised by his own quiet that he nearly said *pop* about it, then thought better of it, and just watched the dewdrop instead.

Then came a soft *fwump-fwump* up the path: Mero Melonpan, eyes still half-closed, his crackly golden-green crust dusted with grass he'd apparently rolled in on the way. "Mmh," he mumbled dreamily. "Is it morning? It feels like the *inside* of a cloud out here." He sat down with a sleepy sigh, sparkly crumbs drifting off him. "Crackle on the outside... dreams on the inside..."

"Welcome, Mero," said Maru.

"Mm," said Mero, and immediately began to doze sitting up.

Yuki the Snow Monkey-Manju arrived next, fluffy and rosy-cheeked, complaining cozily the entire way. "It's cold," she said, hugging herself. "Maru, it's *cold.* Why didn't you do this at an onsen? An onsen would have steam. I *love* steam. Warm cheeks, warm heart, warm —" She stopped.

Because just then, the very first thread of sunlight slid over the far hills.

---

It came so quietly. That was the thing none of them expected. The light didn't *arrive* so much as it *seeped*, soft and golden, into the fog. The mist between the tea rows began to glow from the inside, like someone had switched on a lamp made entirely of breath. Every dewdrop on every leaf caught a tiny spark of sun, until the whole hillside was strung with a million tiny lights, and the green — oh, the midori — went from sleepy-dark to bright wet jewel-green, all at once, all over, like the hill itself had quietly woken up and decided to be beautiful before anyone could ask it to.

Nobody said anything.

Even Pock didn't pop.

Sora the Soft-Cream, who had drifted up the path last and slowest, melty and dreamy as ever, stopped at the top terrace and let out the smallest "...oh." Her gentle sleepy eyes went wide. "It's like a whole field of *soft swirls,*" she whispered. "Slow ones. Everywhere."

"This," said Maru Matcha quietly, lifting her little bamboo whisk like a wand toward the glowing hills, "is the gift. The early hour. It only gives itself to friends who are awake to take it."

A bird — one single brave bird — finally tried out its first note of the day. *Tweet.* A pause. It sounded a little wobbly, the way you sound when you're the first one to speak in a very quiet room. Then, as if it had checked to make sure that went okay, and found that yes, the morning would allow it, the whole hillside burst into a soft tumble of birdsong, dozens of little voices spilling down the terraces like beads off a string.

"They were waiting too," whispered Maru. "Everyone wakes up gentle, if you let them. Even the loud ones." She glanced, fondly, at Pock, who had managed to stay quiet for nearly a whole minute now and looked rather proud of himself about it.

Yuki's eyes were a little wet. "I'm not crying," she said quickly. "It's the dew. It's a very dewy morning. The dew got on my face."

"It's a very dewy morning," Maru agreed kindly.

---

Then Maru did the thing she'd been planning all along. She knelt by her five little cups, and from a tiny pouch she scooped fresh-whisked matcha — green as the hills, smooth as the morning — and she whisked. *Swish, swish, swish,* went the bamboo whisk, slow and round, and a frothy midori foam rose up in each cup like a little green cloud.

"Whisk slow," she murmured. "Feel calm."

She passed a cup to each friend. Pock, who could not sit still, held his cup very still. Mero, who was asleep, woke up just enough to sip and sigh, "Mmm... dreams... but you can *drink* them." Yuki cupped hers in both paws, warming her cheeks on it. And Sora held hers like it might melt — which, given Sora, was always a real worry.

"Careful," said Maru, "it's the same green as the hills. If you sip too fast, you'll drink up the whole morning."

"I would never," said Yuki, who then sipped quite fast, and got a little green froth-moustache, and did not notice for the rest of the story.

They drank their matcha in the glowing fog, on the top of the misty hill, while the sun climbed soft and slow. Nobody hurried. There was nowhere to hurry to. The matcha was warm and grassy-sweet and tasted, Sora decided, exactly like the color green would taste if you could pour the color green into a cup — which is a very Sora thing to think, and also completely true.

"Maru," said Pock at last, very quietly for him, "I think... I think I get it now. *Pop.*" The *pop* was barely a whisper. "The morning is so quiet that the quiet has a *flavor.*"

"And the flavor," said Maru, "is green."

"It's the COOLEST," sighed Kori Kakigori, who had — somehow, dramatically, draped in a tiny blanket — appeared at the very last minute, sunglasses pushed up to actually *see.* "I take it ALL back. Getting up early is the coolest thing. But don't tell anyone I said that. I have a reputation."

"Squish," said Mero sleepily, which made no sense, but everyone scooted closer anyway, until the five — six — friends were a cozy huddle of buns and bottles and shaved ice on the top of the world, sharing the hush.

---

The sun rose all the way. The fog lifted, gentle as a blanket pulled off a sleeping baby, and the tea hills of Shizuoka stretched out below them, green upon green upon green, rolling toward the morning. The day had begun. Down in the valley the village was waking, and the little teahouse would open later. There would be noise and lights and laughter, and that would be lovely too.

But this — *this* small, secret, dewy, green-gold hour — this was Maru Matcha's favorite. And now it was a little bit theirs, too.

"Same time tomorrow?" asked Pock hopefully.

"Maybe not *tomorrow,*" yawned Yuki.

"Maybe sometimes," said Maru, with her small calm smile. "The gift is best when you don't get greedy with it. Whisk slow. Feel calm." She tucked her whisk back behind her ear. "And come back when the green calls you."

And do you know — every single one of them did, eventually. Even Kori. Especially Kori.

But that, sweet dreamer, is another morning's story.

---

## Goodnight Blessing
May your sleep be soft and green and dewy, like a tea hill curled up under the fog. May the quietest hour find you tucked warm and dreaming, and may it leave a tiny gift on your pillow, just for being there. Whisk your worries slow, little one, until they froth away into nothing. The sun will climb when it climbs, and not one moment sooner — so there is no need to hurry, not now, not tonight. Breathe in the green. Breathe out the day. Goodnight.
