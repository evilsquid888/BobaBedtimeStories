# Story 84: Tama Onigiri and the Rice-Harvest Helpers

**Main Character:** Tama Onigiri
**Guest Characters:** The Heron, the valley's self-appointed weather department; the Field Mice of Local Forty-Seven; the Combine, a grand old harvest machine
**Setting:** Golden autumn rice fields at harvest time
**Theme:** A bowl of rice is never made by one — it is made by everyone
**Japanese Spotlight:** **okome** (oh-koh-meh) — rice
**Cameo:** Marin Kitagawa and Wakana Gojo (My Dress-Up Darling) — Marin strides into the uncut rice in a straw-stuffed scarecrow costume and poses, the Heron pecks off the loose button Wakana is apologizing for, and the escaping straw jams the Combine; walk-on: the Grey Heron (The Boy and the Heron)

---

Tama Onigiri had climbed to the golden valley to thank a field. Every grain of her grew there.

Below lay the last terrace of ripe **okome** (oh-koh-meh) — rice, golden, heavy-headed, still uncut.

Behind the mountains: clouds the color of a wet chalkboard, sleeves rolled up, coming this way.

A heron landed beside her, whump, like a folding umbrella. "Skip the speech," said the Heron.

"Rain before the racks are full," said the Heron, "and that whole terrace sits wet. Gone."

"Then we say thank you the useful way," said Tama, tucking her speech away. "We save it."

"I have one job," said the Heron, unfolding wings wide as a gate. "Two, counting judging the frogs."

"Look at you," said the Heron, lifting off. "A general shaped like a lunch."

---

On the terrace path, forty field mice were holding a meeting on an overturned bucket.

"The Brotherhood of Paws, Local Forty-Seven," squeaked the biggest mouse, "demands snack breaks and dry tails!"

"Nobody ties a knot like a mouse," said Tama. "And I packed a little extra, just in case."

Forty mice signed on at once. "Paws UP!" bellowed the big mouse. One hundred sixty paws went up.

From above floated the Heron's voice, dry as straw. "Wind's turned. **Isoide** (ee-soh-ee-deh) — hurry."

---

In the shed sat the Combine: huge, old, deep red, a whale who had seen things.

"Hmph," said the Combine, unstarted for two years. "So you come crawling to the antique."

"I came walking to the expert," said Tama. Somewhere inside him, something went ka-chunk, pleased.

"This is not rust," he announced, creaking out in a dust cloud. "It is PATINA. Mind your toes."

---

Up the terrace he went in heavy yellow storm-light, mowing gold into tidy rows.

Grain hissed into his hopper like soft maracas. Mice swarmed behind, tying glorious knots.

---

Then, striding into the uncut rice: a blond stranger in a straw-stuffed scarecrow costume and floppy hat, posing.

"Isn't it PERFECT?" squealed Marin Kitagawa, arms out stiff. "Sewn overnight! Scarecrow Marin, debut!"

The Combine braked so hard his hopper sloshed. Forty mice dropped their knots to applaud.

Behind her trotted a tall dark-haired tailor, pincushion on his wrist. "Sorry," said Wakana Gojo. "One button's loose."

The Heron dropped out of the sky, inspected the stitching beak-first, and pecked the loose button clean off.

Straw burst from Marin's sleeve into the wind. She held the pose; Wakana knelt and sewed, apologizing softly.

The straw whirled straight into the Combine's intake. Putt. Putt-putt. Pfffff.

"Straw jam," croaked the Combine, mortified. "It's the dignity that goes first."

---

"Local Forty-Seven!" called Tama. "Half on the spilled straw. Every stalk. Half with me!"

She scrambled up the Combine's side and dove arm-deep into the whale's mouth, hauling straw.

FOOMP. The wad came free. Tama sat down hard in the mud, scarecrow straw in her sash.

The engine caught. Putt-putt-PUTT. The Combine surged up the last row roaring his old harvest song.

"DROPS IN THE AIR!" shrieked the Heron, all dryness abandoned. "GO, GO, GO!"

---

At the barn, sheaves came down a living chain, and Tama swung each up under the eaves.

Plip. The first raindrop hit Tama's sash. The thousandth fell on rice already hanging high and dry.

---

Rain drummed on the roof, the too-late-to-hurt-anything kind. A pot of new rice steamed.

A toothy grey heron landed in the doorway, grinning. "I called this rain first," croaked the Grey Heron.

"You called nothing," said the Heron. "You are a bird of fibs." Both herons glared, delighted.

"A bowl of rice," said Tama, warm by the pot, "is never made by one. Thank you, field."

Mice raised crumbs. The Heron raised a leg. The Combine blinked his headlights, a slow happy heartbeat.

---
