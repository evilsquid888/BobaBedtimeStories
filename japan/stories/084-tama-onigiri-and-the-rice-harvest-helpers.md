# Story 84: Tama Onigiri and the Rice-Harvest Helpers

**Main Character:** Tama Onigiri
**Guest Characters:** The Heron, the valley's self-appointed weather department; the Field Mice of Local Forty-Seven; the Combine, a grand old harvest machine
**Setting:** Golden autumn rice fields at harvest time
**Theme:** A bowl of rice is never made by one — it is made by everyone
**Japanese Spotlight:** **okome** (oh-koh-meh) — rice

---

Tama Onigiri had climbed to the golden valley for one reason: to thank a field.

Fields are bad at conversation. But every grain of Tama grew in a paddy like this.

Below lay the last terrace of ripe **okome** (oh-koh-meh) — rice, golden, heavy-headed, still uncut.

Behind the mountains: clouds the color of a wet chalkboard, sleeves rolled up, coming this way.

A heron landed beside her with the soft whump of a folding umbrella. "Skip the speech."

"Rain before the racks are full," said the Heron, "and that whole terrace sits wet. Moldy. Gone."

"Then we say thank you the useful way," said Tama, putting away her speech. "We save it."

---

"I have one job," said the Heron, unfolding wings wide as a gate. "Two, counting judging the frogs."

"Circle high and call the time," said Tama. "Cut, then tie, then carry. The order matters."

"Look at you," said the Heron, lifting off. "A general shaped like a lunch."

---

On the terrace path, forty field mice were holding a meeting on an overturned bucket.

"The Brotherhood of Paws, Local Forty-Seven," squeaked the biggest mouse, "demands snack breaks and dry tails!"

"Nobody ties a knot like a mouse," said Tama. "You're the middle of everything."

"I packed a little extra, just in case," said Tama. Forty mice signed on at once.

"Paws UP!" bellowed the big mouse. One hundred and sixty paws went up.

From above floated the Heron's voice, dry as straw. "Wind's turned. **Isoide** (ee-soh-ee-deh) — hurry."

---

In the shed sat the Combine: huge, old, deep red, a whale who had seen things.

"Hmph," said the Combine, unstarted for two years. "So you come crawling to the antique."

"I came walking to the expert," said Tama.

Somewhere inside the Combine, something went ka-chunk in a pleased sort of way.

"This is not rust," he announced, creaking out. "It is PATINA. Mind your toes, small triangle."

---

Up the terrace he went in heavy yellow storm-light, mowing gold into tidy rows.

Grain hissed into his hopper like soft maracas. Mice swarmed behind, tying glorious knots.

"Half the terrace!" called the Heron, circling. "You're winning, barely. My favorite kind of winning!"

Then, on the last and steepest row, the Combine coughed. Putt. Putt-putt. Pfffff.

Wind combed down the valley. A sheaf leapt off the pile and burst across the mud.

"Straw jam in my intake," croaked the Combine, mortified. "It's the dignity that goes first."

---

"Local Forty-Seven!" called Tama. "Half on the spilled sheaf. Every stalk. Half with me!"

She scrambled up the Combine's side and dove arm-deep into the whale's mouth, hauling straw.

FOOMP. The wad came free. Tama sat down hard in the mud.

The engine caught. Putt-putt-PUTT. The Combine surged up the last row roaring his old harvest song.

"PATINA!" he bellowed, which was not in the song, but the valley let it pass.

"DROPS IN THE AIR!" shrieked the Heron, all dryness abandoned. "GO, GO, GO!"

---

At the barn, the sheaves came home down a living chain: Combine to mice, mice to Tama.

Tama swung each sheaf up under the eaves, heads down, drying sweet and safe.

Plip. The first raindrop hit Tama's sash. Plip. The second hit the Combine. "Patina-PROOF."

The thousandth fell on rice already hanging high and dry, swaying, smelling of summer.

---

Rain drummed on the roof, the too-late-to-hurt-anything kind, the coziest sound there is.

A pot of new rice steamed. The mice voted Tama an honorary member, full snack privileges.

The Combine parked his front half in the doorway, as far in as he fit.

"You came to thank a field," said the Heron, one-legged in the door.

"I did," said Tama, warm by the pot. "A bowl of rice is never made by one."

Mice raised crumbs. The Heron raised a leg. The Combine blinked his headlights, a slow happy heartbeat.

---
