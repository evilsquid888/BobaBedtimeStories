# Story 18: Yuki and the Towel-on-the-Head Trick

**Main Character:** Yuki the Snow Monkey-Manju
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A traditional outdoor rotenburo bath under falling snow
**Theme:** Little traditions make ordinary moments magic
**Japanese Spotlight:** **taoru** (tah-oh-roo) — towel

---

The snow came down so softly that night that it tickled. It landed on noses and made everybody sneeze the smallest, politest sneezes you ever heard — *fwip, fwip, fwip* — like a row of teacups hiccupping. And right in the middle of all that fluffy quiet sat the coziest place in the whole mountain: a steaming outdoor **rotenburo** (roh-ten-boo-roh) — open-air hot-spring bath — bubbling away under the stars like a giant bowl of warm soup that nobody was allowed to slurp.

Yuki the Snow Monkey-Manju was already in it, of course. She was always already in it. Yuki was a fluffy little white steamed bun shaped like a baby snow monkey, with cheeks gone rosy-pink from the heat, and she floated in the water with only her round face poking out, blowing teeny bubbles of pure contentment.

"Warm cheeks, warm heart, warm friends," she hummed, wiggling her toes. "Best night of winter, easily."

Then she heard the shivering.

---

It was a whole crowd of shivering, actually. Yuki paddled to the wooden edge of the bath and peeked over the rim, and there — bunched up in the snow like a sad little snowball convention — stood her friends.

There was Momo Mochi, pillowy and white, hugging herself so hard she'd squished into the shape of a dumpling. There was Tama Onigiri, his nori sash gone droopy, clutching a thermos he'd already drunk empty. There was Maru Matcha, calm even while frosty, her matcha-cream swirl wearing a tiny snow-hat. And there was Mero Melonpan, the sleepy melon-bread, who had simply lain down in a drift and begun to dream.

"Y-Yuki," chattered Momo, "h-how are you so WARM? You look like a marshmallow on vacation!"

"Because I am IN the bath, silly!" Yuki laughed. "Come in! Come in, come in!"

"We *would*," said Tama, "but we don't know the rules. There are rules at an onsen, aren't there? Niko always says there are rules. I don't want to do it wrong."

Maru nodded slowly, breathing out a little cloud. "Whisk slow. Feel calm. But also — what do we do with our feet?"

Yuki blinked. Then she did the warmest thing a snow monkey can do, which is grin from ear to ear.

"Oh, friends," she said. "There aren't scary rules. There are *cozy* ones. Little traditions. And the very best, most important, most secret one of all —" she leaned in, dripping dramatically, "— is the **taoru** (tah-oh-roo) — towel — trick."

---

Now nobody had ever heard Yuki say anything secret before, so everyone leaned in too, which meant Mero woke up, which meant Mero fell forward into the snow with a soft *whump* and had to be dusted off.

"The taoru trick," Yuki announced, "is this. When you slide into a hot onsen on a cold snowy night, you do NOT put your towel in the water. Oh no. That's not what it's for. You fold your towel up small, small, small — like a tiny pillow — and you place it right on TOP of your head."

There was a silence.

"On your *head*," said Tama.

"On your head," said Yuki proudly. "It keeps the cold off your noggin while the warm water cuddles the rest of you. And — this is the important part — it makes you look extremely distinguished."

"Distinguished," repeated Momo.

"Like a very small, very wise mountain wizard," Yuki confirmed, "who is also having a bath."

Maru considered this. "A bathing wizard," she said. "I like it. This is the kind of nonsense I can believe in."

So one by one, they climbed in. There was a great deal of dramatic tiptoeing first, because the snow had made the wooden edge cold, and a great deal of squeaking when warm toes met warm water. Momo gasped (*ooh!*). Tama sighed (*aaah*). Maru lowered herself in like a tea bag being dipped, very slowly, with tremendous dignity. And Mero, who had been carried over still half-dreaming, slid in without waking up at all and simply began to float, content as a dumpling in soup. The steam wrapped around them like a blanket fresh out of the dryer, and within about four seconds everybody had completely forgotten they'd ever been cold in their lives.

"Ohhh," breathed Momo, sinking down to her chin. "Ohhh, this is the BEST thing. Why didn't anybody tell me hot water was like this?"

"It's always been like this," said Yuki gently. "You just had to come in."

Then she handed each of them a folded little taoru, and one by one, very seriously, they placed them on top of their heads.

And reader, I will tell you the truth: they looked ridiculous.

Momo's towel kept sliding off her squishy head because she was too squishy — it would balance for a moment, then go *slooop* down one side of her face like a tiny waterfall, and she'd squeak and put it back, and it would *slooop* again. Tama balanced his perfectly, which made his round face look like a tiny rice ball wearing a tiny mattress. Maru's towel sat on top of her cream swirl like a hat on a hat, which she declared "structurally interesting." And Mero simply fell asleep with his towel over his eyes, snoring the gentlest *crackle-snore* in all of Japan, a happy little crumb-sparkle drifting off him into the steam.

"I feel," said Tama solemnly, "very, very wise."

"You look very, very ADORABLE," said Yuki, "which is even better."

---

For a while they just soaked. The snow drifted. The steam curled. Somewhere in the dark a temple bell went *bonnnng*, soft as a yawn. A single snowflake landed right on the tip of Yuki's nose and melted there, warm, and she crossed her eyes to watch it go. It was, everyone agreed, completely perfect — the kind of perfect that's so quiet you can hear your own happy heartbeat going *thump, thump, thump* like a tiny drum saying *cozy, cozy, cozy*.

But you know how it is with friends in a warm bath. They get cozy. And when they get cozy, they get *silly*. And it was Momo — sweet, bouncy Momo — who started it.

"Yuki," she said, "your towel hasn't moved ONCE. Mine keeps sliding off. How do you keep it so still?"

"Snow monkey balance," said Yuki breezily. "Years of training. Also, my head is round and slightly squishy, which is the ideal towel surface, scientifically speaking."

"I bet I could keep mine on longer than you," said Momo.

Yuki's eyes went wide. "Is that... a CHALLENGE?"

"It's a challenge," said Maru, who had not been challenged but wanted to be included.

"It's a taoru contest!" cried Tama, sitting up so fast his towel slid down over one eye, which made him look like a tiny pirate of comfort. "Last towel on a head WINS!"

"Wins WHAT?" asked Momo.

Everyone thought about it.

"The last hot sweet potato," offered Maru, producing one mysteriously from beneath the water. (Nobody asked how. At an onsen, you do not question the floating sweet potato. It is tradition.)

"THE POTATO IS ON THE LINE!" Tama announced. "Let the contest BEGIN!"

---

What followed was the wobbliest, gigglingest, most dignified-looking competition the mountain had ever seen.

The rule was simple: no hands. Keep the towel on your head. Last one standing — well, last one *sitting in a hot bath* — takes the potato.

At first everyone held very still, very serious, very wise-bathing-wizard. The snow fell. The water steamed. Four tiny towels sat on four tiny heads, perfectly balanced.

Then Yuki, who was a tiny bit cheeky like a real snow monkey, blew a little stream of bubbles at Momo. *Bloop-bloop-bloop.*

Momo giggled. Momo's giggle wobbled her squishy body. Momo's wobble wobbled her towel. "No fair!" she squealed, scrunching up her face to keep it on — which only made her wobblier — and at last her little towel went *flump* into the water and she dissolved into laughter. "I'm OUT! I'm out, I'm out, I can't, you're too funny!"

"One down!" said Tama. But Tama was laughing too now, and laughing is *terrible* for towel balance. His slid one way, then the other, then off entirely, and he flopped backward into the warm water with a delighted *splash*. "I packed extra towels just in case," he announced from underwater, blowing bubbles, "but I think I lost on purpose because this is the best."

That left Maru and Yuki.

Maru sat perfectly still. Calm. Half-moon eyes. The picture of peace, her towel motionless atop her cream swirl. "Whisk slow," she murmured. "Feel calm. The towel does not fall if the mind does not fall."

"Wowww," said Momo. "That's so wise."

"I read it on a tea tin," Maru admitted.

Yuki narrowed her cheeky little eyes. She knew she could not out-calm Maru. Nobody could out-calm Maru. So she did the only thing a clever snow monkey could do.

She started to hum.

It was a tiny tune, a warm little hot-spring melody that her grandmother manju used to hum, and it was so cozy, so snuggly, so perfectly *bath-shaped*, that Maru's calm got even calmer — so calm, so dreamy, so floppy-relaxed — that Maru slowly, peacefully, blissfully began to slide down into the water, towel and all, until only her swirl was left poking out like a little green periscope.

"...I appear," said Maru's swirl, "to have relaxed too well."

The whole bath erupted. Even sleepy Mero woke up, towel still over his eyes, and mumbled, "Did somebody win? I dreamed I won. I dreamed the potato was a cloud," and went straight back to snoring, which made everyone laugh even harder.

---

So Yuki won. Of course Yuki won. It was her trick to begin with.

But here is the part that mattered, and Yuki knew it the moment she lifted the warm sweet potato out of the water like a tiny golden trophy: she didn't want it for herself.

"Friends," she said, peeling it carefully, "the real prize was never the potato."

"It wasn't?" said Tama, disappointed.

"...Okay, the potato is *a* prize," Yuki admitted, breaking it into five warm pieces and passing them around. "But the *real* one is this. A bath is just a bath. Warm water, that's all. But you fold a little towel on your head, and you make a silly contest out of it, and you hum your grandma's song — and suddenly an ordinary night turns into the warmest night of the whole winter. That's the trick. The little traditions are what make the magic."

"Crackle on the outside," murmured Mero dreamily, "magic on the inside."

"Close enough," said Yuki, and squished a little closer.

They sat together then, five cozy friends with five warm potato pieces and four wet towels and one happy snow monkey, while the snow came down soft as feathers and the steam rose up to meet it. And every single one of them kept their towels on their heads for the rest of the night — not for the contest, which was over, but just because it felt nice. Because it was *theirs* now.

And that, reader, is how a towel becomes a tradition.
