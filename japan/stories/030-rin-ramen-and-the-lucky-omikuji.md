# Story 30: Rin Ramen and the Lucky Omikuji

**Main Character:** Rin Ramen
**Guest Characters:** Granny Ume, the umeboshi-plum shrine-keeper; Maru Matcha; Tama Onigiri
**Setting:** A hilltop shrine where you draw paper fortunes
**Theme:** You make your own good fortune by being kind
**Japanese Spotlight:** **omikuji** (oh-mee-koo-jee) — paper fortune
**Cameo:** Haro (Gundam) — bounces out of the fog shouting fortunes at random ("Great luck! Bad luck! Snack luck!") and leads the lost treat-folk in circles; walk-on: Psyduck (Pokémon)

---
Rin Ramen was so excited he was steaming. Little puffs of golden mist curled off his broth.

Up the stone steps he hopped, putt-putt-putt, noodle-hair bouncing, broth sloshing.

"Today," he announced to the whole mountain, "I draw the LUCKIEST fortune anyone has EVER drawn!"

"Whisk slow. Feel calm," said Maru Matcha, drifting up beside him.

"But my LUCK might!" Rin slurped. Broth splashed onto Maru's head. She blinked.

Behind them waddled Tama Onigiri with a cloth bundle. "I packed a little extra, just in case!"

---

At the top sat Granny Ume, a wrinkly pickled plum behind boxes of fortunes.

"The proper order," she said. "Wash. Ring the bell. Bow. Wish. THEN shake."

Rin rang the bell so hard, ka-LONG, he bounced backward into the fountain. Splash.

Beside the fortune box stood a yellow duck, holding its head with both paws, staring at nothing.

"Me first! ME FIRST!" Rin grabbed the box and shook it with his whole heart.

Rattle-rattle-CLACK. Out flew forty sticks. One landed on the duck's head. "Psy..."

Granny Ume unfolded his **omikuji** (oh-mee-koo-jee) — paper fortune. One tiny doodled sprout. "Small blessing."

"SMALL?" Rin's steam fizzled to a wisp. "I'm a ramen bowl! I do not simmer. I BUBBLE!"

---

Tama waved her paper, beaming: great blessing. Rin plopped under the wishing plaques, gloop, bubbles going flat.

"A fortune isn't a finished cake," said Maru. "It's just the flour. You bake the rest."

---

Then a cold grey fog came rolling up the mountain and swallowed the steps whole.

From far below came shivery little voices. Lost treat-folk, halfway up, lanterns unlit.

Maru whisked at the fog. Fwip. The fog got thicker. "Hm," said Maru. "Not that."

Tama opened her bundle. Little empty picnic bowls. "Extra room," she said. "For soup."

---

Out of the fog bounced a lime-green ball robot, melon-sized, ear panels flapping. "Haro! Haro!"

Haro bonked off the bell, ka-LONG, and hopped down the steps toward the shivery voices.

"Haro! Great luck! Haro! Bad luck! Haro! SNACK LUCK!" it squeaked at every bun it passed.

The lost treat-folk followed Haro in a dizzy circle, three steps up, three steps down.

Haro hopped straight into the fountain, splash, and floated, flapping. "Haro! Wet luck!"

It bobbed there, ear panels folded flat, blinking happily, going nowhere at all.

---

"Fog, lost friends, and a melon shouting luck," said Rin. One brave bubble rose. Foomp.

"Everything's better warm and bubbly," he said, and began, at last, to steam.

Rin steamed so hard the fog glowed gold, like a lighthouse made of soup.

Down the steps he hopped, bowl by bowl, sloshing, never once spilling. Nearly. Whoops.

He tipped on the wet edge, teetered over the drop, and Tama's sash yanked him back.

"A little extra," panted Tama, "just in case."

---

A shivery little bun took one slurp, puffed up warm, and its frost melted clean off.

Maru whisked each bowl to a froth. The steam rose up the steps in a golden line.

One by one, the treat-folk followed the steam up through the fog, bowls warm in their arms.

The fog thinned. The lanterns lit. The whole hilltop was slurping.

---

Granny Ume cradled the last bowl like treasure, took one long sip, and sighed the biggest sigh.

"Sixty years on this hill," she said, "and nobody ever climbed it just to warm an old plum."

"THIS is a great blessing," said Granny Ume. "Not on paper. In a bowl."

---

Rin pulled out his small fortune. "The paper didn't know I had soup," he said.

"A fortune just tells you the weather," said Rin. "Kindness is the umbrella AND the sunshine."

He tied his paper to the plaque rack as the first sleepy stars blinked over the pines.

"Everything's better warm and bubbly," sighed Rin. And tonight, finally, truly, it was.

---
