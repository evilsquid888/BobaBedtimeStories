# Story 88: Pepper-kun and the Midnight Garage

**Main Character:** Pepper-kun (Pepper Lunch sizzle plate)
**Guest Characters:** Kei the kei-truck; a tiny moth
**Setting:** A glowing late-night garage in Tokyo
**Theme:** Care and craftsmanship make anything shine
**Japanese Spotlight:** **kaizo** (kye-zoh) — to customize / soup-up
**Cameo:** Mothra (Godzilla) — lands on the garage roof, the tiny moth is star-struck, and one polite wing-fan blows every tire and beef strip across the garage; walk-on: a Tachikoma (Ghost in the Shell)

---

At the end of Pancake Alley, a little garage glowed pink all night. Tonight, it glowed louder.

SIZZLE! In came Pepper-kun, a round iron hot-plate on stubby legs, beef bouncing, bandana tight.

"Good EVENING, Tokyo!" he announced to nobody. "Where is my patient?"

"...Putt?" Under the buzzing neon sat the roundest, butter-yellowest truck, one headlight dim, sweater drooping.

"I used to bounce," said Kei. "Now I sag. Nobody wants the truck everybody waits for."

"Then tonight," said Pepper-kun, "we do a **kaizo** (kye-zoh) — a soup-up! With the BIGGEST finish ever!"

---

He flung open his toolbox: teeny wrenches, a fish-shaped oil can, one emergency corn kernel.

"Engine first! Three, two, ONE, SIZZLE!" He squirted oil everywhere. A puff of steam fogged the window.

The steam cleared. Kei looked exactly as droopy as before, only shinier. Putt. Koff.

"Was that supposed to do something?" asked Kei politely.

---

"BIGGER finish!" He cranked the radio, hopped onto the workbench, and leapt.

He landed on his own oil puddle. Whoosh. A skating hot-plate goes very, very fast.

He crashed into the tire tower. Five tires toppled and rolled off, one straight at Kei.

Kei tipped her flatbed up like a catcher's mitt. Thump. One tire, caught.

The last tire landed around Pepper-kun like a rubber doughnut. He sat inside it, dripping butter.

---

Then the roof went dark. Something enormous and soft settled onto it. The neon stopped buzzing.

Through the skylight peered two glowing blue eyes and feathery antennae: Mothra, wings wider than the alley.

From Kei's dim headlight shot a tiny moth, star-struck, looping up at the skylight.

Mothra chirped a sing-song hello and fanned her wings, just once, politely.

WHOOSH. Every tire took off. Beef strips flew like confetti. Kei's sweater flapped like a flag.

Pepper-kun's plate flared like a bonfire. "TOO much finish! TOO MUCH FINISH!" he yelped.

Mothra folded her wings over the roof like a quilt, the moth snuggled by one antenna.

---

"Right," said Pepper-kun, very quietly, from inside the tire. "Perhaps not bigger."

So Pepper-kun sat still, which for him is harder than lifting a tire. And he looked.

A tiny fluff nest sat over Kei's left headlight, empty now. THAT was why it was dim.

One spring squeaked because of one pebble, stuck there three whole years.

The sweater drooped because one loop had unravelled, down at the bottom where nobody looks.

"Oh," said Pepper-kun, his sizzle dropping to a simmer. "A real kaizo is a hundred small kindnesses."

---

He tucked the fluff nest into a flowerpot by the window. Kei's left eye lit like the moon.

Tink. Out came the pebble. One drop of fish-oil. Boing. The spring hopped like a cricket.

He hooked the sweater loop back into place, slow as butter melting. Snug.

The radio knob was missing. He pressed the emergency corn kernel on. Perfect fit.

"You're being so quiet," said Kei. "I didn't know you could be quiet."

"Neither did I," said Pepper-kun, polishing a headlight. "I'm saving the sizzle for the end."

---

"Kei. Ready for your **pika-pika** (pee-kah pee-kah) — sparkly-shiny moment?"

"Three! Two! ONE!" Sparks of steam, beef bouncing, butter swooping in a golden ribbon.

VROOM-putt-putt-putt. Kei's engine purred. Her headlights blazed. Her springs went boing-boing-boing.

"I'm BOUNCING!" cried Kei. "Putt-putt, let's GO!" She took a lap on nearly two wheels.

In the corner a round blue Tachikoma spider-tank bounced on four legs. "Again! Again!"

"Save some sizzle for the road, sweet pickle!" laughed Pepper-kun, clutching his bandana.

---

They parked side by side under the pink glow, happily tired.

"You didn't just fix me," said Kei. "You noticed me. Even the bottom loop."

"Care and craftsmanship," said Pepper-kun, "make anything shine. Even a sleepy little truck."

In the flowerpot, the tiny moth came home and dimmed his light. The garage slept.

---

## Goodnight Blessing
The world is full of small kindnesses nobody sees: the loop mended, the pebble shaken free. May a friend always notice the parts of you that others miss. Let your engine rest. Your springs can go soft now. Goodnight.
