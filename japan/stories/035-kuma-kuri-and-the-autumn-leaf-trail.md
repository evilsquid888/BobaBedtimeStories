# Story 35: Kuma Kuri and the Autumn Leaf Trail

**Main Character:** Kuma the Kuri (roasted chestnut / kuri kinton sweet)
**Guest Characters:** Maru Matcha
**Setting:** A mountain temple path blazing with red and gold autumn leaves
**Theme:** Change can be the most beautiful thing of all
**Japanese Spotlight:** **momiji** (moh-MEE-jee) — autumn maple leaves

---

Kuma the Kuri was hiding inside a teakettle, and he was not coming out.

"Kuma," said a calm voice down the spout. "Kettles are for tea. You are a chestnut. Different jobs."

"It's still summer in here," echoed Kuma. "Out there, summer is leaving. Also my toes are cold."

"Chestnuts don't have toes," said Maru Matcha.

"Then why are they cold?" demanded Kuma. Maru had no answer. Kuma had won before breakfast.

"I want summer to stay," he said. "The good things should just stay good. Forever."

---

"Walk with me," said Maru. "If it's terrible, you may go straight back in. I'll bring a blanket."

Kuma climbed out, fuzzy cap first, autumn-leaf scarf trailing. And stopped so fast he nearly tipped over.

The whole mountain had turned to fire. The friendly, crackly, marshmallow-toasting kind.

Crimson, burnt orange, buttery gold. A breeze came, and a thousand leaves let go at once.

"**Momiji** (moh-MEE-jee) — autumn maple leaves," said Maru. "The mountain wears them only a little while."

"Oh," whispered Kuma. "They're showing off."

Then the breeze grabbed his scarf, whipped it off his neck, and flung it into the leaves.

---

"MY SCARF!" Kuma hopped after it. But the scarf was leaf-colored, and the path was ALL leaves.

He checked a mossy stone lantern wearing three crimson leaves like a knitted hat.

"Have you seen my scarf?" The lantern, being a lantern, said nothing. Kuma took that as a no.

He checked a puddle with gold leaves swirled on top like a soup of pure autumn.

He stuck his whole head in. Blub. Just a soup. No scarf.

---

Then: crunch. Kuma froze. He had stepped on a leaf. The best sound he had ever heard.

"Maru. MARU. Did you hear that?" Another step. Crackle. Another. CRUNCH.

He waded into a drift and stomped. Crick-crack-crunch. He forgot the scarf entirely.

"Small and roasty, ready and toasty!" he cheered. "The whole mountain is crunchy like ME!"

He took a running start, which for a chestnut is a determined waddle, and flopped backward. FWUMP.

Leaves fountained up and rained down on his fuzzy cap. Maru laughed like a spoon stirring honey.

---

Up where the steps grew steep stood the grandest maple on the mountain, every leaf scarlet.

"Three hundred autumns old," said Maru. "And it isn't tired of this yet."

Kuma looked up. And up. And there, snagged on the highest branch, fluttered his scarf.

He hopped. He hopped higher. He hopped onto Maru's bowl-head. Not even close.

"Please," said Kuma to the tree, very politely. "That's my scarf. It lives next to my heart."

---

A great gust came swooping down the mountain, and the old maple shivered and let go.

Every leaf at once. Thousands. A roaring, glittering river of red pouring down the trail.

Kuma threw both arms up and the leaves swept over him, around him, right up to his cap.

And down with them, twirling, came the scarf, and landed around his neck like it had never left.

"It gives them ALL away," said Kuma, digging out. "Every year. And it never runs out."

"You can let go of something AND love it," said Maru. "Like crisp outsides and soft hearts."

Kuma bowed low to the tree. A single leaf landed on his nose. You're welcome.

---

"Summer didn't take anything," said Kuma, flat in his pile. "It handed everything to autumn. Your turn."

"And winter's next," he said slowly. "Roasting carts. Steam. Somebody cold holding a small warm thing."

"I think winter might be MY turn to show off."

"Now," said Maru, "you understand momiji."

At the bottom the old teakettle waited in the grass. Kuma patted it. "I'm done hiding."

He tucked the reddest leaf into his scarf, right by his heart, and the mountain crackled softly.

---
