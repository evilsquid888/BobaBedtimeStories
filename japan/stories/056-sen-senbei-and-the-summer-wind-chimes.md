# Story 56: Sen Senbei and the Summer Wind-Chimes

**Main Character:** Sen the Senbei
**Guest Characters:** Garan, a glassblower made of glass; Mimi, a small droopy gyoza
**Setting:** A summer wind-chime (fūrin) festival in the leafy courtyard of a Kyoto temple, hundreds of glass bells hanging from the trees
**Theme:** The small things you make and give away can comfort a stranger you will never meet
**Japanese Spotlight:** **fūrin** (foo-reen) — glass wind-chime
**Cameo:** Hatsune Miku (Vocaloid) — lands on the gate roof in a column of teal light, sings one clear note, a thousand chimes answer in harmony and fifty more shake loose, Sen's wobbly fish bell rings loudest; walk-on: Kero (Cardcaptor Sakura)

---

Sen the Senbei, a flat golden rice cracker in a seaweed belt, was exactly coaster-sized, and knew it.

A breeze puffed. Sen went FLUMP, sideways, into a bush.

"This is the trouble with flat," he said, spitting out a leaf. "The wind files you away."

He squeezed under the great gate and nearly cracked with wonder.

---

The whole leafy courtyard was singing. Thousands of glass bells trembled in every tree.

They were **fūrin** (foo-reen) — glass wind-chimes — each with a little paper tail. Tin. Tin. Tinnnn.

Kero, a tiny golden winged lion-cub plush, zipped between the bells. "Any snacks in these? No? Rude."

"Everybody here makes something," sighed Sen. "I just want to make one thing that matters."

A bell above him went TING, very loudly, as if to say: prove it.

---

"Crisp things keep their shape," said a tall see-through glassblower, ribbons of color drifting inside her.

"I'm Garan," she said. "Far too many bells and not enough arms. Paint with me?"

"I'd crack the brush," said Sen. "I'd crack ME. I crack at the slightest—"

Garan pressed a tiny brush into his paw before he could escape.

Sen painted three wobbly blue dots. The bell turned, and the dots became three fish chasing each other.

---

Then the summer wind came over the roof and gave the courtyard one enormous WHOOSH.

A whole string of bells snapped loose and sailed over the moss like glass balloons.

"My bells!" cried Garan, who stayed put, being mostly made of glass.

Sen hopped after them. The first bell landed in a bush. So did Sen.

---

Then teal light poured down onto the gate roof, and every loose bell turned toward it.

In the light stood Hatsune Miku, teal twin-tails long as banners, headphones glowing, one arm raised.

"Laaa," sang Miku, one clear note, and a thousand glass bells answered in perfect harmony.

The harmony shook the trees. Fifty more bells shivered loose and swirled up like glass snow.

Sen's wobbly three-fish bell rang loudest of all, bonging along up in the swirl.

Miku held the last note and the pose, glowing on the gate roof like a teal lantern.

---

"MORE bells," said Sen, flat on the moss. "Wonderful."

Sen planted his feet on the top step and spread himself wide. Flat. Thin. Ready.

The wind grabbed him. This time Sen lifted, tilted, and GLIDED.

A flat cracker makes a fine kite. Sen swooped through the branches like a backwards leaf.

He caught one bell in his belt. Two on his edges. Eleven in a wobbling stack.

He landed on Garan's bench upside down, ringing like a tiny orchestra. "Small, flat, full of surprises."

---

So Sen painted picture-wishes. A fat sun for someone cold. A teacup for someone tired.

And one tiny sesame seed, very proudly, because nobody ever notices sesame seeds.

"Who gets them?" asked Sen. "Who do I give them to?"

"Strangers," said Garan. "At lantern-time each chooses a bell and carries it home. You'll never know who."

---

The lanterns came on. Weary treat-folk drifted in to choose a bell.

Last of all came Mimi, a small gyoza so tired that even her pleats sagged.

A breeze stirred. The sesame-seed bell went tin... tin... tinnnn, right above her droopy head.

Mimi looked up. Slowly, slowly, a smile spread across her like warmth across cold dough.

She lifted the bell down with both paws, hugged it, and shuffled out into the dark.

---

"She'll never know it was me," whispered Sen.

"No," said Garan. "That's the bigness of it. A kindness with no name on it."

Sen sat on the warm step, exactly coaster-sized, and felt exactly the right size.

Kero snored in a bell above, swinging gently.

High in the trees, a hundred glass bells sang his small kindness into the summer night.
