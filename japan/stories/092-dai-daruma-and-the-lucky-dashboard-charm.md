# Story 92: Dai Daruma and the Lucky Dashboard Charm

**Main Character:** Dai Daruma (daruma doll)
**Guest Characters:** Kei the kei-truck, Maru Matcha, Coro the Korokke
**Setting:** A cozy garage workshop restoring a beloved classic car
**Theme:** The luck you feel is the love you put in
**Japanese Spotlight:** **omamori** (oh-mah-MOH-ree) — lucky charm
**Cameo:** Josuke and Crazy Diamond (JoJo's Bizarre Adventure) — a pompadoured teen fills the garage doorway, his glowing pink spirit "fixes" the runaway tires into a tower that blocks the car door and slams the hood shut, and a compliment on his hair earns a very long pose; walk-on: Shy Guy (Mario)

---

In the back of a cozy garage, on the dustiest shelf, sat a small crimson dome.

A spider was using his one painted eye as a doorbell.

"Excuse me," said Dai Daruma. "I am not a button." The spider moved out.

"Fall down seven, bounce up eight!" he sang, tipping left, tipping right, wobbling upright.

The doors rumbled open, and in putt-putted Kei the kei-truck, butter-yellow and bouncing.

Behind her came Maru Matcha, calm and jade-green, and Coro the Korokke, puffing steam.

---

In the middle of the garage, under a dusty sheet, something dozed.

Maru lifted the sheet. Coro gasped so hard a steam puff hit the ceiling.

An old car. Round fenders, headlights like surprised eyes, paint faded to rusty pink.

"She belonged to an old mochi auntie," said Maru. "Our turn to wake her."

"I will ENCOURAGE!" boomed Dai from the shelf. Nobody heard. He wanted down so badly he vibrated.

---

Kei hauled in a tire so big she wobbled like a turtle wearing a surfboard.

"Putt-PUTT!" beeped Kei, and bumped the horn with her bumper. Forty-one times.

"Pass the wrench!" called Coro. "Now the OTHER wrench!"

"There is only one wrench, Coro," said Maru, passing the same wrench again, very nicely.

Day by day the paint went deep glowing red. Daruma red, Dai noted proudly.

---

The big moment. Kei put a hopeful tire on the pedal.

Vrrm. Vrm. Vmm. The engine sighed and went quiet. Coro steamed until the windows fogged.

"She is missing something," said Maru. "Not a part. Something else."

Dai leaned over the shelf edge. It was a VERY high shelf.

"FALL DOWN SEVEN!" he hollered, and tipped off into the air.

---

BOING off the toolbox. BOING off a tire. BOING off Kei's springy roof.

"BOUNCE UP EIGHT!" He landed among the crew, wobbled, and stood tall.

"You've been here the WHOLE time?" squeaked Coro.

"Whole time," said Dai. "She needs an **omamori** (oh-mah-MOH-ree) — lucky charm. The auntie hid one somewhere."

---

Coro yanked open the toolbox. A tape measure sprang out and chased him in circles.

Kei nosed the tire tower. It tipped. Twelve tires bounded for the door.

The doorway filled with a teen with a tall glossy pompadour, arms folded. The tires bounced off him.

"Yo," said Josuke, a glowing pink spirit rising behind him, fist raised. "Everything here needs fixing."

TAP went the pink spirit. Twelve tires flew back into a perfect tower, blocking the car door.

TAP. The hood clanged shut. TAP. Coro's loose crumbs flew back onto Coro, who squeaked.

"Nice hair," said Dai, because it truly was.

Josuke beamed, struck a pose and held it, pompadour gleaming, pink spirit posing behind him.

---

Maru raised her whisk and tapped the tire tower. It flopped flat with one tired sigh.

Dai bounced through the window into the glovebox and found a teeny daruma charm, cherry-sized, both eyes blank.

---

Maru hung the charm from the mirror. It swung like a tiny bell.

"Paint one eye when the wish begins," said Dai. "The other when it comes true."

Coro dipped a tiny brush, steadied his crumbs, and painted one bright eye. Everyone wished.

Then Maru heaved the hood open and gave one stubborn bolt a final quarter-turn.

Kei pressed the pedal. Vrm. Vrm. VROOM! The headlights blazed. The car honked happily.

Maru painted the charm's second eye. Dai's own second cheek stayed blank, waiting.

"The luck you feel," said Dai to the charm, "is the love you put in."

---

That night the old car hummed down the mountain road, Kei putt-putting behind.

On the back seat rode a small red-hooded figure in a white mask, saying nothing.

Dai rode the dashboard, one eye shining, the two-eyed charm swinging softly above him.

The car beeped, very gently, as if to say: exactly.

## Goodnight Blessing
