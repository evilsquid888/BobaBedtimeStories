# Story 92: Dai Daruma and the Lucky Dashboard Charm

**Main Character:** Dai Daruma (daruma doll)
**Guest Characters:** Kei the kei-truck, Maru Matcha, Coro the Korokke
**Setting:** A cozy garage workshop restoring a beloved classic car
**Theme:** The luck you feel is the love you put in
**Japanese Spotlight:** **omamori** (oh-mah-MOH-ree) — lucky charm

---

In the back of a cozy garage, on the dustiest shelf, sat a small crimson dome.

A spider had been using his one painted eye as a doorbell.

"Excuse me," said Dai Daruma. "I am not a button." The spider moved out.

"Fall down seven, bounce up eight!" he sang, tipping left, tipping right, wobbling upright.

The big doors rumbled open, and in putt-putted Kei the kei-truck, butter-yellow and bouncing.

Behind her came Maru Matcha, calm and jade-green, and Coro the Korokke, already puffing steam.

---

In the middle of the garage, under a gray dusty sheet, something slept.

Maru lifted the sheet. Coro gasped so hard a steam puff hit the ceiling.

An old car. Round fenders, headlights like surprised eyes, paint faded from sunset-red to rusty pink.

"She belonged to an old mochi auntie," said Maru. "Our turn to wake her."

"I will ENCOURAGE!" boomed Dai from the shelf. Nobody heard. He was very far back.

Oh, he wanted down. He wanted to help. He wanted it so hard he vibrated.

---

Kei hauled in a tire so big she wobbled like a turtle wearing a surfboard.

"Putt-PUTT!" beeped Kei, and bumped the horn with her bumper. Forty-one times.

"Pass the wrench!" called Coro. Maru passed it. "Now the OTHER wrench!"

"There is only one wrench, Coro," said Maru.

"Then pass the same wrench again, very nicely!" said Coro, steaming with happiness.

Day by day the paint went deep glowing red. Daruma red, Dai noted proudly.

---

Then came the big moment. Kei put a hopeful tire on the pedal.

Vrrm. Vrm. Vmm. The engine sighed and went quiet.

"We did everything right," said Coro, steaming so hard the windows fogged.

"She is missing something," said Maru. "Not a part. Something else."

Up on the shelf, Dai looked down. It was a VERY high shelf.

"FALL DOWN SEVEN!" he hollered, and leaned forward over the edge.

---

BOING off the toolbox. BOING off a tire. BOING off Kei's springy roof.

"BOUNCE UP EIGHT!" He landed in the middle of the crew, wobbled, and stood tall.

"You've been here the WHOLE time?" squeaked Coro.

"The whole time," said Dai. "She needs an **omamori** (oh-mah-MOH-ree) — lucky charm. Every beloved car has one."

"The auntie hid a charm in here years ago," said Dai. "I saw it. I forgot where."

---

Coro yanked open the toolbox. A tape measure sprang out and chased him around the car.

Kei nosed the tire tower. It tipped. Twelve tires bounced loose and bounded for the door.

Maru stepped sideways, raised her whisk, and tapped. The lead tire wobbled and flopped flat. Eleven followed.

"Whisk slow," said Maru. "Feel calm."

Dai bounced through the open window and plopped into the glovebox. Something soft was there.

Wrapped in a cloth lay a teeny daruma charm, cherry-sized, crimson, both eyes blank and patient.

---

Maru hung the charm from the mirror on its red cord. It swung like a tiny bell.

"Paint one eye when the wish begins," said Dai. "The other when it comes true."

Coro dipped a tiny brush, steadied his crumbs, and painted one bright eye. Everyone wished.

Then Maru slipped under the hood and gave one stubborn bolt a final quarter-turn.

"Luck works best," said Maru, "when the bolts are snug."

Kei pressed the pedal. Vrm. Vrm. VROOM! The headlights blazed. The car honked a happy honk.

Maru painted the charm's second eye. Two bright eyes. Dai's own second cheek stayed blank, waiting.

"The luck you feel," said Dai to the tiny charm, "is the love you put in."

---

That night the old car hummed down the mountain road, Kei putt-putting behind.

Dai rode the dashboard, one eye shining, the two-eyed charm swinging softly above him.

The car beeped, very gently, as if to say: exactly.
