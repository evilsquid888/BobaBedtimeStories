# Story 37: Maru Matcha and the Carp Streamers

**Main Character:** Maru Matcha
**Guest Characters:** Mame-bo, a teacup-sized dumpling with a folded red carp
**Setting:** A spring sky filled with koinobori carp-shaped windsocks
**Theme:** Swim bravely against the wind and you'll grow strong
**Japanese Spotlight:** **koinobori** (koy-noh-BOH-ree) — carp streamers
**Cameo:** Magikarp (Pokémon) — flops out of the stream trying to fly like the carp streamers, soaks everyone, and finally gets lifted by a gust; walk-on: Dragonite (Pokémon)

---

On the breeziest, bluest morning of spring, the whole sky above Whisk Hill went fishing.

Below, a gust parted Maru Matcha's matcha-cream swirl straight down the middle. Fwip.

"**Koinobori** (koy-noh-BOH-ree) — carp streamers," she murmured, combing it back. "Whisk slow. Feel calm."

Then came the smallest, wobbliest sniffle in all of spring, from a clover patch.

---

There sat a dumpling no bigger than a teacup, hugging a folded red carp like a scary treasure.

"I'm Mame-bo," it whispered. "This year I fly my own carp. Except I can't. I'm hiding."

A gust rattled the clover. Mame-bo squeaked and vanished behind the folded carp entirely.

"Look how BIG the wind is," said Mame-bo. "Folded-up fish can't fail."

Maru sat down in the clover. "Folded-up fish can't swim, either."

---

They lifted the tall pole. It tipped. Maru walked three steps sideways, three back, a dignified dance.

"Enthusiastic balance," said Maru, jogging backward under the tipping pole. "Very calm. Very balanced."

The breeze gulped into the carp, puffed him fat and round, and bonked Maru on the head. Boomf.

A bigger gust lifted Maru clean off her feet and set her down in the clover.

"And now I have flown," she announced calmly. "Briefly. As a hat."

---

Then the biggest gust of all yanked the pole from both of them.

The red carp sailed off down the hill, string trailing, like a fish who had quit the job.

Mame-bo gasped. Maru hurried after it. Slowly. Hurrying slowly was her only speed.

It bounced across the stream and snagged its string high on the giant black carp's pole.

"He's stuck," whispered Mame-bo. "It's my fault and I'm too small."

---

SPLOOSH. A big orange fish with huge round eyes and yellow whiskers flopped out of the stream.

"Karp!" it announced, and flopped straight up at the sky, which it plainly believed was water.

It rose one foot. It fell. SPLASH. Maru's swirl went flat as a lily pad.

Its tail whapped the black carp's pole. The red carp slid even higher. Mame-bo squeaked.

Then a gust scooped under the Magikarp, lifted it high, and it hung there flapping, utterly amazed.

"Karp," it said softly, and the gust set it back in the stream, floating, eyes up, pleased.

Maru wrung out her whisk. "Small is light. Light things fly. Jump for the string."

---

Mame-bo took the deepest breath a teacup-sized dumpling has ever taken, and jumped.

Two little paws caught the string. The red carp swallowed wind and lifted Mame-bo straight up.

"I'M FLYING!" squealed Mame-bo, feet kicking over the clover. "I'M FLYING AND I DON'T LOVE IT!"

Dragonite, a round-bellied orange dragon with a delivery bag, hummed up alongside and hovered there, waving.

Maru caught the dangling pole and leaned back, calm as a stone in a stream.

The string unwound loop by loop, and down floated Mame-bo, plop, into the clover.

"You caught him," said Maru. "The smallest one on the hill caught the fish nobody could reach."

---

Back on Whisk Hill they planted the pole together, four paws on the bamboo.

"**Ganbatte** (gahn-BAH-teh) — do your best, little carp," said Mame-bo. "One. Two. Three!"

The red carp caught the wind with a great soft FWUMP and dove straight at Maru's swirl. Again.

Mame-bo held on with shaking paws, and the carp stopped fighting the wind and rode it.

Up he swam, long and rippling, beside all the brave fish of spring.

"The wind isn't pushing," said Maru, swirl rumpled. "It's teaching you to fly."

---

Later, in warm gold light, Mame-bo leaned on Maru's arm. "Will you stay and watch him?"

"There is no rushing a carp," said Maru, tucking her whisk away. "And no rushing a friend."

Above them, the red carp turned one slow happy loop, waving at the dumpling who let him go.
