# Story 37: Maru Matcha and the Carp Streamers

**Main Character:** Maru Matcha
**Guest Characters:** Mame-bo, a teacup-sized dumpling with a folded red carp
**Setting:** A spring sky filled with koinobori carp-shaped windsocks
**Theme:** Swim bravely against the wind and you'll grow strong
**Japanese Spotlight:** **koinobori** (koy-noh-BOH-ree) — carp streamers

---

On the breeziest, bluest morning of spring, the whole sky above Whisk Hill went fishing.

Red fish, blue fish, one enormous black fish, all gulping wind on tall bamboo poles.

"**Koinobori** (koy-noh-BOH-ree) — carp streamers," murmured Maru Matcha, twirling her tiny whisk. "Whisk slow. Feel calm."

A gust parted her matcha-cream swirl straight down the middle. She calmly combed it back.

Then came the smallest, wobbliest sniffle in all of spring, from behind a clover patch.

---

There sat a dumpling no bigger than a teacup, hugging a folded red carp like a scary treasure.

"I'm Mame-bo," it whispered. "I'm big enough to fly my own carp this year. Except I'm not."

A gust rattled the clover. Mame-bo squeaked and hid behind the folded carp entirely.

"Look how BIG the wind is," said Mame-bo. "I'll keep him folded. Folded-up fish can't fail."

Maru sat down in the clover. "Folded-up fish can't swim, either."

"Everybody swims wobbly first," said Maru. "My first whisk put green foam on the ceiling."

---

Raising a carp pole is hard when one of you is teacup-sized and the other is mostly cream.

They lifted the tall pole. It tipped. Maru walked three steps sideways, three steps back, a dignified dance.

"This is the calmest panic I've ever seen," said Mame-bo.

"It is not panic," said Maru, jogging backward under the pole. "It is enthusiastic balance."

The breeze gulped into the carp, puffed him fat and round, and bonked Maru on the head. Boomf.

A bigger gust lifted Maru clean off her feet and set her down in the clover, swirl tilted.

"And now I have flown," she announced calmly. "Briefly. As a hat."

---

Then the biggest gust of all yanked the pole from both of them.

The red carp sailed off down the hill, string trailing, like a fish who had quit the job.

Mame-bo gasped. Maru hurried after it. Slowly. Hurrying slowly was her only speed.

It bounced across the stream and snagged its string high on the giant black carp's pole.

"He's stuck," whispered Mame-bo. "It's my fault and I'm too small."

"Small is light," said Maru. "Light things fly. Hold your breath and jump for the string."

---

Mame-bo took the deepest breath a teacup-sized dumpling has ever taken, and jumped.

Two little paws caught the string. The red carp swallowed wind and lifted Mame-bo straight up.

"I'M FLYING!" squealed Mame-bo, feet kicking over the clover. "I'M FLYING AND I DON'T LOVE IT!"

Maru caught the dangling pole and leaned back, calm as a stone in a stream.

The string unwound loop by loop, and down floated Mame-bo, carp flapping overhead, plop into the clover.

"You caught him," said Maru. "The smallest one on the hill caught the fish nobody could reach."

---

Back on Whisk Hill they planted the pole together, four paws on the bamboo.

"He might buck. He might dip," said Maru. "That's a carp learning the river. We hold steady."

"**Ganbatte** (gahn-BAH-teh) — do your best, little carp," said Mame-bo. "One. Two. Three!"

The red carp caught the wind with a great soft FWUMP and dove sideways, twisting.

Mame-bo held on with shaking paws, and the carp stopped fighting the wind and started riding it.

Up he swam, long and rippling, flashing in the sunshine beside all the brave fish of spring.

"The strongest wind made the best swim," said Maru. "The wind isn't pushing. It's teaching you to fly."

---

They sat in the clover all afternoon until the light went warm and gold.

"Will you stay and watch him?" asked Mame-bo, leaning on Maru's arm. "Just a while?"

"There is no rushing a carp," said Maru, tucking her whisk away. "And no rushing a friend."

Above them, the red carp turned one slow happy loop, waving at the dumpling who let him go.

---
