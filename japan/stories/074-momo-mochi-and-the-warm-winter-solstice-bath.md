# Story 74: Momo Mochi and the Warm Winter-Solstice Bath

**Main Character:** Momo Mochi
**Guest Characters:** Kori Kakigori, Maru Matcha, Mero Melonpan
**Setting:** A cozy bathhouse on the winter solstice with floating yuzu fruit
**Theme:** Small warm rituals carry you through the cold
**Japanese Spotlight:** **yuzu** (yoo-zoo) — yuzu citrus

---

On the longest, coldest night of the whole entire year, Momo Mochi waddled up the snowy lane toward the little bathhouse with her arms full of trouble.

Lemony, bumpy, golden trouble.

"Squish me sideways," she puffed, "these are HEAVY for such tiny fruits!" She was carrying eleven plump **yuzu** (yoo-zoo) — yuzu citrus, a kind of round, sunshiny lemon-orange that smells like a hug wearing perfume. They kept rolling out of her stretchy taffy arms and bonking down the steps — *thoomp, thoomp, thoomp* — like a parade of grumpy little suns.

A snowflake landed on Momo's rosy peach cheek and melted instantly. Momo was a soft, pillowy, snow-white rice cake, plump and squishable and dusted all over with potato starch, and tonight she was on a Very Important Mission. Because tonight was **toji** (toh-jee) — the winter solstice — the night the dark stays longest and the cold pinches hardest.

And Momo Mochi had a plan to make it cozy.

---

The bathhouse glowed up ahead like a lantern someone had tucked into the snow on purpose. Steam curled out of its little chimney in soft gray ribbons. Inside, Momo's friends were already shivering and grumbling and doing that funny hoppy dance you do when your toes are cold.

"It's SO cold," wailed Kori Kakigori, which was a bit much, considering Kori was a towering mountain of shaved ice who was *always* cold and usually bragged about it. Tonight Kori's sunglasses had frosted completely over. "I can't even see my own coolness! This is a total meltdown — except, you know, the OPPOSITE!"

"Whisk slow," murmured Maru Matcha from the corner, hugging her tiny bamboo whisk like a teddy bear. "Feel calm." But even calm Maru's matcha-cream swirl was drooping like a sad little soft-serve.

Mero Melonpan had already fallen asleep standing up. "Crackle on the outside," he mumbled, "...dreams on the... zzz."

Momo's heart did a warm little flip. They needed her. Well — they needed her plan.

"Friends!" she announced, bursting through the door in a swirl of snow and starch. "I have brought the winter solstice with me!"

"You brought... lemons?" said Kori, peering over the frosty sunglasses.

"They are not lemons, they are *yuzu*," said Momo proudly, lining them up on the wooden floor where they immediately rolled away in every direction. She chased them with her stretchy arms, gathering them like a flustered octopus. "And on toji, my grandmother always said, you fill a hot bath with floating yuzu, and the warm water and the sunshiny smell chase the cold right out of your bones. It is called a **yuzuyu** (yoo-zoo-yoo) — a yuzu bath! Tonight we are going to fill that big tub with golden fruit until winter gives up and goes home!"

The friends looked at the deep stone tub. They looked at the eleven runaway yuzu. They looked at Momo, who had a yuzu stuck to each cheek now and looked extremely serious about it.

"...Eleven fruits isn't very much," said Maru gently.

Momo's rosy cheeks fell. She had walked all that way. She had counted them three times. And eleven yuzu bobbing in that great big tub did look, she had to admit, a little bit lonely — like eleven golden ducklings in a very large, very empty pond.

"My grandmother's bath," she said quietly, "always had so many yuzu you couldn't see the water. The whole tub looked like a treasure chest spilled open. I wanted it to look like *that*." She poked one yuzu with a starchy toe. It rolled away, as if even the fruit felt a bit shy about not being enough.

"Well," said Kori, who, beneath all the drama, had the biggest soft heart of anyone, "did your grandmother's bath have a melting ice-mountain in it? Because mine does now. That's *rare*. That's a collector's item." Kori struck a pose, sunglasses gleaming. "Quality over quantity, sweetie."

That made Momo giggle in spite of herself, a little hiccupy mochi giggle, and a yuzu fell off her cheek and bonked Kori on the head. "*Pong.*" Everyone laughed.

---

But Momo Mochi did not have a giving-up sort of squish in her.

"Then we share what we have!" she declared, bouncing on her plump little feet. "Everyone gets a turn, and we make the warmth STRETCH. Just like me!" And she stretched one taffy arm aaaall the way across the room to flick on the bath taps, *splash-glug-glug*, while the other arm gave Kori a reassuring pat.

The water roared in, hot and steaming. Momo rolled the eleven yuzu in one by one, and as each one plopped down she gave it a tiny job. "You," she told the first yuzu, "are in charge of the left corner. You — middle. You two, stay together, you're best friends now." She talked to those fruits like a teacher arranging a classroom, and somehow it worked: spread out and given their own little spots, eleven yuzu suddenly looked like *plenty*. Like the bath was wearing a whole crown of tiny suns.

And *oh* — the smell! It rose up with the steam, bright and sweet and citrusy, the smell of every sunny day that winter had stolen, all given back at once.

"Okay, that smells AMAZING," Kori admitted, sunglasses already de-fogging.

"It smells like... morning," said Maru, and her little half-moon eyes went soft.

Mero Melonpan woke up. "It smells," he sighed dreamily, "like the inside of a good nap."

One by one, the friends slipped into the warm yuzu bath. And here is where it got silly, because a bathhouse full of sleepy desserts is a *very* silly place.

Kori Kakigori sat down in the hot water and immediately started to melt around the edges, going *"oooooOOOOH"* in pure dramatic bliss, syrup swirling out in ruby ribbons until the whole bath turned the prettiest sunset-pink. "Don't worry about me, sweeties," Kori sighed, sinking lower, "I am simply becoming a STRAWBERRY YUZU LIMITED EDITION."

Maru Matcha floated very, very still with a yuzu balanced on top of her swirl like a little hat, looking exactly like a tiny calm planet with one golden moon. "Whisk slow," she said, eyes closed. "Feel toasty."

And Mero Melonpan fell asleep *in the bath*, floating gently in a slow circle, snoring tiny crumbly snores, his crackly crust softening just a smidge in the steam. Two yuzu drifted along behind him like ducklings following their dad. Every so often he bumped softly into the side of the tub — *bonk* — mumbled "...five more minutes...", spun the other way, and kept right on sleeping. It was, everyone agreed, the most relaxing thing they had ever watched.

"Should we wake him?" whispered Maru.

"Never," whispered Momo back, very seriously. "Waking a Melonpan from a steam-nap is against the rules of winter."

Kori cracked one eye open. "There are rules of winter?"

"There are now," said Momo, and stuck out her starchy tongue, and the whole bath rippled with giggles.

Momo Mochi watched all of this with her arms hugged around herself, and she felt so warm and full and happy that she nearly forgot the cold had ever existed at all.

---

That is, of course, exactly the moment the trouble came back.

Because Momo Mochi, you must understand, was made of mochi. And mochi gets *very* squishy and stretchy when it is warm.

She had only meant to dip one toe in. But the water was so cozy, and her friends were giggling, and a yuzu bonked her gently on the head, and before she knew it Momo had slipped *all* the way into the hot bath with a happy little *floomp*.

"Ahhh," she said.

And then: "...Oh."

Because warm mochi, dear reader, does not stay a tidy little rice cake. Warm mochi *spreads*. Momo felt herself going soft, then softer, then stretchy as warm taffy, her plump round self melting outward across the surface of the water like the world's coziest puddle, until she was a wide, wobbly, smiling blanket of mochi covering the entire bath.

"MOMO!" gasped Maru. "You're — you're everywhere!"

"I know," said Momo, a little muffled, because part of her face was being a raft for three yuzu now. "This is a tiny problem."

For one tiny tick of a second, Momo worried. What if she couldn't pull herself back together? What if she'd be a puddle forever? But then a warm yuzu nudged against her cheek, and her friends' giggles bubbled up around her, and the worry simply... melted, the way everything melts in a really good bath.

Because the funny thing — the *wonderful* thing — was that Momo-as-a-blanket was the coziest thing any of them had ever felt. The hot yuzu water held her up. The fruit bobbed in her soft dimples like buttons on a quilt. And her friends, floating on top of her warm stretchy self, suddenly stopped shivering altogether, because being wrapped in warm mochi is just about the snuggest a body can ever be.

Maru tucked a yuzu under her swirl like a pillow. Mero, still asleep, drifted onto Momo's middle and sighed the contented sigh of a melonpan who has found the perfect mattress. And Kori — dramatic, frosty, big-hearted Kori — gave one last little sniffle of pure happiness and went quiet for the first time all night.

"Stay there," sighed Kori, no longer melting in panic but melting in *joy*. "Stay JUST like that. This is the warmest I have ever been in my entire frosty life."

"Squish together," said Momo, and even spread out into a blanket she somehow gathered them all close, "stay together!"

---

So that is how they passed the longest, coldest night of the year: floating in a pink-gold yuzu bath, wrapped in a warm mochi blanket, with eleven sunshiny fruits bobbing all around them and the steam writing soft secrets on the ceiling.

When at last Momo cooled enough to gather herself back into a plump round rice cake (with a satisfying *bloop*), nobody was cold anymore. Not even a little.

"You were right, Momo," said Maru, dabbing her swirl dry. "It wasn't about having enough yuzu. Eleven was plenty. It was about all of us being warm in the same small place."

"That's the secret of toji," Momo agreed, hugging a yuzu to her chest like a tiny golden sun. "The night is long. But a small warm thing — a bath, a smell, a friend, a squish — a small warm thing carries you all the way through to morning."

Mero, still half-asleep, raised one drowsy hand. "Can we keep one yuzu?" he asked. "To smell. For the dreams."

So they each took one home: a little pocket of summer-sun smell to tuck beside their pillows on the longest night. And Momo kept the eleventh for herself, and held it the whole sleepy walk home through the snow, and it smelled like morning the entire way.

## Goodnight Blessing
The night is long tonight, little one, but you are wrapped in warm things — soft blankets, soft breaths, soft golden light. May the smell of something sweet drift through your dreams, and may a small cozy warmth carry you gently all the way to morning. Squish in close to your pillow, just like Momo. The dark is only the world tucking you in. Goodnight.
