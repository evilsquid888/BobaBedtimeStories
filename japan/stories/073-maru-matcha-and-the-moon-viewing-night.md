# Story 73: Maru Matcha and the Moon-Viewing Night

**Main Character:** Maru Matcha
**Guest Characters:** Momo Mochi, Pock Ramune, Kori Kakigori
**Setting:** A grassy hill on the autumn moon-viewing night (Tsukimi)
**Theme:** Quiet beauty is worth staying up a little for
**Japanese Spotlight:** **tsuki** (tsoo-kee) — moon

---

On the last warm-and-cool night of autumn, Maru Matcha bonked up a grassy hill behind an enormous basket.

"Whisk slow," she told the basket. "Feel calm."

The basket did not feel calm. It rolled, tipped, and fired a dumpling into her swirl-hair.

"There," said Maru, perfectly calm, with a dumpling on her head. "Now I am dressed for a party."

It was **tsukimi** (tsoo-kee-mee) — moon-viewing night, when friends climb somewhere high to look at the **tsuki** (tsoo-kee) — moon.

Maru wanted one thing: all her friends on one blanket, looking up at the same moon together.

---

Momo Mochi bounced up first, snow-white and pillowy, and tried to hug the entire hill.

"Squish together, stay together!" cried Momo, squishing a shrub.

Pock Ramune zipped in next, trailing bubbles. "Is it moon time, pop? Where's the moon, pop?"

Kori Kakigori swept up last, a strawberry mountain of shaved ice wearing sunglasses. At night.

"The moon is very bright, darling," said Kori. "I have to stay cool."

Maru stacked fifteen **dango** (dahn-go) — round rice dumplings into a neat white tower, round as the moon.

But where the moon should rise lay one fat gray cloud, stretched out like a sleeping cat.

---

"Where IS it?" fizzed Pock. "Did somebody eat it? It's very round. I would eat it."

"A moon-viewing with NO MOON?" wailed Kori, sunglasses sliding. "This is the biggest meltdown of my LIFE."

"I'll blow it away, pop!" said Pock. He sucked in a huge fizzy breath.

POP! He shot straight up like a rocket, bounced off the cloud, and plopped into the dango tower.

The tower wobbled. Fifteen dango bounced off the blanket and down the hill, boing, boing, boing.

"The MOON SNACKS!" cried Momo, and stretched both taffy arms after them.

---

Down the dewy hill they went, Momo bounding, Pock zipping, Kori sliding on his own syrup.

Momo stretched so far she caught three dango and one very surprised beetle.

Kori skidded through a puddle and arrived at the bottom as a strawberry slush with eyebrows.

"I am a SMOOTHIE now, sweeties," said Kori. "Nobody look at me."

Pock chased the last dango around a stone lantern eleven times. Then it bounced off into the dark.

---

Back on the blanket sat Maru Matcha, pouring tea, with the fifteenth dango on her head.

"It came home," said Maru. "Things do, when you sit still long enough."

Three muddy friends flopped down. Steam curled up into the cool night.

"Whisk slow," said Maru. "Feel calm. Now listen."

Far below, one cricket tuned up, then another, then a whole orchestra. Cree-cree-cree.

Kori took off his sunglasses. His eyes underneath were big and shiny and wondering.

Pock went completely, miraculously still. "Maru," he whispered. "I think I'm being patient. Pop."

---

And that was exactly when the fat gray cloud yawned, stretched, and drifted aside.

Up came the tsuki, huge and buttery-gold, like a great glowing dumpling lifted into the sky.

Silver light poured over the grass, the town, and four round friends with open mouths.

"It's so beautiful I could MELT," whispered Kori. "But I won't. I want to keep looking."

Pock pointed one trembling bubble-arm. "Look look look. There's a RABBIT."

---

In the moon's soft gray markings sat a round **usagi** (oo-sah-gee) — rabbit, pounding rice in a bowl.

"He's making dango!" gasped Momo. "Up on the moon! Just like us!"

"He waited behind the cloud the whole time," said Maru. "The waiting was the party."

---

They ate the dango slowly, the way you eat when you are in no hurry.

Momo finally got her group squish, soft and sleepy, with mud on everybody.

Pock dozed off mid-pop, little bubbles rising gently into the moonlight.

Maru looked up at the rabbit and felt her whole jade-green heart go warm and round.

---
