# Story 94: Niko Nigiri and the Omiyage Souvenir Quest

**Main Character:** Niko Nigiri
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A train-station souvenir shop full of regional treats
**Theme:** The most thoughtful gift fits the friend, not the list
**Japanese Spotlight:** **omiyage** (oh-mee-YAH-geh) — souvenir gift

---

Niko Nigiri stood very straight in the middle of the train station, salmon cape gleaming under the warm lantern lights, and unrolled his list with a snap so crisp it could have sliced a cucumber.

"One souvenir for every friend," he announced, to nobody and to the entire station at once. "Purchased in order. Wrapped to match. Carried home without a single crumb out of place."

A loudspeaker chimed somewhere far away. Travelers shuffled past with bulging bags. And right in front of Niko's bright, attentive eyes glowed the most wonderful place in the whole world: the station souvenir shop, packed wall to wall with **omiyage** (oh-mee-YAH-geh) — souvenir gifts, those special little treats you bring home to say *I went somewhere lovely, and I thought of you.*

"Let's do this neatly," Niko said, straightening his nori belt. "...or deliciously."

The shop doors slid open with a friendly *whoosh*, and out rolled a wave of warm caramel-and-rice-cracker air. Niko nearly floated inside.

---

Oh, what a shop it was! Shelves climbed all the way to the ceiling, every one of them stacked with boxes tied in ribbons. There were crackly senbei in fish-shaped tins, jelly sweets that wobbled like tiny jewels, mountain-pear cakes, sea-salt caramels, and chestnut buns sleeping in straw nests. A little sign over the counter read: *Every region, one shelf. Every friend, one perfect gift.*

Niko clapped his hands together. "Perfect indeed!"

He had a System. Niko always had a System. He pulled a tidy pencil from somewhere behind his nori belt and tapped the top of his list.

"Number one," he read aloud. "Momo Mochi. Recommendation card says: *Best-Selling Mochi Box.* Logical. She *is* a mochi. Done!"

He plucked a beautiful box of mochi off the shelf, set it neatly in his basket, and felt very pleased. Two seconds. New record.

But then he paused.

Because something tickled at the edge of his very organized brain. He pictured Momo Mochi — soft, pillowy, squishable Momo, who stretched her arms like warm taffy and shouted *"Squish together, stay together!"* — and he tried to imagine handing her a box of mochi.

"Hmm," said Niko.

It would be like giving a fish a glass of water. Technically correct. Slightly silly. And not really a *gift* at all.

"No," he decided, putting the box back with great care. "Momo doesn't need more mochi. Momo needs..." He drummed his fingers. "...a hug she can keep."

He wandered the aisles, peering at this and that, until — there. A tiny **tenugui** (teh-NOO-goo-ee) — a thin cotton hand-towel — soft as a cloud and printed with little peaches, *momo* peaches, the very fruit Momo was named for. He held it up. It was squishy. It was warm-colored. It practically begged to be hugged.

"For squishing," Niko whispered, "when I'm not there to be squished." He tucked it gently into the basket. His System had just gained a new rule, though he didn't know it yet: *the gift should fit the friend, not the list.*

---

Niko marched to the next aisle with new energy. "Number two: Maru Matcha."

This one seemed easy. The shop had a whole green corner devoted to matcha everything — matcha cookies, matcha chocolate, matcha-dusted beans, even matcha-flavored matcha, which Niko wasn't entirely sure made sense.

He reached for the fanciest matcha box, the one with gold ribbon and a price that made his rice grains tighten. But again he stopped.

He thought of calm, half-moon-eyed Maru, who carried a tiny bamboo whisk like a wand and spoke in cozy little proverbs. *"Whisk slow,"* she always said. *"Feel calm."* Maru could whisk better matcha than any shop could ever box up. Giving Maru matcha would be like giving the moon a flashlight.

"What does Maru actually *love*?" Niko asked himself, tapping his chin.

The answer floated up gentle and sure: *quiet.* Maru loved a slow, quiet moment.

So Niko skipped the green corner entirely and found, on a low shelf, a small bag of **furin** (FOO-reen) — wind chimes, tiny glass bells that sang the softest *ting* when the breeze touched them. He gave one a careful flick. *Ting.* It was the sound of a calm afternoon. It was the sound of *whisk slow, feel calm.*

"Oh, yes," Niko breathed. Into the basket it went.

---

By now Niko had quite forgotten about going in order. His list flapped, half-ignored, as he zigzagged happily through the shop, *thinking* about each friend instead of simply *reading* about them.

For Rin Ramen — hearty, slurpy, big-feelings Rin, who declared *"Everything's better warm and bubbly!"* — Niko found a fat little packet of golden corn snacks shaped like the corn freckle Rin wore on his own cheek. He laughed out loud picturing his face.

For Mero Melonpan, the sleepy dreamer who napped anywhere, Niko found the softest tiny pillow shaped like a melon-pan bun, so Mero could nap *on* a melon-pan while *being* a melon-pan. ("Crackle on the outside, dreams on the inside," Niko murmured fondly.)

For Dai Daruma, who bellowed *"Fall down seven, bounce up eight!"*, he chose a wobble-toy that always rolled upright no matter how you knocked it — courage in a ball.

For Kori Kakigori, the dramatic shaved-ice diva who lived one degree from melting, Niko found... sunglasses. Tiny, glittery, ridiculous sunglasses. "Stay cool, sweeties," Niko said, grinning, "but not TOO cool."

Each gift was a little harder to find than just grabbing the obvious box. Each one took a moment of *remembering* who his friend really was. And each one, Niko realized, felt warmer in his hands than the last.

---

But the shop was big, and the basket was getting heavy, and somewhere around friend number eleven, Niko hit a wall.

He sat down on a tiny stool by the checkout counter and stared at the one name left on his list. The hardest name. The name he'd quietly saved for last because he had absolutely no idea what to do with it.

*Niko Nigiri.*

His own name.

"There's a tradition," the shopkeeper said kindly. She was a round, twinkling rice-cracker of a creature with a dusting of sesame on her cheeks. "You buy one little omiyage for yourself, too. So that when you're home and the trip feels like a long time ago, you have something to hold."

Niko frowned at his neat, ruined, wonderful list. "But I planned everything for everyone *else*. I didn't leave room for me. That's not very... neat."

The shopkeeper chuckled, soft as crinkling paper. "Neat isn't the point, little nigiri. Look at your basket."

Niko looked. The towel for hugging. The wind chime for quiet. The corn snack, the nap-pillow, the wobble-toy, the silly sunglasses. Not one of them was on his original list. Every single one had come from his *heart*, from really *seeing* each friend.

"You didn't shop with a list," the shopkeeper said. "You shopped with your *memory* of them. That's the whole secret of omiyage. The best gift doesn't fit the *shelf*. It fits the *friend*."

Niko sat very still, the way he did when something true clicked neatly into place.

---

So what did Niko Nigiri choose for himself?

He wandered the aisles one last time, and this time he asked the question he'd been asking all along, just pointed inward: *What do I love?*

And the answer was easy. Niko loved *doing things for his friends.* He loved the planning, the wrapping, the carrying-home, the moment a friend's face lit up.

He found a small, simple notebook with a salmon-orange cover. A list-book. For all the future trips, and all the future gifts, and all the future friends he'd think about while standing in shops just like this one.

"For the next quest," he said softly, and held it close.

Then he carried his beautiful, lopsided, heart-shaped pile of omiyage to the counter, where the shopkeeper wrapped each one in crinkly paper and tied each ribbon into a tidy, happy bow — neat enough to make Niko proud, and warm enough to make him glow.

"Let's do this neatly," he whispered to the bundle in his arms as the train hummed into the station, "...or deliciously."

And he stepped aboard, headed home, his cape gleaming and his heart entirely full, carrying not a list — but a whole armful of love that fit each friend exactly right.

## Goodnight Blessing

Sleepy one, somewhere out there is a little shop full of gifts, and the best one in it has your name softly written on it — chosen by someone who thought about you with their whole warm heart. Tonight, you don't need to be neat or perfect or first in line. You only need to be loved exactly as you are, which you already, always are. May your dreams come wrapped in crinkly paper and tied with the gentlest bow. May you wake remembering that the people who love you carry you with them everywhere they go. Goodnight.
