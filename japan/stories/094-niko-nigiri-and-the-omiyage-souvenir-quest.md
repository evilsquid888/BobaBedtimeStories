# Story 94: Niko Nigiri and the Omiyage Souvenir Quest

**Main Character:** Niko Nigiri
**Guest Characters:** Goma, a round sesame rice-cracker who keeps the souvenir shop
**Setting:** A train-station souvenir shop full of regional treats
**Theme:** The most thoughtful gift fits the friend, not the list
**Japanese Spotlight:** **omiyage** (oh-mee-YAH-geh) — souvenir gift
**Cameo:** Naofumi, Raphtalia and Filo (The Rising of the Shield Hero) — Naofumi's shield turns into a shopping bag, Raphtalia sweeps one of everything into it including Niko's basket, Filo eats the wind-chime stand, and Goma charges them twelve coins for the stand; walk-on: Stark (Frieren: Beyond Journey's End)

---

The station glowed warm as a lunchbox, last train home due at seven.

In the middle stood Niko Nigiri, very straight, salmon cape gleaming, nori belt tight.

He unrolled his gift list with a SNAP crisp enough to slice a cucumber.

It kept unrolling. Past his feet. Under a bench. Out the door. Niko bowed to it.

Twelve doodled faces, one per friend. "One gift each," announced Niko. "Wrapped to match."

Through the glass glowed the shop, packed with **omiyage** (oh-mee-YAH-geh) — souvenir gifts for friends back home.

"Let's do this neatly," said Niko, "...or deliciously." The doors whooshed. He floated inside.

---

Shelves climbed sky-high: senbei in fish tins, jelly sweets wobbling like jewels, chestnut buns in straw.

Behind the counter sat Goma, a round rice cracker with sesame freckles, polishing a ribbon.

"Welcome, tidy one," said Goma. "Shout if you need a ribbon. Or a nap."

Niko tapped the first doodle. "Mochi friend. Mochi box. Done!" Two seconds. A record.

"Hmm," said Niko, picturing his pillowy friend unwrapping mochi. "A mochi doesn't need mochi. A mochi needs hugging."

---

Then the platform doors slid open, and the station wind bounded in like a puppy.

It snatched the list from Niko's arms. Up it flapped, like a startled paper seagull.

"MY LIST!" cried Niko, leaping, cape streaming, straighter than any nigiri ever leapt.

The list fluttered up the cracker pyramid, forty tins high, and settled on top. Smugly.

Niko climbed. Tin by tin. Very neatly. The pyramid began, very neatly, to lean.

"Nobody move," said Niko. The pyramid moved.

---

Forty tins went over like a crunchy waterfall. Niko surfed down on a lid, basket first.

A peach-printed towel flopped into the basket. Tiny glitter sunglasses landed on his head.

The list sailed to the wind-chime shelf, where fifty glass **furin** (FOO-reen) — wind chimes — went ting-ting-ting.

---

The doors whooshed again. In strode a green-cloaked traveler, a raccoon-eared companion and a giant white bird.

His round shield flickered and became a shopping bag. "Souvenir Shield," sighed Naofumi. "Of course it did."

"One of everything!" said Raphtalia, sweeping tins, jellies and Niko's whole basket into the bag.

Filo squawked happily and ate the wind-chime stand in one gulp. Fifty chimes rained down.

"The stand, dear bird," said Goma, holding out a paw. "Twelve coins. No ribbon."

Naofumi paid, tipped the bag out, and the three bowed in the doorway and stayed bowed.

Niko jumped for his list, missed, and came down wearing one chime on his belt like a bell.

By a wooden gift chest trembled a red-haired warrior with a huge axe. "It wobbled," said Stark.

---

The list whooshed out the door and flopped into the fountain, soft and blue.

Every doodled face had melted away. Niko's cape sagged like a flag with no wind.

"Twelve friends," said Niko. "No list. Ruined."

"Tidy one," said Goma, hopping up beside him. "Look what you caught."

A peach towel for a friend who squishes. Glitter sunglasses for one who's always melting.

A chime for a friend who loves quiet. Niko stared. Every gift fit exactly.

"Not one was on the list," said Goma. "You shopped with your memory of them."

Niko folded the soggy list into a tidy paper boat and set it sailing.

---

At the counter, Goma wrapped each gift in crinkly paper and tied every bow tidy.

"The best gift," said Niko, "fits the friend. Not the list."

"One more tradition," said Goma. "A little omiyage for yourself, for when the trip feels far."

Niko chose a small salmon-orange book with empty pages. For the next quest.

---

The train hummed in at seven exactly. Niko stepped aboard, arms full of lopsided omiyage.

Goma waved a ribbon from the shop door. Niko's chime went ting.

The glitter sunglasses slid over his eyes. "Let's do this neatly," Niko whispered, "...or deliciously."
