# Story 94: Niko Nigiri and the Omiyage Souvenir Quest

**Main Character:** Niko Nigiri
**Guest Characters:** Goma, a round sesame rice-cracker who keeps the souvenir shop
**Setting:** A train-station souvenir shop full of regional treats
**Theme:** The most thoughtful gift fits the friend, not the list
**Japanese Spotlight:** **omiyage** (oh-mee-YAH-geh) — souvenir gift

---

The station glowed warm as a lunchbox, and the last train home was due at seven.

In the middle stood Niko Nigiri, very straight, salmon cape gleaming, nori belt snapped tight.

He unrolled his list with a SNAP so crisp it could have sliced a cucumber.

The list kept unrolling. Past his feet. Under a bench. Out the door. Niko bowed to it.

"One omiyage for every friend," he announced. "Wrapped to match. Home by seven. Neatly."

Through the glass glowed the shop, packed with **omiyage** (oh-mee-YAH-geh) — souvenir gifts that say: I thought of you.

"Let's do this neatly," said Niko, "...or deliciously." The doors whooshed open. He floated inside.

---

Shelves climbed sky-high: senbei in fish tins, jelly sweets wobbling like jewels, chestnut buns dozing in straw.

Behind the counter sat Goma, a round twinkling rice cracker with sesame freckles, polishing a ribbon.

"Welcome, tidy one," said Goma. "Shout if you need a ribbon. Or a nap."

Niko tapped his list. "Friend one: my mochi friend. The mochi box. Done!" Two seconds. A record.

Then he pictured his pillowy friend unwrapping a box of mochi. Like water for a fish.

"Hmm," said Niko, setting it back with care. "A mochi doesn't need mochi. A mochi needs hugging."

---

Then the platform doors slid open, and the station wind bounded in like a hungry puppy.

It snatched the list straight out of Niko's arms. Up it flapped, like a startled paper seagull.

"MY LIST!" cried Niko, and leapt, cape streaming, straighter than any nigiri has ever leapt.

The list fluttered up to the cracker pyramid, forty tins high, and settled on top. Smugly.

Niko climbed. Tin by tin. Very neatly. The pyramid began, very neatly, to lean.

"Nobody move," said Niko. The pyramid moved.

---

Forty tins went over like a crunchy waterfall. Niko surfed down on a fish-shaped lid, basket first.

A peach-printed cotton towel flopped into the basket. Tiny glitter sunglasses landed on his head.

He bounced off the jelly display, which wobbled thoughtfully, and landed upright in front of the counter.

---

The list sailed on to the wind-chime shelf, where fifty glass **furin** (FOO-reen) — wind chimes — went ting-ting-ting.

Niko jumped for it, missed, and came down wearing one chime on his belt like a bell.

The list dove into the straw nests where the chestnut buns dozed. Niko tiptoed. Niko sneezed.

Fifty chestnut buns bounced awake and glared. The list whooshed out the door toward the platform.

---

It flopped into the stone fountain and went soft and blue. Not a single friend left on it.

Niko's cape sagged like a flag with no wind. "Twelve friends," he said. "No list. Ruined."

Goma hopped up beside him, tinkling the chime on his belt. "Tidy one. Look what you caught."

A peach towel for a friend who squishes. A chime for a friend who loves quiet.

Glitter sunglasses for a friend who lives one degree from melting. Niko stared.

Every gift fit a friend exactly. Not one of them had been on the list.

"You didn't shop with the list," said Goma. "You shopped with your memory of them."

Niko folded the soggy list into a tidy paper boat and set it sailing across the fountain.

---

Back at the counter, Goma wrapped each gift in crinkly paper and tied every bow tidy.

"The best gift," said Niko, "fits the friend. Not the list."

"One more tradition," said Goma. "A little omiyage for yourself. For when the trip feels far away."

Niko thought, and chose a small salmon-orange book with empty pages. For the next quest.

---

The train hummed in at seven exactly. Niko stepped aboard, arms full of lopsided, heart-shaped omiyage.

Goma waved a ribbon from the shop door. The chime on Niko's belt went ting.

The glitter sunglasses slid over his eyes. "Let's do this neatly," Niko whispered, "...or deliciously."
