# Story 59: Dai Daruma and the Calligraphy Brush

**Main Character:** Dai Daruma
**Guest Characters:** Suzuri, an old ink-stone who is the calligraphy master
**Setting:** A traditional calligraphy classroom in Kyoto, with ink stones, soft brushes, and crisp white rice paper
**Theme:** Your first try doesn't have to be perfect to be proud
**Japanese Spotlight:** **kaku** (kah-koo) — to write / draw

---

Dai Daruma bounced into the calligraphy classroom, off a cushion, wobbled left, wobbled right, and stood.

"Today," he announced to the quiet room, "is the day I learn to **kaku** (kah-koo) — to write!"

A startled brush slid off the low table and bonked him gently on the dome.

"Hello to you too," said Dai. The brush was as long as he was. A sleepy noodle.

The floor was soft golden **tatami** (tah-tah-mee) — woven straw mats. The walls hung with perfect scrolls.

In the corner dozed Suzuri, the old ink-stone master, grey and mossy and patient as a hill.

"One bold, beautiful mark," said Dai. "Then it goes on that wall. Center. Top. Today."

---

He dipped the brush in glossy black ink and squared his already-square eyebrows.

He leaned forward for the first stroke, and being round, Dai did what round things do.

He tipped. The brush shot sideways. A black streak flew across the paper like a comet.

Dai landed on his back, four stubby limbs in the air, the brush balanced on his nose.

"Well," he said, rocking upright. "That was certainly A stroke. Fall down seven, bounce up eight!"

---

Try two had splotches. Try three had a blob like a cat curled up in the middle.

Try four was going beautifully, until Dai wobbled with joy and smeared right through it.

He held the brush higher. Ink ran down his arm. Lower. The bristles quacked like a duck.

He closed his eyes for focus, which is famously a bad idea, and painted the ceiling.

He had ink on his eyebrows, his belly, one foot, and the cushion.

"This brush," Dai informed the room, "is being difficult on purpose."

---

Then his elbow caught the ink pot. It tipped. A black river slid across the tatami.

Straight toward the master's perfect scrolls, stacked on the floor by the wall.

"NO no no!" Dai bounced ahead of it and threw down paper. The river soaked through.

He threw down the cushion. The river went around it, politely, and kept going.

So Dai did the only thing a daruma can do. He sat down in it. Hard.

The river stopped. The scrolls were saved. Dai was now ink-black from the belly down.

"Mm," said Suzuri, one eye opening. "Interesting technique."

---

Dai stood up, dripping, and looked at the paper under him.

There, on the white rice paper, was a circle. One bold, round, wobbly circle. His own bottom.

"That," said Suzuri, "is an **ensō** (en-soh) — a single brushed circle. The hardest shape of all."

"I made it," whispered Dai, "by falling down."

"Everyone does," said Suzuri. "Every steady brush was a wobbly one first. We just hang the last try."

Dai looked at his floor. Comet. Blob. Cat. Ceiling. Not failures. A whole floor of trying.

---

He picked up the brush, eyebrows a disaster. "One more. Not for perfect. For proud."

This time, when he tipped, he went WITH it, swaying his whole round body into the stroke.

Shhhk. One big circle, leaning a little, fat on one side, with a happy spray of droplets.

"Hello, circle," said Dai. "You were inside the wobble the whole time."

Suzuri chose the very center of the wall, and Dai, wobbling on a stool, hung it himself.

---

The paper doors went gold, then blue. The classroom hushed.

Dai caught his reflection in the dark window. One painted eye. One cheek still blank.

"Not yet," he told the blank cheek fondly. "You're waiting for a much bigger day."

He yawned a round yawn and tipped onto his side, brush tucked under one arm.

"Fall down seven," he murmured, "bounce up eight. And kaku with your whole brave heart."

---

## Goodnight Blessing
Your first try does not have to be perfect, only brave. Tomorrow you may wobble and smudge, and that is how brave beginnings look. Rest now, soft and round, and bounce up gently in the morning. Goodnight.
