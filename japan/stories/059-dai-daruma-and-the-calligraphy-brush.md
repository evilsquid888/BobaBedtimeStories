# Story 59: Dai Daruma and the Calligraphy Brush

**Main Character:** Dai Daruma
**Guest Characters:** Suzuri, an old ink-stone who is the calligraphy master
**Setting:** A traditional calligraphy classroom in Kyoto, with ink stones, soft brushes, and crisp white rice paper
**Theme:** Your first try doesn't have to be perfect to be proud
**Japanese Spotlight:** **kaku** (kah-koo) — to write / draw
**Cameo:** Will Serfort and Elfaria Albis (Wistoria: Wand and Sword) — a swordsman with zero magic tumbles in, out-wobbles Dai's brush, slices the whole paper stack into perfect squares, and Elfaria floats down to pick the wobbly stroke as her favourite; walk-on: Kuriboh (Yu-Gi-Oh)

---

Dai Daruma bounced into the calligraphy classroom, off a cushion, wobbled, and stood.

"Today," he announced, "is the day I learn to **kaku** (kah-koo) — to write!"

A startled brush, long as he was, slid off the table and bonked him on the dome.

In the corner dozed Suzuri, the old ink-stone master, grey and mossy and patient as a hill.

"One bold, beautiful mark," said Dai. "Then it goes on that wall. Center. Today."

---

He dipped the brush in glossy ink and squared his already-square eyebrows.

He leaned in for the first stroke, and being round, did what round things do.

He tipped. The brush shot sideways. A streak flew across the paper like a comet.

Dai landed on his back, limbs in the air, the brush balanced on his nose.

"Well," he said, rocking upright. "That was A stroke. Fall down seven, bounce up eight!"

---

Try two had splotches. Try three had a blob like a curled-up cat.

Try four was going beautifully, until Dai wobbled with joy and smeared it.

Higher: ink ran down his arm. Lower: the bristles quacked like a duck.

He closed his eyes for focus, famously a bad idea, and painted the ceiling.

"This brush," Dai informed the room, "is being difficult on purpose."

---

CRASH. Through the paper door tumbled Will Serfort, white-haired swordsman. "Zero magic, one sword!" said Will.

He grabbed a brush, swung it like a sword, and left a stroke wobblier than all of Dai's.

"HA! Worse than yours!" said Will proudly, and drew his real sword. Shing, shing, SHING.

The paper stack fell into a hundred perfect squares and snowed over Dai and the table.

Soft light opened through the roof, and Elfaria drifted down it, golden hair floating.

She lifted Will's wobbly stroke. "My favourite," said Elfaria. Will glowed. They hovered at the rafters, waving.

---

Dai shook squares off his eyebrows. "That," he said, "is a LOT of squares."

Then his elbow caught the ink pot. It tipped. A black river slid across the mats.

Straight toward the master's perfect scrolls, stacked by the wall.

"NO no no!" Dai bounced ahead and threw down squares. The river soaked through.

He threw down the cushion. The river went around it, politely, and kept going.

So Dai did the only thing a daruma can do. He sat in it. Hard.

The river stopped. The scrolls were saved. Dai was ink-black, belly down.

"Mm," said Suzuri, one eye opening. "Interesting technique."

---

Dai stood up, dripping. On the paper under him was a circle. One bold, wobbly circle. His bottom.

"That," said Suzuri, "is an **ensō** (en-soh) — a brushed circle. The hardest shape of all."

"I made it," whispered Dai, "by falling down."

"Everyone does," said Suzuri. "Every steady brush was wobbly first. We just hang the last try."

Dai looked at his floor. Comet. Blob. Cat. Ceiling. A whole floor of trying.

---

He picked up the brush. "One more. Not for perfect. For proud."

This time, when he tipped, he went WITH it, swaying his whole body into the stroke.

Shhhk. One big circle, leaning a little, fat on one side, with a spray of droplets.

"Hello, circle," said Dai. "You were inside the wobble the whole time."

Suzuri chose the center of the wall, and Dai, wobbling on a stool, hung it.

---

The paper doors went blue. Dai caught his reflection in the window. One painted eye. One cheek blank.

"Not yet," he told the blank cheek fondly. "You're waiting for a bigger day."

Kuriboh, a brown fuzzball with green claws, fuzzed down from the rafters and curled against Dai's belly.

Dai yawned a round yawn and tipped onto his side, brush under one arm.

"Fall down seven," he murmured, "bounce up eight. And kaku with your whole brave heart."

---

## Goodnight Blessing
Your first try does not have to be perfect, only brave. Tomorrow you may wobble and smudge, and that is how brave beginnings look. Rest now, soft and round, and bounce up gently in the morning. Goodnight.
