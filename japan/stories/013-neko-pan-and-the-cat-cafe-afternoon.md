# Story 13: Neko-Pan and the Cat Café Afternoon

**Main Character:** Neko-Pan (Maneki Melon Cream)
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A sun-warm cat café full of sleepy purring cats
**Theme:** Gentleness earns the softest trust
**Japanese Spotlight:** ねこ (neko) = cat

---

Now, between you and me, there is exactly one place in the whole world where a melon-cream bun shaped like a lucky cat feels completely, gigglingly at home — and that place is a cat café. So when Neko-Pan rolled up to the little wooden door with the painted paw on it, his one raised paw was already waving so fast it nearly took off like a propeller.

"Hello, hello, HELLO!" Neko-Pan chirped, his gold-foil collar bell going *ting-a-ling*. "Wave for luck, share the luck!"

Inside, the café was warm as a freshly baked loaf. Sunbeams poured through the windows in long golden ribbons, and on every cushion, shelf, and sunny windowsill lay a **ねこ** (neko) = cat. There were fat orange ones, sleek gray ones, and one tabby flopped upside-down with all four paws in the air, snoring like a tiny chainsaw.

Neko-Pan's custard heart went all gooey-warm. "So many cats," he whispered. "And I'm a cat too! Sort of! A cat made of bread!"

This was going to be the luckiest afternoon ever.

---

Neko-Pan wobbled from cushion to cushion, waving and beaming. The orange cat let him scratch under its chin and rumbled like a happy little motorboat. The gray cat bumped its head right into Neko-Pan's soft middle, leaving the tiniest dent in his crust — which Neko-Pan thought was the greatest honor of his whole baked life.

"You like me!" he gasped. "You ALL like me!"

And mostly, they did. The cats found the wobbly little bun very interesting indeed: he smelled faintly of melon and sugar, he was warm, and he never, ever stopped smiling. One by one, they came to say hello, until Neko-Pan was buried in a soft purring pile, waving his lucky paw out the top like a flag.

But after a while, Neko-Pan noticed something.

High up on the very tallest shelf, tucked behind a teapot and a stack of saucers, was a small puff of cloud-white fur. It was so fluffy it looked less like a cat and more like a forgotten marshmallow. Two round eyes peeked out from inside all that floof — and the moment those eyes met Neko-Pan's, they went *wide*, and the puff scootched backward into the shadows with a tiny, frightened *mrrp*.

"Oh," said Neko-Pan softly. "Hello, little one."

The café owner — a kind woman with a flour-dusted apron — knelt down beside him. "That's Yuki," she said. "Our shyest **ねこ**. She came to us very small and very scared, and she's never once come down for a cuddle. Not for anyone." She sighed gently. "Some friends just take a little longer."

Neko-Pan looked up at the fluffy white puff. The puff looked down at him and trembled.

And Neko-Pan's whole bready heart went *whoomph* with the biggest, softest feeling. "Everyone deserves a lucky day," he said. "Even the shy ones. *Especially* the shy ones."

---

Now, if you have ever tried to make a shy creature like you, you already know the secret. But Neko-Pan did not know the secret yet. So he did what seemed obvious.

He waved.

He waved his lucky paw as hard and as friendly as he possibly could. "HI YUKI! IT'S ME! I'M A CAT TOO! WAVE FOR LUCK!"

Yuki vanished behind the teapot so fast she knocked a saucer, which spun on the shelf — *wobble-wobble-wobble-CLINK* — and Neko-Pan winced. "Too loud," he realized. "Whoops."

So he tried again, quieter. He stacked himself onto a chair, then a cushion, then a teetering little tower of catnip pillows, climbing up and up toward the high shelf. "Yuki," he whispered, swaying like a sock in the wind, "I just want to say hi up clo—"

The pillow tower went *foomp*.

Neko-Pan tumbled down in a slow, soft, melon-cream somersault and landed — *poof* — right in the middle of the snoring tabby, who woke up, blinked, decided this was fine, and went back to sleep using Neko-Pan as a pillow.

From the high shelf, two round eyes watched. Yuki had not come down. But she had not run, either. She was simply... watching.

Neko-Pan lay there squished under a sleepy cat and thought very hard.

*Waving didn't work. Climbing didn't work. Being loud REALLY didn't work.* He looked at the way the other cats had come to him — not because he chased them, but because he'd stayed warm and soft and still, and let *them* decide.

"Ohhhh," he breathed. "I've been doing it backwards."

---

So Neko-Pan stopped trying to reach Yuki at all.

Instead, he found the sunniest spot on the floor, right below her shelf — a warm golden puddle of afternoon light — and he simply sat down in it. He tucked his paw against his side. He made himself small and round and cozy, like a fresh bun cooling on a windowsill. And he waited.

It is, I'll admit, a very funny thing to watch: a lucky-cat bun sitting *perfectly* still when every wobbly bit of him wanted to bounce and wave. His paw kept twitching with the urge. But each time, he breathed slow and whispered, "Patience, Neko-Pan. Soft and slow. Soft and slow."

Minutes went by, warm and quiet. The clock ticked. A cat snored. Dust sparkled in the sunbeams like floating sugar.

And then — *tip*.

A single white paw appeared at the edge of the high shelf.

Neko-Pan did not move. He did not even let his bell jingle. He just kept glowing his warm, soft glow.

*Tip, tip.* Yuki crept down to the next shelf. She stopped. She stared. Neko-Pan stayed sweet and still as bread.

*Tip, tip, tip.* Down to a chair. Down to a cushion. Closer, closer, until the little marshmallow of a cat was standing right at the edge of the sunny puddle, whiskers twitching, nose working hard at the melon-and-custard smell drifting off the funny round friend who, for once, was being so beautifully, patiently quiet.

Neko-Pan's heart was doing somersaults inside his crust. But on the outside, he only blinked, slow and gentle, the way the calmest cats do — a slow blink that says, in cat language, *you're safe with me.*

Yuki blinked slowly back.

And then — oh, you would have squealed — she took one tiny step into the sunbeam, leaned the very tip of her cold little nose against Neko-Pan's warm cheek, and *boop.*

"Hi, Yuki," Neko-Pan whispered, so softly it was barely a sound at all.

Yuki did not run.

Instead, she did something she had never, ever done for anyone. She circled once, twice, kneaded the air with her fluffy paws — and curled up against Neko-Pan's soft middle, tucked into the warm dent where the gray cat had bumped him earlier, fitting there like she'd been measured for it. A rumble started deep inside all that floof. A purr. Small and shy at first, then bigger, then a great big motorboat purr that shook her whole fluffy body.

The café owner pressed both hands to her mouth. "She's *purring*," she whispered. "She came down. She actually came down."

Neko-Pan did not dare move a single crumb. He just glowed, and waved his paw — very, very slowly, very, very gently — and let the shy little cat sleep against his heart.

---

Soon, all the other cats noticed the new warm pile and came padding over — the orange motorboat, the gray head-bumper, the upside-down tabby — until Neko-Pan was once more buried in purring fluff, with Yuki curled snuggest of all, right over his custard heart.

"You know," Neko-Pan murmured sleepily, "I came here thinking *I* was the lucky one, getting to meet all of you. But I think maybe the luck wasn't mine to keep. I think it was Yuki's, all along. I just had to wait quietly long enough to hand it over."

Outside, the sun went soft and orange. The café smelled of warm milk and clean fur and toasty melon bread. And in the very center of a great purring pile of cats, a small white marshmallow of a creature slept without a single worry — because somebody had been gentle enough, and patient enough, and quiet enough, to be worth trusting.

"Wave for luck," Neko-Pan whispered, his eyes drifting closed, his paw finally, happily still. "Share the luck."

And he did.

Tonight, let your breath go slow and your heart go warm, and curl up snug as a marshmallow in the safest sunbeam there is — your very own cozy bed. Somebody, somewhere, is purring just for you. Sweet dreams, little one. Goodnight.
