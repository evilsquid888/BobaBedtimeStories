# Story 36: Yuki and the First Snowfall

**Main Character:** Yuki the Snow Monkey-Manju
**Guest Characters:** Mero Melonpan, Momo Mochi, Rin Ramen
**Setting:** A quiet village square under the season's first snow
**Theme:** Fleeting things are for sharing fast and remembering slow
**Japanese Spotlight:** **yuki** (yoo-kee) — snow

---

Before sunrise, the village square turned white, soft and silent as a held breath.

In the steam-house, a little black nose twitched, sat up, and dragged Yuki after it.

"**Yuki** (yoo-kee) — snow!" squeaked Yuki the Snow Monkey-Manju. "My own name, falling from the sky!"

She flopped backward into the white with a fwump. One glorious snow angel.

The sky glowed peach over the rooftops. "One hour, then puddles," said Yuki. "I'm waking everybody."

---

The bakery was nearest. From inside came a snore with the slow rhythm of rising dough.

She tapped the window. She tossed pebbles. She played a full drum solo with both paws.

Under his blanket, Mero Melonpan mumbled, "Five more loaves..."

Yuki climbed in and shouted, "Mero! SNOW! Your favorite weather you've never once been awake for!"

Mero's baker paws kneaded her shoulders, folded her arms, and tucked her in like a proofing croissant.

"FREE FROSTING!" Yuki yelled into his ear. "FALLING FROM THE SKY! NO LINE!"

"Save me the corner piece," murmured Mero, smiling beautifully, and snored on.

---

Momo Mochi squished awake at one knock and burst out wearing her blanket as a cape.

"Snow. Thin. Melting. **Hayaku** (hah-YAH-koo) — hurry!" panted Yuki.

"Squish together, stay together!" cried Momo, bouncing down the lane, banging shutters like a soft alarm clock.

The noodle-shop door banged open and Rin Ramen charged out, broth sloshing, absolutely thrilled.

Everywhere Rin stepped, a circle of snow vanished with a small apologetic hiss. "I'm helping!"

"You're a walking spring!" wailed Yuki. "You're erasing it! Bench! You're under snow arrest!"

Rin stood on the bench, steaming with shame, and also just steaming.

---

The first sunbeam slid down a chimney and touched the snow on the bakery awning.

Drip, said the awning. Drip. Drip. The politest possible sound for "you are out of time."

Then the idea landed on Yuki's nose, cold and perfect. Stop bringing friends to the snow.

"Bring the snow to the friends!" she bellowed. "The north porch is shady all morning! Emergency cozy maneuvers!"

---

Yuki flipped two tea trays upside down, heaped them with snow, and sledded them across the square.

Sprint, skid, dump, sprint back. A manju is not built for cargo work. A manju does it anyway.

Momo flopped belly-down in the deepest drift and plowed forward. Snow stuck to her sticky everything.

"I'M A SNOWBALL WITH OPINIONS!" hollered Momo, now a boulder with a face, picking up speed.

Rin stood at the porch corner facing the wall, blocking the wind. "I'm a guard radiator!"

"DO NOT TURN AROUND," called Yuki, as the boulder thundered straight toward his back.

Rin saluted the wall. Momo WHUMPED into the porch beside him. A snow mountain rose up.

"Did something happen?" asked Rin, still facing the wall.

---

When the sun cleared the roofs, the square was wet stone. The porch held knee-deep blue snow.

Everyone made **yukidaruma** (yoo-kee-dah-roo-mah) — snowmen — tiny ones, just big enough for two paws.

Momo's had no bones and collapsed into a happy lump. "Finished," she declared. "Also perfect."

Rin made his by touch, still facing the wall. It looked like a noodle's nightmare. He loved it.

By midmorning the little snow-friends began, very slowly, to drip. Plip. Plip-plip. Ploop.

"It was always going," said Yuki, leaning on Momo. "You run fast so you can sit still together."

---

One snow-friend stayed: a thumb-sized snow-monkey with poked-in cheeks, set on a saucer in the icebox.

Around lunchtime Mero shuffled into the square, blinking at wet stones. "Did I miss weather?"

He lifted the tiny snow-monkey in two careful paws, eyes wide. "I shall save it forever."

"It's already melting," said Yuki.

"Then I shall remember it forever," said Mero. "Keeps better. Takes up less icebox."

And the drips went plip, plip, ploop, all afternoon, like the snow itself was nodding off.
