# Story 36: Yuki and the First Snowfall

**Main Character:** Yuki the Snow Monkey-Manju
**Guest Characters:** Mero Melonpan, Momo Mochi, Rin Ramen
**Setting:** A quiet village square under the season's first snow
**Theme:** Fleeting things are for sharing fast and remembering slow
**Japanese Spotlight:** **yuki** (yoo-kee) — snow
**Cameo:** Marida Cruz and the NZ-666 Kshatriya (Gundam Unicorn) — a huge four-winged robot lands softly in the square, its warm thrusters melt every flake at once, and its very formal pilot asks Yuki to warm her frozen cheeks; walk-on: Pen Pen (Evangelion)

---

Before sunrise, the village square turned white, soft and silent as a held breath.

"**Yuki** (yoo-kee) — snow!" squeaked Yuki the Snow Monkey-Manju. "My own name, falling from the sky!"

She flopped backward into the white, fwump, and sneezed a snowflake off her nose.

The sky glowed peach over the rooftops. "One hour, then puddles," said Yuki. "I'm waking everybody."

---

From the bakery came a snore with the slow rhythm of rising dough.

She tapped the window. She tossed pebbles. She played a full drum solo with both paws.

Under his blanket, Mero Melonpan mumbled, "Five more loaves..."

Yuki climbed in. "Mero! SNOW! Your favorite weather you've never once been awake for!"

Mero's baker paws kneaded her shoulders, folded her arms, and tucked her in like a proofing croissant.

"FREE FROSTING!" Yuki yelled into his ear. "FALLING FROM THE SKY! NO LINE!"

"Save me the corner piece," murmured Mero, smiling beautifully, and snored on.

---

Momo Mochi squished awake at one knock and burst out wearing her blanket as a cape.

"Snow. Thin. Melting. **Hayaku** (hah-YAH-koo) — hurry!" panted Yuki.

"Squish together, stay together!" cried Momo, bouncing down the lane, banging shutters like a soft alarm clock.

Rin Ramen charged out of the noodle-shop, broth sloshing, absolutely thrilled.

Everywhere Rin stepped, a circle of snow vanished with a small apologetic hiss. "I'm helping!"

"You're a walking spring!" wailed Yuki. "Bench! You're under snow arrest!"

Rin stood on the bench, steaming with shame, and also just steaming.

---

The first sunbeam touched the awning. Drip. Drip. The politest possible way to say time's up.

Then an idea landed on Yuki's nose, cold and perfect. Stop bringing friends to snow.

"Bring the snow to the friends!" she bellowed. "North porch! Shady all morning! Emergency cozy maneuvers!"

---

Yuki flipped two tea trays upside down, heaped them with snow, and sledded them across the square.

Momo flopped belly-down in the deepest drift and plowed forward. Snow stuck to her sticky everything.

"I'M A SNOWBALL WITH OPINIONS!" hollered Momo, now a boulder with a face, picking up speed.

Rin stood at the porch corner facing the wall, blocking the wind. "I'm a guard radiator!"

Momo WHUMPED into the porch beside him. A snow mountain rose up. Rin saluted the wall.

"Did something happen?" asked Rin, still facing the wall.

---

Four enormous dark-green wings spread over the square, and a huge hunched robot settled in, soft as snowfall.

Its thrusters sighed warm. Every flake left in the square melted at once. Rin, giant edition.

"A BIGGER walking spring!" wailed Yuki, hopping in the puddle. "Somebody find a giant bench!"

Out stepped the pilot, Marida Cruz, cheeks blue with cold. "Apologies for the puddle," she said, bowing.

"Request permission," said Marida, "to warm my face." Yuki pressed her rosy onsen cheeks to Marida's.

"Report: cheeks warm," said Marida, saluting. The giant folded its wings and knelt, a steaming green hill.

---

The porch held the last knee-deep snow in the village. Everyone made **yukidaruma** (yoo-kee-dah-roo-mah) — snowmen — two-paw size.

Momo's had no bones and collapsed into a happy lump. "Finished," she declared. "Also perfect."

A red-crested penguin, Pen Pen, belly-flopped onto the heap and slid straight into Momo's lump. "Kwaa."

By midmorning the snow-friends began, very slowly, to drip. Plip. Plip-plip. Ploop.

"It was always going," said Yuki, leaning on Momo. "You run fast so you can sit still together."

---

Around lunchtime Mero shuffled in, blinking at wet stones and one saucer of snow-monkey. "Did I miss weather?"

He lifted the thumb-sized snow-monkey in two careful paws, eyes wide. "I shall save it forever."

"It's already melting," said Yuki, cheeks glowing. "Warm cheeks, warm heart, warm friends."

"Then I shall remember it forever," said Mero. "Keeps better. Takes up less icebox."

And the drips went plip, plip, ploop all afternoon, like the snow was nodding off.
