# Story 54: Maru Matcha and the Tea Ceremony

**Main Character:** Maru Matcha
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A serene Kyoto tea house with tatami mats and a tiny garden
**Theme:** Doing one thing slowly and well is a gift
**Japanese Spotlight:** おちゃ (ocha) = tea

---

On a quiet hill in Kyoto, tucked behind a wooden gate and a row of polite little stones, there lived a small jade-green friend named Maru Matcha. Maru had a frothy swirl of matcha-cream hair, calm half-moon eyes, and a tiny bamboo whisk she carried everywhere like a wand. This morning the whisk would not stop trembling — not because of wind, but because Maru was nervous.

"Today," Maru whispered to the whisk, "I host my very first tea ceremony. For ALL my friends."

She had invited everyone. Bouncy ones, sleepy ones, dramatic ones, the kind who tend to knock over teapots. And Maru had exactly one job: to whisk and serve one perfect bowl of **おちゃ** (ocha) = tea.

The whisk trembled harder.

"Whisk slow," Maru told herself, breathing out like fog leaving a window. "Feel calm."

Then the front bell jingled, and chaos arrived wearing sunglasses.

---

Kori Kakigori swept in first, a towering mountain of shaved ice melting one dramatic degree at a time. "Maru, darling, this place is SO peaceful I might cry, which would also melt me, so let's avoid that."

Behind Kori came Tako and Yaki, two golden batter-balls bouncing in unison. "A tea party!" said Tako. "With manners!" said Yaki. "We have NO manners!" they finished together, delighted.

Then Pock Ramune zipped through the door leaving a trail of bubbles. "Pop! Where do I sit? Pop! Can I touch everything? Pop pop?"

"No popping the teaware," said Maru gently.

"Aww. Pop," said Pock, sitting on his fizzy hands.

Last came Mero Melonpan, the sleepy domed bun, already yawning. "Is this the napping room?" he murmured, wandering toward a cushion. "It smells like green dreams in here."

Maru looked at her friends — bouncing, melting, fizzing, dozing — and felt her whisk go cold with worry. A tea ceremony was supposed to be slow. Calm. One careful thing at a time. Her friends were many fast things all at once.

But she breathed out the foggy breath again. "Welcome," she said, and bowed. "Please, sit on the **たたみ** (tatami) = woven straw mats. We will begin... slowly."

---

The tea house was a single small room with paper walls that glowed like the inside of a lantern. A low table waited. Through a round window, Maru's tiny garden held one stone, one mossy patch, and one small maple tree — and that was the whole garden, which Maru thought was exactly enough.

"Only ONE tree?" said Kori. "Sweetie, where's the rest of the forest?"

"One tree," said Maru, "that you actually look at, is better than a forest you walk past." She said it like a cozy little proverb, because that is how Maru said most things.

Kori blinked behind his sunglasses. "...Okay, that was kind of beautiful. Annoying. But beautiful."

Maru knelt at the low table and set out her tools, naming each one like an old friend. The **ちゃわん** (chawan) = tea bowl, wide and warm in her hands. The bamboo whisk. A small scoop. A jar of bright green tea powder, the color of spring grass after rain.

"Now," she said, "watch. I will do only this. Nothing else. Just this."

And the room — for one whole second — went quiet.

Then Pock Ramune raised a fizzy hand. "Can I help? Pop?"

"You may help," said Maru, "by watching. Watching IS helping."

Pock had never once thought of watching as a job. He sat very still, which for Pock lasted about four seconds, which was a personal record.

---

Maru scooped a small mound of green powder into the bowl. She poured warm water — not boiling, just warm, like a bath a snow monkey would approve of — and lifted her bamboo whisk.

Then she began to whisk.

She did not whisk fast. She did not whisk to be done. She whisked in a smooth little zigzag, *shoo-shoo-shoo*, the way you might brush a sleepy cat. Slowly, slowly, the green water grew a cap of soft foam, like the top of a tiny matcha cloud.

The friends leaned in. Even Mero Melonpan cracked one sleepy eye.

"It's like... watching snow fall," whispered Mero. "But green. And drinkable."

"Whisk slow," Maru murmured, eyes half-closed. "Feel calm."

And here is the funny thing, reader — and Maru noticed it too. As she whisked slowly, the whole room slowed down with her. Kori stopped melting so dramatically. Tako and Yaki stopped bouncing and just... swayed. Pock's bubbles drifted up lazy and round instead of zipping. It was as if Maru's slowness was a warm blanket, and one by one her busy friends crawled underneath it.

"Huh," said Tako quietly. "I feel..." "...calm," finished Yaki. "Weird." "Nice-weird."

Maru smiled into the foam.

---

But a tea ceremony, like every good story, must have one little wobble.

Just as Maru lifted the finished bowl — green and frothy and *perfect* — Pock Ramune sneezed.

It was a very fizzy sneeze. "Ah... ah... **POP-CHOO!**"

A burst of bubbles shot across the room. One landed on the maple tree. One landed on Kori's sunglasses. And one floated straight under Maru's elbow at the exact moment she was bowing to present the bowl.

The bowl tipped.

Time slowed down — but this time, not in the cozy way.

Maru watched her one perfect bowl of tea tilt toward the tatami. Her whole morning of breathing and whisking and slowness, about to spill across the mat in one green splash.

And here is where another friend might have lunged, or shrieked, or grabbed. But Maru did the Maru thing. She breathed out. She whisked her little wand once through the air — *shoo* — and instead of grabbing the bowl fast, she simply turned her two soft hands underneath it, slow and sure, like she had all the time in the world.

The bowl settled into her palms. Not a single drop spilled.

The room gasped.

"HOW," said Kori, "did you do that SLOWLY?"

"Because," said Maru, "I was not rushing. When you rush, your hands shake. When you go slow, your hands are steady." She looked at the still-perfect foam. "Slow is not the same as late. Slow is just... sure."

Pock turned a guilty shade of pale blue. "I'm sorry, Maru. Pop. I ruined the quiet part."

Maru shook her head. "You didn't ruin it. You gave us a chance to fix something calmly. That's part of the ceremony too." She winked, which Maru almost never did. "Bless you, by the way."

---

Now came the best part.

Maru turned the warm bowl carefully, three small turns, so the prettiest side faced her first guest. Tea is shared, she explained, by giving the loveliest view to the person you serve. Then she set the bowl before Mero Melonpan, who was the sleepiest, and therefore — Maru decided — needed the first sip most.

Mero blinked his happy eyes. He lifted the bowl in both crusty paws. He took one slow sip of the warm green **おちゃ**.

The room held its breath.

"Oh," said Mero softly. A little crumb-sparkle drifted off his crust. "Crackle on the outside... dreams on the inside." He smiled the smile of a bun who has tasted exactly the right thing at exactly the right moment. "It tastes like a nap feels."

Maru's whisk, which had trembled all morning, finally went still in her hand.

The bowl went around the room, one friend at a time — never two at once, always slow. Tako and Yaki shared a sip and didn't say a single word, which was a first. Kori took a sip and pushed his sunglasses up to dab his eyes. "Stay cool, sweeties," he sniffled, "but this is the warmest cool I've ever felt."

When it was Pock's turn, he held the bowl very, very carefully, like it was made of soap bubbles. He took the tiniest, slowest, most un-Pock-like sip in the history of fizz. "Pop," he whispered — just one, very gently. "That was worth watching."

---

When the last sip was sipped, the friends sat together in the lantern-light room, warm all the way through. Outside, the one maple tree rustled its leaves over the one stone in the one small garden, and it really was enough.

"Maru," said Kori, "you did one thing today. Just one. And it was the nicest thing all week."

Maru cleaned her bowl slowly, dried her whisk slowly, and bowed. "That is the secret," she said. "Anyone can do a hundred things in a hurry. But doing one thing slowly, and well, and with love — that is a gift you give. To them," she nodded at her friends, "and to yourself."

Then she added, because she was still a little bit funny in her quiet way, "Also, Pock, please sneeze outside next time."

"Pop," agreed Pock. "Deal."

And the little jade-green friend sat among her bouncing, melting, fizzing, dozing friends — all of them slow now, all of them calm now — and felt, for the first time all day, perfectly, peacefully still.

---

## Goodnight Blessing
May your hands be steady and your heart be slow, little one, the way warm tea swirls before it settles. May you learn that you do not have to do everything at once — that doing one small thing gently is its own kind of magic. Tonight, breathe out like fog leaving a window, and let the whole busy day go soft and green around the edges. You did enough. You were enough. Whisk slow, feel calm, and drift away like steam off a warm bowl. Goodnight.
