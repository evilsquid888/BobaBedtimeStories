# Story 43: Coro Korokke and the Bakery Rush

**Main Character:** Coro the Korokke
**Guest Characters:** Kori Kakigori; Miru, a small soft milk roll
**Setting:** A busy corner bakery and deli at the morning rush
**Theme:** Helping quietly is its own kind of heroism
**Japanese Spotlight:** **pan** (pahn) — bread
**Cameo:** Denji and Pochita (Chainsaw Man) — a broke, drooling teen presses his face to the window dreaming of bread with jam, his little chainsaw-nosed dog zooms down the counter slicing every loaf into perfect even slices far too fast, and Coro quietly catches them; walk-on: Hamtaro (Hamtaro)

---

Morning crept into the corner bakery, over melon buns, milk loaves and curry **pan** (pahn) — bread.

In the deli case sat Coro the Korokke, crispiest croquette on the shelf, and perfectly humble about it.

Pft. A nervous steam puff. Roughly every nine seconds.

Outside the foggy window, a line of treat-folk curled around the corner like a yawning dragon.

On the top shelf, Kori Kakigori, delivered by mistake, flung a syrupy arm across his forehead.

"It's a MELTDOWN, sweeties!" announced Kori. "I'm here to provide commentary and slowly melt. Two jobs."

Coro wanted one thing: everyone fed and happy before that dragon came through the door.

---

The big oven had overslept. Its timer went off seven times at once.

DING! DING! DING! DING! Like a brass band falling down a staircase.

Trays flew. Sausage rolls bounced into the sweet basket. Red-bean buns bounced into the savory one.

From under a tipped basket came the tiniest voice. "Help. Everyone keeps landing on me."

It was Miru, a small soft milk roll, squished flatter with every loaf.

---

Coro forgot about being small. He hopped out of the case. Thump-bump. "Coming, little pan!"

He nudged the loaves off one by one until Miru popped free. "You're a hero," said Miru.

"Me? No, no," said Coro, going pink under his breadcrumbs. Pft. "Nobody likes a flat pan."

The dragon shuffled closer to the door. Coro gulped. "Faster, then." He shoved a whole basket forward.

It tipped. Twenty curry pan bounced loose and thundered down the counter like tiny golden cattle.

---

Coro chased them, pft-pft-pft, skidded on Kori's syrup puddle, and landed upside down in the napkin holder.

Then a face squashed against the foggy window: a messy-blond teen in a black tie, drooling.

"BREAD," said Denji through the glass. "With JAM. Just once. Just ONCE in my life."

The door burst open and a small orange dog with a chainsaw nose shot down the counter. VRRRM.

Every loaf fell into perfect, even, beautiful slices. "Wan!" said Pochita, and sliced the baguette into fifty coins.

Slices flew like playing cards. Coro caught them in a basket, pft-pft-pft, forty a second.

Denji scooped up Pochita, sat down on the step outside, and pressed his nose to the glass again.

---

"Well," said Coro from under a mountain of slices. "At least it's TIDY chaos."

Then Kori wobbled off his shelf and landed in front of the door. Frosty THUMP.

The herd bumped to a stop against his cold side. "I'm not helping," said Kori. "I fell. Decoratively."

Coro climbed out of the slices and took one slow breath. The steam stopped.

He sorted one thing at a time. Sweet with sweet. Savory with savory. Slices stacked in tidy rows.

"How do you know where everything goes?" asked Miru, hurrying along behind him.

"I sit in the case and watch," said Coro. "Grumpy ones want coffee-bread. Sleepy ones want sweet."

From the door, Kori went suspiciously quiet. "That's actually beautiful, you humble little potato puff."

---

CLICK went the lock. The dragon poured in, hungry and yawning. Coro froze. Pft, pft, PFT.

Every paw found its pan at once, and the terrible rush melted into the smoothest morning ever.

---

Afterward, Miru climbed up beside Coro under the warm lamp. "You saved the morning."

"I just wanted everyone fed and happy," said Coro, going the color of a perfect croquette.

A tiny orange-and-white hamster popped out of the napkin holder, grabbed one crumb, and froze. "Heke?" said Hamtaro.

Coro slid him a whole slice. The hamster tipped over backward with joy.

Kori dabbed a syrup tear from his sunglasses. "He's PERFECT. I can't stand it. Somebody eat me."

"Loud heroes save the day with a bang," said Coro. "Quiet ones save it with a tidy shelf."

Then he dozed off under the lamp, crispy outside, soft heart inside, the smallest hero of all.
