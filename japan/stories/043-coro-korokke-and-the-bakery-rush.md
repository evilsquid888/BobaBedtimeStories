# Story 43: Coro Korokke and the Bakery Rush

**Main Character:** Coro the Korokke
**Guest Characters:** Kori Kakigori; Miru, a small soft milk roll
**Setting:** A busy corner bakery and deli at the morning rush
**Theme:** Helping quietly is its own kind of heroism
**Japanese Spotlight:** **pan** (pahn) — bread

---

Morning crept into the corner bakery, over melon buns, milk loaves and curry **pan** (pahn) — bread.

In the deli case sat Coro the Korokke, crispiest croquette on the shelf, being perfectly humble about it.

Pft. A nervous steam puff. Roughly every nine seconds.

Outside the foggy window, a line of treat-folk curled around the corner like a yawning dragon.

On the top shelf, Kori Kakigori, delivered by mistake, flung a syrupy arm across his forehead.

"It's a MELTDOWN, sweeties!" announced Kori. "The COOLEST catastrophe I have ever witnessed!"

"I'm not here to help," sniffed Kori. "I'm here to provide commentary and slowly melt. Two jobs."

Coro wanted one thing: everyone fed and happy before that dragon came through the door.

---

The big oven had overslept. Now its timer went off seven times at once.

DING! DING! DING! DING! DING! DING! DING! Like a brass band falling down a staircase.

Trays flew. Sausage rolls bounced into the sweet basket. Red-bean buns bounced into the savory one.

A tower of melon buns toppled. A baguette see-sawed and flung one sesame bun into the napkin holder.

And from under a tipped basket came the tiniest voice. "Help. Everyone keeps landing on me."

It was Miru, a small soft milk roll, squished flatter with every loaf.

---

Coro forgot all about being small. He hopped out of the case. Thump-bump. "Coming, little pan!"

He nudged the loaves off one by one, gentle as anything, until Miru popped free. Fwoomp.

"You're a hero," said Miru, fluffing back into shape.

"Me? No, no," said Coro, going pink under his breadcrumbs. Pft. "Nobody likes a flat pan."

The dragon shuffled closer to the door. Coro looked at the mess and gulped. "Faster, then."

He shoved a whole basket of curry pan toward the front. It tipped. Twenty bounced loose.

---

They bounced down the counter like a herd of tiny golden cattle, straight toward the door.

Coro chased them, pft-pft-pft, and hit a puddle of Kori's strawberry syrup.

He skidded. He flipped. He sailed past the herd and landed upside down in the napkin holder.

"Oh," said Coro, nose to nose with the sesame bun. "So THAT'S how you got in here."

Then Kori wobbled off his shelf and landed in front of the door. Frosty THUMP.

The herd bumped to a stop against his cold side. "I'm not helping," said Kori. "I fell. Decoratively."

---

Coro climbed out of the napkin holder, dusted off, and took one slow breath. The steam stopped.

He sorted one thing at a time. Sweet with sweet. Savory with savory. Hot up front.

"How do you know where everything goes?" asked Miru, hurrying along behind him.

"I sit in the case and watch," said Coro. "Grumpy ones want coffee-bread. Sleepy ones want sweet."

From the door, Kori went suspiciously quiet. "That's actually beautiful, you humble little potato puff."

---

The last loaf settled into the last basket. CLICK went the lock. DING-a-ling went the bell.

The dragon poured in, hungry and yawning. Coro froze. Pft, pft, PFT.

But every paw found its pan at once. Coffee-bread here. Sweet rolls there. Melon buns at hopping height.

The terrible rush melted into the smoothest morning the bakery ever had. Almost nobody knew why.

---

When the rush calmed, Miru climbed up beside Coro under the warm lamp. "You saved the morning."

"I just wanted everyone fed and happy," said Coro, going the color of a perfect croquette.

Kori dabbed a syrup tear from his sunglasses. "He's PERFECT. I can't stand it. Somebody eat me."

"Loud heroes save the day with a bang," said Coro. "Quiet ones save it with a tidy shelf."

Then he dozed off under the lamp, crispy outside, soft heart inside, the smallest helper of all.
