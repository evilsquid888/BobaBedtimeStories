# Story 72: Mero Melonpan and the Tanabata Wishes

**Main Character:** Mero Melonpan
**Guest Characters:** Pock Ramune; Manny the steamed bun
**Setting:** A summer street strung with bamboo and colorful Tanabata wish-papers
**Theme:** A written wish is a dream you dare to share
**Japanese Spotlight:** **tanabata** (tah-nah-bah-tah) — star festival
**Cameo:** Shinji, Asuka and Mari with Evangelion Unit-01 (Evangelion) — the purple giant crouches over the street and gathers the whole bamboo grove into one humming bouquet, shaking every wish loose, while Asuka claims the biggest bamboo, Mari hangs her wish upside down and Shinji apologizes to the bamboo; walk-on: Warawara (The Boy and the Heron)

---

Mero Melonpan was dozing on a bamboo branch. Not under it. ON it, like a crackly blanket.

"Meeero," hummed the breeze. "It's **Tanabata** (tah-nah-bah-tah) — the star festival. Everyone's coming."

"Five more minutes," murmured Mero. "There's a marshmallow staircase to a custard cloud."

The bamboo bent politely, and Mero tipped off into a puffy heap on the warm street.

All down the street, bamboo fluttered with **tanzaku** (tahn-zah-koo) — wish-strips in pink, green, blue and yellow.

"Every wish on the bamboo before the first star," yawned Mero. "That's my job. After a nap."

---

A worried voice piped up. Manny, the smallest steamed bun, held a blank green strip.

"I can't," whispered Manny, strip rattling. "What if my wish is silly? What if the stars laugh?"

"Stars don't laugh," said Mero, flopping down beanbag-style. "They twinkle. What's your big quiet dream?"

"Bread," whispered Manny. "Bread for everyone, so nobody is ever hungry."

Manny dipped the brush and drew a wobbly loaf with steam curls. He smiled at last.

---

Soon everybody wanted one. "Then we open the Melonpan Wish Workshop," announced Mero. "No laughing stars. I checked."

Pock Ramune zipped up, fizzing. "My wish is ENORMOUS, pop, and the strip is tiny!"

They taped three blue strips into a ribbon, and Pock drew a whale. A very big whale.

---

Then the evening wind WHOOSHED. Pock's ribbon swooped up like a kite and dragged Pock off his feet.

The whale-kite swung through the bamboo and swept every wish-strip off the branches like confetti.

Pink, green, blue, yellow, tumbling down the street. "MY LOAF!" wailed Manny, bouncing after it.

Pock crashed into the fountain, fizzing, and the whale-kite snagged on the tallest bamboo.

Manny caught his loaf on his head, lost it to a gust, and caught it in his mouth.

---

The street shook. A huge lanky purple-and-green giant with a horned helmet crouched over the rooftops.

It gathered every bamboo pole into one humming bouquet, and the last wish-strips snowed down.

Three pilots hopped from its palm. "The biggest wish goes on the biggest bamboo," said Asuka. "Mine. Obviously."

Mari pinned her strip upside down on the giant's thumb. "Upside-down wishes land faster," sang Mari.

Shinji bowed to the bamboo, strip still blank. "Sorry," said Shinji. "About the giant. About everything."

The giant lowered the leaning grove and crouched behind the roofs, three pilots waving from its shoulder.

"The Wish Workshop," said Mero, from under a drift of strips, "is closed for paper."

---

Pock popped his marble and fizz-rocketed up. Past the top. Past the roof. "Too much pop!"

Manny hopped on Pock's shoulders. Pock wobbled. Both tumbled onto Mero, who was somehow dozing again.

"Mero," panted Manny, "the stars can't see our wishes from the gutter."

Mero stood and stretched. Melonpan aren't famous stretchers. But Mero was so relaxed.

His body went looong like warm dough, lifting Manny to tie the loaf and whale-ribbon at the top.

The first star blinked on right above the loaf, as if it had been waiting.

---

The street gasped a happy gasp and hung every rescued wish back, higher than before.

"A wish you draw," said Mero, drifting back to bun-shape, "is a dream you dared to share."

---

By full dark the bamboo glowed, rustling dreams up toward the **ama-no-gawa** (ah-mah-no-gah-wah) — the Milky Way.

"Mero," said Manny, leaning on his warm side. "You never drew your own wish."

Mero took the last green strip and drew everyone cuddled under the bamboo, star on top.

He tied it in the middle. Every wish-strip rustled toward it, like leaves to the sun.

A drift of small white puffball spirits floated up out of the bamboo toward the Milky Way.

"Crackle on the outside," yawned Mero, "dreams on the inside. And tonight, on the outside too."

Pock fizzed one last sleepy pop. One by one, beginning with Mero, they drifted off.

---
