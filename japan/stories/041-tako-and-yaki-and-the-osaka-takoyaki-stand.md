# Story 41: Tako and Yaki and the Osaka Takoyaki Stand

**Main Character:** Tako & Yaki
**Guest Characters:** Granny Oko, an old okonomiyaki pancake; her tiny dog Mame; Roundabout, a runaway batter blob
**Setting:** A lively Dotonbori street-food stall in Osaka
**Theme:** Flipping fast is easier when someone flips with you
**Japanese Spotlight:** **takoyaki** (tah-koh-yah-kee) — octopus batter balls

---

On Dotonbori, Osaka's blinkiest street, past the giant glowing crab, sits a stall the size of a bathtub.

Behind the counter bounced two round golden friends, joined on top by a wobbly wave of bonito flakes.

"WELCOME, WELCOME!" shouted Tako.

"...to the BEST stall on the street!" finished Yaki, and bounced so hard he bonked the awning.

Tonight, for the first time, the twins ran the stall alone. They wanted one thing: a perfect tray.

"Do you think we can really do this?" whispered Yaki.

"We are literally **takoyaki** (tah-koh-yah-kee) — octopus batter balls," whispered Tako. "Who better?" Their wave wibbled with pride.

---

Mixing the batter went beautifully, if you count batter on the awning and a splat on Tako's nose.

Tako set out the pickled ginger and ate half of it. "Quality control," he said. "Extremely fresh."

Twenty-eight iron cups waited on the griddle. Flip each ball just right, it turns golden. Flip wrong: ooze.

"How hard can it be?" said Tako, grabbing two picks. Cooks have said that for a thousand years.

---

The line grew: three sleepy lanterns, a tour of giggling dumplings, and Granny Oko with her tiny dog.

Granny Oko was an old okonomiyaki pancake, wide and wise. "One tray, nice and crispy!"

Tako lunged left and flipped like a windmill. Yaki lunged right and flipped the opposite way.

The twins are joined on top, remember. Their wave stretched flat, sproinnng, and snapped them together. BONK.

"OW," said Tako.

"...my bottom," said Yaki.

Half the balls burned. Half stayed raw. One ambitious blob hopped off the griddle entirely and rolled away.

---

"Catch it!" cried Tako. The blob rolled down Dotonbori, past the noodle shops, picking up speed.

The tiny dog chased the blob. The twins chased the dog. The giggling dumplings chased the twins.

Tako went left around a lamppost. Yaki went right. Sproinnng. BONK. The blob rolled on.

It bounced off a drum. It bounced off a bicycle. It bounced straight up at the giant crab.

The crab's enormous claw swung down, slow and gleaming, and caught the blob like a baseball.

Then it gently lowered the blob into the tiny dog's waiting paws. Mame had never been prouder.

---

Back at the stall, the twins flopped down back to back, sauce on their cheeks, panting.

"We're the worst takoyaki ever to make takoyaki," said Yaki. "Too small. Too bouncy."

"Too US," said Tako sadly.

Granny Oko leaned over the counter. "You two keep fighting the griddle. You were born stuck together."

"Flip in one direction," she said. "**Issho ni** (ee-shoh nee) — together. Like a dance."

Tako looked at Yaki. Yaki looked at Tako. Their wave gave one slow, thoughtful wibble.

---

They stood. They picked up their picks. They leaned the SAME way.

"**Ichi, ni, san** (ee-chee, nee, sahn) — one, two, three. FLIP!" counted Tako.

The first ball turned over with a perfect crispy click. Golden side up. Round as a marble.

"Did you SEE that?" breathed Yaki.

Click-click-click. A whole row turned, the twins swaying together, picks dipping like two oars of one boat.

Tako flipped a ball high. Up it went, a tiny golden planet. Yaki caught it in its cup.

"Showoffs!" laughed Granny Oko.

"We prefer," said Tako, flipping two at once...

---

"...ENTERTAINERS!" finished Yaki, catching both.

Twenty-eight perfect golden balls. "One tray," said Tako, sliding it over. "Nice and crispy."

Granny Oko took one bite and closed her eyes. Mame wagged so hard he nearly took off.

The runaway blob, renamed Roundabout, got the very first crumb and was the happiest blob in Osaka.

Later, the griddle ticked as it cooled, and the twins snuggled close behind the counter.

"Roll us once," they yawned as one, "we come as two." Their wave folded down like a blanket.

---
