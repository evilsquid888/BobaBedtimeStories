# Story 41: Tako and Yaki and the Osaka Takoyaki Stand

**Main Character:** Tako & Yaki
**Guest Characters:** Granny Oko, an old okonomiyaki pancake; her tiny dog Mame; Roundabout, a runaway batter blob
**Setting:** A lively Dotonbori street-food stall in Osaka
**Theme:** Flipping fast is easier when someone flips with you
**Japanese Spotlight:** **takoyaki** (tah-koh-yah-kee) — octopus batter balls
**Cameo:** Freedom Gundam (Gundam SEED) — descends on ten fanned blue-and-white wings, blocks all of Dotonbori with one foot in the canal, and the runaway batter blob bounces off its toe straight back into the chase; walk-on: an Inkling (Splatoon)

---

On Dotonbori, Osaka's blinkiest street, past the giant glowing crab, sits a stall the size of a bathtub.

Behind the counter bounced two round golden friends, joined by a wobbly wave of bonito flakes.

"WELCOME to the BEST stall on the street!" shouted the twins, bouncing so hard they bonked the awning.

Tonight, for the first time, the twins ran the stall alone. They wanted one thing: a perfect tray.

"We are literally **takoyaki** (tah-koh-yah-kee) — octopus batter balls," said Tako. "Who better?" Their wave wibbled with pride.

---

Mixing went beautifully, if you count batter on the awning and a splat on Tako's nose.

Tako set out the pickled ginger and ate half. "Quality control," he said. "Extremely fresh."

"How hard can it be?" said Tako, grabbing two picks. Cooks have said that for a thousand years.

---

The line grew: three sleepy lanterns, giggling dumplings, and Granny Oko with her tiny dog.

Granny Oko was an old okonomiyaki pancake, wide and wise. "One tray, nice and crispy!"

Tako lunged left and flipped like a windmill. Yaki lunged right and flipped the opposite way.

The twins are joined on top, remember. Their wave stretched flat, sproinnng, and snapped them together. BONK.

"OW," said Tako.

"...my bottom," said Yaki.

Half burned. Half stayed raw. One ambitious blob hopped off the griddle and bounced away.

---

"Catch it!" cried Tako. The blob bounced down Dotonbori, past the noodle shops, picking up speed.

The tiny dog chased the blob. The twins chased the dog. The giggling dumplings chased the twins.

Tako went left around a lamppost. Yaki went right. Sproinnng. BONK. The blob bounced on.

---

Ten blue-and-white wings fanned open above the street, and a great robot came down softly.

Freedom Gundam landed, one foot in the canal and one in the street, blocking all of Dotonbori.

The blob bounced off its giant toe, BOING, and shot straight back the way it came.

Everyone turned at once and piled into Freedom's ankle like giggling dominoes. The robot looked down, puzzled.

Off a drum. Off a bicycle. The blob zipped between the robot's feet, straight up at the crab.

Freedom folded its wings one by one, like a fan closing, and knelt very politely to watch.

The crab's enormous claw swung down, slow and gleaming, and caught the blob like a baseball.

Then it lowered the blob gently into the tiny dog's paws. Mame had never been prouder.

---

"We're the worst takoyaki ever to make takoyaki," panted Yaki, flopped at the stall. "Too small. Too bouncy."

Granny Oko leaned over. "You two keep fighting the griddle. You were born stuck together."

"Flip in one direction," she said. "**Issho ni** (ee-shoh nee) — together. Like a dance."

Tako looked at Yaki. Yaki looked at Tako. Their wave gave one slow, thoughtful wibble.

---

They stood. They picked up their picks. They leaned the SAME way.

"**Ichi, ni, san** (ee-chee, nee, sahn) — one, two, three. FLIP!" counted Tako.

One ball turned with a crispy click. Click-click-click, a whole row, picks dipping like oars of one boat.

Tako flipped a ball high, a tiny golden planet. Yaki caught it in its cup.

A small orange squid popped from an ink puddle by the counter. "Woomy!" cheered the Inkling, squirting confetti.

"Showoffs!" laughed Granny Oko.

"We prefer," said Tako, flipping two at once...

---

"...ENTERTAINERS!" finished Yaki, catching both.

Twenty-eight perfect golden balls. "One tray," said Tako, sliding it over. "Nice and crispy."

Granny Oko took one bite and closed her eyes. Mame wagged so hard he nearly took off.

The runaway blob, renamed Roundabout, got the first crumb and was the happiest blob in Osaka.

Later, the griddle ticked as it cooled, and the twins snuggled close behind the counter.

"Roll us once," they yawned as one, "we come as two." Their wave folded down like a blanket.

---

## Goodnight Blessing
