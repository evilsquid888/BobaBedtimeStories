# Story 32: Momo Mochi and the Pink Petal Snow

**Main Character:** Momo Mochi (mochi rice cake)
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A hilltop park during a swirling cherry-blossom snowfall
**Theme:** Catching a little joy is better than chasing all of it
**Japanese Spotlight:** はなみ (hanami) = flower-viewing

---

On the very first morning of pink, Momo Mochi rolled out of bed so fast she bounced twice off the floor and once off the ceiling. *Boing-boing-BONK.* "Today's the day!" she squealed, her snow-white cheeks going rosy as ripe peaches.

She squished her nose against the window. The whole hilltop park had turned into a fluffy cloud of cherry blossoms — thousands and thousands of soft pink petals drifting down like the gentlest snow that ever fell. It was **はなみ** (hanami) = flower-viewing day, the cosiest holiday of spring, when everyone packs a picnic and goes to sit under the blossom trees.

"Oh, the petals," whispered Momo, and her arms stretched out long as warm taffy, *streeetch*, until her fingertips pressed right against the glass. "Pink snow! I am going to catch every single one!"

A petal landed on her dimple. Momo went cross-eyed trying to look at it and toppled over with a happy *floomp*.

This was going to be the best day ever.

The hill was already filling up with friends when Momo came bouncing up the path, her potato-starch dusting puffing off her in little clouds. There were picnic blankets in every color, baskets of snacks, and overhead — petals, petals, *petals*, swirling so thick the sky looked like the inside of a strawberry milkshake.

"Squish together, stay together!" Momo called out, which was her favorite thing to say. But today she didn't have time to squish. Today she had a Very Important Job.

"I," she announced to nobody in particular, "am going to catch ALL of them."

She held out both squishy hands like little cups. A petal floated down — *this one!* — and Momo lunged. *Splat.* She landed face-first in the grass. The petal drifted past her ear and landed three steps away, looking, Momo thought, rather smug about it.

"That's fine!" she said, springing back up. "Lots more where that came from!"

And there were. That was the trouble.

Momo dashed left for a petal — *missed.* She dashed right for another — *missed.* She did an enormous bouncy leap for one twirling extra-high, her arms stretching up and up like pink-tipped noodles, and that petal swooped sideways at the last second as if it were giggling. *Whump.* Momo bonked down onto her round bottom, bounced, and bonked down again.

A blossom landed gently on her head.

"AHA — gotcha!" she cried, and snatched at her own head, and missed, and smacked herself in the dimple. "Ow. I mean — squish. I mean — where did it GO?"

The petal had already floated off to sit on somebody's rice ball.

Now, here is a true and tested fact about cherry blossoms, which Momo was learning the hard way: the more you chase a petal, the more it wants to wander. Petals are shy show-offs. They love an audience but hate a grabber. The harder Momo grabbed, the cleverer they got at dodging — swirling up just out of reach, drifting sideways with a little *whee*, slipping right through her stretchy fingers like they were made of nothing but a kind idea and a puff of perfume.

Momo chased and chased. She zigzagged across the whole hilltop. She left a wobbly trail of starch behind her like a tiny, very determined comet. She stretched so far reaching for one high-up petal that her arm wound twice around a tree trunk, and she had to spin herself loose — *fwip-fwip-fwip* — and sat down dizzy with the world going pink and round and pink and round.

By lunchtime she had run after approximately nine hundred petals.

She had caught exactly zero.

Momo flopped down on the grass with a soft, defeated *poomf.* Her arms, all stretched out and tired, lay around her in two long loops like a deflated hug. Her cheeks had gone from rosy-excited to pink-pooped. A petal drifted down and landed right on the tip of her nose, and she was too worn out to even reach for it.

"I just," she huffed, "wanted to catch the magic. There's so MUCH of it. I didn't want to miss any."

And she lay there, soft and squished and a little bit sad — which, if you have ever chased something joyful so hard you forgot to enjoy it, you will understand completely.

That was when Momo noticed the sky.

She hadn't really *looked* at it all morning, you see. She'd been too busy looking at petals — at this one, that one, the next one, the one getting away. But now, flat on her back with her arms in their tired loops, there was nothing left to do but look up.

And oh.

Oh, the sky was *full* of pink.

From down here, lying still, the blossoms weren't nine hundred separate petals to be caught one at a time. They were one enormous, swirling, drifting, swooping river of soft pink snow, tumbling slowly across a pale blue sky, turning and twirling and floating down in no hurry at all. It was so big and so gentle and so beautiful that Momo's breath went out of her in a tiny "...oh" and didn't bother coming back for a moment.

A petal landed on her cheek. She let it.

Another landed on her tummy. She let that one, too.

One settled into the little dimple where her sweet filling hid, soft as a secret, and Momo giggled — a real giggle, bubbly and surprised — because it tickled.

"Huh," she said softly. "When I stop chasing... they come to ME."

She lay very, very still. And the petals, who had spent all morning dodging her grabby stretchy fingers, now drifted down one by one and made themselves at home. One on her forehead. Two on her round shoulder. A whole little pile gathering in the cup of her open, resting hand — the very hand that had caught nothing all day, now that she'd stopped trying to catch anything at all.

"Momo? You okay down there?" came a voice. Several of her friends had wandered over, drawn by the sight of a mochi lying spread out in the grass like a starfish made of marshmallow.

"Come lie down," Momo said dreamily, not even sitting up. "Just — come lie down and look up. You won't believe it."

They did. One by one, her friends flopped onto the grass beside her, all in a cozy row, blinking up at the slow pink river in the sky. Nobody chased anything. Nobody grabbed. They just lay there, a little patchwork of soft and squishy friends, letting the **はなみ** (hanami) happen *to* them instead of running after it.

"I was doing it all wrong," Momo admitted, and a petal chose that exact moment to land on her open mouth, so she said the rest around it: "I fwas twying to catfh *aw* of it." She blew the petal off with a *pfft* and it spiraled up, delighted. "But you can't hold a whole sky in two little hands. You're not *supposed* to. You're supposed to lie back and let some of it land on you."

Someone beside her sighed the happiest sigh. The grass was warm. The breeze was kind. The petals kept coming, soft as falling sleep, and not one of them needed catching, because catching had never been the point.

"This," Momo declared, "is the BEST part of the whole day. And I almost missed it running around."

That made everyone laugh — the good, soft kind of laugh that makes your whole tummy jiggle.

They stayed like that a long, long time, all afternoon, while the pink snow fell and fell. They shared snacks passed hand to squishy hand. They named the silliest-shaped petals (one looked exactly like a tiny pig, and they argued cheerfully about it for ten whole minutes). And Momo, who had wanted so badly to catch *everything*, found she didn't mind at all that most of the petals drifted right past her.

Because a few — just a few — had landed.

And a few, lying in your open hand under a whole sky of pink, with all your favorite friends squished beside you, turned out to be exactly, perfectly, more-than-enough.

When the sun went golden and low, Momo sat up at last. The petals in her hand spilled into the grass, and she didn't chase a single one. She looked at her friends, all rosy-cheeked and sleepy and dusted in pink, and her whole peachy face crinkled into the warmest smile.

"Squish together," she said, holding out her stretchy arms, "stay together."

And they did — one enormous, gentle group squish, right there on the hilltop, with the last of the pink petal snow settling softly over the whole happy pile of them like a blanket tucking them in.

## Goodnight Blessing

Little one, the day is full of so much you might want to catch — but you don't have to grab every bit of joy with both hands. Lie back, like soft Momo on the warm spring grass, and let the good things drift down and land where they like. A few petals in your open hand are more than enough to make a heart full. Close your sleepy eyes now; somewhere a pink sky is still gently snowing, and a little mochi is dreaming sweet beside her friends. Let the quiet land softly on you, too. Goodnight.
