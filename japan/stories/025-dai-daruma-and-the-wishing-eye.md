# Story 25: Dai Daruma and the Wishing Eye

**Main Character:** Dai Daruma
**Guest Characters:** Fuwa, a shy little steamed bun; a fat shrine pigeon
**Setting:** A shrine stall selling lucky daruma dolls, at dusk
**Theme:** A wish is a promise you make to yourself
**Japanese Spotlight:** **negai** (neh-gah-ee) — wish

---

On the tippy-top step of the old stone shrine, the lanterns glowed like sleepy fireflies.

There sat a small crimson dome of a fellow who absolutely would NOT stay still.

"Fall down seven, bounce up eight!" sang Dai Daruma, and tipped waaay over to the left.

Wobble. Wobbobble. BOING. Upright again, grinning under his enormous determined eyebrows.

A fat shrine pigeon applauded. Well, it flapped. Same thing.

Dai ran the lucky-doll stall, where shelves of round red cousins waited, blank-eyed, full of **negai** (neh-gah-ee) — wishes.

---

Then came the smallest, sniffliest sound in the whole world. It came from behind the brush bucket.

Dai bounced over and found a wisp of a steamed bun, pale and fluffy, hiding very hard.

"I'm Fuwa," whispered the bun. "I need a daruma for a wish. I'm too scared to pick."

"Scared of a WISH?" boomed Dai, so loudly he tipped over and had to bounce back up.

"What if I pick wrong?" said Fuwa. "What if the eye's crooked and the luck leaks out?"

"I've been rained on," said Dai. "I'm watertight. I'm basically a tiny round umbrella. Come on."

---

Up and down the shelves they went, Fuwa on tiptoe, Dai wobbling along like a friendly bowling ball.

"This one's very shiny," said Dai. "Good for wishing to become a mirror. Or a polite doorknob."

Fuwa hid behind Dai from a daruma with tremendous eyebrows, which is hard, because Dai is round.

Every daruma was too shiny, too fancy, or too proud. Fuwa's fluffy curl drooped like a candle.

---

Then the evening wind came up the mountain and gave the stall one enormous WHOOSH.

The top shelf tipped. Forty red darumas went over the edge like a spilled bag of apples.

They bounced down the stone steps, boing-boing-boing, a crimson avalanche heading for town.

"MY COUSINS!" cried Dai, and launched himself after them, eyebrows first.

Fuwa stood frozen at the top. Chasing things was not a thing she did.

---

Dai caught one daruma on his head, one on his belly, and one with his eyebrows.

He tripped over the pigeon. The pigeon tripped over him. The darumas kept bouncing.

The giant daruma plopped into the wishing pond and floated there, enormously, like a proud red boat.

"Help!" called Dai from under a wobbling pile of cousins. "Fuwa! Anybody! Pigeon!"

At the top of the steps, Fuwa took one breath. Then two. Then she jumped.

She bounded down the steps, fluffy and fast, scooping darumas into her curl like a basket.

---

At the very bottom, one last daruma lay tipped on its side in the moss.

It was small, smudgy, and a tiny bit crooked. Fuwa nudged it gently.

It wobbled. It teetered right at the edge of falling. And it bounced straight back up.

"This one," breathed Fuwa. "This one wobbles like me."

"A daruma isn't lucky because it never falls," said Dai. "It's lucky because it gets back up."

---

Under the biggest lantern sat the painting table, a dish of black ink, and the softest brush.

"Make your negai and paint ONE eye," said Dai. "The other stays blank until it comes true."

"A daruma doesn't grant wishes," said Dai. "He's a promise-keeper. You promise yourself you'll keep trying."

Fuwa dipped the brush. Her paw shook. Her tongue poked out. She painted the eye.

It was lopsided. It was smudgy. It was the most beautiful eye Dai had ever seen.

"My wish," said Fuwa, "is to be brave. Like a bun who chases darumas down a mountain."

Dai bounced so high with joy he landed in the ink dish. Three new freckles.

---

Fuwa hugged her crooked daruma and hopped down the shrine steps into the soft purple evening.

Dai winked at the rescued cousins with his one good eye. "Everybody's wish is coming. Wobbles and all."

---
