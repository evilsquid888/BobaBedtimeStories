# Story 25: Dai Daruma and the Wishing Eye

**Main Character:** Dai Daruma
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A shrine stall selling lucky daruma dolls
**Theme:** A wish is a promise you make to yourself
**Japanese Spotlight:** ねがい (negai) = wish

---

On the very tippy-top step of the old stone shrine, where the lanterns glowed like sleepy fireflies and the breeze smelled of toasted rice, there sat a small crimson dome of a fellow who absolutely would NOT stay still.

"Fall down seven, bounce up eight!" sang Dai Daruma, and to prove it he leaned waaaay over to the left — *wobble* — paused dramatically at the very edge of tipping — *wobbobble* — and then *boinged* upright again, grinning under his enormous determined eyebrows.

A passing pigeon applauded. Well, it flapped. Same thing.

Dai Daruma worked at the shrine's lucky-doll stall, which was a wonderful job for a daruma, because Dai *was* a lucky doll — a roly-poly bun the color of a sunset, with one big painted eye open and one cheek left blank and patient, still waiting for a wish. The shelves around him were lined with dozens of round red cousins, all blank-eyed, all bursting with possible **ねがい** (negai) = wishes.

That was when he heard the smallest, sniffliest sound in the whole world.

---

It came from behind the bucket of paintbrushes. Dai wobbled over — *boing, boing* — and found a little visitor curled up tight, trying very hard not to be seen.

It was a wisp of a friend, soft and pale, a steamed bun with rosy cheeks and a fluffy curl on top. Not one of his cousins. A guest. And clearly, a worried one.

"Well now," said Dai, in his peppiest coach-voice. "What's a fluffy little dumpling doing hiding behind the brushes? The brushes are for *painting*, not for *hiding*! Though I suppose you could hide behind them while painting, if you were very quick about it."

The little bun sniffled. "I came to buy a daruma," she whispered. "For a **ねがい**. But — but I'm too scared to pick one."

"Scared!" boomed Dai, so loudly he tipped over and had to bounce back up. "Of a *wish*? Why, a wish is the bravest, fluffiest, most exciting thing there is!"

"That's exactly the problem," she said miserably. "What if I pick the wrong daruma? What if I make the wrong wish? What if I paint the eye crooked and the luck leaks out the side?"

Dai blinked his one painted eye. He had to admit, he had never once worried about luck leaking out the side. But he could tell this was a Very Serious Worry to her, and Dai Daruma never laughed at a Very Serious Worry. (He saved his laughing for himself tipping over, which honestly happened plenty.)

"Come here," he said gently. "Let's pick a daruma together. And I promise — no luck will leak anywhere. I'm watertight. I've been rained on. I'm basically a tiny round umbrella."

The little bun giggled, just a tiny bit. And a giggle, Dai knew, was the first crack in the shell of a worry.

---

They went up and down the shelves together, the bun on tiptoe and Dai wobbling along beside her like a friendly red bowling ball.

"This one's very shiny," said Dai, nudging a glossy daruma. "Good for wishes about, oh, becoming a mirror. Or a very polite doorknob."

"*Dai.*"

"This one has the biggest eyebrows. Tremendous eyebrows. Wishes feel very *seen* near this one."

"They're staring at me," the bun whispered.

"That's the eyebrows doing their job," Dai agreed solemnly.

They looked at round ones and rounder ones. They looked at a daruma with gold paint so bright it practically shouted *LOOK AT ME*, and a daruma with tiny painted plum-blossoms, and one so enormous Dai had to bounce three times just to see over the top of him.

"What about him?" the bun tried, pointing at the giant. "He's so big. A big daruma must hold a big wish, right?"

"Could be," Dai said, squinting up. "Though I find the *enormous* ones a bit show-offy, if you ask me. All that size and they still can't bounce any better than I can. It's not the *bigness* of the daruma. It's the *bounce*." He demonstrated, naturally, and toppled into a basket of lucky charms with a cheerful *thunk*, then bounced out wearing one on his head like a tiny hat. "See? Style."

But the little bun kept shaking her head. None of them felt *right*. The gold one was too loud. The plum-blossom one too fancy. The giant one too proud. And slowly the worry crept back into her shoulders, and her steam-puff curl began to droop like a candle in the warm.

"Maybe I should just go home," she mumbled. "Maybe I'm not a wishing sort of bun."

Now, if there is one thing in this whole world that lights a fire under a daruma's round little bottom, it is somebody saying *maybe I should just give up.* Dai felt his eyebrows go positively electric.

"Stop right there!" he cried, bouncing up so hard he did a full somersault and landed perfectly upright, which had honestly never worked before and surprised him quite a lot. "You don't pick the *fanciest* daruma. You don't pick the *shiniest* daruma. You pick the one that wobbles like *you*."

The little bun stared. "Like... me?"

"Watch," said Dai. He leaned over slowly — *wobble* — teetered — *wobbobble* — and bounced up. "See that? Every daruma falls. Every single one. We're built round on the bottom on *purpose*, so that when we tip over — and we always tip over — we come right back up. A daruma isn't lucky because it never falls. It's lucky because it *gets back up.*"

He let that sit a moment under the lantern light.

"So don't pick a daruma that looks perfect. Pick the one that looks like it would bounce back up with *you*."

---

The little bun went very quiet. Then she walked — slowly, then less slowly — past all the shiny ones and the gold ones and the tremendous-eyebrow one, all the way to the dusty back corner of the bottom shelf.

There, leaning a tiny bit crooked, sat a small daruma. He was not the shiniest. His paint had a smudge on one side. But his blank face looked patient and kind, and when the bun reached out and gave him the gentlest nudge — he *wobbled*, teetered at the edge, and bounced right back up with a cheerful little tilt.

"*This* one," she breathed. "This one wobbles like me."

"Now THAT," declared Dai, "is a wish waiting to happen. Bring him to the painting table!"

The painting table sat beneath the biggest lantern of all, warm and golden, with a little dish of black ink and the softest brush. Dai explained how it worked, hopping with excitement at every word.

"Here is the secret of the daruma," he said. "When you get him, both his eyes are blank. You make your **ねがい** — your wish — and you paint *one* eye. Just one! That open eye watches over your wish every single day. And the other eye stays blank..."

"Until?" asked the bun, brush trembling in her hands.

"Until your wish comes *true*. THEN you paint the second eye, and he can finally see the whole wonderful thing he helped you do." Dai's painted eye sparkled. "Imagine it! He's been sitting on your shelf for weeks, maybe months, watching you with that one patient eye, cheering you on without a single word. And then one day — *plink!* — you give him his second eye, and for the very first time he sees you. Both eyes wide open. And do you know the first thing he sees?"

"What?" the bun whispered.

"*You,*" said Dai warmly. "Having done the brave thing. That's the best view in the whole world, and he waited the whole time just for it." He wobbled with feeling. "But here's the part most folks forget. The daruma doesn't make the wish come true for you."

The bun's face fell. "He doesn't?"

"Nope!" said Dai cheerfully. "A daruma is a *promise-keeper*, not a wish-granter. When you paint that first eye, you're not asking for magic. You're making a promise to yourself — a promise that you'll *try*, even on the wobbly days. The daruma just sits on your shelf, one-eyed and patient, reminding you: *you said you'd try.* And every time you wobble, you look at him, and you bounce back up. That's the real **ねがい**." He puffed up proudly. "A wish is a promise you make to yourself."

---

The little bun looked at her crooked, smudgy, perfect daruma for a long, long moment.

"My wish," she said softly, "is to stop being so scared of doing things wrong. To be braver. Like... like a bun who bounces back up."

"Oh, that is a *spectacular* wish," whispered Dai, and a happy little tear shone in his one painted eye. "Now. Take a breath. Whisk slow — er — that's not my line, that's Maru's — POINT IS, breathe, and paint that eye. It doesn't have to be perfect. It just has to be *brave*."

The bun dipped the brush. Her paw shook. She breathed in the warm rice-and-lantern air. And then — slowly, carefully, with her tongue poking out in concentration — she painted the daruma's first eye.

It was a little lopsided. A tiny bit wobbly. The ink smudged just the smallest smudge.

It was the most beautiful eye Dai Daruma had ever seen.

"There," the bun whispered. The daruma seemed to gaze back at her, one eye open, watching, waiting, *promising*.

"How does it feel?" Dai asked.

The little bun thought about it. She had been so scared the eye would be crooked. And it was! And the luck had not leaked out the side, not one drop. The shrine had not fallen over. The pigeons had not laughed. The wish was just... hers now, lopsided and brave and real.

"It feels," she said slowly, "like I already started keeping the promise."

Dai bounced so high with joy that he knocked over the brush bucket, tipped into the ink dish, rolled across the table, and came to rest upside-down in a pile of paper lanterns — and then, because he was a daruma, he wobbled, teetered, and bounced right back up, splattered with three new freckles of ink and grinning wider than ever.

"See?" he laughed. "Fall down seven, bounce up eight! Now go home, brave little bun. Put that daruma somewhere you'll see him every day. And whenever you wobble — remember. You promised."

The little bun hugged her crooked daruma tight to her fluffy chest, hugged Dai too (which is harder than it sounds, since he's quite round), and bounced down the shrine steps into the soft purple evening, lighter than she'd arrived.

Dai watched her go, glowing warm under the lanterns. Then he turned to his blank-cheeked cousins on the shelves, all those waiting wishes, and gave them a wink with his one good eye.

"Don't worry, you lot," he said. "Everyone's wish is coming. Wobbles and all."

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, may your wishes be like the daruma's eye — brave even when they're a tiny bit crooked. When tomorrow gets wobbly, remember you are round on the bottom on purpose, built to bounce right back up. Make your promise softly to yourself as you close your eyes, and let it keep watch over you all night long. You don't have to be perfect to be lucky. You only have to keep trying, one gentle bounce at a time. Goodnight.
