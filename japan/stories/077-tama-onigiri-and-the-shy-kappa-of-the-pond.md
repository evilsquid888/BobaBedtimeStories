# Story 77: Tama Onigiri and the Shy Kappa of the Pond

**Main Character:** Tama Onigiri
**Guest Characters:** Kappy, a shy little kappa; a frog on a lily pad
**Setting:** A lily-padded village pond home to a gentle little kappa
**Theme:** A small gift can melt the biggest shyness
**Japanese Spotlight:** **kappa** (kah-pah) — friendly water-sprite
**Cameo:** Squirtle (Pokémon) — surfaces with a splash, hides in its shell at the exact moment Kappy does, then water-guns Tama's snack stack; walk-on: Shippo (Inuyasha)

---

Tama Onigiri had packed entirely too many snacks. This was not unusual.

Rice balls teetered on her shoulders. Big ones. Little ones. One shaped like a duck.

"I packed extra!" she announced to the pond. "Now I just need somebody to share with."

A frog on a lily pad blinked. "Oh good, an audience," said Tama, and took a bow.

The bow was a mistake. The duck rice ball slid off, bop, bop, bop, into the reeds.

Then came a splash. A very small, very shy splash. Tama's plum cheek went pink with hope.

---

Gold afternoon light lay on the pond, lily pads like green dinner plates.

Tama tiptoed to the edge and peered in. Two enormous round eyes peered back.

A teapot-sized green creature with a little shell and a water-filled dish on its head.

"A **kappa** (kah-pah) — friendly water-sprite!" whispered Tama, delighted.

The kappa gasped and ducked under, leaving one ring of ripples and one guilty bubble.

"I'm only a rice ball," Tama called softly. "My scariest trait is humming off-key."

---

Tama set her smallest rice ball on a flat stone and scooted back. Bloop. Eyes.

A webbed paw crept toward the rice ball, slow as cold syrup.

Then the pond went SPLOOSH. A wave soaked Tama from nori to toes.

---

A small blue turtle with a brown shell and a curly tail landed on the stone. "Squirtle!"

The kappa saw the turtle. The turtle saw the kappa. Clonk. Clonk. Two heads vanished into two shells.

Two shy shells sat side by side on the stone, with one rice ball between them.

"Hello, shells," said Tama. "I have snacks." Squirtle peeked. The kappa peeked. Clonk. Clonk.

Squirtle popped out in round black sunglasses, nodded bravely, and squirted a fountain straight at Tama.

The fountain blasted every rice ball off her shoulders. Squirtle sank backward until only its sunglasses showed.

---

"Well," said Tama, dripping, "that is one way to make a splash."

The reeds rustled. A tiny fox-kit with a bushy tail popped up, hugging the duck rice ball.

"Caught it with my bare paws! Fox skill!" squeaked Shippo, and sat on a lily pad to munch.

The kappa's shell wobbled, and a small damp voice said, "I'm Kappy. I don't talk to many."

"I talk to plenty," said Tama warmly. "We balance out." She held out her last rice ball.

Kappy nibbled. "Salty plum in the middle," he whispered, and his eyes went round as rice bowls.

---

Kappy dove and came back holding one dewy **kyuuri** (kyoo-ree) — cucumber. "The best one. From my garden."

Then the cucumber slipped. It rolled down the muddy bank, bump, bump, toward the reeds.

Kappy lunged, tumbled head-over-shell, and landed upside down. His head-dish tipped. Glug.

The water ran out. Kappy went wilted and floppy as overnight noodles.

"A kappa's pep lives in that dish!" cried Tama. "Hold on! I'll get water!"

---

She scooped pond water in her nori sash. It drained out before she took two steps.

She tried a lily pad as a bowl. It folded. The frog underneath glared.

Then Tama remembered. Her bundle. "I packed extra! I ALWAYS pack extra!"

Inside sat a folded leaf brimming with water, packed that morning, just in case.

Carefully, the most careful she had ever been, she poured it into Kappy's dish.

---

Kappy popped upright like a cork, cheeks bright, pep fizzing back.

"You carried water for me," breathed Kappy. "Just in case."

"That's the whole point of just in case," said Tama. "You never know which case."

The frog paddled over, nosing the runaway cucumber ahead of it. Crunch. Half each.

Later they sat shoulder-to-shell on the stone while the pond went gold and still.

Kappy set a tiny cucumber on the stone for Tama, scooted back, and admired a cloud.

"A small gift and a patient cloud," said Tama. "Melts the biggest shyness every time."
