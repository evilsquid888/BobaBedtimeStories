# Story 77: Tama Onigiri and the Shy Kappa of the Pond

**Main Character:** Tama Onigiri
**Guest Characters:** Kappy, a shy little kappa; a frog on a lily pad
**Setting:** A lily-padded village pond home to a gentle little kappa
**Theme:** A small gift can melt the biggest shyness
**Japanese Spotlight:** **kappa** (kah-pah) — friendly water-sprite

---

Tama Onigiri had packed entirely too many snacks. This was not unusual.

Rice balls were stacked on her round shoulders. Big ones. Little ones. One shaped like a duck.

"I packed extra!" she announced to nobody. "Now I just need somebody to share it with."

A frog blinked at her from a lily pad. "Oh good, an audience," said Tama.

She tripped over a root. Three rice balls bounced into the reeds, bop, bop, bop.

That was when she heard the splash. A very small, very shy splash.

---

The pond glowed gold in the late afternoon, lily pads floating like green dinner plates.

Tama crept to the edge and peered in. Two enormous round eyes peered back.

A small green creature, teapot-sized, with a little shell and a water-filled dish on its head.

It gasped and ducked under, leaving one ring of ripples and one extremely guilty bubble.

"A **kappa** (kah-pah) — friendly water-sprite!" whispered Tama, delighted.

"It's all right," she called softly. "I'm only a rice ball. My scariest trait is humming off-key."

Nothing. So Tama set her smallest rice ball on a flat stone, scooted back, and admired a cloud.

---

"That one's for you," she told the cloud. "No rush."

Bloop. The kappa surfaced, closer. It eyed the rice ball. It eyed Tama. Rice ball again.

A small webbed paw snatched the rice ball and vanished.

From under the water came a tiny, muffled, delighted sound.

"Salty plum in the middle," said Tama to the cloud. "My specialty."

The kappa rose up to its shell, head-dish wobbling. "I'm Kappy. I don't talk to many."

"I talk to plenty," said Tama warmly. "We balance out."

---

Kappy dove, and came back holding one dewy **kyuuri** (kyoo-ree) — cucumber. "The best one. From my garden."

Tama gasped. A kappa loves a cucumber with his whole heart. This was an enormous gift.

Then the cucumber slipped. It rolled down the muddy bank, bump, bump, toward the reeds.

Kappy lunged. He tumbled head-over-shell and landed upside down, his head-dish tipping out.

Glug. The water ran out. Kappy went wilted and floppy as overnight noodles.

"Hold on!" cried Tama. "A kappa's pep lives in that dish! I'll get water!"

---

She scooped pond water in her nori sash. It drained out before she took two steps.

She tried the duck-shaped rice ball as a cup. It floated away, the frog riding it.

"Quack," said the frog, which was wrong, but the duck appreciated it.

Then Tama remembered. Her bundle. "I packed extra! I ALWAYS pack extra!"

Inside sat a folded leaf, brimming with water, packed that morning just in case.

---

Carefully, the most careful she had ever been, she poured it into Kappy's dish.

Kappy popped upright like a cork, cheeks bright, strength fizzing back into his arms.

"You carried water," breathed Kappy. "For me. Just in case."

"That's the whole point of just in case," said Tama. "You never know which case."

The frog paddled back on the duck, nosing the runaway cucumber ahead of it.

They split the cucumber on the stone, half each, crunching together in the gold light.

"Crunch together, stay together," said Tama, and the kappa laughed like a reed flute.

Kappy hugged her so hard one grain bounced into the pond. The frog ate it, very pleased.

---

Later they sat shoulder-to-shell on the flat stone while the pond went still.

Kappy set a tiny cucumber on the stone for Tama, scooted back, and admired his own cloud.

Then he waved at the frog. The frog waved back, like it was the most ordinary thing.

"A small gift and a polite cloud," said Tama sleepily. "Melts the biggest shyness every time."

That is why she always packs a little extra.

---
