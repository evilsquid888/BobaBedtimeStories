# Story 51: Tako and Yaki and the Osaka Castle Adventure

**Main Character:** Tako & Yaki (takoyaki twins)
**Guest Characters:** Miss Yunomi, a tidy teacup tour guide; Chizu, an old folding map
**Setting:** The grand grounds of Osaka Castle with its golden details
**Theme:** Curiosity is twice as fun with a partner
**Japanese Spotlight:** **shiro** (shee-roh) — castle

---

Osaka Castle rose above the trees like a stack of green-roofed pancakes, each smaller than the last.

Through the stone gate bounced two round golden batter-balls, joined on top by one wobbly bonito-flake wave.

"**Shiro** (shee-roh) — castle!" they cheered together, and high-fived so hard they bonked foreheads.

"Tako, LOOK," said Yaki, pointing up with his whole body, which tipped him over.

At the tippy-top of the tower, something flashed. Gold. Fishy. A tail gave a flick.

"A golden fish on a roof," said Tako. "We are going to see it up close."

Their guide, a tidy little teacup named Miss Yunomi, waved a small plain flag.

"We walk slowly," said Miss Yunomi. "We listen carefully. We touch NOTHING golden."

---

To two giggly takoyaki, that last part sounded exactly like a dare.

Past the **ishigaki** (ee-shee-gah-kee) — stone wall they went, every rock as big as an elephant.

"A rock elephant," whispered Tako, and patted one very respectfully.

"Good morning, rock elephant," said Yaki, bowing until his bonito flakes brushed the moss.

Miss Yunomi talked about a great lord and the year fifteen-hundred-something.

The twins drifted, the way a curious smell drifts toward a kitchen. Then gone.

---

At the tower's foot leaned Chizu, an old paper map, creased, friendly, drawn in little pictures.

"Shachihoko," said Chizu, nodding at the roof. "Golden fish with tiger faces. Always two. One east, one west."

Tako looked at Yaki. Yaki looked at Tako. The same wonderful thought landed in both at once.

"Roll us once, we come as two!" they sang, and Chizu chuckled dust off his corners.

"Follow the picture-clues," said Chizu. "Lantern, plum tree, well. They lead to the best view."

---

On a stone lantern was carved a tiny tower with stripes across it. The twins frowned.

"That makes no sense," said Tako.

"It makes PERFECT sense," said Yaki. "You just have to look at it wobbly."

They tilted sideways until their sauce ran uphill. "Count the layers!" shouted Yaki.

They bounced backward to count the tower. "One, two, eleventy, a bunch," said Tako. "Five!"

---

The second clue hung high in a plum tree, a little wooden fish on a string.

Tako bounced onto Yaki's head. Yaki bounced onto Tako's head. Neither way reached.

So they bounced together, higher and higher, until the whole tree shook like a sneeze.

Down came the blossoms. Down came the fish. Down came two pink-dusted takoyaki, giggling.

"We look like sakura mochi," said Yaki, and sneezed out a petal.

---

The last clue sat on the rim of a well so old its stones were smooth as candy.

Tako leaned to look. Then leaned more. Then leaned entirely too much.

PLOP went Tako, straight into the dark, and the bonito wave snapped tight like a rope.

"YAKI!" came a small echo from the deep. "It's dark down here! And lonely! And DARK!"

Yaki dug in, puffed up, and heaved. The wave stretched. The wave held.

Up popped Tako, dripping, into a hug. "Curious alone is scary," he said. "Curious together is a game."

---

Up the long stone steps they puffed, arriving just as the afternoon melted into gold.

At the peak curled the two shachihoko, gleaming like they'd been dipped in sunshine.

"Which one's shiniest?" whispered Yaki. The east fish blazed. The west fish blazed right back.

"Neither," said Tako. "Together they're twice as shiny. Like us."

Miss Yunomi puffed up the steps. "I lost two batter-balls," she wheezed. "A teacup's worst day."

"Ishigaki! Shachihoko! Rock elephants!" babbled Yaki, hopping. "We learned the WHOLE lesson. Twice as fast!"

---

The sun slipped lower and the whole shiro glowed warm and rosy, like a castle made of honey.

The twins leaned together, shoulder to round little shoulder, bonito wave finally still.

"Best treasure?" yawned Tako.

"Knowing stuff," said Yaki. "And you." High-five. Of course.

---
