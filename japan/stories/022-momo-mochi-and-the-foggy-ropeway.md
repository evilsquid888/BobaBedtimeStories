# Story 22: Momo Mochi and the Foggy Ropeway

**Main Character:** Momo Mochi
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A mountain cable-car ropeway gliding above a sea of clouds
**Theme:** Holding hands turns fear into wonder
**Japanese Spotlight:** **kumo** (koo-moh) — cloud

---

Momo Mochi had never ridden a ropeway before, and the moment she squished into the swaying little cable car, she let out the biggest, happiest "WHEEE-squish!" you ever heard.

The car wobbled. Momo bounced. Her potato-starch dust puffed up in a tiny snow-cloud and made her sneeze, which made her bounce again, which made her giggle, which — well. You get the idea. A mochi in a swinging box is a recipe for a lot of bouncing.

"Friends!" she called, stretching one warm taffy arm to pat the window. "We're going up the mountain in a teeny floating house! Squish together, stay together!"

Below her, the whole valley spread out like a picnic blanket — green tea fields, red rooftops, a winding silver river. The little car climbed and climbed, humming along its wire toward the very top, where the air was cool and sweet and smelled faintly of pine.

It was, Momo decided, the *coziest* adventure of her squishy little life.

She had packed a tiny bag for the trip, the way you do for a Very Important Outing. Inside was: one extra scarf (in case the mountain was chilly), a small round mirror (for checking that her dimple was dimpling correctly), and four soft red-bean candies (for emergencies, and also for not-emergencies, and also for in-betweens). She hummed a little tune and watched a hawk drift by on lazy wings, far below the window now, getting smaller and smaller as the car climbed higher and higher into the cool blue.

"Up and up and up we go," she sang. "Toward the tippity-top of the world!"

And then the mountain ate it.

Not really, of course — mountains don't eat things (that's Momo's job, and she's *very* good at it). But a great soft wall of white came rolling down the slope, swallowing the trees, the river, the rooftops, all of it. One moment the world was wide and bright. The next, the window showed only **kumo** (koo-moh) — cloud, thick as whipped cream pressed against the glass.

The cable car stopped with a gentle *clunk*.

It hung there in the middle of the white nothing, swaying just a little, the wire above disappearing into fog in both directions.

For one long second, nobody said anything at all.

Then a small voice piped up from the bench across from Momo. It belonged to a tiny dumpling on a stick — well, three tiny dumplings, riding up to see the autumn leaves. The white one, Shiro, had gone even whiter than usual.

"Wh-where did the mountain *go*?" Shiro squeaked. "Where did *everything* go? Is it gone forever? Are *we* gone forever? I think we're gone forever."

"We are not gone forever," said the green one, Cha, calmly. "We've simply stopped because of the fog. Probably the wind. The machine waits for the wind to settle."

"Stopped!" wailed the pink one, Pinku, who was bold about everything except dangling in a cloud. "We're STOPPED! In the SKY! In a little SWINGING BOX!"

And that, my dear, was when the whole car began to worry.

A taiyaki near the door pressed his fins to the window and couldn't see the ground. A soft-serve in the corner started to wobble her swirl. Even the kei-truck — for there was a tiny butter-yellow kei-truck riding up too, parked snugly on the little luggage shelf — let out a nervous *putt... putt...* and went quiet.

Worry, you should know, is a sneaky thing. It spreads from one friend to the next like a yawn, or like a giggle, or like the smell of something delicious. In about four seconds, the whole little car was full of round, frightened faces and one trembling soft-serve swirl.

Momo Mochi felt it too. Of course she did. Her tummy did a small flip, and her rosy peach cheeks went the tiniest bit pale. Hanging in a sea of white, with no up and no down and no ground — it was a great deal of nothing, and nothing can be every bit as scary as something.

But Momo Mochi had a secret. The secret was this: when she got scared, she got *squishier*. And a squishy mochi is a mochi that can do something rather wonderful.

She reached out one soft arm — stretching, stretching, the way warm taffy does — and she gently took Shiro's tiny hand.

"Hello," she said, warm as a hug. "I've got you. Squeeze my hand if you're scared, okay? Just give it a little squeeze."

Shiro squeezed. Hard.

"Ooh!" said Momo, and giggled. "That's a *good* squeeze. You're very strong for a dumpling. Now — squeeze your sister's hand the same way. Pass it along."

Momo dug into her tiny bag, too. "And here," she said, popping a soft red-bean candy gently toward Shiro, "is a brave-button. Whenever you feel scared, you press the brave-button — by which I mean you have a little nibble — and it reminds your tummy that good things are still happening."

Shiro nibbled. His shiny black eyes went round. "It's... sweet," he admitted, as if sweetness were a great surprise to find in the middle of a scary cloud. (It usually is. That's rather the point of it.)

Shiro blinked. Then, very carefully, he reached over and squeezed Pinku's hand.

"Hey!" said Pinku. "What's the —"

"It means *I've got you*," whispered Shiro.

Pinku's lip stopped wobbling. "...Oh," she said. Then, after a moment, she reached out and squeezed Cha's hand. Cha, who pretended not to need it, squeezed back twice as hard.

"Now reach to the taiyaki!" called Momo, bouncing happily on her bench. "Everyone hold a hand — a fin — a flipper — a paw — whatever you've got! We're making a chain!"

And so they did.

It was the silliest, sweetest, most tangled chain of hand-holding you ever saw. Tai the taiyaki held a fin out to Sora the soft-serve, who held her cool creamy hand out to the kei-truck's little side-mirror, who beeped softly and stretched her windshield-wiper like a friendly arm. The Dango Three held the stick they shared and the hands beside them. And Momo Mochi stretched her warm arms all the way around so that the chain looped back to her on both sides, and she held on, and she squeezed, and she said the magic words:

"Squish together, stay together!"

The whole car squeezed at once.

And the strangest thing happened. The fear didn't vanish — fear rarely just *poofs* away, no matter what the picture books tell you. But it got *smaller*. It shared itself out along the chain, hand to hand to fin to paw, until each friend was only holding a little tiny piece of it, light as a soap bubble, easy to carry.

"See?" said Momo gently. "Scary feels big when you hold it alone. But pass it around the squish-chain, and it gets little. Now — let's *play* with the cloud while we wait!"

"Play... with the cloud?" said Pinku, sniffling but curious.

"Of course!" Momo pressed her squishy face to the window. "Look. What does *that* kumo look like?"

The friends looked. A great soft lump of fog drifted past the glass.

"...A bunny?" whispered Shiro.

"A *huge* bunny," agreed Tai, brightening. "With one floppy ear!"

"That one's a bowl of ramen," said Sora dreamily, pointing her swirl. "And that one's a sleeping cat."

"That one," declared Pinku, who was now bold again because boldness comes back fast when you're holding hands, "is *clearly* a giant dumpling. Like us. But fluffier."

"There is no such thing as fluffier than us," said Cha, and even Shiro laughed.

The little car swayed, and they swayed with it, and they named the clouds — a turtle, a teapot, a snoring grandpa, a noodle that went on forever — and Momo's potato-starch puffed up and joined the fog so that for a moment the whole car was inside its own tiny cloud, soft and white and full of giggles.

"Do you think," said Tai the taiyaki dreamily, his red-bean heart glowing through his smile, "that if I opened the window, I could swim out into the kumo? Like a fish in a great big soft sea?"

"You'd come back covered in cloud-fluff," said Cha.

"I would look MAGNIFICENT covered in cloud-fluff," said Tai, and waddled in a happy little circle, and the whole chain wobbled and laughed.

Even the kei-truck got into it, beeping out a tune that sounded suspiciously like a song about going for a drive, while Sora hummed along and Pinku conducted with her free hand as if she were the boss of the entire orchestra (which, in her opinion, she always was). Nobody was watching the white nothing anymore. They were watching the *wonder*.

Momo Mochi held the chain, and squeezed every time a friend went quiet, and felt her own little flip of fear melt away into something warm. Because here is the thing about being the cozy heart of a group: when you help everyone else feel brave, your own bravery sneaks back in through the side door, carrying snacks.

Far off, deep in the white, the wind began to shift.

It started as a thinning — the fog stretching wispy as cotton candy pulled too far. A patch of pale gold glowed somewhere above. The kei-truck's headlight-eyes went wide. The wire overhead gave a soft, businesslike *hummmm*, and the car began, ever so gently, to climb again.

"It's moving!" gasped Shiro. "We're MOVING!"

"Hold on!" sang Momo. "Hold hands! Here we GO!"

The car rose. The cloud thinned. And then —

Oh, my dear. Then.

The car burst up out of the top of the kumo like a cork popping out of a bottle, and the whole world *opened*.

Above them: sky so blue it looked freshly painted, with the late sun pouring gold across everything. And below them — below them stretched a *sea of clouds*, mile after mile of soft white rolling hills, glowing pink and peach and rose in the evening light, with the dark green tips of mountains poking through like islands. It was an ocean made of pillows. It was a sky you could nap in. It was the single most beautiful thing any of them had ever seen.

The whole car gasped at once.

"It's..." Pinku couldn't finish.

"It's a peach," whispered Momo Mochi, and a happy tear sparkled at the corner of her eye. "The whole sky is a peach, just like me."

They pressed their faces to the windows — fins, flippers, swirls, and all — still holding hands, every single one of them, because nobody wanted to let go now. Not when the scary cloud had turned, all by itself, into the most wonderful sight in the world.

"You know," said Cha thoughtfully, "if we'd let go when we were frightened, we'd have spent the whole ride staring at our feet. And we'd have missed *this*."

"That's the secret," said Momo, squishing the whole chain into one enormous group-hug, dumplings and taiyaki and soft-serve and kei-truck all squished warm together in the golden light. "Holding hands doesn't make the cloud go away. It just helps you stay long enough to see it turn beautiful."

The little car climbed on toward the mountaintop, full of friends, full of giggles, full of gold.

And every single one of them agreed it was the coziest adventure of their squishy little lives.

## Goodnight Blessing
Tonight, little one, if something feels big and foggy and you can't see the other side, remember Momo's secret: reach out, hold a hand, and pass the worry along until it's small and soft as a cloud. The fog always thins. The sun always pours back in. And the people who held your hand in the white nothing will be right there beside you when the whole sky turns to peach. So snuggle down, squeeze your blanket like a little hand, and drift up gently through your own warm kumo. Goodnight.
