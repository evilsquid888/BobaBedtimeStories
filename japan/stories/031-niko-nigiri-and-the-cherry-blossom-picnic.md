# Story 31: Niko Nigiri and the Cherry Blossom Picnic

**Main Character:** Niko Nigiri
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A riverbank under tunnels of blooming pink cherry trees
**Theme:** The prettiest things don't last long, so enjoy them now
**Japanese Spotlight:** さくら (sakura) = cherry blossom

---

Niko Nigiri woke up before the sun, before the birds, and — most impressively — before his own alarm clock, which was so surprised it forgot to ring.

"Today," Niko announced to his bedroom, standing very straight in his pearly white rice with his glossy salmon cape neatly pressed, "is the day of the **花見** (hanami) = flower-viewing picnic. And it will be PERFECT."

He had a checklist. Niko loved a checklist the way Kori loved sunglasses — completely, dramatically, and at all times. This one was eleven pages long, color-coded, and rolled up like a tiny scroll he could whap against his nori belt for emphasis.

*Whap.*

"Blankets: packed. Snacks: triple-checked. Optimal petal-viewing angle: calculated using a protractor." He squinted at item forty-seven. "Backup protractor: also packed."

Down by the river, the **さくら** (sakura) = cherry blossom trees were just beginning to open, pink and shy, like a whole crowd of friends blushing at once. Niko did not have time to notice. Niko had a schedule to keep.

"Let's do this neatly," he said, marching out the door, "or deliciously!"

The riverbank, when Niko arrived, took his breath clean away — for exactly four seconds, which was how long he allowed himself for "spontaneous wonder" (item two, scheduled for 6:14 a.m. sharp).

The cherry trees arched over the water in soft pink tunnels, their petals catching the early light like a thousand tiny lanterns. The river chuckled along below, carrying loose blossoms like little pink boats.

"Wonderful," said Niko, checking a box. "Now. To work."

He unrolled his blanket and smoothed every wrinkle with the side of his cape. He arranged the rice balls in rows. He lined up the cups so their handles all pointed the same direction (east, naturally — the most organized direction). He measured the distance between snacks with a ruler.

By the time his friends came bouncing down the path, the picnic looked less like a picnic and more like a museum exhibit titled *A Very Tidy Lunch.*

"NIKO!" cried Pock Ramune, zipping in on a trail of bubbles. "This looks — *pop!* — incredible!"

"Whisk slow," murmured Maru Matcha, drifting up with her little bamboo whisk. "Feel the morning."

"I have *scheduled* the morning," Niko said proudly, tapping page three. "Feeling commences at 6:40."

Momo Mochi squished up and immediately tried to hug everyone at once, which knocked over a perfectly aligned row of cups. "Oops! Squish together, stay together!"

"Please don't squish the cups," said Niko gently, re-aligning them. "They were east."

The morning rolled on, soft and golden. And here is the thing about Niko's perfect picnic: it kept *almost* being perfect, and then — wobbling.

First, a breeze came along and ruffled the blanket. Niko smoothed it. The breeze ruffled it again. Niko smoothed it again. The breeze, who was clearly enjoying itself, ruffled it a third time, and Niko smoothed it so fast his salmon cape made a little *fwip* sound.

"The wind," Niko informed the wind sternly, "is not on the schedule."

The wind did not seem to care. The wind, in fact, seemed to think the whole thing was hilarious, and went off to ruffle the cherry trees instead, sending down a little shower of giggling petals.

"Now THAT," said Pock Ramune, doing a loop-the-loop through the falling pink, "is the best part — *pop!* — of the whole day!"

"It is item nineteen," Niko corrected, "scheduled for after lunch. Please don't enjoy it early. We'll throw off the whole afternoon."

But Pock was already enjoying it early, and so were the petals, and so, secretly, was the wind.

Then a petal landed in Rin Ramen's golden broth.

"A blossom!" Rin gasped, steam puffing happily off the top of his bowl. "It fell right into me! Everything's better warm and bubbly — and now flowery!"

Niko hurried over with a tiny spoon. "Hold still, I'll remove the contaminant—"

"The what?" said Rin.

"The petal," said Niko. "It's not on the menu."

"But it's *pretty,*" said Rin, hugging his bowl protectively so the petal floated in a slow, happy circle. "I'm keeping it."

Niko blinked. He looked at his checklist. There was no box for *keeping a pretty petal.* He felt, very faintly, the way you feel when you've packed for a trip and forgotten something, but you can't remember what.

He pushed the feeling down and checked the next box.

Then came the Great Snack Incident.

Niko had built — and he was quietly very proud of this — a pyramid of rice balls so perfectly stacked that it could have been in a textbook titled *How to Stack Rice Balls, by Someone Who Cares Very Much.* It had a base of nine, then four, then one tiny rice ball balanced on the very top like a crown.

A blossom landed on the crown rice ball.

Then another.

Then the wind, who you will remember was having an excellent day, came whooshing down the river tunnel and gave the whole pyramid one cheerful nudge.

"No," said Niko.

"Uh-oh," said the Dango Three, all three at once.

The crown rice ball wobbled. The four wobbled. The nine wobbled. And then the entire perfect pyramid came tumbling down in a slow, soft, snowy avalanche of rice balls and petals, rolling in every un-east direction across the blanket.

There was a long pause.

"...Snack landslide!" announced Pock joyfully, and zipped off to chase a runaway rice ball before it reached the river.

Niko stared at the wreckage of his beautiful pyramid. A single petal drifted down and landed right on top of the rubble, as if to say *there, that's better.*

By mid-morning, the trees were in full bloom — an explosion of pink so soft it looked like the sky had grown fur. Petals had started to let go, just a few at first, drifting down lazy and slow. The Dango Three rolled over on their stick to watch.

"Look at them fall!" said Pinku, the bold pink one.

"What if they ALL fall and then there are none left?" worried Shiro, the shy white one.

"Then we'll have *seen* them," said clever green Cha, "which is the whole point. Three on a stick, stuck together for good!"

Niko was not watching the petals. Niko was rebuilding his snack pyramid, which the wind had knocked into a snack *avalanche.*

"Niko," said Maru Matcha, settling beside him with her calm half-moon eyes. "May I tell you a small thing?"

"Is it on the schedule?" asked Niko, balancing a rice ball on top of the pyramid.

"It is older than your schedule," said Maru. "It is older than mine." She tilted her frothy matcha head toward the trees. "The さくら only bloom for a little while. A week, maybe two. Then they let go. That is not a sadness. That is the *whole reason* they are beautiful."

Niko paused, one rice ball still in his hands. "But if I look away to fix the pyramid," he said slowly, "and then look back — will it be the same?"

"No," said Maru, smiling her tiny smile. "It will be different. It always is. That is the deal."

Niko frowned. This was a very inefficient deal. Niko liked things he could plan for, count on, and check off. A thing that was only beautiful *because* it was leaving felt like a riddle he hadn't packed the right tool for. There was, he was fairly certain, no protractor for this.

"But that's backwards," he said. "If a thing is wonderful, shouldn't it last as *long* as possible?"

"Should the sunset last all night?" asked Maru.

Niko opened his mouth. Then closed it. Then opened it again, looking remarkably like a fish considering a difficult question.

"That," he admitted, "is a very annoying point."

"Whisk slow," said Maru kindly. "The good things don't ask to be fixed. They only ask to be noticed."

The rice ball wobbled in Niko's grip.

And right then, the breeze swept down the whole tunnel of trees at once, and a great soft rush of petals lifted off the branches — thousands of them — and tumbled through the air like the gentlest snow that had ever fallen. Pink everywhere. Pink on the blanket. Pink in the river. Pink caught in the steam rising off Rin, in the bubbles trailing off Pock, in the matcha-cream swirl on Maru's head.

His friends froze, faces turned up, mouths open in wonder.

And Niko Nigiri — tidy, planning, eleven-page-checklist Niko — was the only one still looking down.

He looked at his pyramid. He looked at his ruler. He looked at his backup protractor.

Then a single petal landed, very softly, right on the tip of his nose.

He went almost cross-eyed looking at it. It was so small. So light. It had ridden the wind all the way down from the very top of the tallest tree just to land, of all places, on *him.*

"Oh," said Niko.

He set down the rice ball.

He set down the ruler.

And — this was the brave part, braver than any protractor — he rolled up his eleven-page checklist, tucked it neatly under the blanket where he couldn't see it, and *looked up.*

The world had gone pink and slow and impossibly soft. Petals spun and drifted and danced. The river carried them away in shining ribbons. His friends laughed and caught them and let them go.

"Niko's watching!" cried Pock. "Niko's actually — *pop!* — watching!"

"Get over here!" called Rin, scooting his flowery bowl aside to make room. "There's space! There's always space!"

Niko walked — not marched, *walked* — to the middle of the blanket and sat down right in the swirl of it all. A petal landed on his salmon cape. He didn't brush it off. Another landed on his rice. He let it stay.

"It's not on the menu," he said softly, "but I think I'll keep it."

Momo Mochi gathered everyone into one big squish, and this time Niko squished too, petals raining down over the whole pile of friends like soft pink confetti. Somebody knocked over the cups. Nobody fixed them. The handles pointed every which way, in every direction at once, and the morning did not mind one bit.

They stayed until the light turned gold, then amber, then soft sleepy rose. They ate the slightly-rumpled snacks, which tasted exactly as good un-pyramided. They watched the very last of the day's petals let go and float downriver.

"Will they be here tomorrow?" Shiro asked.

"Some," said Maru. "Fewer."

"Then we're glad we came *today,*" said Cha.

Niko nodded, a petal still riding on his nose. He had not checked a single box in over an hour. He had, he realized, never felt more perfectly, deliciously, un-tidily happy in his whole organized life.

"You know," he said, "I think I planned the wrong thing. I planned a *picnic.* I should have just planned to *be here.*"

"That's the best plan there is," said Maru. "Whisk slow. Feel calm."

And as the first stars blinked awake over the river, Niko Nigiri folded his checklist into a tiny paper boat, set it gently on the water, and watched it float away with the blossoms — east, of course, but that hardly mattered now.

## Goodnight Blessing
Sleep softly now, little one, under a sky full of drifting petals. May you remember that the prettiest things — pink blossoms, golden mornings, warm hugs from good friends — do not last forever, and that this is exactly what makes them so dear. Don't spend your whole day fixing the cups; sometimes the loveliest thing to do is simply look up and watch the world go pink. Tomorrow there will be fewer blossoms, and that is all right, because you were here for these. Snuggle down, breathe slow like Maru, and let the petals fall behind your sleepy eyes. Goodnight.
