# Story 53: Niko Nigiri and the Kyoto Kimono Day

**Main Character:** Niko Nigiri
**Guest Characters:** Ojii-san, an old wooden tea-whisk who keeps the kimono shop; Granny Kinako, a plump powdered old mochi
**Setting:** The historic Gion district of Kyoto, with its traditional wooden streets
**Theme:** Dressing with care honors where you are
**Japanese Spotlight:** **kimono** (kee-moh-noh) — traditional robe
**Cameo:** Takuya Seo, Kei Amane and Kotoko Ijichi (Gals Can't Be Kind to Otaku!?) — two gals drag a flustered otaku into the silk avalanche, Kotoko shouts every colour, Kei picks the tiger one with a single cool nod, Takuya wears it inside out and poses anyway; walk-on: Luna (Sailor Moon)

---

Gion's old lane looked like a storybook picture, wooden houses leaning together, lanterns swaying.

In the middle stood Niko Nigiri, pearly rice, coral salmon cape, standing so straight he squeaked.

"Today I wear a **kimono** (kee-moh-noh) — traditional robe," he announced to absolutely no one. "The RIGHT way."

"In four minutes," he added, tapping a pocket watch. A pigeon cooed: good luck with that.

Luna, a slim black cat with a crescent on her forehead, sighed from the shop roof. "Honestly."

"Let's do this neatly," said Niko, and marched under a deep-blue curtain into the shop.

---

Inside, silk lay folded on every shelf like rolled-up rainbows: plum pink, barley gold, deep indigo.

The keeper was Ojii-san, an old wooden tea-whisk with bristles like a silver mustache.

"One kimono, please," said Niko. "Ready in approximately four minutes."

Ojii-san chuckled. "A kimono is a conversation, rice-friend. Left over right, like wrapping a present."

Niko grabbed an indigo silk dotted with silver cranes. "It matches my belt. Proceed."

---

Niko dove in. The silk slipped. It slid. It billowed up like a friendly ghost.

Niko vanished entirely. "I have this completely under control," said the puff of indigo.

"You have a sleeve on your head," observed Ojii-san.

"I am aware," said the sleeve.

Niko tried again, faster. Wrapped. Wound. Tied himself tight as a maki roll.

"Still under control," said the maki roll, and hopped. And wobbled. And bumped the big shelf.

Down came the rainbows, a silk avalanche swallowing the whole shop.

Two silver bristles poked up from the pile. "Four minutes," said Ojii-san, muffled. "Hm."

---

The curtain flew wide. "KIMONO SHOPPING!" yelled Kotoko, a gal with streaked hair and jangling accessories.

Cool pale-blond Kei followed, towing Takuya, a flustered otaku in glasses, by his shopping bag.

"PINK! No, GOLD! No, TIGERS!" shrieked Kotoko, flinging silk. A gold wave buried Niko again.

"Twelve minutes," squeaked the Niko-pile. Takuya sank in beside it, up to his glasses.

Kei gave one cool nod. Takuya surfaced in the tiger kimono, inside out, and struck a hero pose.

"Loudest kimono in Kyoto," said Kei. "It's you." The three held the pose, glowing.

---

Niko surfaced, salmon cape backward, one silver crane poking into his ear.

In the mirror he looked like a present wrapped by a puppy. "Was THAT fashion?"

"I rushed," said Niko, drooping. "Louder, faster, and now the crane is in my ear."

Ojii-san waded over, slow as honey. "A kimono asks you to slow down."

Niko breathed. In, slow. Out, slow. His arms slid through the sleeves like a river.

Left over right, neat as a closing book. The **obi** (oh-bee) — sash puffed into a soft bow.

Twenty-six minutes, not four. In the mirror the cranes flew right, and Niko belonged.

---

Out into Gion he stepped in wooden **geta** (geh-tah) — clogs, clip, clip, small careful steps.

Small steps made wide eyes: gold light on stones, a tiny shrine, one flower.

From the roof, Luna looked him up and down and gave one prim approving nod.

A gust whooshed down the lane and the plum tree let go every petal at once.

Pink snow, everywhere. Niko stood still and let it settle on his sleeves.

---

On an arched bridge sat Granny Kinako, a plump powdered mochi. "You wear it well!" she called.

Niko bowed the careful bow. "Thank you. It took twenty-six minutes."

Granny Kinako laughed like a fluffed cushion. "Dressing with care says thank you to the day."

"Sit," she said. "The tea is warm. No hurry for sitting." So Niko sat.

---

The sun painted the rooftops plum-pink, and Niko clip-clipped back to the shop.

He folded the kimono himself, slowly, left over right, smoothing every crane down gently.

"Worth the whole day," Niko told Ojii-san, and tucked his pocket watch away for good.

"Slow," he yawned, "is its own kind of neat."
