# Story 79: Neko-Pan and the Lucky Beckoning Spirits

**Main Character:** Neko-Pan (Maneki Melon Cream)
**Guest Characters:** Maneki, Koneki and Teeny, three tiny beckoning-cat spirits; Chiyo, a field mouse; Mame, her sneezing little brother
**Setting:** A tiny roadside shrine guarded by cheerful good-luck cat spirits
**Theme:** Look for the why before you blame — and mend the crack instead of pouring forever
**Japanese Spotlight:** **nazo** (NAH-zoh) — a mystery

---

On a sleepy bend in the road stood a shrine no taller than a teapot.

"GONE!" wailed a tiny voice inside. "Again! Six mornings in a ROW!"

Neko-Pan skidded to a stop so fast his collar bell went ti-i-i-ing like a startled bicycle.

Inside, three pearly cat-spirits stared into an old clay jar: Maneki, Koneki, and Teeny, raisin-sized and worried. Empty.

"We fill it each morning with the travelers' thanks," said Maneki. "By lunch, someone has taken every drop."

"It's the toad," whispered Teeny. "He's been looking very smug lately."

---

Neko-Pan's eyes sparkled. "A **nazo** (NAH-zoh) — a mystery! I have always wanted to be a detective."

Then he found it: golden glitter-dust, and tiny footprints, each the size of an apple seed.

"Aha! Our luck-taker has feet!"

"But the toad's feet are plum-sized," said Neko-Pan. "One suspect cleared. We're very good at this."

---

"So at noon," he said, "we stake out the shrine. Very still. Very quiet. Very awake."

"You fell asleep waving yesterday," said Teeny. "Mid-wave. Standing up."

---

At noon, beside the shrine, stood a brand-new lucky cat statue. It smelled of warm custard.

When a butterfly drifted past, the statue's paw twitched and gave it a tiny friendly wave.

"Statues don't wave," hissed Koneki from the cushion.

"This one is very welcoming," whispered the statue.

Then, pitter pitter: a grey field mouse crept up the moss, a thimble in both paws.

"Just a pinch," she whispered. "I'm sorry. Just a pinch." She bowed three times and scurried off.

But the golden glow kept sinking anyway, slow and steady, like bathwater with the plug half out.

---

Neko-Pan crept close and squinted. Low on the jar's belly, under the moss, ran a hairline crack.

Through it, the luck seeped out in a slow golden thread, down into the soil.

"Oh," whispered Neko-Pan. "A very small thief and a very big crack."

The footprints led to a burrow under the persimmon tree, where Chiyo the mouse knelt beside her brother.

Mame sneezed the tiniest sneezes in Japan. Choo. Choo. Choo. His whiskers drooped like wet noodles.

"A pinch of luck under his pillow each day," said Chiyo, ears pink. "I always bowed."

"Come with me, both of you," said Neko-Pan. "Nobody will be cross. The detective won't allow it."

---

At the shrine, Koneki puffed up like a small offended cloud. "THE LUCK THIEF!"

"Before anyone points a paw," said Neko-Pan, "ask the why." Chiyo told them about Mame.

"And she only took a pinch," said Neko-Pan. "This is the culprit." He tipped the jar sunward.

The jar tipped too far. It rolled off the cushion and down the road, picking up speed.

Neko-Pan bounced after it. Three spirits zoomed. Chiyo sprinted. The jar headed straight for the puddle.

Mame sneezed. CHOO. The jar wobbled, stopped, and bumped gently against the persimmon tree.

Everyone looked up. The persimmon tree was the greenest, gladdest, most ridiculously fruity tree on the road.

"It's been drinking your luck for a week," said Neko-Pan. "No wonder it looks so pleased."

---

"Then we mend it," said Neko-Pan, stirring one fleck of his collar's gold foil into warm custard.

With Teeny's softest whisker for a brush, he painted the crack, slowly, all the way down.

It set like glaze: a gleaming golden river, lovelier than the clay around it.

"**Kintsugi** (keen-TSOO-ghee) — mending with gold," said Neko-Pan. "You make the crack the most beautiful part."

---

Next morning the jar brimmed, glowing like a sunrise in a pot, golden seam shining.

The first ribbon of luck went to the burrow, and Mame's sneezes shrank to hiccups, then giggles.

"Look for the why before the who," said Neko-Pan, waving at a butterfly. "Then mend the crack."

The bell went ting, soft as a wish. Every drop of luck stayed exactly where it belonged.
