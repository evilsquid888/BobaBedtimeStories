# Story 79: Neko-Pan and the Lucky Beckoning Spirits

**Main Character:** Neko-Pan (Maneki Melon Cream)
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A tiny roadside shrine guarded by cheerful good-luck cat spirits
**Theme:** Luck shared is luck doubled
**Japanese Spotlight:** うん (un) = luck

---

On a sleepy bend in the road, just past the persimmon tree and exactly one hop from the puddle that always looked like a pancake, there lived a little shrine no taller than a teapot. And bouncing toward it that morning, raised paw waving at absolutely everyone, came Neko-Pan.

Neko-Pan was a cream-filled bun shaped like a beckoning lucky cat, with a gold-foil collar bell that went *ting* when he was happy — which was always. He waved at a snail. He waved at a cloud. He waved at his own shadow and then apologized to it for startling it.

"Good morning, road!" he called. "Good morning, puddle-that-looks-like-a-pancake! Wave for luck, share the luck!"

The bell went *ting*.

And from inside the tiny shrine, three very small voices gasped all at once — "Did you hear that? Someone's SHARING luck!"

---

Neko-Pan peeked into the little shrine. Inside, sitting in a row on a moss cushion, were three of the tiniest cat-spirits he had ever seen. They were round and pearly and see-through-soft, like soap bubbles wearing whiskers, and each one had a raised paw — though the paws drooped a little, the way your arms droop when you've been carrying groceries too far.

"Hello!" Neko-Pan waved. "I'm Neko-Pan. Are you the lucky cats of this shrine?"

"We *were*," sighed the biggest spirit, who was about the size of a plum. "I'm Maneki. These are my little ones, Koneki and Teeny."

"We grant **うん** (un) = luck," said Koneki proudly.

"Or we *did*," whispered Teeny, whose voice was so small it sounded like a pebble dropping into tea.

Maneki lifted her drooping paw and gave it a sad little shake. Where a lucky sparkle should have shot out, there was only a tiny puff of dust, like a sneeze made of nothing.

"We've run all the way out," Maneki admitted. "Empty. Not a single sparkle left in the whole shrine. Travelers come by hoping for a wave of good fortune, and all we can give them is... *pfft*."

She demonstrated. *Pfft.* A moth, who had been waiting hopefully, flew off looking unimpressed.

Neko-Pan's whiskers wobbled. He hated to see a drooping paw more than almost anything. "But where does luck come from? Surely we can fetch some more!"

The three spirits looked at each other. Koneki scratched his see-through ear. Teeny chewed her tiny paw. Maneki finally said, very slowly, as if remembering a dream:

"That's the funny thing. We always *gave* luck. We never thought about where it *came* from. It just... was there. And then one day it wasn't."

"Hmm," said Neko-Pan. He sat down on the moss to think, which for him meant waving thoughtfully at a beetle. "When was the last time the shrine felt really, truly lucky? Bursting-full lucky?"

The spirits thought hard.

"Long ago," Maneki said at last. "Back when travelers stopped to rest here. They'd share their rice balls with each other under the persimmon tree. They'd laugh. They'd help a stranger fix a wobbly wheel. The road was *busy* with kindness." Her ears perked, then drooped again. "But now everyone hurries past. Nobody stops. And the shrine got quiet. And the luck got... thin."

Neko-Pan's bell gave a slow, thoughtful *ting*.

"I think," he said, "I know what's wrong. Luck isn't something you scoop out of a jar until it's gone. Luck is more like a fire — you have to keep feeding it kindling, or it goes out. And the kindling..." He grinned his sweet custard grin. "...is *kindness*."

Koneki blinked. "You mean we don't *find* more luck?"

"You *make* more!" Neko-Pan bounced to his feet, bell ringing like a tiny bicycle. "And I know exactly how. Today, we spread joy all up and down this road. Every kind thing we do — every laugh, every helping paw — refills the shrine a drop at a time. Luck shared is luck doubled!"

The three spirits looked deeply uncertain, the way you do when a friend suggests cleaning your room as a fun game.

"But our paws are tired," said Teeny.

"Then we'll wave together," said Neko-Pan warmly. "Many small paws make a big wave. Climb aboard!"

---

And so they set off, the three little spirits riding in the curl of Neko-Pan's raised paw like sleepy seeds in a melon slice, drifting down the **道** (michi) = road.

The first traveler they met was a fat old toad sitting beside a cart with a wheel sunk deep in the mud. He was muttering grumpily at the wheel, as if scolding it would help. (It would not. Wheels are famously bad listeners.)

"Good morning!" called Neko-Pan. "Need a paw?"

"I need four paws and a miracle," grumbled the toad.

"We have one and a half paws and three small spirits," said Neko-Pan cheerfully, "which is nearly the same thing."

They all heaved together — Neko-Pan pushing, the three spirits shoving with their tiny see-through shoulders, the toad pulling, everybody making the dramatic *nnnnngh* noise that makes any heavy thing twenty percent easier to lift. With a glorious *SCHLOOP*, the wheel popped free.

The toad blinked, astonished. Then he laughed — a great big *ribbity* laugh that bounced off the persimmon tree. "Well, I'll be dipped in mud! Thank you, little ones. That was the best luck I've had all week!"

And the moment he said the word *luck* — *ting.* A single warm sparkle drifted off his cart, floated all the way back up the road, and *plinked* into the tiny shrine like a coin into a pond.

Teeny gasped. "Did you SEE that? The shrine — it caught a sparkle!"

Maneki pressed her see-through paws to her see-through cheeks. "It's true. The kindness came *back*."

"Of course it did," said Neko-Pan, waving the toad goodbye. "Luck doubled. He felt lucky, *and* the shrine got fuller. Everybody wins, and nobody loses a thing!"

---

After that, you could not have stopped them with a wall.

They found a beetle who had lost her way home, and Neko-Pan waved her in the right direction with such confidence that she found her door in under a minute. *Ting* — a sparkle floated home to the shrine.

They found a baby sparrow who'd dropped his only sunflower seed down a crack, and Teeny — *tiny* Teeny, who could fit in a thimble — squeezed down and nudged it back up. The sparrow chirped so joyfully that *three* sparkles came spinning back at once, bumping into each other on the way like puppies.

They shared songs with a lonely cricket. They helped a snail cross a worrying twig. Koneki, who turned out to be very funny once he warmed up, told a joke so silly that a passing field mouse laughed milk out of her nose, which made *everyone* laugh, which sent a whole little fountain of sparkles fizzing back toward the shrine.

And the more they gave, the more the spirits *glowed*. Their droopy paws lifted. Their see-through bodies turned warm and golden, like lanterns waking up at dusk. Even their whiskers stood up straighter.

"I feel..." Maneki searched for the word. "...*full*."

"That's the secret," said Neko-Pan softly. "You can't pour luck out of an empty cup. But every kind thing you do fills your *own* cup right back up. So you never actually run dry — as long as you keep sharing." He waved at a butterfly. The butterfly, charmed, did a little loop. "Wave for luck, share the luck!"

By the time the sky went peach-and-gold, the road behind them was humming again. The toad had stopped to help a hedgehog. The hedgehog was sharing acorns with the field mouse. The sparrow was singing the cricket's song. Kindness, it turned out, was *catching* — like yawns, but lovely.

---

They walked the long way back as the first stars blinked on. And when they reached the tiny shrine no taller than a teapot, the three spirits stopped and stared, paws over mouths.

The shrine was *glowing*. Brimming. Sparkles pooled in it like fireflies in a jam jar, spilling gently over the moss cushion, more luck than the little spirits had held in a hundred quiet years.

"It's so *full*," whispered Teeny.

"Fuller than ever," said Maneki, her voice wobbly and warm. "We thought we'd lost our luck forever. But we just had to give it away to get it back." She turned to Neko-Pan, eyes shining. "How can we ever thank you?"

Neko-Pan tilted his round head, bell going *ting*. "Easy," he said. "Tomorrow, when a tired traveler stops here, give them a wave. And tell them the secret. Then *they'll* share it down the road, and the shrine will never run empty again."

The three spirits raised their paws — all together, bright and strong now — and waved. And out shot the most beautiful sparkle yet, a great golden ribbon of **うん** (un) = luck that wrapped clean around Neko-Pan in a hug.

"For you," said Maneki. "The luckiest cat we ever met."

Neko-Pan glowed gold from ears to paw. He waved back, custard heart full to the brim.

And as he bounced off home beneath the stars — past the persimmon tree, one hop from the puddle-that-looks-like-a-pancake — three tiny voices called after him into the warm night:

"Wave for luck, share the luck!"

The bell went *ting,* and the whole road seemed to smile.
