# Story 79: Neko-Pan and the Lucky Beckoning Spirits

**Main Character:** Neko-Pan (Maneki Melon Cream)
**Guest Characters:** Maneki, Koneki and Teeny, three tiny beckoning-cat spirits; Chiyo, a field mouse; Mame, her sneezing little brother
**Setting:** A tiny roadside shrine guarded by cheerful good-luck cat spirits
**Theme:** Look for the why before you blame — and mend the crack instead of pouring forever
**Japanese Spotlight:** **nazo** (NAH-zoh) — a mystery
**Cameo:** The Baron (The Cat Returns) — a statue among the shrine's lucky cats steps down, bows, balances a cup of tea on the disguised Neko-Pan's head, and welcomes the luck thief so loudly she flees; walk-on: Jibanyan (Yo-kai Watch)

---

On a quiet bend in the road stood a teapot-sized shrine, ringed by lucky cat statues.

"GONE!" wailed a tiny voice inside. "Again! Six mornings in a ROW!"

Neko-Pan skidded up so fast his collar bell went ti-i-i-ing like a startled bicycle.

Inside, three raisin-sized cat-spirits stared into an old clay jar. Empty.

"We fill it each morning with travelers' thanks," said Maneki. "By lunch, gone."

"It's the toad," whispered Teeny. "He's been looking very smug lately."

Neko-Pan's eyes sparkled. "A **nazo** (NAH-zoh) — a mystery! I have always wanted to be a detective."

---

He bounced to the moss, nose down, bottom up. Glitter-dust, and footprints like apple seeds.

"Aha! Our luck-taker has tiny feet!" he cried. "The toad's are plum-sized. One suspect cleared!"

"So at noon," said Neko-Pan, "we stake out the shrine. Very still. Very quiet."

---

At noon, among the lucky cat statues stood a brand-new one. It smelled of warm custard.

When a butterfly drifted past, the new statue's paw twitched and gave it a tiny friendly wave.

"Statues don't wave," hissed Koneki from the cushion.

"This one is very welcoming," whispered the statue.

---

Then the statue beside him stretched: a dapper orange cat in a grey top hat, stepping down.

"Welcome to my shrine," said the Baron, bowing low over his cane. "You simply must have tea."

He poured a cup from nowhere and balanced it, steaming, on the custard statue's head.

Pitter pitter: a grey field mouse crept up the moss, thimble raised. "A GUEST!" cried the Baron. "Tea!"

The mouse squeaked, bowed three times at top speed, and vanished into the grass.

"Splendid visit," said the Baron, and settled back on his pedestal, hat tipped, frozen mid-bow.

---

The golden glow kept sinking anyway, like bathwater with the plug half out.

Neko-Pan crept close, teacup still on his head. Low on the jar ran a hairline crack.

"Oh," whispered Neko-Pan, watching a golden thread seep out. "A very small thief and a very big crack."

---

The footprints led to a burrow under the persimmon tree. Chiyo the mouse knelt beside her brother.

Mame sneezed the tiniest sneezes in Japan. Choo. Choo. His whiskers drooped like wet noodles.

"A pinch of luck under his pillow each day," said Chiyo, ears pink. "I always bowed."

"Come with me, both of you," said Neko-Pan. "Nobody will be cross. Detective's orders."

---

At the shrine, Koneki puffed up like a small offended cloud. "THE LUCK THIEF!"

"Before anyone points a paw," said Neko-Pan, "ask the why." Chiyo told them about Mame.

"She only took a pinch," said Neko-Pan. "THIS is the culprit." He tipped the jar sunward.

It tipped too far, rolled off the cushion and down the road, picking up speed.

Neko-Pan bounced after it. Three spirits zoomed. Chiyo sprinted. The jar headed for the puddle.

Mame sneezed. CHOO. The jar wobbled, stopped, and bumped the persimmon tree.

Everyone looked up. It was the greenest, gladdest, most ridiculously fruity tree on the road.

"It's been drinking your luck for a week," said Neko-Pan. "No wonder it looks so pleased."

---

"Then we mend it," said Neko-Pan, stirring a fleck of his collar's gold foil into custard.

With Teeny's softest whisker for a brush, he painted the crack, all the way down.

"**Kintsugi** (keen-TSOO-ghee) — mending with gold," said Neko-Pan. "You make the crack the most beautiful part."

---

Next morning the jar brimmed, glowing like a sunrise in a pot, golden seam shining.

The first ribbon of luck went to the burrow, and Mame's sneezes shrank to giggles.

Among the statues, a red two-tailed cat with blue flames on its tails stretched. "Nyan," it said.

"Look for the why before the who," said Neko-Pan, waving at a butterfly. "Then mend the crack."

The bell went ting, soft as a wish. Every drop of luck stayed home.
