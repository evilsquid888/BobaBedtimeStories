# Story 39: Rin Ramen and the Secret Broth Recipe

**Main Character:** Rin Ramen
**Guest Characters:** Old Bowl, an ancient crackle-glazed ramen master; Mame, a small lost rice ball
**Setting:** A steamy back-street ramen counter run by a kindly old bowl
**Theme:** The secret ingredient is always care
**Japanese Spotlight:** **raamen** (RAH-mehn) — ramen

---

Down the twistiest back-street in town, under lanterns like warm orange eggs, something was steaming.

It was Rin Ramen, a bowl on stubby legs, bubbling so hard his noodle hair had frizzed.

"Tonight," he announced to the empty alley, "I learn the most LEGENDARY secret in the world!"

He skipped. His broth sloshed. A whole noodle slapped him in the eye.

At the alley's end lived Old Bowl, master of **raamen** (RAH-mehn) — ramen, keeper of a Secret Recipe.

"A SECRET," whispered Rin, steam puffing from both ears. "I'll learn it. Then I'll be famous."

---

Behind the indigo curtain: six stools, a hundred ladles, and a cat-shaped clock ticking extremely slowly.

There sat Old Bowl, enormous and ancient, his chipped rim like a smile missing one tooth.

"Welcome, little one," rumbled Old Bowl. "You are steaming an awful lot."

Rin slurped his steam back in and hopped onto a stool, legs swinging in midair.

"Please," he breathed, leaning until his name-tag dipped in the soup. "Teach me the SECRET."

Old Bowl chuckled like a soup pot. "I'll show you. But you must watch the slow parts."

"I love slow parts," said Rin, who had never met one.

---

Old Bowl lifted each bone to the lantern, hummed to it, and lowered it gently in.

"You're talking to the bones?" said Rin.

"How rude it would be not to," said Old Bowl. "Now we wait. **Yukkuri** (yook-KOO-ree) — slowly."

Rin sat very still. He lasted nine seconds. "Is it ready?"

"Bloop," said the pot. "NOW?" Bloop. The cat-clock ticked. Rin's egg cheek twitched.

His eyes drifted to a high shelf, where one small jar hid under a cloth. The secret spice!

---

While Old Bowl hummed, Rin stacked three stools and climbed, wobbling, broth sloshing.

He stretched. He tipped. He grabbed the cloth, and a shelf of ladles came clattering down.

Ladles bounced off Old Bowl's rim like a xylophone. Old Bowl said, "Mm."

Rin landed bottom-first on the stove knob. The flame roared up tall.

The pot bubbled. The pot burped. It sneezed a noodle onto the cat-clock, which did not care.

Then the broth rose like a golden tide and poured over the top.

"I'LL CATCH IT!" cried Rin, holding out his own bowl, and got a hot golden hat.

---

Broth flooded the floor. Bones bobbed past like little boats. Six stools floated after them.

Rin splashed after them, scooping, slipping, scooping, until all seven bones dripped in his arms.

Old Bowl lowered the flame with one slow nudge. The tide sighed and stopped.

The secret jar lay on the floor, unbroken. Rin lifted the lid with shaking arms.

It was empty.

"There's NOTHING in it," said Rin. His steam sagged flat. "Where's the secret?"

"That jar is full of time," said Old Bowl. "You just poured it all over my floor."

---

Rin's broth went quiet. He set the bones, one by one, gently into the empty pot.

"You waited all week to be warm," he told the first bone. "Sorry about the swim."

He lit the littlest flame. He sat. He listened to the bubbles. He let the clock tick.

Old Bowl's glaze glowed a shade warmer. "There," he said. "The secret ingredient is always care."

"There's no measuring spoon for caring," said Rin.

"No," said Old Bowl. "Isn't that wonderful? Nobody can steal it."

---

Later the curtain rustled. A small lost **onigiri** (oh-nee-GHEE-ree) — rice ball — stood there, shivering.

"Sit, little one," said Rin, patting a stool. "Everything's better warm and bubbly."

He ladled the slow bowl. Mame took one sip. His eyes went enormous and shiny.

"What's your secret?" whispered Mame.

Rin grinned, steam curling softly off his head. "You'll have to watch the slow parts."
