# Story 52: Kuma Kuri and the Nara Deer Bows

**Main Character:** Kuma the Kuri (roasted chestnut / kuri kinton sweet)
**Guest Characters:** Momiji, a young deer with cinnamon-stick antlers; Okaki, a crusty old rice-cracker with a cracker cart
**Setting:** Nara Park, where polite wild deer bow for treats
**Theme:** Good manners are a language everyone understands
**Japanese Spotlight:** **shika** (shee-kah) — deer
**Cameo:** Zero Two (Darling in the Franxx) — a pink-haired girl with little red horns vaults the lantern Kuma is hiding behind, calls him darling, licks her honey stick and bows to the herd, and every deer in Nara bows back deeper than they ever bowed for a cracker; walk-on: Yakul (Princess Mononoke)

---

Nara Park in autumn was full of deer, big ones, small ones, fluffy-bottomed ones.

In hopped a glossy mahogany chestnut in an autumn-leaf scarf three sizes too big.

Kuma the Kuri tripped on it, bounced twice, and landed cap-first.

"Small and roasty, ready and toasty," he whispered. His courage-spell. Today it wobbled.

"Today," Kuma told the leaves, "I say ONE brave hello. To one **shika** (shee-kah) — deer."

One deer looked right at him. Kuma squeaked and hid behind a stone lantern.

A stone lantern is skinny. A chestnut is round. The deer saw the whole chestnut.

---

It had velvet ears, soft brown eyes, and tiny antlers like two cinnamon sticks.

"Hello," managed Kuma. It came out the size of a sesame seed.

The deer dipped its head and bowed. Slow. Neat. Perfectly polite.

"Oh," breathed Kuma. "That's very nice manners. I can do manners."

He bowed back. Chestnuts are built for tipping. Kuma tipped, kept tipping, and bonked the hooves.

"Meant to be dignified," he squeaked. The deer bowed again, as if to say: on purpose, surely.

---

Kuma named it Momiji, and off they went, Momiji walking, Kuma hopping.

Every deer they passed bowed. Kuma bowed back and tipped over. By the fourth, Momiji was catching him.

Then, from the far end of the path: a clatter and a wail.

---

Okaki, a crusty old rice-cracker, had tipped her cart. **Shika senbei** (shee-kah sen-bay) — deer crackers — everywhere.

The deer came. From the grass, the shrine steps, the trees. A fuzzy, polite tide.

"Wait your turn," wailed Okaki. A hundred velvet heads dipped, a hundred hooves closed in. Polite stampede.

Among them, Yakul, a red elk with wide antlers, bowed so low he scooped up three crackers.

Kuma's tummy went cold. Herds have hooves. He hid behind the lantern. Again.

---

Then a tall pink-haired stranger in a red uniform vaulted the lantern, landing among the crackers.

Little red horns, white gloves, a honey stick in her teeth. "Found you, darling," purred Zero Two.

Kuma squeaked. She licked the honey stick, turned to the hundred deer, bowed low and slow, horns first.

Every deer in Nara bowed back, deeper than they ever bowed for a cracker. Crackers lay forgotten.

"Now they're bowing at HER!" wailed Okaki. Zero Two bowed for the bowing. The deer bowed deeper.

"Bye, darling," purred Zero Two, and perched on the lantern's stone step with her honey stick, watching.

---

The deer dove back at the crackers. Kuma hopped into the middle and shouted "STOP!" Sesame-seed sized.

He bowed to everyone at once, tipped, and bounced across the crackers, bonk bonk bonk.

Upside down, Kuma watched. Every deer was saying please. Just all at once.

"Please deserves a turn," said Kuma, and hopped upright, scarf and all.

He faced the nearest deer and bowed. Slow. Patient. The deer stopped, startled.

It bowed back. Kuma nudged over one cracker. Next deer. Bow, bow, cracker.

One by one, the fuzzy tide became a line. A wobbly, antler-y line.

---

A big bold deer shouldered to the front, nose first, manners nowhere.

Kuma hopped up, looked up its very long nose, bowed, and waited.

The big deer looked at the line. At the chestnut. Bowed, sheepishly, and went to the back.

"You speak deer!" laughed Okaki, righting her cart.

"Everyone speaks this one," said Kuma. "Please and thank you, with your whole body."

Momiji stepped to the front, bowed, and booped a velvet nose against Kuma's cap.

---

The sun slid low over Nara, lanterns glowing, and the deer drifted off, dipping their heads.

Okaki pressed a warm cracker into Kuma's arms. Kuma set it between Momiji's hooves.

Momiji folded its legs into the grass, and one roasty chestnut leaned against its side.

"Small and roasty," yawned Kuma. "Ready and toasty. And not alone."

---
