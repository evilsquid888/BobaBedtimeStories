# Story 80: Mero Melonpan and the Dream-Eating Baku

**Main Character:** Mero Melonpan
**Guest Characters:** Fuku, a little round dumpling; Buku, a teapot-sized dream-eating baku
**Setting:** A cozy bedroom at night visited by a gentle dream-eating baku
**Theme:** A good friend can chase away a bad dream
**Japanese Spotlight:** **yume** (yoo-meh) — dream

---

If you ever want to find Mero Melonpan, look for the snore. Hooo... pah. Hooo... pah.

Tonight he was curled on a moonlit windowsill, dreaming of a marshmallow as big as a beanbag.

"Crackle on the outside," he murmured, "dreams on the inside." Then he heard a sniffle.

Across the room, a little dumpling named Fuku slept with her eyebrows scrunched up tight.

Mero floomped off the sill and waddled over, leaving crumbs like a snail made of pastry.

"No," whimpered Fuku, fast asleep. "Don't take the lantern. Not the lantern."

She was stuck in a bad **yume** (yoo-meh) — dream. Mero wanted those eyebrows unscrunched before morning.

---

Mero shuffled to the window and hummed a sleepy little tune, like a music box winding down.

"Baku-baku-baaa-kuuu," he hummed. "Come gobble the grumpy bits, please and thank you."

The curtains breathed inward, and in floated the **baku** (bah-koo) — dream-eater.

You might be picturing too many teeth. This baku was the size of a teapot: plush, cloud-grey, curly-trunked.

"Mero!" whispered Buku the baku, bobbing in on invisible wings. "Is it snack time?"

---

Buku sniffed over Fuku's pillow, trunk twitching like a curious noodle. "Worry-flavored. Salty. Stormy."

"I only nibble out the worried bit," said Buku, "like picking the bad raisin from a bun."

"Oh, I love that," said Mero. "I do that with raisins."

"Everyone does that with raisins," said Buku solemnly. Then his ears drooped. "But this one's knotted."

"A knotted worry gives me hiccups," said Buku. "Last week I hiccuped a dream onto a cat."

"Someone soft has to go inside and sit beside her," said Buku. "Someone... bready?"

Mero gulped, puffed his crust up bravely, and asked how.

"Easy," said Buku, patting the pillow. "You take a nap."

---

Mero snuggled in, closed his eyes, and did the thing he did best. Whoosh. Down he tumbled.

The dream was grey and rainy: a lonely path, and a wind that tugged at everything.

Far ahead stood Fuku, clutching a little paper lantern, while the wind yanked at it.

"Mero!" cried dream-Fuku. "If it goes out I'll be all alone in the dark!"

The wind won. The lantern whirled away down the path, bobbing like a runaway balloon.

"After it!" said Mero, and set off at top speed, which for Mero is a brisk waddle.

---

He bounced over a puddle. In dreams, bounces go up and keep going. Mero floated away.

"This is the opposite of a nap," said Mero, upside down in the air.

Fuku hauled him down by one foot. They ran across a wobbly bridge. The lantern danced ahead.

They scrambled up a hill of pillows that squished under every step. The lantern blinked, and went out.

Dark. Grey. Fuku's eyebrows scrunched tighter than ever.

So Mero stopped running. He sat down, leaned against her, and smiled his widest smile.

His crackly crust began to glow, the warm gold of a kitchen at breakfast.

"Even if the lantern goes out," said Mero, "you wouldn't be alone. I'm right here. And I glow."

---

Fuku squished up close. The storm shrank to a breeze. The knot went loose.

Up in the bedroom, Buku's trunk wiggled. "There it is. Soft as warm mochi."

Snrrf, snrrf. He nibbled out the cold wind, the lonely path, the worried part. The sweet stayed.

His tummy went round as a balloon. He gave a tiny milk-scented burp. "Excuse me."

Fuku's eyebrows smoothed out at last, and her little paws opened like two flowers.

---

"Teamwork," whispered Buku at the curtain, glowing faintly from the cozy dream inside him.

Poof. A smell of warm milk and clean laundry, and the gentlest eater was gone.

Fuku, fast asleep, reached over and squished Mero close, like a stuffed animal.

Hooo... pah. Hooo... pah. Two friends on one little bed, and all the dreams were good ones.

---

## Goodnight Blessing
May your pillow be soft and your dreams sweeter still. If a worried thought tiptoes in, a kind friend can nibble it gently away. You are never alone in the dark. Breathe slow, squish your blanket tight. Goodnight.
