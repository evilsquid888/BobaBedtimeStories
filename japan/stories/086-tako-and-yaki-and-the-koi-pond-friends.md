# Story 86: Tako and Yaki and the Koi Pond Friends

**Main Character:** Tako & Yaki (takoyaki twins)
**Guest Characters:** Aki and Mizu, two slow and shimmering garden koi
**Setting:** A peaceful garden koi pond with shimmering carp
**Theme:** Calm friends can balance your bouncy energy
**Japanese Spotlight:** **koi** (koy) — carp

---

Through a round moon-gate, into the quietest garden in all of Japan, bounced two golden troublemakers.

Tako and Yaki never walked anywhere. They bounced, joined on top by one wobbly wave of bonito flakes.

"Roll us once—" sang Tako.

"—we come as TWO!" sang Yaki, and their high-five flung sauce in a happy zig-zag.

"It's SO quiet," said Tako. "We're going to make this the most fun garden in the whole world."

There lay a pond, big and glassy as a mirror. They cannonballed. SPLOOSH. A frog went straight up.

---

When the ripples settled, two enormous orange-and-white fish were staring at them, blinking very, very slowly.

"Hello, little splashers," said the big one. "I am Aki. We are **koi** (koy) — carp."

"We don't cannonball," said Mizu, the speckled one. "We drift. Watch this."

He did one loop-the-loop. It took roughly forever.

The twins watched a leaf land. They watched a cloud move about one inch across the sky.

"Okayokay we get it BYE," said Yaki, and the twins boinged off to find something louder.

---

They booped the wind chime forty times. They bounced across the little bridge like a tiny drum solo.

They challenged a stone lantern to a staring contest and lost twice, both times from giggling.

"Best. Garden. EVER," said Tako, bouncing so high he bonked a plum branch.

A shower of pink petals came down on Yaki. "Hey. I'm a flower now. Look at me."

Then they spotted it: the biggest lily pad in the pond, flat and green as a trampoline.

---

BOING. They landed dead center, and the lily pad shot away from the bank like a green raft.

"Lean left!" said Tako.

"Lean RIGHT!" said Yaki.

The pad tipped. Tako went in. Yaki went in. The bonito wave went in last, waving.

They scrambled back on, dripping, and bounced even bigger. The pad flipped like a pancake.

"Stop bouncing!" spluttered Tako.

"YOU stop bouncing! We're ATTACHED!" spluttered Yaki.

The pad drifted to the very middle of the pond, and the twins drooped, every bounce bounced out.

---

Two slow shadows rose beneath them. Aki's long whiskers came up first, then Mizu's speckled nose.

"Sit still," said Aki. "Sitting still is the fastest way home."

Sitting still was the hardest thing the twins had ever tried. Tako's arm twitched. Yaki sat on it.

The two koi nosed the lily pad toward the shore, slow as honey off a spoon.

Then the wind held its breath, and the whole pond turned into a mirror.

"There's a SKY in the water," whispered Yaki.

"And two takoyaki faces in it," whispered Tako, "and we almost bounced right past it."

"This is **nonbiri** (nohn-bee-ree) — relaxed and easygoing," said Mizu. "The speed of clouds."

---

The pad bumped the bank. The twins stepped off like two very dignified eggs.

Tako lifted one arm. Yaki lifted one arm. They inched toward each other like sleepy snails.

Pat. The softest high-five in the history of high-fives.

"Worst high-five we ever did," said Tako.

"And somehow the very best one," said Yaki.

"Drift together," said Aki, "stay together."

"DRIFT TOGETHER, STAY—" began Tako, then remembered, and finished it in a whisper.

---

The koi sank slowly into the golden water, like two orange lanterns going dim.

"Slow friends keep a calm pond ready for fast friends," said Mizu. "Come back when you're too zoomy."

Home went the twins down the mossy path: boing, boing... pause, look at the flower... boing.

---
