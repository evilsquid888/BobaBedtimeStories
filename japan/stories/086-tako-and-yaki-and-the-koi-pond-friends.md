# Story 86: Tako and Yaki and the Koi Pond Friends

**Main Character:** Tako & Yaki (takoyaki twins)
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A peaceful garden koi pond with shimmering carp
**Theme:** Calm friends can balance your bouncy energy
**Japanese Spotlight:** koi (koy) — carp

---

Now, you should know that Tako and Yaki had never, not once in their crispy little lives, walked anywhere. They *bounced*. They came as a pair — two round golden batter-balls joined by a wobbly wave of bonito flakes that fluttered like tiny dancing ribbons — and they bounced everywhere, finishing each other's sentences and high-fiving in midair.

"Roll us once—" bounced Tako.

"—we come as TWO!" bounced Yaki, and *boing*, they smacked their little hands together so hard a drizzle of sauce zig-zagged off the top of them.

This particular morning they had bounced clear out of the night market, down a mossy lane, through a round moon-gate, and straight into a garden so quiet they could hear their own bonito flakes flutter. There was a pond. A big, still, glassy pond. And the moment they saw it, they did what the twins always did.

They cannonballed.

"WHEEEEEEE—" went Tako.

"—BLOOP," finished Yaki, as the whole pond went *sploosh*.

---

When the ripples settled, two enormous, beautiful fish were staring at them. The fish were orange and white and shimmery, with long whiskers and tails like floating silk scarves. They blinked very, very slowly.

"Oh," said the bigger fish, in a voice as smooth and unhurried as honey sliding off a spoon. "Hello, little bouncy things. I am Aki. We are **koi** (koy) — carp." She drifted in a lazy half-circle. "And you have made our pond go *splash*."

"Sorrysorrysorry!" said Tako.

"We didn't mean to—" said Yaki.

"—we just LOVE—"

"—a good cannonball!"

The second koi, a smaller speckled fellow named Mizu, did a slow loop-the-loop that took roughly forever. "We do not cannonball," he said gently. "We *drift*. Watch."

And the twins watched. They watched the koi glide. They watched a leaf land on the water. They watched a dragonfly think very seriously about a lily pad. They watched a cloud move approximately one inch across the sky.

It was, the twins decided, the most boring thing they had ever seen in their entire lives.

"Okayokay we get it bye!" said Tako, and the twins bounced off to find something fun.

---

They bounced around the garden the way they bounced everywhere — fast and loud and gloriously. They raced the stepping stones. They booped a wind chime forty times. They startled a frog so badly he hiccupped. They challenged a stone lantern to a staring contest and lost, twice, because the lantern was a statue and they kept blinking from laughing.

They found a little arched bridge and bounced across it so many times the boards went *thump-thump-thump-thump* like a tiny drum solo. They found a patch of soft green **koke** (koh-keh) — moss and used it as a trampoline until a very surprised beetle asked them, quite politely, to please stop. They found an echo behind the garden wall and shouted "TAKOYAKI!" into it eleven times just to hear it shout back. They invented a brand-new game called Bounce-Tag-No-Tag-Backs-Octopus-Edition, which had no rules and somehow they both won.

It was, for a while, the most fun a garden had ever contained.

And then a funny thing happened. By lunchtime, the twins felt... wobbly. Not their normal happy wobble. A tired, dizzy, too-much-bouncing wobble. Their bonito flakes drooped. When Tako tried to high-five Yaki, he missed completely and bonked the wind chime instead.

"I feel—" started Tako.

"—weird," finished Yaki, and for the first time anyone could remember, neither of them bounced. They just sat. Two slightly dented golden balls in a garden, blinking.

"Maybe," said Tako quietly, "we bounced out all our bounce."

"That's not a real thing," said Yaki.

"It might be a real thing," said Tako.

A slow ripple crossed the pond. Aki the koi rose just enough for her gentle eyes to peek over the surface. "You look," she said, "like two soda bottles that have lost their fizz."

"We do NOT—" began the twins, and then they sighed, because they sort of did.

---

"Come into the water," said Aki. "Not a cannonball. Just... ease in. Like settling into a warm bath."

The twins looked at each other. Easing into anything was not in their nature. The twins had two speeds: *zoom* and *more zoom*. But they were so tired, and the water did look soft, so they wobbled to the edge and — very slowly, very carefully, biting their little lips with the effort of going slow — slipped in.

The pond held them up like a cool, gentle hand. They bobbed. They didn't have to do anything. The water did the bobbing *for* them.

"Oh," whispered Tako.

"Ohhh," whispered Yaki.

"This is the part," said Mizu, gliding past, "that we call... oh, what is the word." He thought about it for a slow, slow moment. "Not the excited word. The *other* one. This is **nonbiri** (nohn-bee-ree) — relaxed and easygoing." He smiled with his whole whiskery face. "Nonbiri is the best speed of all. It is the speed of clouds."

The twins floated. Above them the sky was a soft swirl of afternoon gold. A koi nosed gently at Yaki's bonito flake to see if it was a snack, decided it wasn't, and drifted off, unbothered. Somewhere a bamboo fountain went *tok... tok... tok*, slow as a sleepy heartbeat.

And the strangest, sweetest thing happened. The twins' fizz came back. Not the zooming kind. A warm, glowy, full-up-again kind, like a teakettle that's been refilled and set on the softest flame.

---

"How do you DO it?" Tako asked, paddling in a tiny lazy circle. "How do you go so slow all day and not get bored?"

Aki considered this for the length of a passing cloud. "We are never bored," she said. "When you go slow, you *see* things. The bouncy ones zoom right past the best parts." She tilted toward a lily pad. "Did you notice this flower opened today? It will only be open for three days. We watched it all morning."

The twins looked at the flower. Honestly, they had bounced past it eleven times and never once seen it. It was, they had to admit, extremely beautiful. Pink as a peach. Soft as Momo Mochi's cheek.

"We never see flowers," said Yaki.

"We see flowers go BY," said Tako, "really fast."

"That," said Mizu kindly, "is not the same as seeing them."

So the koi began to show the twins all the small slow things they'd been zooming past their whole bouncy lives. Mizu showed them a water-strider skating on the surface without ever falling in, its little feet making rings like tiny invisible plates. Aki showed them where a silver fish-shadow lived under the big flat rock, shy as Shiro the dango. She showed them the way the willow dipped its long green fingers in the water and stirred it ever so gently, like Maru Matcha whisking her tea.

"Watch the very middle of the pond," said Aki. "When the wind stops, it becomes a mirror. You can see the whole sky inside the water."

The twins watched. And sure enough, the wind held its breath, the ripples smoothed away, and there it was — the whole golden afternoon sky tucked inside the pond like a secret, with two round little takoyaki faces peeking down at it.

"There's a sky in the water," whispered Yaki, amazed.

"There's a sky in the water," whispered Tako, "and we almost missed it."

For two friends who could never normally agree on anything quietly, they found they agreed on this very quietly indeed: the small slow things were not boring at all. They had simply been moving too fast to notice that the slow things were the most magical of all.

---

So the twins stayed all afternoon. And here is the wonderful part — they didn't *stop* being bouncy. That would never work; bounce was simply what they were made of. Instead, they learned a marvelous secret: you can be bouncy AND calm. Not at the same instant, but like a song that has fast parts and slow parts, and both make the music better.

They taught the koi a game where everyone splashed once, all together, just for the joy of it — *SPLOOSH!* — and the koi laughed their slow, bubbly laughs and said it was the most exciting thing that had happened in the pond in years.

And the koi taught the twins to float on their backs and count clouds. The twins were terrible at it at first ("THAT cloud looks like a takoyaki—" "—EVERYTHING looks like a takoyaki to you—"), but slowly, slowly, they got the hang of going nowhere at all.

"You know," said Tako as the sky turned the color of warm peach syrup, "I think we needed slow friends."

"To balance out all our fast," agreed Yaki.

"Squish—no, that's Momo's line," said Tako.

"*Drift* together," offered Aki, "stay together?"

"DRIFT TOGETHER, STAY TOGETHER!" cheered the twins, and then, remembering, they whispered it instead. "...drift together, stay together." Much better. Much cozier.

They practiced, after that, all the rest of the golden afternoon. They learned how to do a *slow* high-five, which is exactly as silly as it sounds — two little hands inching toward each other through the air like sleepy snails, until at last they touched with the softest *pat* in the history of high-fives. They both burst out laughing, which made the pond ripple, which made the koi laugh, which made everything wobble and shine.

"That," declared Tako, "was the worst high-five we have ever done."

"And somehow," said Yaki, "the very best one."

And here is the thing the twins finally understood, floating there with the sky in the water and the slow friends all around: their bounciness was wonderful. It had always been wonderful. But a bounce without a rest is just a bounce that gets tired and bonks a wind chime. Their fizz needed a calm pond to come back to, the same way a kite needs both wind *and* a hand on the string. The koi weren't the opposite of the twins. The koi were the other half of a very good day.

---

When the first lantern-bugs began to glow, the twins climbed out of the pond, full of new fizz and old happiness all mixed together. They were still going to bounce home, of course. But they promised to bounce a little slower. And to stop, sometimes, and look at a flower. And to come back to the quiet garden whenever their fizz ran low, because now they knew exactly where to fill it up.

"Will you visit?" asked Mizu, his whiskers drooping just a little.

"Every time we get—" said Tako.

"—too zoomy for our own good," said Yaki.

Aki rose one last time, her orange scales catching the last of the gold light like tiny lanterns. "Then you will always have a pond," she said softly. "Slow friends keep a calm place ready for fast friends. That is what we are *for*."

And the twins bounced home through the cooling evening — *boing, boing, pause... look at that flower... boing* — two happy golden balls who had learned that the very best speed is the one right between zooming and drifting, where the whole world gets to be both exciting and peaceful at once.

---

## Goodnight Blessing
May your day have been full of bounces, little one, and may your night be soft as a koi drifting in a moonlit pond. When your thoughts feel too zoomy to settle, remember the warm water that holds you up so you don't have to do the holding. Breathe slow. Float easy. There is a calm place inside you, always ready, just like the koi keep a quiet pond ready for their bouncy friends. Tomorrow you can splash again. Tonight, simply drift. Goodnight.
