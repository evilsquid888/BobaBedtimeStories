# Story 45: Niko Nigiri and the Conveyor-Belt Sushi

**Main Character:** Niko Nigiri
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A spinning conveyor-belt (kaiten) sushi restaurant
**Theme:** Going with the flow can be the most fun of all
**Japanese Spotlight:** おすし (osushi) = sushi

---

Niko Nigiri arrived eleven minutes early, which was exactly as planned, because Niko Nigiri planned everything. He stood very straight, smoothing his glossy coral-orange salmon cape until it gleamed, and adjusted his neat little nori belt for the fourth time.

"Tonight," he announced to absolutely nobody, "we shall enjoy **おすし** (osushi) = sushi in a calm, orderly, sensible fashion."

Then the door slid open, and Niko's tidy little world went *whoosh*.

Inside spun the most marvelous, dizzying, marvelously-dizzying thing he had ever seen: a long looping belt, gliding round and round the whole restaurant, carrying tiny plates of glistening snacks on a gentle endless river. Plate after plate floated past — *swish, swish, swish* — never stopping, never pausing, never once asking permission.

Niko's bright attentive eyes went very wide.

"But — but where is the *menu*?" he gasped. "Where is the *order*? Where is the *list*?"

The belt simply hummed and kept on spinning, as belts do.

---

A round little plate-puck near the entrance lit up with a cheerful blinking smile. "Welcome to the Kaiten-ya!" it chirped. "Sit anywhere, sweet rice friend! The **お皿** (osara) = little plate boats come round and round. See one you like? Snatch it!"

"Snatch it?" Niko repeated, scandalized. "One does not *snatch* dinner. One requests it. Politely. In a queue."

"No queues here!" the plate-puck giggled. "Just the flow, the flow, the flow!"

Niko hopped onto a stool, sat up perfectly straight, and folded his little rice hands. He decided he would simply *watch* first. He would observe the system, understand the schedule, and select his food in a careful, dignified order. Surely there was a system. There was always a system.

A plate of plump pink shrimp drifted past. *Swish.*

"That one looks lovely," Niko thought. "But I shall wait for it to come around again, once I have analyzed the full rotation."

A plate of golden tamago egg floated by. *Swish.*

"Also nice. But first, a complete survey."

A wobbly tower of avocado roll glided past, wearing a tiny sesame hat. *Swish.* A cucumber roll. A salmon roll. A little dish of edamame that waved a cheerful bean at him.

*Swish, swish, swish.*

Niko nodded thoughtfully and made a list in his head. *Item one: shrimp. Item two: egg. Item three: possibly avocado, pending review.* He felt very proud. He felt very prepared.

He did not feel very full, because he had not eaten a single thing.

---

By the time the shrimp came back around, a fluffy tempura plate had cut in front of it, and Niko hesitated — and *swish* — they both sailed past before he could decide which one his list said came first.

"Bother," said Niko, who was far too polite to say anything stronger.

He waited for the next loop. This time he reached out — but he reached *so* carefully, smoothing his cape mid-reach to keep it neat, that *swish*, the plate slipped right under his fingers and away.

He waited again. He stretched. He paused to double-check the plate was on his approved list. *Swish.* Gone.

"This is most irregular," Niko muttered, his proud posture beginning to droop just a little at the edges, the way a rice ball does when it sits out too long. "The food will not *hold still*. How is one meant to eat **おすし** if it refuses to *wait its turn*?"

A wise old voice gurgled up from beside the belt. It was the wasabi pot — a round green fellow with a curl of steam for an eyebrow and a smile like a comfy frog.

"Little nigiri," said the wasabi pot kindly, "you have been here twenty minutes and eaten zero plates."

"I am *being organized*," said Niko, with great dignity, and a small grumble of an empty tummy.

"Mmm," said the wasabi pot. "Tell me — what happens to the plate you do *not* grab?"

"It... goes around again," Niko admitted.

"And the one after that?"

"Also comes around. Eventually."

"So." The wasabi pot's steam-eyebrow wiggled. "There is no *wrong* plate. There is no *late* plate. There is only the next one, and the next, and the next. You cannot fall behind on a circle, small friend. A circle has no end to fall behind on." He winked. "On the belt, the only mistake is forgetting to *enjoy it*."

Niko blinked his bright eyes.

He had never, in all his tidy life, considered that.

---

Just then, a plate came spinning round that Niko had never seen before — and oh, it was *strange*. A funny little gunkan boat of bright orange fish roe, each tiny egg glittering like a bead, topped with a single curl of something he couldn't name. It was not on his list. It was not in his plan. It was a complete and total surprise.

Niko's hand twitched.

"Go on," whispered the wasabi pot.

And Niko — straight-backed, proper, list-making Niko — did the most un-Niko thing he had ever done.

He *snatched* it.

The little plate came off the belt with a satisfying *clink*, and Niko held it up, and looked at it, and took a tiny careful nibble.

His coral cape *fluffed*.

"Oh," he breathed. "Oh, that is — that pops! Like tiny ocean fireworks! I did not plan that AT ALL and it is *delightful*!"

The wasabi pot laughed a warm gurgly laugh. "Surprise tastes best, doesn't it?"

"Let's do this neatly," Niko began out of habit — then he caught himself, and a brand-new, slightly silly grin spread across his rice. "...or *deliciously*!"

---

And then, dear reader, Niko Nigiri let go.

The next plate that floated past was a bright purple something with a wobble. He grabbed it. *Clink!* Sweet potato — squishy and warm! The next was a roll with a little flag of seaweed. *Clink!* Crunchy! The next was a mystery wrapped in a mystery sitting on a third mystery, and Niko didn't analyze it, didn't list it, didn't survey the rotation — he just laughed and *snatched* it right off the river.

"This one!" he cried. "And this one! And — ooh, what is THAT one?"

He wasn't choosing the right plate anymore. He was choosing the *next* plate, the *now* plate, the *wheeee* plate. His tidy stack of empty dishes grew into a wonderful wobbly tower beside him, taller and sillier with every loop.

A plate spun by carrying a single round cherry tomato, all by itself, looking very surprised to be there.

"YOU TOO!" said Niko joyfully, and snatched the tomato.

The plate-puck near the door rolled over, blinking with delight. "Look at you go, neat one! You've gone with the flow!"

"I have!" Niko beamed, a little rice grain stuck happily to his cheek. "Do you know, I think the *flow* is the system. The flow was the plan all along! I simply could not see it because I was too busy making a different one."

"That," said the wasabi pot warmly, "is the wisest thing a nigiri has said all night."

---

When at last Niko's tummy was round and happy and his tower of plates leaned like a cheerful little tower of Pisa, he climbed down from his stool and patted the belt fondly, the way you'd pat a friendly dog.

"Thank you, river of **おすし**," he said. "You taught me something my lists never could."

The belt hummed and *swished* on, carrying a fresh parade of surprises round to the next hungry friend.

And Niko — who had arrived eleven minutes early, perfectly pressed, and absolutely certain he knew the right way to eat dinner — walked out eleven plates fuller, one rice grain messier, and a whole great deal happier than he came in.

He still loved a good plan. He always would.

But now he knew a secret: the very best plans leave a little room for *swish*.

## Goodnight Blessing
Sleepy one, you don't have to have everything in order to be happy tonight. Some of the sweetest surprises come floating by when you stop holding on so tight and simply let the gentle river carry you. Let your worries spin off down the belt; you don't need to catch a single one. Snuggle in, soft and warm, and let tomorrow bring whatever delicious little plate it brings. Whisk slow, breathe easy, and go with the cozy flow. Goodnight.
