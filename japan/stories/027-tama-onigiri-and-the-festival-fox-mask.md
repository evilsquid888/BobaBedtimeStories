# Story 27: Tama Onigiri and the Festival Fox Mask

**Main Character:** Tama Onigiri
**Guest Characters:** Konko, a shy little dumpling in a fox mask; a firefly
**Setting:** A summer night matsuri with food stalls and lantern stands
**Theme:** Sharing a snack turns strangers into friends
**Japanese Spotlight:** **matsuri** (mah-tsoo-ree) — festival

---

Down the hill toward the river bounced a plump triangle of rice with a backpack that jingled.

The backpack was not full of toys. Oh no. It was full of snacks.

"I packed a little extra, just in case!" hummed Tama Onigiri, patting it.

A firefly bonked her right on the nose. "Oh! Hello," said Tama. "Are you lost too?"

It blinked twice, which Tama decided meant yes, but happily, and together they went into the glow.

"Somewhere down there," said Tama, "is a stranger who needs a rice ball. I can feel it."

Lanterns hung like a hundred glowing tangerines. Tonight was the **matsuri** (mah-tsoo-ree) — festival.

---

Tama bumped a candy-apple cart, apologized to the cart, then apologized to the apple, just to be safe.

Then she saw it. Behind the goldfish stall, somebody small sat alone in the blue shadow.

They wore a **kitsune** (kee-tsoo-neh) — fox — mask, white with red swirls. Their toes tapped a worried tippy-tap.

"That's a tummy-rumble feeling," said Tama. "And I happen to be an expert at tummy-rumble feelings."

---

"I'm Tama. I'm a rice ball," she said, plopping down. "The plum is real. The worry is optional."

"Everybody already has a friend," whispered the fox mask. "What if I do the games wrong?"

Tama unzipped her backpack. Steam puffed out. "Big feelings shrink once your tummy is happy. It's science. Probably."

Two careful paws took the rice ball. The mask tipped up a smidge. A nibble.

"Salty-soft," came the tiny voice. "Warm. And there's a surprise plum in the middle!"

"I'm Konko," said the fox mask, a little louder. "With the mask on, nobody sees me being shy."

---

WHOOSH. A river wind swooped under the mask and lifted it clean off Konko's face.

Up it sailed, grinning its fox grin, over the goldfish stall and into the lanterns.

"MY MASK!" squeaked Konko, both paws over a round, pink-cheeked, very surprised face.

"Backpack on," said Tama. "We're going to get it. Squish close, we go together."

---

The mask landed on the cotton-candy stall, on a pink sugar cloud bigger than both of them.

Tama leapt for it, face first. The mask flew on. Tama came out wearing a sticky pink beard.

She stroked it thoughtfully. "I'm a wise old grandfather now. Also delicious."

Konko giggled, a real giggle, then clapped both paws over it in surprise.

---

The mask hopped to the ring-toss stall. Ring! A hoop landed neatly around one pointy ear.

Tama tossed a ring. It landed on her own head. Her sash went crooked.

Konko tossed one, soft and slow, like scooping a sleepy kitten. It hooked the mask.

The wind yanked harder. Off went mask AND hoop, straight toward the big drum tower.

---

The mask landed flat on the great drum. DON. It bounced. DON. It bounced higher.

"It's dancing," said Tama. "Your mask is a better dancer than me."

Konko looked up. Every lantern-lit face in the festival was looking at that drum.

Toes tapped once. Then Konko climbed, round and quick, straight up the drum tower's ladder.

DON. The mask leapt. Konko leapt. They met in midair, and Konko came down hugging it.

Konko looked at the mask, then at all the faces, and perched it on top of their head.

"I like both your faces," said Tama. "But this one shares snacks better."

---

They split a shaved ice, blue syrup one side, strawberry the other, and swapped bites.

They compared rainbow tongues under the lanterns. The firefly compared too. It only had the one color.

"Thank you for the rice ball," said Konko. "Only it wasn't really about the rice ball."

"A snack is never just a snack," said Tama. "It says: here, there's room for you too."

The drum slowed to a sleepy heartbeat. The firefly blinked twice. Family now.

---
