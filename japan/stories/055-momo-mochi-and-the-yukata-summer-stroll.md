# Story 55: Momo Mochi and the Yukata Summer Stroll

**Main Character:** Momo Mochi (mochi rice cake / daifuku)
**Guest Characters:** Auntie Kinako, the kindly bean-powder shop auntie; Mame, a tiny green pea-cake; Anzu, a dignified old apricot who sells candied plums
**Setting:** A summer evening river festival in Kyoto
**Theme:** Comfort and beauty can go hand in hand
**Japanese Spotlight:** **yukata** (yoo-kah-tah) — summer cotton kimono

---

Momo Mochi had a small squishy problem: she could not stop wiggling.

"Hold STILL, little peach," laughed Auntie Kinako, the round bean-powder auntie, wrapping her in cotton.

It was a **yukata** (yoo-kah-tah) — summer cotton kimono, evening-sky blue, sprinkled with tiny painted goldfish.

"Keep bouncing," said Auntie Kinako, "and the goldfish will swim right off."

"It's SOFT," breathed Momo, patting a sleeve. "So soft I could nap in it forever."

"But is it pretty enough?" said Momo. "For the festival? I want to look festival-pretty."

Auntie Kinako winked, tied the last bow, and nudged her out the door.

---

Kyoto's river festival had unrolled itself like a ribbon of light along the water.

Paper lanterns glowed apricot and rose in the warm dusk. Stalls steamed. Somewhere, a flute.

Everywhere, treat-folk strolled in beautiful yukata, tall and graceful. "Comfy isn't fancy," Momo whispered. "No bouncing."

She lasted four seconds. Mochi is not built for tall. Momo wobbled, sproinged, and bounced.

"Sorry, pardon, BOING!" She ricocheted off a candied-plum stall and its keeper, an old apricot.

"Festivals," muttered Anzu the apricot, catching his plums, "were calmer before the dessert learned to bounce."

---

Behind a stall of spinning paper windmills came a very small, very sad sniffle.

There sat Mame, a round green pea-cake no bigger than a thumb, in a leaf-green yukata.

Her **obi** (oh-bee) — sash had come undone and knotted itself into the shape of a confused snail.

"I look like a laundry pile that fell downstairs," hiccuped Mame. "I'm going home. Under a leaf."

"Nobody hides under a leaf at the best festival of summer," said Momo, plopping down. Floomp.

---

Momo tugged. She tucked. She looped the sash the fancy way, like the graceful strollers.

The obi slipped. It sagged. Fwump. It pooled around Mame's feet like a sad green puddle.

Momo pulled it crisp and tight and proper. "Too tight!" squeaked Mame. "I can't bend! I'm a STICK!"

She tipped over like a plank. Momo caught her, bounced, and they tumbled into the windmill stall.

Paper windmills whirled everywhere. Momo surfaced wearing three. A cricket chirped: well, that didn't work either.

---

Then a memory floated up, warm as steam. At home, blankets were tucked snug, never squeezed.

"Comfort first," Momo murmured. "Pretty comes along for the ride. Mame, I'm a COZY kind of mochi."

She hugged the sash around Mame like tucking in a friend. Snug but soft. Room to bounce.

Where it slipped, a sticky mochi paw pressed it still. Mochi is sticky. Sometimes that's a superpower.

At the back she folded a round pillowy bow, like a cloud, or a sleeping bunny.

---

Mame peered into the dark shine of the river and gasped. The cloud-bow glowed like a tiny moon.

"It's the nicest I've ever looked," whispered Mame. "And it doesn't even pinch!"

"Comfort and beauty aren't taking turns," said Momo. "They're holding paws."

Then Momo looked along the riverbank and saw it. Everyone was tugging. Pinching. Sagging.

"Wobbly sash? Pinchy obi?" she called, pushing up her goldfish sleeves. "The cozy way is open for business!"

A line formed. A flustered croquette. Twins knotted together, shuffling like one crab. A bun dozing upright.

Momo tied them all, snug-not-squeezy, and soon the riverbank wore a sky of gentle little moons.

---

Last came Anzu the apricot, clearing his throat. "My obi has been slightly imperfect all evening. Ahem."

Momo tied him a cloud-bow. Anzu studied his reflection, then gave a slow, creaky, respectful bow.

"I was wrong, small bouncing dessert," said Anzu. "Less calm. Far more comfortable. Far more lovely."

---

Then **hanabi** (hah-nah-bee) — fireworks bloomed over the river, soft golds and dreamy pinks, slow as flowers.

Momo flopped onto the cool grass, snug in her goldfish yukata, and went gloriously soft.

Mame leaned against her squishy side, cloud-bow rising and falling with each sleepy breath.

"Soft outside," yawned Momo. "Soft inside. Pretty all the way through."
