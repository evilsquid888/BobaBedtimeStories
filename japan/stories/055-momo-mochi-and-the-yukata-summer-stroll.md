# Story 55: Momo Mochi and the Yukata Summer Stroll

**Main Character:** Momo Mochi (mochi rice cake / daifuku)
**Guest Characters:** Auntie Kinako, the kindly bean-powder shop auntie; Mame, a tiny green pea-cake; Anzu, a dignified old apricot who sells candied plums
**Setting:** A summer evening river festival in Kyoto
**Theme:** Comfort and beauty can go hand in hand
**Japanese Spotlight:** **yukata** (yoo-kah-tah) — summer cotton kimono
**Cameo:** Rimuru, Milim, Ranga and Gobta (That Time I Got Reincarnated as a Slime) — a polite slime rides in on a giant wolf stuffed with snacks, the goblin drops them all, and the tiny demon lord claims Momo's goldfish sash as a prize, stretching Momo clear across the riverbank; walk-on: the Chiikawa trio (Chiikawa)

---

Momo Mochi had a small squishy problem: she could not stop wiggling.

"Hold STILL, little peach," laughed Auntie Kinako, the bean-powder auntie, wrapping her in cotton.

It was a **yukata** (yoo-kah-tah) — summer cotton kimono, sky-blue, sprinkled with tiny painted goldfish.

"Keep bouncing," said Auntie Kinako, "and the goldfish will swim right off."

"It's SOFT," breathed Momo, patting a sleeve. "But is it pretty enough? I want festival-pretty."

Auntie Kinako winked, tied the last bow, and nudged her out the door.

---

Kyoto's river festival had unrolled like a ribbon of lantern light along the water.

By a candy stall the Chiikawa trio, white blob, yellow cat, blue-grey rabbit, squeaked in a row.

Everywhere, treat-folk strolled in graceful yukata, tall and still. "No bouncing," Momo whispered.

She lasted four seconds. Mochi isn't built for tall. Wobble, sproing, BOUNCE.

"Sorry, pardon, BOING!" She ricocheted off a candied-plum stall and its keeper, an old apricot.

"Festivals," muttered Anzu the apricot, catching plums, "were calmer before dessert learned to bounce."

---

Behind a stall of spinning paper windmills came a very small, very sad sniffle.

There sat Mame, a green pea-cake no bigger than a thumb, in a leaf-green yukata.

Her **obi** (oh-bee) — sash had knotted itself into the shape of a confused snail.

"I look like a laundry pile that fell downstairs," hiccuped Mame. "I'm going home. Under a leaf."

"Nobody hides under a leaf at summer's best festival," said Momo, plopping down. Floomp.

---

Momo tugged. She tucked. She looped the sash the fancy way, like the tall strollers.

The obi slipped, sagged, fwump, and pooled around Mame's feet like a sad green puddle.

Momo pulled it crisp and tight and proper. "Too tight!" squeaked Mame. "I can't bend! I'm a STICK!"

She tipped like a plank. Momo caught her, bounced, and they tumbled into the windmill stall.

Windmills whirled everywhere. Momo surfaced wearing three. A cricket chirped: that didn't work either.

---

Then up trotted Ranga, a giant black wolf with a star on his forehead, mouth stuffed with snacks.

On his back bounced a glossy sky-blue slime. "Good evening," said Rimuru politely. "Excuse my friends."

"SNACKS! ALL OF THEM!" shrieked Milim, a tiny pink-twin-tailed demon lord, as Gobta the goblin dropped them all.

Milim spotted Momo's goldfish sash. "OOH. PRIZE!" She grabbed the bow and pulled.

Momo stretched. And stretched. Mochi stretches. She became a white ribbon across the riverbank.

"Milim, no," sighed Rimuru. One bound later the party posed on the far bridge, Milim waving the sash.

---

TWANG. Momo snapped back like a rubber band, sashless, yukata flapping open like a tent.

"Well," said Momo, "now we're BOTH laundry piles." Mame's hiccups turned into giggles.

"Comfort first," Momo murmured, remembering tucked-in blankets. "Pretty comes along for the ride."

She hugged the sash around Mame like tucking in a friend. Snug, soft, room to bounce.

Where it slipped, a sticky mochi paw pressed it still. Sticky is a superpower.

At the back she folded a pillowy bow, like a cloud, or a sleeping bunny.

---

"The nicest I've ever looked," whispered Mame, cloud-bow glowing like a tiny moon. "It doesn't pinch!"

Anzu shuffled up with a strip of plum-colored cloth. "For the bouncing dessert. Ahem."

Momo tied herself a cloud-bow, snug-not-squeezy. "Comfort and beauty aren't taking turns. They're holding paws."

Along the riverbank everyone tugged and pinched. "The cozy way is open for business!" called Momo.

A line formed: a flustered croquette, crab-knotted twins, the squeaking Chiikawa trio. Momo tied them all.

Anzu studied his cloud-bow in the river and gave a slow, creaky bow. "Far more lovely."

---

Then **hanabi** (hah-nah-bee) — fireworks bloomed over a riverbank of gentle little cloud-bow moons.

Momo flopped onto the cool grass and went gloriously soft.

Mame leaned against her squishy side, cloud-bow rising and falling with each sleepy breath.

"Soft outside," yawned Momo. "Soft inside. Pretty all the way through."
