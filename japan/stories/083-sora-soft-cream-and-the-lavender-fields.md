# Story 83: Sora Soft-Cream and the Lavender Fields

**Main Character:** Sora the Soft-Cream
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** The rolling purple lavender fields of Furano, Hokkaido
**Theme:** Slow days are full of quiet color
**Japanese Spotlight:** はな (hana) = flower

---

On the slowest, sunniest afternoon of the whole summer, Sora the Soft-Cream stood at the top of a gentle hill in Furano and did absolutely nothing at all — and it was glorious.

Below her, the lavender stretched on forever. Row after row after row of soft purple, rolling over the hills like a blanket somebody had ironed with sunshine. Bees hummed. A breeze wandered by, sniffed Sora, and wandered off again, satisfied.

"Sora!" called a tiny voice from far down the hill. "SORA! You've been standing there for an HOUR!"

Sora's pale-gold swirl tilted, just slightly, like a cat opening one eye. A little drip-curl wobbled on top of her head.

"Mm," said Sora dreamily. "Has it been an hour? It felt like one very nice breath."

And that, dear reader, is the trouble with Sora. She does not hurry. She has never hurried. If hurrying were a hat, Sora would have left it at home, on purpose, with a little note that said *back soon — slowly.*

---

The tiny voice belonged to Pock Ramune, the frosty pale-blue soda bottle, who came fizzing up the hill leaving a trail of happy bubbles behind him. *Pop! Pop-pop!*

"Sora, we have to GO," Pock said, zipping in a circle around her cone. "Everybody's waiting at the bottom! Momo and Rin and the Dango Three and EVERYBODY! We planned a whole afternoon! There's a list! It has SEVEN things on it! *Pop!*"

"Seven," Sora repeated, in the gentle, melty way she said most things. "That's a lot of things for an afternoon. Afternoons are usually only big enough for two or three."

"This one's EXTRA big!" Pock buzzed. "Come ON, slow swirl, before you melt right into the dirt!"

So Sora, who never argued because arguing was faster than she liked to move, drifted down the hill after the fizzing little bottle. She didn't walk so much as *flow*, the way soft-serve flows when you tip the cone — a slow, smiling slide.

At the bottom, by a crooked wooden fence, the whole gang was indeed waiting. Momo Mochi bounced on her toes, soft and snow-white. Rin Ramen steamed faintly with excitement, golden broth sloshing. And the Dango Three — Pinku, Shiro, and Cha — wobbled on their stick like a tiny purple-stained carousel, because Pinku had already been rolling through the flowers.

"FINALLY!" said Pinku, the bold pink one. "Okay! Plan time! We are going to —"

"— see the lavender maze," squeaked Shiro, the shy white one.

"— ride the flower train, photograph the postcard hill, buy lavender soft-cream, find the giant scarecrow, race to the windmill, AND be home before the sun sets," finished Cha, the clever green one, all in one neat clever breath.

Rin clapped his stubby bowl-hands. "A perfect plan! Everything's better warm and bubbly — and BUSY!"

Sora looked at the seven-thing list. Then she looked at the lavender, which was doing something very interesting, which was nothing.

"That's a wonderful plan," Sora said softly. "But — has anybody actually *looked* at a flower yet?"

Everybody stopped.

"Looked at — a flower?" said Pinku.

"There are nine MILLION flowers," said Cha. "Looking at one would take all day."

"That," said Sora, with the tiniest dreamy smile, "is exactly the right amount of time."

---

But nobody was listening, because Pock had already fizzed off toward the lavender maze, and everyone tumbled after him in a giggling clump.

The maze was tall green-and-purple hedges, twistier than a noodle and twice as confusing. The gang charged in, certain, fast, and absolutely sure they knew the way.

Three minutes later they were extremely, hopelessly lost.

"Left!" said Pinku.

"Right!" said Shiro.

"We came from THERE," said Cha, pointing at a wall.

Rin's steam puffed faster, which it always did when he was flustered. "Oh no, oh no, we're going to be stuck in here until WINTER!"

And then, from somewhere behind them, came the slow, soft voice of Sora, who had not run at all and had only just now wandered in.

"You're going the wrong way," she said gently. "Follow the bees."

"Follow the — *what?*" said Pinku.

Sora pointed one melty arm. A fat, fuzzy honeybee bumbled past, heavy with pollen, humming a sleepy little song. "Bees always know the way out," she said. "They're going home to make honey. And home is always toward the open sky."

So the gang, very doubtfully, followed the bee.

It floated lazily over one hedge, then bumbled along another, in no rush at all, stopping now and then to dip into a flower and have a little snack. The gang had to slow right down to keep behind it, tiptoeing through the purple, and a funny thing happened: when they slowed down, they stopped feeling lost. They started feeling *found.*

"It's not in a hurry," whispered Momo.

"Bees are never in a hurry," said Sora. "And they never get lost, either. Funny how those two things go together."

The bee floated over the last hedge and then *out* — right through the maze's exit, into the wide gold afternoon. The whole gang spilled into the sunshine, blinking and laughing and amazed.

"How did you KNOW that?" gasped Momo.

Sora shrugged, which on a soft-serve looks like a gentle little wobble. "I wasn't running, so I had time to watch the bees. When you go slow," she said, "you notice the things that already know the way."

---

The next thing on Cha's list was the flower train — a teeny open-air sightseeing train that chugged through the fields. The gang scrambled aboard and immediately pressed their faces to the front, watching the track zoom by, faster, faster.

"Look how FAST!" cheered Pock. *Pop!*

"Look how PRETTY!" cheered Pinku.

But the faster the train went, the more the flowers blurred — purple smearing into purple into purple, until the whole world looked like one long grape smudge.

Sora sat at the very back, facing the other way. And while everyone else watched the future rushing toward them, Sora watched the flowers *they'd already passed* drift slowly away, each one perfectly still, perfectly clear.

"Sora, you're facing the wrong way!" laughed Momo. "You're missing it!"

"Mm," said Sora. "I think *you're* missing it. Come sit by me."

Momo, who could never resist a cozy invitation, squished down beside her. And there, looking backward at the slow part instead of forward at the fast part, Momo saw it: a single perfect **はな** (hana) = flower, deep velvet purple, with a tiny bee curled asleep inside it like a fuzzy little bean in a tiny bed.

Momo gasped. "Oh," she whispered. "*Oh.* It's so... small. And so... *complete.*"

And it was. Up close, the little flower wasn't just purple — it was a hundred purples at once. Deep grape-purple at the bottom, soft lilac at the tip, a tiny stripe of silvery-blue down each petal where the light slid along it. There was a whole tiny world in there, and the sleepy bee was its only, lucky, slumbering citizen.

"I've passed a thousand of these today," Momo breathed. "And I never saw even one."

"That's the secret nobody tells you," said Sora. "You can't *rush past* something and *see* it. You have to let it catch up to you. Or — " she smiled her melty smile " — you have to slow down enough that it can."

"Squish together, stay together," Sora murmured, doing her best Momo impression, "and slow down, see together."

Momo giggled and squished closer. One by one, the others noticed the two of them being so happy at the slow end, and one by one they came and sat down too, facing backward, watching the flowers stay still — until the whole train was full of dessert friends going forward but *looking back*, and seeing absolutely everything.

---

By the time they reached the photograph hill, something had changed in the gang. Nobody charged. Nobody rushed.

Rin lay down in the lavender and let it tickle his noodle-curl hair. "It smells," he announced grandly, "like a warm bath made of *clouds.*" His steam, which usually puffed fast when he was excited, drifted up in slow, lazy curls instead, the way smoke rises from a cozy chimney on a still night.

"It sounds like the field is *humming a lullaby,*" said Shiro, who never said much, listening to the thousand soft bees. "I never noticed because I was always talking over it."

Pock, the zippy little bottle, tried very hard to lie still and managed it for almost four whole seconds before fizzing one tiny *pop.* "It's hard," he admitted, "but it's *nice.* Like the field is fizzing very, very slowly, and so am I."

Cha — clever Cha, who loved his list — pulled it out and looked at it. Five things left. The flower train. The postcard hill. The lavender soft-cream. The scarecrow. The windmill. The race. Home before sunset.

He looked at the list. He looked at the sky, which was turning the soft pink-gold of late, slow afternoon. He looked at his friends, who were lying in the flowers, perfectly, completely happy.

And clever Cha did the cleverest thing he had ever done.

He folded up the list and put it away.

"New plan," Cha announced. "One thing. We're going to lie here and watch the sun go down over the purple."

"But — the windmill —" Pinku started.

"The windmill," said Cha gently, "will still be a windmill tomorrow. Today the *light* is only doing this *now.*"

Pinku looked at the gold light pouring sideways through the lavender, turning every flower into a tiny purple lantern. She opened her mouth to argue — bold pink dumplings almost always do — and then she closed it again.

"Okay," she said quietly. "But only because the windmill would look *better* tomorrow anyway. With morning light. Obviously. I'm not doing this because *you're* right."

"Of course not," said Cha, with a clever little smile.

"I'm doing it because *I'm* right," Pinku declared, and lay down in the flowers with great dignity, which lasted until a bee landed on her nose and she giggled so hard she rolled clean off the stick. Shiro and Cha had to roll after her, and then all three Dango were laughing in a purple pile, and that was that.

---

So the whole gang lay in the lavender of Furano while the slow afternoon turned into a slower evening.

Sora drifted among them, dripping just a tiny bit at the edges, the way she always did, the way she didn't mind at all. She'd lost a little of her swirl to the warm day — but she'd gained a whole field of friends who finally understood her.

"Aren't you sad you're melting?" Momo asked softly.

"Not even a drop's worth," said Sora. "A soft-cream that never melts is just a statue. Melting's what happens when you've stayed somewhere lovely long enough. I'd melt all the way down for an afternoon like this and call it the best trade I ever made." She gave a slow, happy wobble. "Besides — they sell more soft-cream at the bottom of the hill. I'm allowed to come back tomorrow."

"You know," said Rin softly, "I always thought a good day had to be *full.* Lots of things. Big and bubbly."

"It can be," Sora said. "But sometimes a good day is just *deep.* One flower. One bee. One sky. Looked at long enough to really see."

A breeze wandered by again, sniffed the whole sleepy gang, and decided to stay this time, rocking the lavender like a slow purple sea.

Pock, who had not stopped moving since the day he was bottled, finally went still. Just one small *pop* — soft as a goodnight kiss — and then he floated, quiet, in the warm.

"Slow swirls," Sora whispered, watching the sun melt like soft-cream into the hills, "slow smiles."

And every single flower, all nine million of them, turned the deepest, dreamiest purple, as if the whole field had quietly fallen asleep before they did.

## Goodnight Blessing

May your busy days have a slow, soft end, little one, like a long afternoon melting gently into evening. May you remember that one flower, looked at kindly, holds more wonder than a thousand things rushed past. Tonight, let your list of seven things drift away on a warm purple breeze, and keep only this: you are here, you are loved, and the day was enough. Breathe slow. Smile soft. Goodnight.
