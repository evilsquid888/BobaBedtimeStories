# Story 90: The Sushi-ya Express and the Touge Sunrise

**Main Character:** The Sushi-ya Express (sushi food truck)
**Guest Characters:** Niko Nigiri, Rin Ramen, Kei the kei-truck
**Setting:** A winding mountain pass (touge) at dawn
**Theme:** The joy is the journey you share
**Japanese Spotlight:** **toge** (toh-geh) — mountain pass
**Cameo:** the tofu-shop AE86 and Takumi (Initial D) — drifts uphill through the fog to galloping music with the tofu box perfectly still, spooks the runaway tire down a shortcut, and parks sideways on a ledge; walk-on: Turbo Granny (Dandadan)

---

Long before the sun even thought about getting up, the Sushi-ya Express was humming in the dark.

Ting-ting-ting! "Wake up, sleepy sushi! We have a sunrise to catch!"

"This is technically still yesterday," mumbled Niko Nigiri, straightening his salmon cape.

"Is anything better warm and bubbly at four in the morning?" sighed Rin Ramen, steam curling.

"The view is!" said the Sushi-ya Express. "We're climbing the **toge** (toh-geh) — mountain pass!"

"Fresh sushi at sunrise, on the very top, for the little tea house. Nobody ever has."

"Putt-putt, let's GO!" Kei bounced up in front. "I know every wiggle of this road!"

---

The road climbed. And climbed. Then it wiggled, left-right-left, like a noodle around a bowl.

"Ooh," said Rin, sloshing. "I feel like soup being stirred."

Then **kiri** (kee-ree) — fog — poured over the road, and Kei's taillights faded to milky white.

"We're behind schedule," fretted Niko, clutching his tidy plan-paper. "The sun will not wait for fog."

---

Clunk-a-clunk-CLUNK. Up ahead, the beeps went putt... putt... pffft.

Kei limped onto a wide bend and sagged. "A rock bonked me. I've got a flat."

"Fall down seven, bounce up eight!" boomed Niko in his best coach-voice, which worked surprisingly well.

The spare tire hung under the Sushi-ya Express. Rin unbuckled it with a steamy grunt.

It dropped, bounced once, and rolled away downhill into the fog.

---

"After it!" The Sushi-ya Express swung around, lanterns blazing, and the chase was on.

The tire took the first hairpin like a champion. Rin's broth sloshed up his left side.

Niko leaned out the window and lassoed with his nori belt. He caught a pine cone.

Rin swung a steaming ladle out the other window. The tire rolled through the steam, unbothered.

Then, from somewhere below, came music: a thumping, galloping, synth-happy beat, getting louder.

---

Headlights popped up in the fog. A boxy white hatchback with a black hood drifted UPHILL, sideways.

At the wheel, Takumi, a teen in a plain white shirt, steered with one sleepy arm.

In the back rode a wooden tofu box. The car slid like soap. The box stayed perfectly still.

The AE86 swept past so close the tire hopped, spooked, and bounced off down a shortcut.

"Oh. Sorry. Tofu delivery," said Takumi flatly, drifting past the Express the other way.

The AE86 slid onto a misty ledge above and stopped sideways, headlights blinking twice. Takumi yawned.

---

"Shortcut!" said Rin. The Express bumped down a grassy path after the tire, broth flying.

Something tiny overtook them on all fours, cackling: Turbo Granny, white hair streaming, eyes glowing.

Turbo Granny lapped the tire twice, cackled, and perched on a stone marker, watching.

The road curled back uphill, and the tire slowed... slowed... and wobbled to a stop.

Right against Kei's bumper. "Told you I know this road," said Kei, beeping along to the faraway beat.

---

They got to work. Rin warmed the stiff bolts with puffs of broth-steam.

Niko lined up the tools in tidy rows. "Wrench... please... spare... thank you."

Sproing! Kei bounced up on four good tires. "Better than new!"

Niko looked at his plan-paper. Then he folded it up and tucked it under his cape.

---

The fog thinned and turned pink. The trees fell away. The valley opened below like a green bowl.

"Counter down! Lanterns up!" laughed Niko. "Breakfast is served right HERE."

"**Itadakimasu** (ee-tah-dah-kee-mahs) — let's eat!" sang the Sushi-ya Express, as gold peeked over the peaks.

---

Around the next bend, a tiny tea house blinked its round window on, like a yawn.

"I thought the sunrise was the point," said Niko. "It wasn't, was it?"

"The joy," said the Sushi-ya Express softly, "is the journey you share."

Kei dozed on the bend, four good tires, headlights half-closed, a warm rice ball on her hood.

---
