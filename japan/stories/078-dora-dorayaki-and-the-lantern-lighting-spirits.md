# Story 78: Dora Dorayaki and the Lantern-Lighting Spirits

**Main Character:** Dora the Dorayaki
**Guest Characters:** Three tiny lantern-lighting spirits; a chorus of riverbank frogs
**Setting:** A summer Obon evening with floating paper lanterns on a gentle river
**Theme:** Remembering someone keeps their warmth glowing
**Japanese Spotlight:** **chochin** (choh-cheen) — paper lantern

---

On the warmest, hushiest evening of summer, Dora the Dorayaki sat on the riverbank reading to nobody.

"And then," he announced to the frogs, "the brave little dumpling discovered the treasure was friendship."

One frog burped. Dora chose to take it as applause. He took a tiny bow.

It was **Obon** (oh-bohn) — the summer evening when warm memories come to visit.

Down by the reeds, three spirits no bigger than rice grains bobbed in worried circles, glowing gold.

"We've lost our **chochin** (choh-cheen) — paper lanterns," chimed the first. "Without them we can't float home."

"A problem with a story-sized solution," said Dora. "Three lanterns before the river-song ends. Let's begin!"

---

The first lantern hung snagged in the reeds, soggy and folded shut like a sad fan.

Dora tugged, heaved, PULLED. The lantern came loose, and he tumbled backward down the bank, fwump.

"That," he announced from upside down, beret over one eye, "was on purpose."

The smallest spirit fluttered inside. The lantern went fffp. River water dribbled out, then a snail.

"Well," said Dora. "That's a no from the snail too."

---

The second lantern lay wedged under a stone, dry and crinkly-new. "Aha!" cried Dora.

The first spirit zipped in. The lantern stayed dark. The spirit drooped like a cold noodle.

"A chochin doesn't light from a candle," whispered the spirit. "It lights from a warm memory."

"And we floated so far," sniffled the second, "we forgot ours. Every single one."

"Lantern three first," said Dora. "Where did you see it last?" Three tiny glows pointed straight up.

---

High over the water, the third lantern dangled from a willow branch, swinging in the breeze.

Dora climbed. Pancakes are not built for climbing. He climbed anyway, beret first.

Out along the branch he wobbled. The branch bent. Dora dangled over the dark river.

His beret dropped. A frog caught it, wore it, and looked extremely distinguished.

Dora stretched one flap, snagged the lantern, and the branch sprang back with a BOING.

He landed in the shallows, splash, holding the lantern high and dry. "Also on purpose."

---

On the bank, Dora sat very seriously. "Memories are stories that already happened. What's your warmest one?"

"A dumpling-auntie," said the first spirit slowly. "She made dumplings every summer. She let me lick the bowl."

"She called me her sweetest snack," it whispered, and burst into light like a tiny sunrise.

"Into the lantern!" cried Dora. FOOM. The chochin lit up, swaying like a little floating moon.

"Mine!" said the second. "A cat named Mochi who slept on my feet and purred like an engine."

"A cat named Mochi," said Dora, pressing a flap to his cheek. "Excellent taste in names."

The second spirit shone sunset-pink, lit its lantern, and bonked Dora gently on the head.

---

The third spirit hid behind Dora. "I don't have one. I've forgotten too much."

Dora's red-bean heart went squeeze. "Close your eyes," he said softly. "What does home smell like?"

"Tea," said the spirit. "Rain on a roof. And someone reading to me. Every night."

"Why," said Dora, voice wobbling, "that sounds just like me."

"It IS you!" laughed the spirit, looping his head. "You've been someone's bedtime story all along!"

It blazed honey-gold, the exact color of two pancakes hugging red bean, and lit the last lantern.

Dora dabbed his eyes with his beret. He was running out of dry beret.

---

One by one, gentle as a goodnight kiss, Dora set the three lanterns on the dark water.

They found the current and drifted, three soft golden lights gliding away under the summer stars.

"As long as someone remembers us," chimed the spirits, "we keep glowing."

"I'll keep your story," said Dora, waving both flaps. "Story-keepers always do."

The frogs were already half asleep. One still wore the beret.

---
