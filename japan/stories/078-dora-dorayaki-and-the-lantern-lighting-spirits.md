# Story 78: Dora Dorayaki and the Lantern-Lighting Spirits

**Main Character:** Dora the Dorayaki
**Guest Characters:** Three tiny lantern-lighting spirits; a chorus of riverbank frogs
**Setting:** A summer Obon evening with floating paper lanterns on a gentle river
**Theme:** Remembering someone keeps their warmth glowing
**Japanese Spotlight:** **chochin** (choh-cheen) — paper lantern
**Cameo:** Saitou, Raelza, Morlock and Lafanpan (Handyman Saitou in Another World) — Saitou fixes the tipping lantern post with one turn of a screwdriver, Morlock forgets the light spell halfway and lights his own beard, Lafanpan charges the frogs a coin for watching, Raelza holds a ladder and glares at the river; walk-on: Litwick (Pokémon)

---

On the hushiest evening of summer, Dora the Dorayaki sat on the riverbank telling a story to nobody.

"And then," he told the frogs, "the brave dumpling discovered the treasure was friendship."

One frog burped. Dora took it as applause and bowed.

It was **Obon** (oh-bohn) — the summer evening when warm memories come to visit.

By the reeds, three spirits no bigger than rice grains bobbed in worried circles.

"We've lost our **chochin** (choh-cheen) — paper lanterns," chimed the first. "Without them we can't float home."

"Three lanterns before the river-song ends," said Dora, flaps up. "Let's begin!"

---

The first lantern hung snagged in the reeds, soggy and folded shut like a sad fan.

Dora tugged, heaved, PULLED. The lantern came loose, and he tumbled down the bank, fwump.

"That," he announced from upside down, beret over one eye, "was on purpose."

The smallest spirit fluttered inside. The lantern went fffp. River water dribbled out, then a snail.

---

The second lantern lay wedged under a stone, dry and crinkly-new. "Aha!" cried Dora.

The first spirit zipped in. The lantern stayed dark. The spirit drooped like a cold noodle.

"A chochin lights from a warm memory," whispered the spirit, "and we floated so far we forgot ours."

Three tiny glows pointed up: the third lantern dangled from a tall wooden post, leaning like a heron.

---

Dora climbed. Pancakes are not built for climbing. He climbed anyway.

The post wobbled. Dora wobbled. His beret dropped. A frog caught it and looked extremely distinguished.

Dora stretched one flap, snagged the lantern, and the whole post tipped toward the dark river.

---

A glowing doorway opened on the bank. Out stepped a handyman in a tool belt, screwdriver ready.

"Loose bolt," said Saitou mildly. One turn. Click. The post stood straight. Dora slid down it, splash.

Raelza the knight held a ladder nobody needed and glared at the river.

Morlock the tiny wizard aimed his staff at the lantern. "Lumi... lumi..." FOOF. His beard glowed instead.

A palm-sized fairy buzzed at Dora, purse open. "One coin for the show," said Lafanpan. "Frogs too."

"All fixed," said Saitou, bowing from the glowing doorway, knight glaring, wizard smoking, fairy jingling.

Dora sat in the shallows holding a dark lantern that smelled of beard. "Also on purpose."

---

Dora sat very seriously. "Memories are stories that already happened. What's your warmest one?"

"A dumpling-auntie," said the first spirit slowly. "Every summer she let me lick the bowl."

It burst into sunrise light and zipped inside. FOOM. The chochin glowed like a small moon.

"Mine!" said the second. "A cat named Mochi who slept on my feet and purred like an engine."

The second spirit shone sunset-pink, lit its lantern, and bonked Dora gently on the head.

On a lily pad, a small white candle creature lit its own purple flame. "Show-off," said Dora.

---

The third spirit hid behind Dora. "I don't have one. I've forgotten too much."

Dora's red-bean heart went squeeze. "Close your eyes," he said. "What does home smell like?"

"Tea," said the spirit. "Rain on a roof. And someone telling me a story. Every night."

"Why," said Dora, voice wobbling, "that sounds just like me."

"It IS you!" laughed the spirit, looping his head. "You've been someone's bedtime story all along!"

It blazed honey-gold, the color of two pancakes hugging red bean, and lit the last lantern.

Dora dabbed his eyes with his beret. He was running out of dry beret.

---

Gentle as a goodnight kiss, Dora set the three lanterns on the dark water.

They found the current and drifted, three soft golden lights gliding under the summer stars.

"As long as someone remembers us," chimed the spirits, "we keep glowing."

"I'll keep your story," said Dora, waving both flaps. "Story-keepers always do."

The frogs were already dozing. One still wore the beret.
