# Story 19: Sora Soft-Cream and the Mount Fuji Climb

**Main Character:** Sora the Soft-Cream
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** The slopes of Mount Fuji at sunrise
**Theme:** One small step at a time carries you to the top
**Japanese Spotlight:** やま (yama) = mountain

---

Sora the Soft-Cream stood at the bottom of the biggest **やま** (yama) = mountain she had ever seen, and her swirl drooped just a tiny bit at the very top, the way it always did when she was nervous.

"That," whispered Sora, tilting her creamy pale-gold head all the way back, "is a very TALL snack of a hill."

It was Mount Fuji. It went up and up and UP, with a soft white cap of snow on its peak like a scoop of vanilla that had been left out by a giant. Pink clouds drifted around its middle. Somewhere far above, the sky was getting ready to do something marvelous.

"We're going to climb it!" announced Maru Matcha, twirling her tiny bamboo whisk. "All the way to the top. To watch the sunrise."

Sora gulped. A soft-serve gulp, which is mostly a wobble.

"Maru," she said gently, "I am made of ice cream. If I climb a whole mountain... I might melt before I even reach the middle."

---

The friends had gathered in the cool blue dark before dawn — Maru Matcha, calm as a still pond; Momo Mochi, bouncing on her pillowy toes; and Dai Daruma, who was already doing little warm-up wobbles and shouting encouragement at a rock.

"FALL DOWN SEVEN, BOUNCE UP EIGHT!" Dai told the rock.

"Dai, that's a rock," said Momo.

"It's never too early to inspire a rock," said Dai proudly.

Sora looked up the long, long trail. Her gentle sleepy eyes went a little wide. She could see the first rest hut, far away. And above it, another. And above THAT, a tiny one no bigger than a sesame seed. And above even that — nothing but cloud and sky and the white snowy peak.

"I really do want to see the sunrise from the top," Sora admitted, her drip-curl trembling. "Everyone says it's the most beautiful thing in the whole world. But..." She pressed her cold little hands to her cone. "What if I'm a puddle by lunchtime? Soft-serve isn't built for mountains. Soft-serve is built for sitting in a nice cool bowl and being admired."

Maru Matcha floated over and patted Sora's cone with her whisk. "Whisk slow," she said. "Feel calm. You will not melt today, Sora."

"How do you know?"

"Because," said Maru, with her tiny half-moon smile, "it is dawn on a mountain in the cool season. The higher we climb, the colder it gets. A mountain is the one place in the world that LOVES soft-serve."

Sora blinked. She hadn't thought of that.

"And besides," Maru added, "we do not climb the whole mountain at once. Nobody does. We climb it one step. Then one more. The yama does not ask you to leap. It only asks you to step."

"One step," Sora repeated softly. "I think I can do one step."

"That's the spirit!" boomed Dai Daruma. "And when you've done one — you'll have one whole step DONE, which is one more than zero, which is MATH, which is COURAGE!"

"That's not quite math," said Momo.

"Everything is math if you BELIEVE," said Dai, and he wobbled so hard with belief that he rolled three full circles and bonked gently into the rock he had been inspiring. "See? The rock and I are friends now. Mountains start with friendship."

"Mountains start with rocks," said Momo.

"Same thing," said Dai.

Sora laughed — a soft, melty little laugh, the first one all morning — and felt the worried droop at the top of her swirl lift just the tiniest bit. Maybe, she thought, just maybe, she could do this. One step. Just one. And then she would see.

---

So they began.

The first part of the trail was wide and soft, winding up through a sleepy forest. Sora took one step. Then another. Her cone made a cozy little *crunch-crunch* on the path.

"See?" she said, delighted. "I'm not melting at all! In fact, I'm rather chilly!"

"Chilly is good!" cheered Momo, bouncing along beside her. "Chilly means crisp! Chilly means COMFY!" She stretched a taffy arm around Sora in a half-squish. "Squish together, stay together!"

They climbed past the first rest hut, where Tama Onigiri was waiting with — of course — extra snacks.

"I packed a little extra, just in case!" said Tama, handing out warm rice balls. "Mountain climbing makes everyone hungry. Even you, Sora — here, I packed you something cold."

It was a tiny cup of crushed ice, sparkling and frosty. Sora's drip-curl perked right up.

"You thought of ME?" she said.

"I always pack a little extra," said Tama warmly. "Especially for the friend who's a little worried. Worried friends climb better with full tummies." She tucked a spare scarf around Dai's wobbly bottom, too, and slipped Momo a warm sticky rice cake, because that is simply the kind of friend Tama is. "Now. Slow and steady, everyone. The mountain isn't going anywhere, and neither are we."

"Slow and steady," murmured Maru approvingly. "The tortoise teaches the mountain."

"I don't think that's a real proverb," said Momo through a mouthful of rice cake.

"It is now," said Maru serenely.

---

Up they went, and up. The trees got shorter. The air got thinner and crisper and cleaner, like the inside of a brand-new freezer. Sora discovered, to her amazement, that the higher she climbed, the BETTER she felt. Her swirl stood up straight and tall. Her gold turned firm and frosty. She wasn't melting — she was getting *crisper*.

"This is the best I've felt in my whole creamy life!" she gasped, doing a little twirl on the trail.

"You see?" said Maru. "You feared the mountain would melt you. But the mountain is keeping you cool."

But about halfway up, something changed.

The trail got STEEP. It zigzagged sharply, full of crumbly red gravel that slid under everyone's feet. The little rest huts seemed impossibly far apart now. Sora looked up — and the summit was hidden in cloud, like it didn't even exist anymore.

Her swirl drooped again.

"I'll never make it," she whispered. "Look how far it still is. We've been climbing for HOURS and the top isn't even there. Maybe I should just... slide back down and watch the sunrise from the bottom. The bottom is nice. The bottom is flat."

Dai Daruma wobbled up beside her, huffing and puffing, his determined eyebrows fierce with effort.

"Sora," he wheezed. "Don't look at the top."

"But the top is the whole POINT—"

"Don't look at the top," said Dai again, more gently this time. "The top is too big. The top is scary. Looking at the top just shows you everything you HAVEN'T done yet." He pointed one stubby arm at the ground right in front of her. "Look there. At that rock. Can you step onto that one rock?"

Sora looked at the rock. It was a small, friendly, ordinary rock.

"...Yes," she said. "I can step on that rock."

"Then step on it."

Sora stepped on the rock.

"NOW," declared Dai, "you have done a thing that was impossible five seconds ago! You CLIMBED! You are a MOUNTAINEER!"

Sora couldn't help giggling. "I climbed one rock."

"And what's the next rock?"

Sora looked. There was another little rock just ahead. "That one."

"Then we climb THAT one. One rock. One step. Fall down seven—"

"—bounce up eight," finished Sora, smiling.

---

And that is how they climbed the steepest, hardest part of the whole **やま**.

Not all at once. Not by staring up at the impossible faraway top. But one small rock at a time. One step. Then one more. Sora stopped looking up at the scary summit and started looking down at the next friendly little rock, and somehow — step by patient step — the friendly little rocks added up.

Momo squished her whenever she wobbled. Maru reminded her to breathe — *whisk slow, feel calm* — whenever her cone got shaky. Tama produced exactly the right snack at exactly the right moment, as if she could read the inside of everyone's tummy. And Dai Daruma cheered for every single step like it was an Olympic gold medal, which, when you are a soft-serve climbing a mountain, it absolutely is.

"Rock!" Dai would shout. "Step! VICTORY! Next rock!"

"You're going to lose your voice," Momo warned.

"I'll cheer with my EYEBROWS," Dai declared, and waggled them so fiercely that Sora laughed all the way up two more steps without even noticing how steep they were.

And that, it turned out, was the secret. The hardest part of the climb wasn't the steepness, or the gravel, or the thin cold air. The hardest part was the worried little voice that whispered *you'll never make it.* But every time that voice spoke up, a friend was right there — with a squish, or a snack, or a breath, or a perfectly silly eyebrow waggle — to whisper back something kinder. And kinder voices, Sora discovered, carry you up mountains.

The sky began to change.

From deep blue to soft gray. From gray to the faintest, sleepiest pink at the edge of the world.

"Friends," breathed Maru, stopping suddenly. "Look up now. Not before — but now."

Sora looked up.

And there it was.

The summit. The very top of Mount Fuji. Close enough to touch. Just a few more friendly rocks away.

"We're nearly there," she whispered. "We're ACTUALLY nearly there."

---

The last few steps were the easiest of all, because now Sora wasn't scared anymore — she was *excited*. She scrambled up the final stretch of crunchy snow, her cone leaving tiny tidy footprints, and burst out onto the very tip-top of the mountain just as—

The sun rose.

It came up over the curve of the whole wide world, slow and golden and warm, and it painted everything PINK. The clouds below them turned to a rolling ocean of rose-colored cotton. The snow glowed peach. Sora's own creamy swirl caught the light and shimmered like the inside of a seashell.

Nobody said anything for a long, long moment.

"Oh," Sora finally whispered. "Oh, it really IS the most beautiful thing in the whole world."

She thought about the bottom of the mountain, hours and hours ago, when the top had seemed so impossibly far that she'd nearly turned back. And she'd reached it. Not in one giant leap. Just one small, cool, creamy step at a time.

"I didn't melt," she said softly, half to herself. "I climbed a whole mountain... and I didn't melt at all."

"You did better than not melt," said Maru, watching the pink sky. "You glowed."

Momo wrapped all of them in one enormous group squish, soft-serve and matcha and mochi and daruma all squished together at the top of the world.

"Squish together," she sighed happily, "stay together."

And way down below, lit pink by the rising sun, an entire mountain full of friendly little rocks sparkled in the dawn — every single one a step that Sora had taken, one at a time, all the way to the top.

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, the biggest mountains are climbed the same way as the smallest hills — one gentle step, and then one more. You do not have to do the whole thing tonight; you only have to do the next small thing, softly and slowly. So let your shoulders melt into the pillow like soft-serve settling in its cone, and let the day's worries roll away down the slope behind you. The morning will rise all pink and golden, just for you, and your own little summit will be there waiting, one easy step at a time. Whisk slow. Feel calm. Goodnight.
