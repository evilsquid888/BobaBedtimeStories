# Story 99: Momo Mochi and the Final Shinkansen Home

**Main Character:** Momo Mochi
**Guest Characters:** Sho the conductor (a tidy nigiri cousin of Niko's), Mero Melonpan, Kei the kei-truck
**Setting:** The bullet train gliding home past Mount Fuji at golden hour
**Theme:** Home is the people you carry in your heart
**Japanese Spotlight:** **kaeru** (kah-EH-roo) — to go home / return

---

Momo Mochi had never, in her whole squishy life, sat so still.

She was perched on a plum-jam velvet seat aboard the **shinkansen** (sheen-KAHN-sen) — bullet train.

Outside, trees became green ribbons and a cow became a long brown mooo.

"Oh my squish," whispered Momo, bouncing. "I'm going so fast my dimple is wobbling."

She poked her dimple. It was. Starch sparkled in the golden light.

This was the last ride. She was going **kaeru** (kah-EH-roo) — home. Home, squishy home.

Beside her sat a cloth bundle: two rice balls, packed by a friend. Homesickness insurance.

Her taffy arms itched. Two whole hours and not one single hug.

---

The conductor came by: Sho, a neat nigiri in a salmon cape and a tiny cap.

"Tickets, please. Neatly, if you would," said Sho.

Momo stretched both arms extra long to pass it, without getting up.

"Traveling alone, little mochi?" asked Sho.

Momo thought hard. "I thought so. But I think I'm traveling with everybody."

Sho tilted his cap, puzzled but kind, and bowed off down the car.

---

The train leaned into a curve. The bundle tipped off the seat and rolled down the aisle.

"My homesickness insurance!" Momo stretched one arm after it. It snagged on a seat.

The train leaned the other way. The bundle rolled right back past her.

She stretched the other arm. Now both arms were tied round a seat like a bow.

The bundle bounced into the next car. Momo squished free and bounced after it.

Sho stepped neatly aside. "Not the right way," he called. "But it is delicious."

It hopped over a suitcase, under a seat, and plopped into a lap two rows down.

A familiar golden crust stirred. "Crackle on the outside," murmured Mero Melonpan, dozing, "dreams on the inside."

---

"MERO!" Momo gasped. "You've been on this train the WHOLE time?"

"Since the first stop," yawned Mero. "I dozed before it moved." He hugged the bundle and dozed again.

Momo gave him the hug she'd been saving for two hours. Crumbs sparkled like glitter.

She shared the first rice ball with him. It tasted like always having a little extra.

---

And then she saw it.

**Fuji-san** (FOO-jee-sahn) — Mount Fuji rose out of the golden haze, broad and blue, capped with bright snow.

It looked like a strawberry kakigori turned upside down. The coziest dessert in the world.

She stretched both arms across the whole car and squish-hugged the window. "Mwah! I love you, mountain!"

Sho passed by, pretended not to notice, and set a tiny cup of tea on her armrest.

---

The gold deepened to honey, then rose, then plum-jam purple.

A hot-spring valley flashed past, and Momo thought of a snuggly friend soaking there.

A tea field flashed past, and she thought of a calm friend with a tiny whisk.

The closer the train carried her home, the MORE homes she seemed to have. Strange arithmetic.

She pressed both starchy paws over the spot where her sweet filling lived.

"Home isn't only a place you go," she whispered. "Home is everybody you carry in here."

Her eyes went happy-leaky. She dabbed them with her arm, which sproinged back and booped her nose.

---

The train slowed into violet dusk. Platform lanterns glowed, warm as a hug.

And there, bouncing on her springs, was a teeny yellow truck. "PUTT-PUTT! You're HOME!" beeped Kei.

Momo tumbled off in an avalanche of starch and wrapped Kei in the biggest hug ever.

Mero waddled off behind her, still hugging the second rice ball. "Dreams on the inside."

"I went all the way around the island," Momo whispered into Kei's hood, "and found a hundred homes."

Kei's headlights blinked like two little hearts, and they putt-putted off under the first shy stars.

---
