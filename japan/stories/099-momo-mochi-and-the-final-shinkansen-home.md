# Story 99: Momo Mochi and the Final Shinkansen Home

**Main Character:** Momo Mochi
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** The bullet train gliding home past Mount Fuji at golden hour
**Theme:** Home is the people you carry in your heart
**Japanese Spotlight:** **kaeru** (kah-EH-roo) — to go home / return

---

Momo Mochi had never, in all her squishy little life, sat so still.

She was perched on a velvet train seat the color of plum jam, her plump snow-white body dusted with potato starch, her peach-pink cheeks pressed to the cool window glass. Outside, the whole world was zipping by so fast it looked like someone had smeared the scenery with butter. Trees became green ribbons. Rice paddies became silver mirrors. A startled cow became a brown blur that went *mooo-ooo-oooo* in a long, stretched-out way, the way everything sounds when you are aboard the **shinkansen** (sheen-KAHN-sen) — the bullet train.

"Oh my squish," Momo whispered, bouncing on the cushion. "I am going SO fast that even my dimple is wobbling."

She poked the little dent where her sweet filling hid. It was, in fact, wobbling. She giggled and bounced again, sending a tiny puff of starch into the golden afternoon light like sugar fairies.

This was the last ride. The ride home.

---

It had been the longest, sweetest journey of Momo's whole life. She had traveled the entire island, hugging every friend she could reach, and now — at golden hour, with the sky turning the color of warm honey — she was finally going **kaeru** (kah-EH-roo) — going home.

"Kaeru," she practiced out loud, stretching her taffy-soft arms over her head. "I am going to *kaeru*! Home, home, squishy little home!"

But the funny thing about a heart — and Momo had a very large, very squishy one — is that it does strange arithmetic. The closer the train carried her toward home, the *more* homes she seemed to have, not fewer. Because every window-flash of scenery reminded her of someone.

*Whoosh* — a hot spring puffing steam in a mountain valley.

"Yuki!" Momo gasped, pressing both starchy palms to the glass. She could practically see her snuggly snow-monkey friend, the steamed manju bun with rosy onsen cheeks, paddling happily in the warm water and saying *Warm cheeks, warm heart, warm friends* before slipping under for a sleepy soak.

Momo's chest went all warm and gooey, like the inside of a mochi left in the sun. That valley wasn't *her* home. But it held a friend. And somehow, that made it feel like home anyway.

*Whoosh* — a tea field, rows and rows of round green bushes climbing a hillside.

"Maru!" Momo bounced so hard her starch puffed. She imagined Maru Matcha standing among the leaves, her frothy matcha-cream swirl catching the breeze, her tiny bamboo whisk raised like a wise little wand. *Whisk slow,* Maru would say in her calm, half-moon-eyed way. *Feel calm.*

"I'm trying, Maru!" Momo whispered to the empty seat beside her. "But it's hard to feel calm when your bottom keeps bouncing!"

---

The train conductor came by then — a tidy, polite fellow who was, Momo was delighted to discover, none other than a traveling cousin of Niko Nigiri: a neat oval of pearly rice in a glossy salmon cape, with a nori belt and a little conductor's cap perched just so.

"Tickets, please! Neatly, if you would," he said, and Momo handed hers over with both arms, stretching them extra long so she wouldn't have to get up.

"You're doing it the *right way*," Momo said admiringly. "Niko would be so proud."

The conductor's bright eyes twinkled. "We Nigiri do love a tidy train. Are you traveling alone, little mochi?"

Momo considered this very seriously, the way you consider a question that turns out to be much bigger than it looked.

"No," she said at last. "I thought I was. But I think I'm traveling with everybody."

The conductor tilted his rice-cap, puzzled but kind. "Well. Wherever you're headed, I hope it's somewhere lovely."

"I'm going kaeru," Momo said again, just for the joy of the word. "Home. Although—" she scrunched up her starchy little face, "—I'm starting to think *home* is a much bigger place than I packed for."

The conductor blinked, decided this was the sort of thing mochi sometimes said, and moved along down the swaying car with a tidy little bow.

Momo turned back to the window. And because the scenery kept tugging memories loose, she opened the little cloth bundle Tama Onigiri had pressed into her arms back at the station. Inside were two perfect rice balls, each with a single crisp band of nori, each with a tiny pickled-plum blush — and a note in Tama's round, careful handwriting: *For the trip. And the second one's for when you miss me. — T.*

Momo's eyes went leaky right there in the train car.

"Oh, Tama," she sniffled, hugging the bundle. "You packed me *homesickness insurance.*"

She ate the first rice ball slowly, savoring every grain, and it tasted exactly like the morning Tama had taught her that comfort isn't about having a lot — it's about always having *a little extra to share.* She tucked the second one safely away. Some things you keep. Some homes you save for later.

And that's when she saw it.

---

**Fuji-san** (FOO-jee-sahn) — Mount Fuji.

It rose up out of the golden haze like a scoop of strawberry-and-cream kakigori turned upside down — broad and blue at the bottom, capped with bright snow at the top, glowing pink-gold in the sinking sun. It was the most beautiful thing Momo had ever seen, and she had seen a LOT of beautiful things, including the time Kori Kakigori wore three pairs of sunglasses at once and called himself "extremely cool, possibly too cool."

"Fuji-san," Momo breathed. Her whole pillowy body went soft with wonder. "You look like a dessert. The biggest, coziest dessert in the whole wide world."

And then — because she was Momo Mochi, and Momo Mochi could not contain a big feeling any more than a kettle can contain a boil — she stretched her arms WAY out, all the way across the empty seats, taffy-long, and gave the window a great big squish-hug.

"Mwah! I love you, mountain!"

Two seats over, a familiar golden crust stirred: Mero Melonpan — of course it was Mero, who had boarded at the very first stop and fallen asleep before the train even moved. He murmured, "Crackle on the outside... dreams on the inside..." and smiled without quite waking up. A few crumbs sparkled off him and drifted down like tiny stars.

Momo grinned. Even on a train full of strangers, a little piece of home was dozing two seats over.

---

The sun sank lower. The golden hour deepened into honey, then into rose, then into the soft purple of a plum about to become jam. Mount Fuji glowed and glowed, and Momo's heart did its strange, wonderful arithmetic again.

She thought of Rin Ramen, steaming with happy tears, slurping *Everything's better warm and bubbly!*

She thought of Dai Daruma wobbling over and bouncing right back up — *Fall down seven, bounce up eight!*

She thought of the Dango Three bickering lovingly on their single stick, and Tako & Yaki high-fiving so hard they bounced, and Tai Taiyaki "swimming" through the air, and Pock Ramune zipping by going *pop! pop! pop!*

She thought of Tama Onigiri, whose second rice ball still sat tucked in its little cloth beside her — homesickness insurance, saved for exactly the right moment.

She thought of Mero Melonpan, still dozing two seats over, murmuring soft dreamy things at the ceiling. She thought of Sora the Soft-Cream and her wide Hokkaido skies, and Dora the Dorayaki who always said *A good story is the best filling* — and Momo realized, with a little gasp, that *she* was inside a good story right now, the very best one, a story about going home.

"I'm a filling!" she whispered, delighted, patting her own squishy middle. "I'm the sweet part in the middle of my own adventure!"

"That's a hundred homes," Momo realized, her voice gone wobbly and warm. "I left home to see the island... and somehow I came back with a *hundred* homes tucked inside my squish."

She pressed her starchy little hands over the spot where her sweet filling lived — over her heart.

"Home isn't only a place you go to," she whispered, watching Fuji-san glow. "Home is everybody you carry in here."

---

Now, here is a thing about mochi: when a mochi is very, very full of feeling, she gets a little leaky around the eyes. Not a sad leak. A *happy* leak — the kind that comes when your heart is so stuffed with good things that some of it has to spill out the top, like custard from a too-full bun.

So Momo leaked happily, and giggled at herself for leaking, and dabbed her rosy cheeks with her own stretchy arm, which only made her giggle MORE, because her arm sproinged back like warm taffy and accidentally bopped her gently on the nose.

"*Boop,*" she said to herself, and laughed so hard her dimple did a little dance.

The conductor passed by again and pretended not to notice the leaking, the way kind people do. But he set down, very neatly, a tiny cup of warm tea on her armrest.

"For the journey home," he said. "Neatly. Or deliciously."

"Both," Momo decided, and hugged the warm cup with both hands.

---

The train began to slow. Outside, the lights of Momo's home town came twinkling up out of the violet dusk — little lanterns, little windows, little glowing signs — each one warm and yellow and familiar as a hug.

The doors went *shhhh*.

And there, on the platform, blinking under the lanterns, was the dearest sight of all: a teeny butter-yellow kei-truck with big headlight eyes and a knitted seat-cover sweater, bouncing on her springs with excitement.

"PUTT-PUTT!" beeped Kei the moment she spotted Momo. "You're HOME! Putt-putt, you're really HOME!"

Momo Mochi tumbled off the train in a happy avalanche of starch and squish, stretched both arms ALL the way out — long, long, longer than she'd ever stretched them — and wrapped Kei up in the biggest, softest, most enormous mochi-hug the platform had ever seen.

"I went all the way around the island," she said into Kei's warm little hood, "and do you know what I found out?"

"What?" beeped Kei, springs wobbling.

Momo squished the little truck tighter, and the lanterns glowed, and far behind them Mount Fuji was a soft purple dream against the first shy stars.

"I found out," she whispered, "that the best part of going anywhere... is getting to *kaeru*. Squish together, stay together."

And Kei beeped so happily that her headlights blinked like two little hearts, and the two of them putt-putted off down the lantern-lit road toward home — which was a place, yes, but mostly, Momo now knew, was a feeling she would carry forever, a hundred times over, tucked sweet and safe inside her squish.

---

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, wherever your travels carry you tomorrow — across mountains, past glowing windows, all the way to the edge of a golden-honey sky — remember that you, too, gather homes inside your heart. Every friend you hug, every kindness you tuck away, becomes a cozy room in there, warm and lantern-lit, waiting for you always. So when the day grows long and the train of your dreams begins to slow, let your heart do its sweet arithmetic and count up all the love you carry. You are never truly far from home, because home is everybody who loves you, riding along right inside your chest. Snuggle down now, soft as fresh mochi, warm as a window in the dusk. Goodnight.
