# Story 99: Momo Mochi and the Final Shinkansen Home

**Main Character:** Momo Mochi
**Guest Characters:** Sho the conductor (a tidy nigiri cousin of Niko's), Mero Melonpan, Kei the kei-truck
**Setting:** The bullet train gliding home past Mount Fuji at golden hour
**Theme:** Home is the people you carry in your heart
**Japanese Spotlight:** **kaeru** (kah-EH-roo) — to go home / return
**Cameo:** Astro Boy (Astro Boy) — flies up to the window waving, races the train while the whole car tilts to cheer, loses politely to a tunnel and salutes until he is a dot; walk-on: Tails (Sonic the Hedgehog)

---

Momo Mochi had never, in her whole squishy life, sat so still.

She was perched on a plum-jam velvet seat aboard the **shinkansen** (sheen-KAHN-sen) — bullet train.

Outside, trees became green ribbons and a cow became a long brown mooo.

"Oh my squish," whispered Momo. "I'm going so fast my dimple is wobbling." She poked it. It was.

This was the last ride. She was going **kaeru** (kah-EH-roo) — home. Home, squishy home.

Beside her sat a cloth bundle: two rice balls, packed by a friend. Homesickness insurance.

---

Along came Sho the conductor, a neat nigiri in a tiny cap. "Tickets, neatly please," said Sho.

Momo stretched both arms extra long to pass it, without getting up. Sho's cap tipped in alarm.

"Traveling alone, little mochi?" asked Sho.

Momo thought hard. "I thought so," said Momo. "But I think I'm traveling with everybody."

---

The train leaned into a curve. The bundle tipped off the seat and rolled down the aisle.

"My homesickness insurance!" Momo stretched one arm after it. It snagged on a seat.

The train leaned the other way. The bundle rolled right back past her.

She stretched the other arm. Now both arms were tied round a seat like a bow.

---

Tap-tap on the window. A small robot with glossy black hair and red boots flew alongside, jet-flames blazing.

"Good afternoon!" called Astro, bright as a bell. "Shall we race? I will try my best!"

He zoomed ahead. Every treat-folk rushed to one side to cheer. The car tilted. The bundle rolled faster.

A small orange two-tailed fox whizzed up beside him, tails spinning like a propeller. "Race you!" called Tails.

WHOOSH. A tunnel swallowed the train. Dark. Then light: Astro hovered far behind, waving smaller and smaller.

"You win!" called Astro, and saluted the train with a little bow until he was a dot.

Momo squished free and bounced after the bundle, bonking a suitcase on the way.

Sho stepped neatly aside. "Not the right way," called Sho. "But it is delicious."

---

The bundle hopped over a suitcase, under a seat, and plopped into a lap two rows down.

A familiar golden crust stirred. "Crackle on the outside," murmured Mero Melonpan, dozing, "dreams on the inside."

"MERO!" Momo gasped. "You've been on this train the WHOLE time?"

"Since the first stop," yawned Mero. "I dozed off before it moved." He hugged the bundle.

Momo gave him the hug she'd been saving for two hours. Crumbs sparkled like glitter.

She shared the first rice ball with him. It tasted like always having a little extra.

---

**Fuji-san** (FOO-jee-sahn) — Mount Fuji rose out of the golden haze, broad and blue, capped with bright snow.

It looked like a strawberry kakigori turned upside down. The coziest dessert anywhere.

She stretched both arms across the car and squish-hugged the window. "Mwah! I love you, mountain!" said Momo.

Sho passed by, eyes politely on the ceiling, and set a tiny cup of tea on her armrest.

---

The gold deepened to honey, then rose, then plum-jam purple.

The closer the train carried her home, the MORE homes she seemed to have. Strange arithmetic.

She pressed both paws over her sweet filling. "Home is everybody you carry in here," she whispered.

Her eyes went happy-leaky. She dabbed them with her arm, which sproinged back and booped her nose.

---

The train slowed into violet dusk. Platform lanterns glowed, warm as a hug.

And there, bouncing on her springs, was a teeny yellow truck. "PUTT-PUTT! You're HOME!" beeped Kei.

Momo tumbled off in an avalanche of starch and wrapped Kei in the biggest hug.

Mero waddled off behind her, still hugging the second rice ball. "Dreams on the inside," murmured Mero.

"I went all the way around," Momo whispered into Kei's hood, "and found a hundred homes."

Kei's headlights blinked like two little hearts, and they putt-putted off under the first shy stars.
