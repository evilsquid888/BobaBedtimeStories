# Story 20: Dai Daruma and the Mountain of Never-Give-Up

**Main Character:** Dai Daruma (daruma sweet-bean-paste manju)
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A misty mountain trail with a little shrine at the top
**Theme:** Falling down is just practice for getting up
**Japanese Spotlight:** がんばって (ganbatte) = do your best / keep going

---

The morning Dai Daruma decided to climb Mount Mochimaki, he fell down before he even started.

This was not unusual. Dai was a roly-poly crimson dome of a bun, round as a teakettle and twice as bouncy, with one big painted eye, two enormously determined eyebrows, and absolutely no legs whatsoever. So mostly, Dai got around by wobbling, tipping, and rolling — and occasionally by toppling off the porch into a flowerpot, which is exactly what happened that morning.

*Bonk. Wobble-wobble. BOING.*

He popped right back up, geranium petals stuck to his head like a silly hat.

"AHA!" he announced to the entire mountain. "Fall down seven, BOUNCE UP eight!"

A pebble rolled past. It did not answer. Daruma decided this counted as encouragement anyway, and he set off up the misty trail, eyebrows pointed bravely at the clouds.

---

Mount Mochimaki was tall, steep, and wrapped in fog like a dumpling in steam. Somewhere at the very top sat a tiny red shrine where, the old stories said, if you climbed all the way up and made a wish with your whole heart, the mountain would paint in your second eye.

Dai had only one eye painted. The other was a smooth, blank, waiting circle — saving itself for the biggest wish of his life.

But Dai had not come to climb alone. Halfway up the first slope, he found his friends sitting in a glum little heap on a mossy log.

There was Mero Melonpan, the puffy crackly-crusted bun, already half-asleep and snoring tiny crumb-bubbles. There was Coro the Korokke, the golden croquette, puffing a worried little steam-puff. And there was Kori Kakigori, the towering mountain of strawberry shaved ice, slumped under his sunglasses and fanning himself with a leaf.

"We can't do it," moaned Kori dramatically, syrup drizzling like tears. "This mountain is SO tall and I am SO meltable. One more step and I shall become a puddle of tragic strawberry soup!"

"It's just so HIGH up," whispered Coro, steaming. "What if I trip? What if I roll all the way back down? What if I land in a bush?"

"Bushes are soft," murmured Mero sleepily. "I would like a bush. Wake me at the top."

Dai planted his round little body in front of them and threw out his stubby painted arms.

"Friends!" he boomed, eyebrows blazing. "You can DO this! The shrine is waiting! And I will lead the way — watch how I climb!"

He turned, faced the steep path, and rolled forward with magnificent confidence.

He made it three whole steps.

Then a loose stone went *click*, his round bottom went *whoops*, and Dai Daruma tumbled head-over-dome all the way back down the slope — *bonk-bonk-bonkity-BONK* — and landed upside-down in, yes, a bush.

There was a long silence on the misty trail.

Then the bush rustled. A pair of determined eyebrows poked out. Dai wobbled upright, twigs sticking out of his head, beaming.

"SEE?" he cried. "Easy!"

His friends burst out laughing so hard that Mero woke up.

---

And so the climb began.

Dai went first, because that is what brave round buns do. The trouble was that being round and brave is wonderful for *bouncing* and absolutely terrible for *staying put on a steep wet mountain*.

He rolled. Oh, how he rolled.

He toppled over a tree root — *bonk* — and bounced back up. "**頑張って** (ganbatte) = do your best!" he shouted to the others, which is what you say in Mochimaki when you want someone to keep going with all their heart.

He slid backward on a slick patch of mist — *whooosh, BONK* — and bounced back up. "Ganbatte, Coro! You're stronger than a slippery rock!"

He bonked his dome on a low branch — *DONK* — spun in three full circles, and bounced back up, dizzy but grinning. "Ganbatte, Kori! Cool slushies climb mountains too!"

Every single time Dai fell, his friends winced. And every single time, before they could even feel sorry, he was *already up again*, eyebrows higher than ever, cheering THEM on like HE wasn't the one covered in moss.

"Dai," puffed Coro, climbing past a boulder, "doesn't it HURT? You keep falling!"

"Falling?" Dai blinked his one eye, genuinely surprised. "Oh, that's not falling, my crispy friend. That's just *practice for getting up*. A daruma who never tips over never learns how good it feels to bounce!"

Coro thought about this. Then he climbed a little taller.

---

Up they went, into the thick white **霧** (kiri) = mist, where the trail grew narrow and the trees dripped like sleepy faucets. The world turned soft and gray and quiet, the kind of quiet that makes small friends feel very small.

Kori was the first to wobble for real. The shaved-ice mountain had been melting drip by drip all morning, and now his magnificent peak drooped sadly to one side.

"I'm shrinking," he whispered, all his drama gone. "I'm getting smaller and tireder and I just... I don't think I can make it, sweeties. Go on without me."

The friends gathered close in the mist. Even Dai's eyebrows softened.

He rolled gently up beside the slumping shaved ice and leaned his round red side against Kori's cold strawberry one.

"Kori," he said quietly, "do you know WHY I keep getting up?"

"Because you're extremely bouncy and slightly ridiculous?"

"Well, yes," Dai admitted. "But also because every time I bounce up, I get to see my friends are still here. And that's the best view on the whole mountain. So you can't melt away yet — because I'd lose my favorite thing to bounce back up TO."

Kori sniffled a syrupy sniffle. Then, very slowly, he straightened his drippy peak.

"...Okay," he said. "But if I become soup, you have to drink me respectfully."

"With a tiny spoon and great honor," Dai promised solemnly.

And the friends laughed, and the laughing made them warm, and the warmth made them brave, and bravery, it turns out, weighs nothing at all when you carry it together.

---

The last stretch was the steepest.

It rose up before them like a wall, slick with mist, and at the very top — *there!* — glowed a tiny red shrine, its little bell swaying in the breeze.

"Almost there!" cried Dai. "Ganbatte, everyone! Last push! Up, up, UP!"

He charged.

He made it nearly to the top — closer than he'd ever climbed — and then the steep wet slope did what steep wet slopes do best.

Dai's round bottom slipped.

This time the tumble was a big one. *Bonk-bonk-bonk-BONK-BONK* — all the way down past Coro, past Mero, past Kori, down, down, down to the very bottom of the last slope, where he came to rest in a dizzy crimson heap.

The friends gasped. From way up high, they could barely see him through the mist — one tiny red dot at the bottom of the enormous mountain.

"DAI!" they shouted. "Are you okay?!"

There was a pause.

Then, faint but absolutely unmistakable, drifting up through the fog, came a tiny determined voice:

"FALL DOWN SEVEN..."

A wobble.

"...BOUNCE UP **EIGHT!**"

And from the bottom of the whole mountain, the littlest round bun began to climb again — bonking, rolling, slipping, bouncing — never once stopping, never once frowning.

That was when something wonderful happened.

Coro the Korokke, watching that brave little dot climb and climb and refuse to quit, felt his worried steam turn into something else entirely. He turned and scrambled up the last steep wall — and made it.

Mero Melonpan, wide awake now, dreamed himself right to the top. Kori, drippy and proud, slid himself up the final slope on a trail of his own strawberry syrup, hollering "I'M COMING, YOU RIDICULOUS BUN!"

And one by one, cheering all the way, they reached out and they did not pull Dai up — because that wasn't the point. They simply climbed beside him, matching his bounces, falling and rising together, all the way to the little red shrine at the top of Mount Mochimaki.

---

At the summit, the mist parted like a curtain.

Below them stretched the whole world, soft and golden in the late afternoon — valleys and rooftops and tiny far-off lanterns just beginning to glow. The shrine bell rang once, sweet and clear.

Dai Daruma stood at the very front, covered head to bottom in moss, twigs, and one stubborn geranium petal, grinning the biggest grin a round bun has ever grinned.

"We did it," whispered Coro.

"YOU did it," said Dai. "All I did was keep bouncing."

"That," said Kori, draping a cold, happy arm around him, "was the whole entire point, you marvelous meatball."

And as the friends huddled close at the top of the world, a warm breeze swept up from the valley — and on Dai Daruma's smooth blank circle, soft as a brushstroke, the mountain painted in his second eye.

Now he could see everything. And everything he saw was friends.

He had not even needed to make a wish.

It had already come true on the way up.

## Goodnight Blessing

Little one, the climb may be steep, and you may tumble more than once — but tumbling is only how brave hearts practice bouncing. When you slip, you do not stay down; you simply find your eyebrows, point them at the clouds, and whisper *ganbatte* to yourself. Your friends are climbing beside you, and the shrine bell is ringing just for you, and every bounce brings you closer to the top. So rest now, snug and round and warm, knowing that getting up is something you already know how to do. Goodnight.
