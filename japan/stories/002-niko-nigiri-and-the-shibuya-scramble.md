# Story 2: Niko Nigiri and the Shibuya Scramble

**Main Character:** Niko Nigiri (nigiri sushi — rice topped with salmon)
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** The famous Shibuya Crossing in Tokyo at night, neon everywhere
**Theme:** Even the most organized plan needs room to wiggle
**Japanese Spotlight:** だいじょうぶ (daijoubu) = it's okay / don't worry

---

Niko Nigiri stood at the edge of the busiest crossing in the entire world, holding a clipboard nearly as big as he was. This was a problem, because Niko was a tidy little oval of pearly white rice wearing a glossy coral-orange salmon cape, with a neat nori belt cinched just so — and clipboards do not fit neatly into capes. But Niko had *brought* the clipboard, because Niko brought clipboards everywhere, the way other friends bring snacks or good moods.

"Step one," he read aloud, standing very straight and proud. "Arrive at Shibuya Crossing. Done." He drew a crisp little check mark. Around him, Tokyo blazed and buzzed. Neon signs the size of houses splashed pink and blue and lime-green light across everybody's faces. A giant screen showed a dancing dumpling. Somewhere a train sang a cheerful jingle.

"Step two," Niko continued. "Cross the street. Neatly."

He looked up. The crossing roared. Thousands of feet, paws, wheels, and waddles waited at every corner, all of them about to surge into the middle at once.

Niko's bright eyes went very wide.

"Oh," he said. "Oh dear."

---

You see, Niko Nigiri loved doing things *the right way*. He labeled his socks (he did not have feet, but he labeled where feet would go). He alphabetized his worries. He once made a twelve-step plan just for brushing his teeth, and steps four through nine were all about rinsing.

So naturally, for the most famous crossing in the world — the **渋谷スクランブル** (Shibuya sukuranburu) = Shibuya Scramble, where five streets meet and everybody crosses every-which-way all at the very same moment — Niko had prepared a Plan. A beautiful Plan. A twelve-step, color-coded, laminated Plan, because rice gets soggy and laminating is just *sensible*.

He cleared his throat and read it to nobody in particular.

"Step three: Walk in a perfectly straight line. Step four: Maintain exactly one arm's length from all other crossers. Step five: Do not, under any circumstances, get bumped, jostled, swirled, or — " he shuddered — "*scrambled*."

A pigeon landed on his clipboard and pecked at step seven.

"Excuse me," said Niko, very politely, "you are not on the schedule." The pigeon, who could not read and did not care, hopped off again. Niko smoothed the page and checked his tiny wristwatch, which he wore on his nori belt because nigiri do not have wrists either. "We are forty seconds ahead of plan," he announced to the night. "Excellent. A forty-second buffer is a *responsible* buffer."

Somewhere behind him, the giant dancing dumpling on the screen wiggled its tail. Niko did not wiggle back. Niko did not wiggle, full stop. Wiggling was step nothing. Wiggling was not on the list.

The little WAIT signal glowed red. Beside Niko, a crowd gathered. There was a tall swirl of soft-serve humming sleepily. There was a fizzy blue bottle bouncing on its toes. There were three little dumplings on a stick, arguing about which way was north.

Niko did not know any of them. Niko had planned to cross *alone*. Alone was tidier.

"Step six," he whispered to himself, gripping his clipboard. "Begin crossing the instant the light turns green. Reach the other side in precisely forty-two steps."

The signal turned green.

And the world went *whoosh*.

---

It was not like anything Niko's Plan had prepared him for. The whole ocean of crossers poured into the middle from all five directions at once — left, right, diagonal, backwards, every-which-way — a great swirling soup of feet and bubbles and steam and laughter. Someone's umbrella spun him sideways. A skateboard zipped under his cape. The soft-serve drifted dreamily across his perfectly straight line, leaving a little curl of cream in the air.

"That's not — wait — you're going the WRONG way — step seven says — STEP SEVEN — " Niko spun in a circle. His clipboard flew out of his hands.

"My PLAN!" he cried.

The laminated pages scattered into the swirl like startled birds. Step nine floated past a streetlamp. Step eleven did a little dance on the wind. Step four landed on the head of a small jade-green friend with a frothy matcha-cream swirl and very calm half-moon eyes, who was sitting peacefully on the curb as the whole crossing churned around her.

"Excuse me," said Niko, sloshing toward her in a panic, "that's step four, I need step four, I need ALL my steps, I've lost my Plan and now I'll never cross neatly and the light's going to turn red and I'll be stuck in the middle and scrambled and — "

"だいじょうぶ," said the green friend.

Niko stopped sloshing.

"*Daijoubu*," she said again, soft as snowfall. "It's okay. Don't worry." She lifted the bamboo whisk she carried like a little wand and gave it a gentle stir in the air. "I'm Maru Matcha. Whisk slow. Feel calm."

"But my Plan — "

"Your Plan ran away," said Maru, half-smiling. "Plans do that, in Shibuya. It's a scramble, not a straight line." She patted the curb beside her. "Watch."

Niko, who had never in his life *watched* when he could be *checking things off*, sat. And watched.

It was, he had to admit, a little bit beautiful. The neon poured down in waterfalls of color. A noodle bowl on stubby legs steamed cheerfully past, leaving a warm savory cloud. A puffy domed bun with a crackly crust drifted by half-asleep, murmuring something about dreams. Niko had been so busy guarding his Plan that he hadn't *looked* at Shibuya at all — and Shibuya, it turned out, was the most wonderfully alive place he had ever seen.

"I made a list of everything that could go wrong," Niko confessed, very small. "It has thirty-one items. Number one is *getting scrambled*. Number two is *being late*. Number three is — "

"That's a lot of worrying for one little rice ball," said Maru gently.

"I'm a *nigiri*," Niko corrected, on reflex. Then, quieter: "...but yes. It is."

---

And the funny thing was — it *worked*. Not neatly. Not in straight lines. But it worked. Thousands of crossers, all going every-which-way at once, somehow flowed around each other like fish in a pond. Nobody crashed. Nobody got scrambled. The soft-serve swirled, the fizzy bottle zigged, the dumplings on a stick pivoted like a tiny carousel — and they all reached the other side, grinning, just before the light blinked back to red.

"How?" breathed Niko. "There's no system. There's no order. There's no — "

"There's *trust*," said Maru. "Everyone wiggles a little so everyone fits. That's the whole secret." She tucked step four neatly back into Niko's cape, which Niko appreciated more than he could say. "A plan that can't wiggle isn't a plan. It's a trap with corners."

Niko looked down at his single rescued page. Then he looked up at the great glittering scramble.

"I want to cross," he said quietly. "But I'm scared I'll get lost in the middle."

That was when the fizzy blue bottle popped up beside him. "Then don't cross alone — *pop!*" He stuck out one frosty hand. "I'm Pock Ramune! Hold on and we fizz together!"

The three dumplings rolled over. "Three on a stick, stuck together for good!" chirped the pink one. "We'll be your stick! Sort of! Metaphorically!"

The soft-serve drifted close, gentle and dreamy. "Soft swirls, slow smiles," it murmured. "I'll keep the pace easy. We don't rush a scramble. We *flow* it."

Niko looked at the row of friendly hands — er, fins, bubbles, and dumpling-bits — held out toward him.

"This isn't in my Plan," he said.

"No," agreed Maru, taking his other hand. "It's better. Now — when the light turns green, we don't count steps. We just go. And we hold on. Daijoubu?"

Niko took a deep breath. He felt the warm chain of friends on either side of him. He felt, for the first time all night, like he didn't have to do everything perfectly by himself.

"Daijoubu," he said. And he was surprised to find he meant it.

---

The signal turned green.

The world went *whoosh* — and this time, Niko went *whoosh* with it.

He did not walk in a straight line. He swooped. He swirled. He got gently bumped by a giant friendly mushroom and laughed instead of gasping. A breeze caught his salmon cape and for one glorious second he was almost *flying*. Pock Ramune fizzed bubbles into the air that popped into tiny rainbows, and one landed right on Niko's nose with a cool, ticklish *pop!* that made him snort with surprise.

"Step seven says — " Niko started, out of pure habit.

"NO STEPS!" sang all five friends at once, and Niko laughed so hard he nearly tipped over, and Pock Ramune steadied him, and the soft-serve kept the pace gentle and dreamy so nobody ever once felt rushed or lost. The dumplings spun the whole group in a happy little twirl right in the dead center of the most famous crossing on Earth, under all that splashing neon, with the giant dancing dumpling on the screen above seeming to dance just for them.

"This is — this is — " Niko couldn't find the word. His salmon cape flapped like a flag. His nori belt came slightly untucked and, to his enormous shock, he *did not even mind*.

"FUN?" offered the pink dumpling.

"FUN!" agreed Niko, who had not used that word about a logistics challenge in his entire tidy life.

The dumplings spun him one way; Maru steadied him the other; and right in the middle of it all, Niko realized something marvelous: the scramble wasn't *fighting* him. It was *dancing* with him. Every wiggle made room for the next. Every bump was just a friend saying *hello, mind if I pass?* You couldn't plan it. You could only join it.

They reached the other side in a giggling, swirling clump — not in forty-two steps, not neatly, not according to any laminated page — but *together*, and right on time, just as the light blinked red behind them.

Niko looked back at the great glittering scramble, breathing hard and beaming.

"My Plan said cross alone in a straight line," he said. "My Plan was wrong."

"Plans aren't wrong," said Maru kindly. "They're just *guesses you make before you have friends.*"

Niko thought that was the wisest, coziest thing he had ever heard. He pulled out his one rescued page, found a pencil, and at the very bottom, beneath all twelve color-coded steps, he wrote a brand-new thirteenth:

*Step 13: Hold hands. Wiggle a little. Daijoubu.*

Then he tucked the page away, looked at his new friends glowing in the neon, and said the most un-Niko thing imaginable:

"Let's do it again! Neatly, or — " he grinned — "*deliciously.*"

And they did. Three more times, just for the joy of it, while Tokyo sparkled all around them like a sky that had decided to come down and play.

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, the world is a big bright scramble sometimes, with lights and noise and far too many directions all at once. But you don't have to cross it alone, and you don't have to cross it perfectly. Reach for a warm hand, take a slow breath, and let yourself wiggle a little — everything still fits. Whatever feels too big tonight, whisper it soft and let it go: daijoubu, daijoubu, it's okay. The neon will dim, the crossing will quiet, and you are already exactly where you need to be — tucked in, safe, and loved. Goodnight.
