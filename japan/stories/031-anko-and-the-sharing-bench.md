# Story 31: Anko and the Sharing Bench

**Main Character:** Anko — a round, soft, sweet red-bean bun with a shiny top and a generous heart
**Guest Characters:** Kina, a kinako-dusted mochi; the Dango Three (Pinku, Shiro, Cha); Tai Taiyaki
**Setting:** A sunny park bench by a koi pond
**Theme:** A snack shared is a snack doubled
**Japanese Spotlight:** **wakeru** (wah-keh-roo) — to share
**Cameo:** Char Aznable and his red Zaku (Mobile Suit Gundam) — a masked figure in red claims the bench from orbit while his one-eyed Zaku kneels behind it, tries to sit too, and tips the whole bench into a seesaw; walk-on: Pompompurin (Sanrio)

---

By the koi pond stood the best bench in the whole park, and Anko was on it.

He was a round red-bean bun with a top so shiny he checked his reflection in it. Often.

He spread his stubby arms across the whole bench. "Mine. All mine. Every glorious inch."

A grumpy orange koi blooped at the surface, as if to say, we'll see about that.

Anko's castella cake sat beside him, a wobbly golden square, right where a friend might sit.

"Friends," sniffed Anko, "take up bench." He polished his shiny top with a petal.

---

"Excuse me," said a small breathless voice. A white mochi dusted in kinako hopped from foot to foot.

"Could I sit? Just the edge," said the mochi. "I'm mostly squish. I get tired standing."

"Fine," sighed Anko, scooting over exactly one bun-width. "But just the edge."

Poff. The mochi sat, and squished, and a puff of kinako went straight up Anko's nose.

"AH-CHOO." Anko sneezed so hard he bounced. The mochi giggled. "Bless you. I'm Kina."

"Ooh, castella. Did you make it?" asked Kina. Nobody had ever asked.

"Want to **wakeru** (wah-keh-roo) — share — it?" Anko heard himself say. "Half each?"

He broke it in two. And the half, somehow, tasted sweeter than the whole.

---

Along bounced the Dango Three on their stick. "We ate our snack on the way," mumbled Pinku.

"Space is a snack too," said Anko, before he'd decided to say it. "Sit."

The dango climbed the bench-back and wobbled there like a tiny pink-white-green carousel.

Then, swimming through the air, came Tai Taiyaki. "Is this a party? I've been to four."

He landed tail-first on the far end. The bench creaked. Everyone held their breath.

---

THOOM. The pond jumped. A crimson robot with one glowing pink eye knelt behind the bench.

From its palm stepped a masked figure in red, arms folded. Anko had met a bigger bench grump.

"This bench is mine," said Char smoothly. "Everything red is mine. The bench is brown. Close enough."

Behind him the Zaku pressed one enormous metal thumb onto the far end, hoping to sit too.

The bench tipped like a seesaw. Everyone slid squeaking. The castella sailed off. The koi leapt. GULP.

"The bench has rejected me," said Char, and leaned on the Zaku's knee, arms folded, watching.

---

The koi sank back down, chewing, and for the first time ever, smiled.

"MY CASTELLA," wailed Anko, dangling from the bench-back by one stubby arm.

"Everybody to the middle!" called Cha. "Balance is just sharing, done sideways."

They scooted. Anko scooted. Tai squeezed in. The bench thumped level, and stayed.

"I have senbei!" said Kina. Crunch. The koi popped up in a line to listen.

---

Anko hummed along to Kina's frog song, badly. The bad humming was the funniest part.

He looked down the long, squished, giggling bench. He'd eaten less castella than ever.

"I gave most of it away," he said to Kina. "So why does it feel like more?"

"A snack you eat alone is a snack," said Kina. "A snack you wakeru turns into an afternoon."

Anko scooted once more, for no reason, just to feel the bench get cozier.

"Friends don't take up bench," he said. "Friends MAKE bench."

---

The sun slid low and gold. The last petals drifted down like slow pink snow.

A pudding-shaped puppy in a brown beret padded up and climbed onto the very last inch.

"Purin," said Pompompurin slowly, and dozed off against Tai's fin before anyone could say sit.

One perfect strawberry went round the whole bench, so everyone got one tiny, magnificent bite.

"Same time tomorrow," yawned Anko. "I'll bring castella. Two slices. The koi gets one."

And the sharing bench leaned together in a warm squishy heap, watching the koi turn slow gold circles.
