# Story 68: Dai Daruma and the New-Year First Shrine Visit

**Main Character:** Dai Daruma
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A bustling shrine on New Year's morning for the first visit (hatsumode)
**Theme:** A fresh year is a fresh chance to try
**Japanese Spotlight:** akemashite omedetou (ah-keh-mah-shee-teh oh-meh-deh-toh) — Happy New Year

---

The very first sunrise of the new year peeked over the shrine gate, and Dai Daruma rolled out of his cozy box like a little crimson cannonball.

"Wobble-wobble — WHEE!" he cried, tipping left, tipping right, and bouncing perfectly upright every single time. That was Dai Daruma's whole magic: he was a round, roly-poly bun the color of a ripe pomegranate, with big determined eyebrows and one painted eye. The other eye was blank and waiting — saving its spot for a brand-new wish.

"Friends! FRIENDS!" he hollered, rolling down the snowy path toward the **jinja** (jeen-jah) — shrine. "It's the first morning of the whole year! Do you know what that means?"

Momo Mochi sleepily peeked out of a paper lantern. "It means... a group squish?"

"It means," boomed Dai Daruma, eyebrows wiggling like two excited caterpillars, "a brand-new BOUNCE!"

---

The shrine was already buzzing. This was **hatsumode** (hah-tsoo-moh-deh) — the year's first shrine visit, and every dessert-friend in town had come to make a wish for the months ahead.

Paper lanterns glowed like little moons. A giant bronze bell hung over the wooden offering box, waiting to be rung. And everywhere, friends were calling out the most important words of the morning.

"**Akemashite omedetou** (ah-keh-mah-shee-teh oh-meh-deh-toh) — Happy New Year!" sang Niko Nigiri, bowing so neatly that his little nori belt stayed perfectly flat.

"Akemashite omedetou!" bubbled Pock Ramune, zipping past in a trail of fizz. "Pop! Pop! New year, new bubbles!"

Dai Daruma bounced up onto a stone lantern so everyone could see him. "Today, my dumplings — my darling little snacks — we choose our wishes for the year! And I, Dai Daruma, the bounciest bun in all the land, shall be your wish-coach!"

"Coach?" said Maru Matcha, calmly sipping her own frothy hair. "Do wishes need a coach?"

"EVERYTHING needs a coach," said Dai Daruma firmly. "Especially courage. Now! Who has a wish?"

There was a long, shy silence. Somewhere, a crow said "caw," which is crow for "ooh, awkward."

---

It turned out that making a New Year's wish was harder than it looked.

Coro the Korokke went first, because he was too polite to make anyone else go first. He shuffled forward, a tiny puff of nervous steam rising off his crispy golden head.

"I... I want to wish for something," he mumbled, "but what if I pick the WRONG wish? What if I wish to be brave, and then a really big spider shows up, and I'm not brave at all, and everyone sees, and — " A whole cloud of steam burst out of him. "Phoooosh."

"Aha!" Dai Daruma rolled right up to him, nose to crispy nose. "Coro, my crunchy friend, there is no such thing as a wrong wish."

"There isn't?"

"A wish isn't a promise that everything goes perfectly. A wish is just you, pointing at the thing you'd like to TRY." He gave a proud little wobble. "You know my motto?"

"Fall down seven..." Coro started.

"BOUNCE UP EIGHT!" Dai Daruma finished, popping straight up into the air and landing with a satisfying *boing.* "Wishing to be brave doesn't mean you'll never feel scared. It means that when you DO feel scared — phoosh and all — you point yourself at the brave thing anyway. Now. What do you really wish for?"

Coro thought. The steam settled. His voice came out small but clear. "I wish I could learn to make soup for everybody. The really warm kind. The kind that makes a cold day feel like a hug."

"Now THAT," said Dai Daruma, eyebrows soaring, "is a delicious wish."

---

Word spread fast that Dai Daruma was helping with wishes, and soon a whole wobbly line had formed in front of the **ema** (eh-mah) — little wooden wish-plaques that you hang up at the shrine.

Tako and Yaki bounced to the front together, naturally, because they did everything together.

"We want to wish—" began Tako.

"—for something AMAZING—" continued Yaki.

"—but we can't decide—" said Tako.

"—because there's TOO MANY good things!" they finished in unison, and high-fived.

"Hmm," said Dai Daruma, stroking his round chin (which was really just his round everything). "A wish that's too big to pick. A classic problem. Here is what I, your wise wish-coach, recommend: don't wish for ALL the good things. Wish for ONE good thing, and do it so well that it bumps into the next good thing."

Tako and Yaki blinked.

"Like dominoes!" said Dai Daruma. "Tip one, and the rest go *clackety-clack-clack* all on their own."

"OHHHH," said the twins. They huddled, whispering very fast, then popped back up. "We wish to make ONE brand-new friend this year!"

"And once we've got one—"

"—maybe they've got a friend—"

"—and THAT friend has a friend—"

"—clackety-clack-clack!"

Dai Daruma beamed so hard his painted eye sparkled. "Perfect. A small wish with a big, bouncy heart."

---

Next in line came Maru Matcha, calm as a still green pond, holding her tiny bamboo whisk like a wand.

"I don't think I need a wish," she said in her gentle, deadpan way. "I am already quite calm. I whisk slow. I feel calm. What is there to try?"

Dai Daruma had been waiting his whole career for this exact challenge. He rubbed his nubby little hands together.

"Maru," he said, "calm is wonderful. But even a still pond likes to throw ONE pebble now and then, just to watch the ripples." He pointed a stubby arm at a stall across the path, where Pepper-kun was sizzling something that smelled outrageously delicious. "What's one thing you've never, ever tried because it seemed a little too... loud?"

Maru's calm half-moon eyes flickered. Just a little. "...The big drum," she admitted. "Every New Year they let one friend strike the great festival drum. It is very loud. It is the OPPOSITE of whisking slow. I have always wanted to, and I have always sat down instead."

"AHA!" Dai Daruma bounced so high a clump of snow plopped off the lantern above. "A secret wish! The juiciest kind! You see, my frothy friend, a fresh year is the perfect time to surprise even yourself."

"But what if I'm bad at it?" said Maru. "What if I make a silly little *tap* instead of a great BOOM, and everyone laughs?"

"Then," said Dai Daruma, "you will have made the calmest, most graceful silly little *tap* anyone has ever heard, and we will love you for it." He winked. "Whisk slow, feel calm — and then, just once this year, go BOOM."

A very small, very rare smile spread across Maru's froth. "Boom," she repeated softly, as if tasting the word. "I wish... to strike the drum. One time. This year."

And then — because she was Maru, and Maru never wasted a chance to be still — she added, "I will breathe first."

"You will breathe first," Dai Daruma agreed warmly. "That's the best part."

---

But not everyone was ready to wish.

Off to the side, half-hidden behind a snowy stone fox, sat Mero Melonpan, looking sleepy and a little sad. His crackly golden crust drooped at the edges.

Dai Daruma rolled over gently. "Mero? Why so glum, you dreamy bun?"

Mero sighed a soft, crumbly sigh. "Last year I made a wish. I wished I'd stop falling asleep in the middle of things." He yawned enormously, which rather proved the problem. "And then I... fell asleep during the wish. And I never got better. So this year I figured, why even bother? A new year is just the old year wearing a party hat."

Now, Dai Daruma was a bun built almost entirely of encouragement, and hearing this made his eyebrows do something they almost never did — they went soft and kind.

"Mero," he said quietly, settling beside him in the snow. "May I tell you a secret about us daruma?"

Mero nodded.

"When somebody paints my first eye and makes a wish, the wish doesn't always come true that very year. Sometimes I sit on a shelf for ages. Sometimes I tip over. Sometimes I roll right off and bonk my head." He chuckled. "But I always wobble back up. And every single new year, people don't throw the old wish away in shame. They thank it. They say 'thank you, old wish, for the trying I did' — and then they paint a fresh, brand-new bounce onto the next year."

"Thank the old wish?" Mero blinked his sleepy eyes. "Even if it didn't work?"

"ESPECIALLY then. Because trying counts, even when it's wobbly. *Maybe* you didn't stop napping. But I bet you tried a little. I bet some days you stayed awake for a whole story when before you'd have snoozed at the start."

Mero thought about it. A tiny smile crackled across his crust. "...I did stay awake for the whole sledding trip in autumn. I only fell asleep at the very, very end."

"There it is!" Dai Daruma bounced for joy. "A new year doesn't erase you. It just hands you a clean, fluffy page and says: go on, give it another try. That's the whole gift! The trying is allowed to begin again, fresh as morning."

Mero sat up a little straighter. His crumbs began, ever so faintly, to sparkle. "Then this year... I wish to stay awake for one more story than last year. Just one more. And next year, one more after that."

"A wish that grows!" said Dai Daruma. "The very best kind."

---

By now the whole shrine had gathered round, and the wishes came tumbling out like marbles from a tipped jar.

Niko Nigiri wished to try one thing the *messy* way, just to see how it felt (he was very proud and very nervous about this). Rin Ramen wished to learn one brand-new recipe so steamy it fogged up the windows. Momo Mochi, of course, wished for "more group squishes than the whole sky has stars."

And then everyone turned to Dai Daruma.

"What's YOUR wish, Coach?" asked Coro.

Dai Daruma looked up at his own blank eye, reflected in the shiny offering box. He grinned his determined grin.

"My wish," he said, "is the same as always. To help every wobbly friend remember that falling down is just the windup before the bounce. And — " he winked at the reader, because he could feel you watching, you cozy little night-owl — "to never, EVER run out of bounce."

Then they all crowded to the big bronze bell. Together they bowed, clapped twice, and rang it — *BONNNNG* — a sound so warm and round it seemed to roll all the way up to the morning sun.

And while it still hummed, Maru Matcha stepped quietly to the great festival drum. She closed her half-moon eyes. She breathed in slow, the way she always did. Then she lifted the mallet and — *BOOOOM!* — struck it with all her froth. The whole shrine cheered. Maru opened her eyes, looking faintly amazed at herself.

"That," she said calmly, "was extremely loud." And then she did something no one had ever seen Maru do: she giggled.

"Akemashite omedetou!" they shouted, all at once, in a great cheerful tangle of voices.

And as the bell's hum faded into the frosty air, Dai Daruma tipped sideways — and bounced right back up, ready as ever.

A brand-new year. A brand-new bounce. And a whole soft world of trying, waiting to begin.

## Goodnight Blessing

Little one, the year ahead is a clean, fluffy page, and not one word is written on it yet. You don't have to know everything tonight — you only have to be ready to try, the way a sleepy seed is ready for spring. If today wobbled, that's all right; tomorrow you simply bounce back up, soft and brave and new. May your wishes be small enough to start and big enough to grow, and may every fall be just the gentle windup before your next happy bounce. Snuggle down now, warm as a daruma in his cozy box, and let the new morning come when it's good and ready. Goodnight.
