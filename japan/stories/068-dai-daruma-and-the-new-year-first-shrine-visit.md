# Story 68: Dai Daruma and the New-Year First Shrine Visit

**Main Character:** Dai Daruma
**Guest Characters:** Maru Matcha; Coro the Korokke; Mero Melonpan
**Setting:** A bustling shrine on New Year's morning for the first visit (hatsumode)
**Theme:** A fresh year is a fresh chance to try
**Japanese Spotlight:** **akemashite omedetou** (ah-keh-mah-shee-teh oh-meh-deh-toh) — Happy New Year

---

The first sunrise of the year peeked over the snowy shrine gate, and a box lid popped open.

Out hopped Dai Daruma, crimson as a pomegranate. "WOBBLE-WOBBLE, WHEE!" Straight into a snowdrift. Only eyebrows showing.

This was **hatsumode** (hah-tsoo-moh-deh) — the year's first shrine visit, and the lanterns glowed like warm peaches.

"**Akemashite omedetou** (ah-keh-mah-shee-teh oh-meh-deh-toh) — Happy New Year!" hollered Dai from the top of a stone lantern.

"This year," said Dai, "I want every wobbly friend to make one brave wish. I'm the wish-coach!"

"Do wishes need a coach?" asked Maru Matcha, calmly sipping her own frothy hair.

"EVERYTHING needs a coach," said Dai. Somewhere a crow said "caw," which is crow for "ooh, awkward."

---

Coro the Korokke shuffled up first, nervous steam rising off his crispy head.

"What if I pick the WRONG wish?" said Coro, and a whole cloud burst out. "Phoooosh."

"A wish isn't a promise it goes perfectly," said Dai. "It's you, pointing at the thing you'll try."

Behind a snowy stone fox dozed Mero Melonpan, crust drooping. "I'm not wishing," he yawned.

"Last year I wished to stop dozing off," said Mero. "Then I dozed off during the wish."

"A new year," sighed Mero, "is just the old year wearing a party hat."

Dai opened his mouth to coach. Then the great festival drum on the steps creaked.

---

The stand had iced over. The huge round drum tipped, wobbled, and rolled off down the snowy steps.

"THE DRUM!" cried Dai, and hurled himself in front of it, eyebrows first.

The drum bounced him clean over its top. BOING. "I'm fine," said Dai, upside down.

It gathered snow as it rolled, growing from drum to boulder to a very round avalanche.

Coro planted himself in the path and steamed as hard as he could. "PHOOOOSH!"

The steam melted the path to slick ice. The drum went FASTER. "Oops," said Coro.

It thundered past the stone fox and scooped Mero, dozing mid-snore, onto its front.

---

Mero's eyes popped open. The whole shrine whooshed past. "I'm AWAKE," he yelled. "I'm so AWAKE!"

The snow-drum shot across the frozen pond and spun to a wobbling stop in the middle.

Mero clung on top, crumbs sparkling. "Most awake I've ever been," said Mero, amazed.

Dai bounced onto the ice and skidded clean past the drum. Coro followed and slid into Dai.

"Fall down seven," panted Dai from the bottom of the pile, "bounce up... hang on."

---

Maru stepped onto the ice. She breathed in slow. "Whisk slow," she said. "Feel calm."

She walked across the pond like a pond that was walking: steady, green, unhurried.

She slid her bamboo whisk under the drum's edge and leaned. The drum tipped upright with a creak.

Maru climbed up beside Mero, lifted the big mallet, and closed her half-moon eyes.

"I have always wanted to," she said quietly, "and always sat down instead. Not this year."

BOOOOM. The ice hummed. The lanterns swung.

The shrine roof shed all its snow at once, WHUMP, and the red gate blazed in the sun.

"That," said Maru calmly, "was extremely loud." And then she giggled. Nobody had ever seen that.

---

"I wish to make soup for everybody," said Coro, steaming gently. "The kind that feels like a hug."

"I wish to stay awake for one more story than last year," said Mero. "Then one more."

"Trying counts, even wobbly," said Dai. "A new year doesn't erase you. It gives you a clean page."

They bowed, clapped twice, and rang the bronze bell. BONNNG. "Akemashite omedetou!" they all shouted.

---

The bell's hum faded into the frost. The sun lay warm on the red gate.

Mero yawned, dozed, and woke. "Still awake," he murmured. "Mostly."

Dai tipped sideways and bounced back up. A brand-new year. A brand-new bounce.
