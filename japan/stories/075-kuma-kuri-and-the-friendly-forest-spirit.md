# Story 75: Kuma Kuri and the Friendly Forest Spirit

**Main Character:** Kuma the Kuri
**Guest Characters:** Mori, a small fluffy kodama forest spirit who lives at the shrine
**Setting:** A mossy forest shrine where a small, cuddly forest spirit lives
**Theme:** Kindness makes even shy spirits smile
**Japanese Spotlight:** **mori** (moh-ree) — forest
**Cameo:** Puck (Re:Zero) — yawns out of a tree hollow to complain about the noise, freezes the whole pond solid by stretching, sends the tipping shrine skating across the ice, and curls back up to doze; walk-on: Arrietty (The Secret World of Arrietty)

---

Kuma the Kuri was the shyest chestnut in the whole wide world, possibly several worlds beyond.

He waddled deep into the **mori** (moh-ree) — forest, counting fallen acorns. Two hundred and eleven.

A tiny bell went ting ahead, and Kuma leapt into his own scarf. "Oh dear," said the scarf.

The bell sounded lonely. Kuma tugged his scarf for courage and marched on, like a small acorn-knight.

---

The trail opened on the smallest shrine in the world, mossy-roofed, next to a puddle-sized pond.

Behind the bell cord, something fluffy wobbled. Round, white, ears like a dandelion puff.

It saw Kuma, squeaked, and dove behind the shrine. One wobbly ear stayed out.

"H-hello?" said Kuma, then panicked at his own boldness and dove behind a mushroom.

Peek. Hide. Four more times. "This," thought Kuma, "is the most exhausting conversation I've almost had."

"Are you here to make a wish?" peeped the fluffy thing, in a dewdrop-sized voice.

---

"I'm a **kodama** (koh-dah-mah) — tree spirit," it whispered. "I grant wishes. Only nobody comes."

"They think the forest is spooky," it sniffled. "I've practiced a hundred years. Watch!"

It scrunched its face, waved a leaf-arm, and a mushroom sneezed and fell over.

"The flower was supposed to BLOOM," it wailed. "Bloom, sparkle, traveler says ooh. I never get the ooh."

---

"BIGGER," it said, waving both arms. All of Kuma's acorns rose, hovered, and rained down like hail.

Kuma hid under his cap. Plink, plink, plonk. "I counted those," he said. "Twice."

"BIGGEST," squeaked the kodama, and the bell cord whipped around like a jump rope.

The cord yanked. The little shrine tipped slowly toward the pond, bell clanging.

---

From a tree hollow above the pond came a yawn, then a small grey cat spirit, stretching.

"Morning," said Puck, tail fluffing. "Could somebody keep the chaos down? Some of us are dozing."

He stretched longer. Frost crackled from his paws, and the pond froze solid with a crisp TINK.

The tipping shrine slid onto the ice and skated, slow and wobbly, toward the far bank.

"Oops," said Puck, blinking. "I do that when I stretch. Also when I sneeze. Also generally."

He curled back into the hollow, nose under tail, one ear still listening, and dozed.

"The pond is ice now," said Kuma, under his cap. "Today has so many parts."

---

Kuma dashed onto the ice, legs going everywhere, and bonked into the shrine's step.

Small and sturdy, he wedged under the edge, his roasted glow melting a grip into the frost. Nnngh.

"Small and roasty," he grunted. "Ready and... extremely... toasty."

The kodama pushed from the other side, fluff flattening. Creak. The shrine settled onto the moss.

"That," panted Kuma, flopping on the moss, "was more exercise than my entire life."

---

"Nobody's ever stayed long enough to give me a name," said the kodama, scuffing one leaf-foot.

"You're round and soft, and you're the heart of the forest," said Kuma. "What about Mori?"

Mori went pink with joy, bounced straight up, and rang the bell with its head. TING!

"I think I WAS the wish," giggled Mori. The mushroom stood back up, as if to say finally.

---

He gathered the acorns into a cozy ring and laid his leaf scarf on the cold step, nest-soft.

"That's the easy magic," said Kuma, glowing warm. "Making a place where someone feels safe."

Mori waved both leaf-arms, softly this time. The moss grew warm, like sun-sat stone. "No sneezing," whispered Mori.

---

Two shy friends sat on warm moss, not saying much, because shy friends don't need to.

Under a maple leaf by the step, a thumb-sized figure in a red dress whispered, "Shh."

Every so often Mori bounced and rang the bell. Ting. Kuma only smiled.

Kuma leaned his cap against Mori's fluff, and the little bell hummed, and the forest hummed back.

---
