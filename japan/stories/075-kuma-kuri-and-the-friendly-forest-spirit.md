# Story 75: Kuma Kuri and the Friendly Forest Spirit

**Main Character:** Kuma the Kuri
**Guest Characters:** Mori, a small fluffy kodama forest spirit who lives at the shrine
**Setting:** A mossy forest shrine where a small, cuddly forest spirit lives
**Theme:** Kindness makes even shy spirits smile
**Japanese Spotlight:** **mori** (moh-ree) — forest

---

Kuma the Kuri was the shyest chestnut in the whole wide world, and possibly several worlds beyond.

He waddled deep into the **mori** (moh-ree) — forest, counting fallen acorns. Two hundred and eleven.

Then a tiny bell went ting, somewhere ahead, and Kuma leapt straight into his own scarf.

"Oh dear," said the scarf, which had Kuma inside it.

The bell went ting again, and it sounded lonely. Kuma wanted to find it and keep it company.

So he tugged his scarf for courage and marched on, like a very small acorn-knight.

---

The trail opened on the smallest shrine in the world, mossy-roofed, no taller than Kuma.

Behind the bell cord, something fluffy wobbled. Round, white, with ears like a dandelion puff.

It saw Kuma, squeaked, and dove behind the shrine. One wobbly ear stayed poking out.

"H-hello?" said Kuma, then panicked at his own boldness and dove behind a mushroom.

The fluffy thing peeked. Kuma peeked. They both hid again. This happened four more times.

"This," thought Kuma, "is the most exhausting conversation I have ever almost had."

"Are you here to make a wish?" peeped the fluffy thing, in a dewdrop-sized voice.

---

"I'm a **kodama** (koh-dah-mah) — tree spirit," it whispered. "I grant wishes. Only nobody ever comes."

"They think the forest is spooky," it sniffled. "I've practiced for a hundred years. Watch!"

It scrunched its face, waved a leaf-arm, and a mushroom sneezed and fell over.

A beetle minding his own business got a tiny crown of dew. Quite stylish. Nobody had asked.

"The flower was supposed to BLOOM," it wailed. "Bloom, sparkle, traveler says ooh. I never get the ooh."

"I'll try BIGGER," it said, and waved both arms at once.

---

Every one of Kuma's two hundred and eleven acorns rose up, hovered, and rained down like hail.

Kuma hid under his cap. Plink, plink, plonk. "I counted those," he said. "Twice."

"BIGGEST," squeaked the kodama, and the bell cord whipped around like a jump rope.

The cord yanked. The whole little shrine tipped slowly toward the mud, bell clanging.

Kuma dashed. Small and sturdy, he wedged himself under the edge and held on. Nnngh.

"Small and roasty," he grunted. "Ready and... extremely... toasty."

The kodama pushed from the other side, fluff flattening. With a creak, the shrine settled back.

They flopped on the moss, panting. "That," said Kuma, "was more exercise than my entire life."

---

"Nobody's ever stayed long enough to give me a name," said the kodama, scuffing one leaf-foot.

"You're round, and soft, and you're the heart of the forest," said Kuma. "What about Mori?"

Mori went pink with joy, bounced straight up, and rang the bell with its head. TING!

"Did you just grant a wish?" asked Kuma.

"I think I WAS the wish," giggled Mori. The mushroom stood back up, as if to say finally.

---

"You've been practicing the hard magic," said Kuma. "Let's try the easy kind."

He gathered the fallen acorns into a cozy ring, like seats around a campfire.

He unwound his autumn-leaf scarf and laid it on the cold shrine step, soft as a nest.

"That's the magic," said Kuma, glowing warm. "Making a place where someone feels safe."

Mori waved both leaf-arms, softly this time. The moss beneath them grew warm, like sun-sat stone.

"I did it," whispered Mori. "I made it warm. No sneezing."

---

Two shy friends sat on warm moss, not saying much, because shy friends don't need to.

Every so often Mori bounced a little and rang the bell. Ting. Nobody startled anymore.

"Shy and brave might be the same thing," said Mori. "Just brave that takes its time."

Kuma leaned his fuzzy cap against Mori's fluff, and the little bell hummed, and the forest hummed back.

---
