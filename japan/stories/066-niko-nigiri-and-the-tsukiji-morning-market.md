# Story 66: Niko Nigiri and the Tsukiji Morning Market

**Main Character:** Niko Nigiri
**Guest Characters:** The Sushi-ya Express; a clam in an ice barrel who has not moved since Tuesday; Master Katsu, an old bonito stall-keeper
**Setting:** The bustling outer fish market at dawn in Tokyo
**Theme:** Preparation is a kind of magic
**Japanese Spotlight:** **junbi** (joon-bee) — preparation
**Cameo:** Rock Lee (Naruto) — runs his five-hundredth lap of the market before dawn, bowing to every stall at full speed and using Niko as a push-up weight; walk-on: Krillin (Dragon Ball)

---

Three in the morning, and the Sushi-ya Express rang its bell three times. Emergency.

"Friends!" called the truck, headlights flickering. "Today needs **hatsu-gatsuo** (haht-soo gah-tswoh) — the first bonito, and I have none!"

A blanket heap popped open. Out hopped Niko Nigiri, cape gleaming, headlamp already on.

"I volunteer," said Niko. "I did my **junbi** (joon-bee) — preparation. I have a pocket for my other pockets."

"Then ride with me," said the Express, noren fluttering. "We race the sun." Off they roared.

---

Niko marched into Tsukiji's lantern maze, to where the bonito stall had stood for thirty years.

Gone. In its place: three hundred pickle jars and an ice barrel with a clam in it.

"Everything moves here," said the clam, surfacing one inch. "I haven't moved since Tuesday. It's a lifestyle."

Niko spotted a drip-trail of melting ice and bowed. "You have been one-half helpful," he said.

"That's my record," said the clam, sinking, deeply satisfied.

---

The drips led to Master Katsu, a huge old bonito in a headband, sharpening an enormous knife.

Behind him, on glittering ice, lay three silver first bonito, polished by the sea.

"One first bonito, please!" said Niko, bowing so low his nori belt squeaked.

"Good bow, bad timing," rumbled Katsu. "Two are promised. The last goes to whoever finds my wasabi."

"Then I shall find wasabi," said Niko. "Six minutes. I have budgeted for seven."

---

Niko hopped through the pickle alley, the clam alley, and the squid alley.

A cart rattled past piled with something green. Niko gave chase. Cabbage. "Impostor," said Niko, very politely.

Then every lantern swung. Footsteps thundered. A green blur in orange leg warmers shot past.

"LAP FOUR HUNDRED AND NINETY-EIGHT!" shouted Rock Lee, bowing to the squid stall without slowing down.

His slipstream snatched Niko's headlamp, his satchel, then Niko, who sailed behind like a kite.

Lap four hundred ninety-nine: Rock Lee scooped Niko up and did push-ups. "Excellent training weight!"

"FIVE HUNDRED!" shouted Rock Lee. He set Niko on a barrel, bowed, and held a handstand.

---

Niko wobbled through a side door into the tuna auction: frozen tuna like silver submarines.

"NEW BIDDER!" boomed the bell-stand. "HE BIDS THE SATCHEL!"

"I am not a bidder!" squeaked Niko. "I am a pedestrian!"

"A PEDESTRIAN! BOLD STRATEGY! DO I HEAR TWO PEDESTRIANS?" Forty flags shot up.

A short bald fighter in an orange gi held up one coin. "For the whole tuna?" asked Krillin.

Niko went under, between two tuna like canoes, and popped out the far door sideways.

Cape backwards. Dignity at sixty percent. And the sky was PINK.

---

Niko slumped against the ice barrel. "I prepared for everything," he sighed, "except the market."

"Still here," said the clam. "Still thriving. You smell desperate. Is it wasabi?"

The clam rose one inch. Beneath it, in the ice, sat a knobbly green wasabi root.

"Been sitting on it since Tuesday," said the clam. "Lifestyle."

Niko bowed so fast his belt squeaked twice, scooped up the root, and hopped off.

---

He arrived, wasabi high like a tiny green trophy. The ice bed was empty. All three fish, gone.

Something inside Niko went quiet, like a lantern blown out. Then he remembered a doodled gate-whisper.

Katsu keeps one back, for whoever asks properly. Niko set the wasabi down and bowed his deepest bow.

"**Ohayō gozaimasu** (oh-hah-yoh goh-zah-ee-mahss) — good morning, Master Katsu," said Niko. "I respectfully ask for the hatsu-gatsuo."

Katsu laughed a harbor-bell laugh and lifted a cedar box: inside, the most beautiful fish of all.

"The loud ones grab," rumbled Katsu. "The hurried ones point. The prepared ones notice."

---

The Express purred home through sunrise honey-light. "Junbi," yawned Niko, "is magic you do in advance."

At the market, the clam watched the sunrise. "Big day," it said. "Time to rest." It already was.
