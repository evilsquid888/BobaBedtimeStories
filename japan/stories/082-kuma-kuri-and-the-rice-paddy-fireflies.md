# Story 82: Kuma Kuri and the Rice-Paddy Fireflies

**Main Character:** Kuma the Kuri (roasted chestnut / kuri kinton sweet)
**Guest Characters:** A thumb-sized frog who says "Kero"; Mister Croak, a fat ancient toad; one small shy firefly
**Setting:** Terraced rice paddies glowing with summer fireflies at dusk
**Theme:** The smallest lights make the most magical night
**Japanese Spotlight:** **hotaru** (hoh-tah-roo) — firefly
**Cameo:** Gamakichi (Naruto) — puffs in on orange smoke to visit Mister Croak, the two toads argue about whose pond is bigger, and his proving belly-flop sweeps the firefly toward the spout; walk-on: Teto (Nausicaä)

---

If you have never seen a chestnut try to be invisible, you have missed something funny.

Kuma the Kuri was hiding behind one blade of rice grass, which covered, generously, half his cap.

A thumb-sized frog plopped onto his head. "Kero?" Which in frog means: I can see you, brown lump.

Down the hillside ran the **tanada** (tah-nah-dah) — terraced rice paddies, silver step after silver step.

The village festival glittered far below. Kuma wanted one quiet evening where nobody looked at him.

"If I stay very still," he whispered, "nobody will notice me, and that will be lovely."

---

He climbed onto the low stone wall of the top paddy, the frog riding his cap.

Then, out of the rice, one tiny green-gold spark floated up: glowing, fading, glowing.

"That," said a gravelly voice beside him, "is a **hotaru** (hoh-tah-roo) — firefly. And he's dithering."

On the wall sat a fat ancient toad in a dried-leaf hat. Mister Croak, mid-yawn.

"Little ones worry they're not bright enough," said Mister Croak. "Sound like anyone you know, lumpy?"

---

A puff of wind tipped the firefly, and plip, he landed in the paddy water, wings wet.

He drifted, blinking weakly, toward the stone spout where water poured to the next terrace.

Kuma's roasty heart thumped. He flung his scarf out like a rope. Two chestnut-lengths short.

He grabbed a rice stalk and leaned way out. It bent, bent, and flicked him into the mud.

"Kero," said the frog, which meant: very funny, and also, hurry up.

---

POOF. Orange smoke burst over the wall, and a toad the size of a chair landed beside Croak.

"Yo, old-timer!" croaked Gamakichi, bright orange in a little blue vest. "Came to see your famous pond."

"It is a paddy," said Mister Croak, narrowing his eyes. "And bigger than yours."

"Bigger?" Gamakichi puffed up his chest until the vest buttons strained. "My pond has a WATERFALL."

Gamakichi belly-flopped in to prove his point. SPLOOSH. A wave swept the firefly toward the spout.

Gamakichi clambered out dripping, plonked down on the wall, and blew one smug smoke ring.

"Still smaller than my paddy," muttered Mister Croak, and shut his eyes to snore about it.

---

Kuma clambered onto the frog. Boing. Boing. A chestnut on a frog is not graceful, but fast.

At the spout, Kuma held out his cap. The spark tumbled in as the frog's hop ran out.

Over the ledge went all three. SHLORP. A long sticky tongue caught them midair, gently.

"Tol' you I wasn't asleep," said Mister Croak, and went back to snoring.

---

Kuma cupped his paws around the wet spark, not to catch it. Just a warm room.

The firefly's wings dried, but he only flickered, unsure.

So Kuma stood up on the wall, in full view, and let his roasty glow show.

"You don't have to be the brightest," he whispered. "Just brave enough to be seen."

The firefly rose from his paws and stayed bright, climbing like a lantern that had decided to shine.

---

And the valley answered: one light from the bottom paddy, then ten, then the whole hillside breathed.

Thousands of hotaru rose from every silver step in slow spirals, until the valley was floating stars.

"Best show in the **inaka** (ee-nah-kah) — countryside," mumbled Mister Croak. "Takes one to go first."

Fireflies landed on Kuma's cap, his scarf, his round shoulders, until he glowed head to toe.

"Kero," said the frog on his head, which meant: now THAT is interesting.

---

The moon came up silver, and the fireflies drifted down to rest in the rice.

A small fox-squirrel with a striped bushy tail hopped onto Kuma's shoulder and chirped. Teto.

The smallest firefly floated beside them down the glowing terraces, blinking thank you.

And a muddy chestnut walked home feeling, for once, exactly the right size.
