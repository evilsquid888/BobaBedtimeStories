# Story 82: Kuma Kuri and the Rice-Paddy Fireflies

**Main Character:** Kuma the Kuri (roasted chestnut / kuri kinton sweet)
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** Terraced rice paddies glowing with summer fireflies at dusk
**Theme:** The smallest lights make the most magical night
**Japanese Spotlight:** ほたる (hotaru) = firefly

---

Now, if you have never seen a chestnut try to be invisible, you have missed one of the funniest sights in all the world. Kuma the Kuri was glossy and mahogany-brown, with a fuzzy little cap and an autumn-leaf scarf he wore even in summer because it made him feel brave — and right now he was attempting to hide behind a single blade of rice grass that was, generously, half his height.

"If I stay very still," he whispered to himself, "nobody will notice me, and I won't have to say anything clever, and that will be lovely."

A frog the size of a thumb plopped down beside him, looked him up and down, and said, "Kero?" which in frog means roughly *"You are a brown lump behind a green stick and I can absolutely see you."*

Kuma sighed his small roasty sigh. "I know," he admitted. "I'm not very good at being seen."

---

It was the warm blue hour at the edge of a mountain village, the time when the day folds itself up like a quilt and the evening tucks the valley in. All down the hillside ran the **棚田** (tanada) = terraced rice paddies, step after silver step of water-mirror, each one holding a little wobbling reflection of the sky. The young rice stood in tidy green rows, swishing softly, smelling of clean water and warm mud and something faintly sweet that might have been the night itself getting ready.

Kuma had wandered up here on his own. He liked being on his own, mostly. The festival down in the village was bright and loud and full of friends shouting his name in the cheerful way that made his roasted heart glow and also, somehow, made him want to climb inside his own cap and stay there.

"There's a lot of *noticing* at festivals," he told the frog, who was now sitting on his head. "People notice you, and then you have to be interesting. I'm not very interesting. I'm a chestnut. I'm mostly just... warm."

"Kero," said the frog, which this time meant *"Warm is underrated, my dude."*

Kuma decided he liked this frog.

He climbed up onto the low stone wall of the very top paddy and sat with his stubby legs dangling, watching the water go from blue to violet to a deep soft grey. It was so quiet up here. Not lonely-quiet. *Cozy*-quiet, the kind that wraps around you like a scarf. He pulled his autumn-leaf scarf a little tighter and felt, for once, perfectly the right size.

That was when the first light came on.

It floated up out of the rice, slow and dreamy as a yawn — a tiny, tiny green-gold spark, glowing soft, fading, glowing soft again. It drifted right up past Kuma's nose and he held his breath so as not to blow it away.

"Oh," he breathed. "Oh, hello."

"That," said a tired, gravelly little voice, "is a **ほたる** (hotaru) = firefly. And he's showing off."

Kuma turned. On the stone beside him sat a fat, ancient toad with droopy eyes and a voice like a creaky gate, wearing — Kuma was fairly sure — a single dried leaf as a hat.

"Showing off?" Kuma said.

"Best he can," said the toad, whom Kuma decided to call Mister Croak. "He's a small one. The little ones always worry they're not bright enough. They sit down in the rice all evening, dithering. *Is my light too small? Will anybody see me? Maybe I'll just stay down here in the dark where it's safe.*" The toad gave Kuma a long, pointed look. "Sound like anyone you know, lumpy?"

Kuma turned the exact color of a chestnut that has just been caught.

---

"I'm not dithering," Kuma protested. "I'm... *resting.* Bravely."

"Mm," said Mister Croak.

The single firefly drifted lower, hovering just above the dark water, and Kuma watched it blink its little heart-light on, and off, and on. And he noticed — because Kuma, for all his shyness, noticed *everything* — that the firefly really did seem nervous. It would glow up bright as if to say *here I am!*, and then dim down quick as if to say *never mind, sorry, forget I said anything.*

"He thinks he's too small to matter," Kuma whispered. "He thinks one little light can't do anything in all this big dark valley."

"Does he now," said Mister Croak, and yawned a yawn so wide Kuma could have parked Kei the kei-truck in it.

Kuma looked at the firefly. The firefly, in its blinky firefly way, looked at Kuma. And Kuma felt something warm and roasty and unexpectedly brave wake up inside his chest, the way it always did — quietly, at the last possible moment, exactly when somebody needed it.

He leaned down close to the water and cupped his little chestnut hands around the tiny glowing thing, not to catch it, just to make a cozy room of warmth around it.

"Hey," he said softly. "Hey, little **ほたる**. You don't have to be the brightest. You just have to be the bravest. And the bravest thing isn't being big." He swallowed. "It's letting yourself be *seen.* Even when you're scared. Even when you're small. *Especially* then."

He felt extremely silly saying this to a bug.

He felt extremely surprised to realize he was saying it to himself.

The firefly glowed — once, soft.

Then it floated up, up, out of Kuma's gentle hands, and it did not dim. It blazed its little green-gold heart out across the water, steady and proud and unafraid, climbing into the darkening air like a tiny lantern that had finally decided it deserved to shine.

And the valley answered.

---

It was as if the first firefly had whispered a secret down through all the rice, and the secret was: *it's all right. You can come out now.*

From the bottom paddy a light winked on. Then two. Then ten. Then the whole green hillside seemed to *breathe* — out of every silver step of water rose the **ほたる**, hundreds of them, thousands, drifting up in slow lazy spirals, blinking and glowing and swirling, until the entire valley was filled floor to sky with floating soft-gold stars.

The dark paddy water held them all twice — once in the air and once in the mirror — so that Kuma sat in the middle of a whole *universe* of gentle, bashful, beautiful little lights, above and below, glowing all around him like the inside of a warm dream.

"Mister Croak," he whispered, and his voice wobbled with wonder. "Mister Croak, *look.*"

But the toad was already nodding off, and only mumbled, "Tol' you. Small ones. Best show in the **田舎** (inaka) = countryside, every single summer. All it takes is one to go first."

Kuma laughed — a soft, delighted, roasty laugh that bubbled right up out of him before he could think to hide it. He stood up on the wall. He spread his stubby arms wide. And for the first time in his whole quiet life, surrounded by ten thousand tiny lights that were each exactly as small and as scared and as brave as he was, Kuma the Kuri did not try to be invisible at all.

He glowed. Not with light, of course — he was a chestnut, and chestnuts do not glow, that would be ridiculous. But the fireflies landed all over him, on his fuzzy cap and his autumn-leaf scarf and his round little shoulders, dozens of them blinking gently, until Kuma stood there on the stone wall lit up soft and gold from head to toe, looking exactly like the bravest, warmest, most twinkly little lantern the mountain had ever grown.

"Squish — I mean —" He didn't have a catchphrase ready for this. He thought about it. He settled on his own. "*Small and roasty,*" he whispered to the swirling lights, "*ready and toasty.*"

The frog on his head blinked. "Kero," it said, which meant *"Now THAT is interesting."*

---

He stayed until the moon came up silver over the highest paddy, and the fireflies began, one by one, drifting back down to rest in the cool sweet rice. He stayed until Mister Croak snored a snore that rippled the water. He stayed until his eyes went soft and heavy and the warm mud-and-water smell of the valley had wrapped clear around his heart.

And as he finally climbed down to wander sleepily home, the very last firefly — a tiny one, the smallest of all — floated up beside him and blinked, once, as if to say *thank you for going first.*

Kuma smiled. "Anytime, little friend," he said. "Turns out the smallest lights make the most magical night. We just have to be brave enough to shine."

Then he tucked his scarf up snug, and walked down the glowing terraces toward home, leaving one set of small round chestnut footprints in the warm summer dark — and feeling, for the very first time, absolutely the perfect size to be exactly who he was.

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, you do not have to be the brightest light in the whole wide valley. You only have to be brave enough to let your own soft glow be seen. Somewhere out in the warm summer dark, the **ほたる** are rising over the quiet rice, blinking their tiny hearts on and off, on and off, just for you. Close your eyes and let them carry you gently down into sleep, where everything is cozy and nothing is too small to matter. You are warm. You are loved. You are exactly the right size. Goodnight.
