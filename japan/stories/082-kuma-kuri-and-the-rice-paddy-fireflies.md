# Story 82: Kuma Kuri and the Rice-Paddy Fireflies

**Main Character:** Kuma the Kuri (roasted chestnut / kuri kinton sweet)
**Guest Characters:** A thumb-sized frog who says "Kero"; Mister Croak, a fat ancient toad; one small shy firefly
**Setting:** Terraced rice paddies glowing with summer fireflies at dusk
**Theme:** The smallest lights make the most magical night
**Japanese Spotlight:** **hotaru** (hoh-tah-roo) — firefly

---

If you have never seen a chestnut try to be invisible, you have missed something funny.

Kuma the Kuri was hiding behind one blade of rice grass, generously, half his height.

"If I stay very still," he whispered, "nobody will notice me, and that will be lovely."

A thumb-sized frog plopped down beside him. "Kero?" Which in frog means: I can see you, brown lump.

Down the hillside ran the **tanada** (tah-nah-dah) — terraced rice paddies, silver step after silver step.

The village festival glittered far below. Kuma wanted one quiet evening where nobody looked at him.

---

He climbed onto the low stone wall of the top paddy, the frog riding on his cap.

"Festivals are full of noticing," he told the frog. "I'm not interesting. I'm just warm."

"Kero," said the frog, which meant: warm is underrated, my dude.

Then, out of the rice, one tiny green-gold spark floated up: glowing, fading, glowing.

"That," said a gravelly voice beside him, "is a **hotaru** (hoh-tah-roo) — firefly. And he's dithering."

On the wall sat a fat ancient toad in a dried-leaf hat. Mister Croak.

"Little ones worry they're not bright enough," said Mister Croak. "Sound like anyone you know, lumpy?"

"I'm not dithering," said Kuma. "I'm resting. Bravely."

---

A puff of wind tipped him, and plip, he landed in the paddy water, wings wet.

He drifted, blinking weakly, toward the stone spout where the water poured down to the next terrace.

Kuma's roasty heart thumped. The toad was already snoring. It was up to him.

He flung his scarf out like a rope. It landed two chestnut-lengths short.

He grabbed a rice stalk and leaned out. It bent, bent, and flicked him into the mud.

"Kero," said the frog, which meant: very funny, and also, get on.

---

Kuma clambered onto the frog. Boing. Boing. A chestnut on a frog is not graceful, but it's fast.

The firefly reached the spout. The water tugged him toward the edge.

Kuma held out his cap. The spark tumbled in just as the frog's hop ran out.

Over the ledge they went, all three, toward the paddy below.

SHLORP. A long sticky tongue caught them midair and set them gently on the wall.

"Tol' you I wasn't asleep," said Mister Croak, and went back to snoring.

---

Kuma cupped his paws around the wet spark, not to catch it. Just to make a warm room.

"You don't have to be the brightest," he whispered. "Just brave enough to be seen. Especially when small."

He felt silly saying this to a bug. He felt surprised to be saying it to himself.

The firefly's wings dried. He glowed once, soft. Then he rose from Kuma's paws, and stayed bright.

He blazed his tiny heart out across the water, climbing like a lantern that had decided to shine.

---

And the valley answered: one light from the bottom paddy, then ten, then the whole hillside breathed.

Thousands of hotaru rose from every silver step, drifting in slow spirals, until the valley was floating stars.

The water held them twice, above and below, so Kuma sat in a universe of bashful little lights.

"Best show in the **inaka** (ee-nah-kah) — countryside," mumbled Mister Croak. "Takes one to go first."

Kuma stood up on the wall, spread his stubby arms wide, and let himself be seen.

Fireflies landed on his cap, his scarf, his round shoulders, until he glowed head to toe.

"Small and roasty," he whispered to the swirling lights. "Ready and toasty."

"Kero," said the frog on his head, which meant: now THAT is interesting.

---

The moon came up silver, and the fireflies drifted down to rest in the rice.

The smallest firefly floated beside Kuma down the glowing terraces, blinking thank you.

And a muddy chestnut walked home feeling, for once, exactly the right size.
