# Story 87: Yuki and the Snow-Country Village

**Main Character:** Yuki the Snow Monkey-Manju
**Guest Characters:** Granny Gyoza, a round pleated dumpling granny; Old Kurumi, a gray-shelled walnut; a lane of rice-ball neighbors
**Setting:** The thatched-roof snow village of Shirakawa-go in deep winter
**Theme:** A village keeps each other warm
**Japanese Spotlight:** **ie** (ee-eh) — house / home

---

Yuki the Snow Monkey-Manju hopped off the bus and landed nose-first in a snowdrift. Poff.

"Mmmmf," said Yuki, which is monkey-manju for hello, snow.

She wiggled free and gasped at **Shirakawa-go** (shee-rah-kah-wah-goh), roofs steep as giant mittens, elbow-deep in white.

Snow sat in little hats on every fence post. Yuki was quite sure one winked.

"A whole village made of cozy," she sighed. "I'll nap by a fire for a hundred years."

"You'd be buried by morning!" called a voice from a doorway.

---

It was Granny Gyoza, a round pleated dumpling with flour on her apron, waving a wooden spoon.

"In, in! This is my **ie** (ee-eh) — my house, my home. Before the cold nibbles your ears."

Inside: a crackling hearth, a roof curved like a sleepy turtle, a smell of toasty rice and woodsmoke.

Yuki melted onto the hearth mat. "Warm cheeks, warm heart, warm friends. I live here now."

Granny tapped the window with her spoon. "The big snow comes this afternoon. Taller than the houses."

"Taller than the HOUSES?" said Yuki, and her nap got up and quietly left.

"Everybody digs," said Granny. "Even sleepy monkey-buns. ESPECIALLY sleepy monkey-buns."

---

Out in the lane stood a row of rice-ball neighbors in scarves, holding shovels taller than themselves.

"We work **yui** (yoo-ee) — all together as one!" called Granny. "No ie gets left buried!"

Yuki raised her shovel. The shovel tipped her backwards into a drift. Poff.

"...Yui?" squeaked the drift.

Granny pulled her out. "First lesson. Hold the shovel. Don't let the shovel hold YOU."

Then the sky went the color of a wool blanket, and down came the snow in fat fistfuls.

---

The neighbors dug in a line, scoop-toss-step, scoop-toss-step, humming an old song to keep warm.

Yuki was too short for the drifts. But she was round. Boing, boing: she bounced the snow flat.

"The bun is making a ROAD!" cheered the littlest rice-ball neighbor, pointing at the squashed trail.

"I'm a snow-pounder!" puffed Yuki, who did not know what she was saying, but everyone clapped.

One ie cleared. Then another. Then the baker-bun's, where warm rolls came down the line.

---

At the very end of the lane, the snow had piled clean over a tiny round door.

"H-hello?" came a thin voice from inside. "My door won't open."

It was Old Kurumi, a walnut so elderly his shell had gone gray. He lived there alone.

The neighbors dug. The drift slid right back down, like a lazy white pillow.

They all pushed together. The drift pushed back. Everyone slid down in one scarfy heap.

---

"I can fit," said Yuki.

Everyone turned. She pointed at the little chimney poking out of the snow.

"I'm small and squishy and I bounce. Boost me up. We dig from both sides and meet."

Paw over mitten over spoon, they boosted her up onto the mitten roof.

She took one brave breath, squeaked, and dropped down the warm chimney. POFF.

---

A fluffy bun popped out of the fireplace, dusted with soot, like a very surprised snowball.

"You came," whispered Kurumi. "I thought I'd be alone all winter."

"Nobody's alone here," said Yuki. "That's the whole point of a village. Now, DIG!"

Kurumi pushed with his walnut shoulders. Yuki bounced. Outside, the humming came closer.

A wooden spoon poked through the drift.

"GRANNY!" cheered Yuki, and the drift collapsed in a glittering whoosh.

---

There stood the whole village in the dark, every door glowing gold, arms wide for one group squish.

"A house keeps the cold out," said Granny. "A village keeps you warm."

---

Later, by Granny's fire, everyone piled under quilts with sweet soup, and Kurumi's shell looked less gray.

"Warm cheeks," yawned Yuki, "warm heart, warm..." and she was fast asleep before friends.
