# Story 87: Yuki and the Snow-Country Village

**Main Character:** Yuki the Snow Monkey-Manju
**Guest Characters:** Granny Gyoza, a round pleated dumpling granny; Old Kurumi, a gray-shelled walnut; a lane of rice-ball neighbors
**Setting:** The thatched-roof snow village of Shirakawa-go in deep winter
**Theme:** A village keeps each other warm
**Japanese Spotlight:** **ie** (ee-eh) — house / home
**Cameo:** Edward and Alphonse Elric (Fullmetal Alchemist) — a neighbor calls Ed small, he claps up a snow wall that falls on him and reburies the lane, and Alphonse apologizes with a kitten in his chest plate; walk-on: Alolan Vulpix (Pokémon)

---

Yuki the Snow Monkey-Manju hopped off the bus and landed nose-first in a snowdrift. Poff.

She wiggled free and gasped at **Shirakawa-go** (shee-rah-kah-wah-goh): roofs like giant mittens, elbow-deep in white.

"A whole village made of cozy," she sighed. "I'll nap by a fire for a hundred years."

"You'd be buried by morning!" called a pleated dumpling granny from a doorway, waving a spoon.

---

"In, in!" said Granny Gyoza. "My **ie** (ee-eh) — my house, my home. Before the cold nibbles your ears."

Inside: a crackling hearth, a roof curved like a dozing turtle, a smell of toasty rice.

Yuki melted onto the hearth mat. "Warm cheeks, warm heart, warm friends. I live here now."

Granny tapped the window. "The big snow comes this afternoon. Taller than the houses. Everybody digs."

"Taller than the HOUSES?" said Yuki, and her nap got up and quietly left.

---

Out in the lane stood a row of rice-ball neighbors in scarves, shovels taller than themselves.

"We work **yui** (yoo-ee) — all together as one!" called Granny. "No ie gets left buried!"

Yuki raised her shovel. The shovel tipped her backwards into a drift. Poff.

"...Yui?" squeaked the drift.

Granny pulled her out. "Hold the shovel. Don't let the shovel hold YOU."

---

Down came snow in fat fistfuls. The neighbors dug in a line, scoop-toss-step, humming.

Yuki was too short for the drifts. But she was round. Boing, boing: snow bounced flat.

"The bun is making a ROAD!" cheered the littlest rice-ball neighbor.

"I'm a snow-pounder!" puffed Yuki, who had no idea what she was saying, but everyone clapped.

---

At the very end of the lane, the snow had piled clean over a tiny round door.

"My door won't open!" came a thin voice: Old Kurumi, a walnut so elderly his shell was gray.

The neighbors dug. The drift slid right back down, like a lazy white pillow.

They all pushed together. The drift pushed back. Everyone slid down in one scarfy heap.

---

Up the lane crunched a short teen in a red coat, blond braid swinging, one arm shiny metal.

Behind him clanked a tall hollow suit of grey armor, a kitten peeking from its chest plate.

"Ooh, another small one!" cheered the littlest rice-ball neighbor. "Small ones make the ROADS!"

"WHO are you calling a pipsqueak BEAN?!" roared Edward, and clapped his palms with a crack.

Blue sparks. The drift reared into a snow wall taller than the roofs, wobbled, and fell on him.

WHUMP. Edward vanished. So did Yuki's whole snow road. Only a blond braid poked out, twitching.

"So sorry about my brother," said Alphonse gently, and sat down beside the braid.

---

Yuki popped out of the new drift. "That was... a lot. New plan."

She pointed at the chimney poking from the snow. "I'm small and squishy and I bounce."

Paw over mitten over spoon, they boosted her up onto the mitten roof.

She took one brave breath, squeaked, and dropped down the warm chimney. POFF.

---

A fluffy bun popped out of the fireplace, dusted with soot, like a very surprised snowball.

"You came," whispered Kurumi. "I thought I'd be alone all winter."

"Nobody's alone here," said Yuki. "That's the whole point of a village. Now, DIG!"

Kurumi pushed. Yuki bounced. Outside, the humming came closer. A wooden spoon poked through.

"GRANNY!" cheered Yuki, and the drift collapsed in a glittering whoosh.

---

There stood the whole village in the dark, every door glowing gold, arms wide for one group squish.

On the drift sat an Alolan Vulpix, a snow-white fox with six tails, yipping coolly.

"A house keeps the cold out," said Granny. "A village keeps you warm."

---

Later, by Granny's fire, everyone piled under quilts with sweet soup, and Kurumi's shell looked less gray.

"Warm cheeks," yawned Yuki, "warm heart, warm..." and she was fast asleep before friends.
