# Story 15: Dora Dorayaki and the Robot-Cat Pen Pal

**Main Character:** Dora the Dorayaki
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A cozy stationery and gadget shop full of curious gizmos
**Theme:** Kindness travels farther than any gadget
**Japanese Spotlight:** **tegami** (teh-gah-mee) — letter

---

Dora the Dorayaki adjusted his comfy beret, pushed open the jingly door of the little shop on Crumb Corner, and immediately fell in love with everything inside.

"Oh, hello," he whispered to a stapler shaped like a duck. "Aren't you wonderful."

The shop was called Gizmo & Quill, and it was the coziest, most curious place in the whole neighborhood — a stationery and gadget store stuffed wall to wall with marvels. There were pens that smelled like strawberries, erasers that squeaked "oops!", a teapot with arms, and a clock that ran backward "just to keep things interesting." Dora's red-bean smile stretched so wide you could see his sweet filling.

He had come to buy a single sheet of nice paper. He was the kind of dorayaki who collected little facts the way other folks collect buttons, and tonight he wanted to write down his very favorite fact so he wouldn't forget it.

But then, from the dustiest, snuggliest shelf in the back, a small voice went: *"Beep. Hello, friend. Do you have a tegami for me?"*

---

Dora waddled toward the voice. There, between a jar of glitter and a sleeping calculator, sat a chubby toy robot shaped like a cat — round and tin-blue, with a bell on its collar, two little antenna-whiskers, and a belly door that opened like a mailbox slot.

"A *tegami*?" Dora repeated.

"A **tegami** (teh-gah-mee) — letter," the robot-cat said proudly, its whiskers twitching. "I collect them. I keep them warm in my tummy. Then I deliver them to friends who are feeling a teeny bit lonely. Beep!" It blinked two heart-shaped eyes. "My name is Postcat. Most of my springs are rusty and one of my wheels squeaks, but my mail-tummy works *perfectly*."

Dora clapped his pancake-hands. "That is the loveliest job I have ever heard of! And I LOVE jobs that involve snacks and stories and being kind. That's basically my three favorite things stacked into one."

Postcat's bell gave a sad little *tink*. "But there is a problem, friend. Tonight I have three letters to deliver — three friends waiting in three corners of the neighborhood. And my squeaky wheel finally gave up. I cannot roll anywhere." A tiny puff of steam wheezed from its ears. "The letters will get cold. And a cold tegami is no good to a lonely heart."

Dora straightened his beret with great seriousness. He was a cozy fellow, not a fast one — but he had pancake-feet, a warm middle, and a heart full of *let's-go*.

"Postcat," he said, "I have legs. Squishy ones, but legs. Pop open that tummy. We're delivering some kindness."

---

*Clink!* Postcat's belly door swung open, and three letters tumbled out, each folded into a different little animal shape. Dora gasped with delight, because if there's one thing better than a letter, it's a letter that's also *origami*.

"The first," said Postcat, "goes to Maru Matcha, up on Quiet Hill. She has been very calm lately. Maybe a *little too* calm. I think she could use a hello."

Dora tucked the folded-crane letter under his beret to keep it toasty and set off into the soft blue evening. The streetlamps were just blinking awake, one by one, like shy fireflies clearing their throats.

Up on Quiet Hill, Maru Matcha sat whisking a tiny bowl, her frothy matcha-cream swirl perfectly still. "Whisk slow," she murmured to no one. "Feel calm." But her half-moon eyes were just the *tiniest* bit lonesome.

"Special delivery!" Dora announced, a touch out of breath. "One warm tegami, hand-toasted under an official beret."

Maru blinked. She unfolded the paper crane carefully, the way she did everything. Inside, in wobbly robot-printing, it said: *"Dear Maru. I sat outside your window yesterday and watched you whisk. It was the most peaceful thing I ever saw. Thank you for being a quiet I can rest in. — A Friend."*

Maru Matcha did not cry, because matcha is far too composed for that. But her swirl gave one happy little wobble, the way a calm pond ripples when a single leaf lands on it. "Oh," she said softly. "Someone *noticed.* I always thought being quiet meant being invisible." She turned the little crane over in her hands as if it were a warm coal on a cold night. "But quiet isn't the same as alone, is it?"

"Not even a little bit," Dora agreed, settling beside her. "Quiet is just kindness with its voice turned down low."

Maru offered him a sip of warm tea, frothy and grassy-sweet. "Tell me — who sent it?"

"A robot-cat with a mail-tummy and a big heart," said Dora. "His wheel broke, so I'm his legs tonight."

Maru smiled her slow, gentle smile, and for the first time all evening the loneliness slipped out of her half-moon eyes like steam off a fresh bowl. "Then kindness has found itself good legs. Whisk slow, friend." She paused, then added with the faintest deadpan twinkle: "But also — *hurry*. Two more hearts are waiting, and hearts are terribly impatient things." And she pressed a warm rice cracker into his hands for the road, because that is simply what you do, and tucked a sprig of dried sweetgrass behind his beret "for luck, and for the smell."

---

By the time Dora reached the second address, the moon had climbed up like a soft-boiled egg into the sky. The letter wanted to go to the canal, where Tai Taiyaki liked to "swim" through the air above the water on warm nights.

"AHOY!" splashed Tai, doing a happy loop. His see-through smile glowed with his red-bean heart. "Are you here to swim with me? Swim toward the sweet stuff!"

"Better," said Dora. "I'm here with a tegami." He handed over the letter, which was folded — naturally — into a tiny fish.

Tai unfolded it with both fins, fluttering. *"Dear Tai,"* it read. *"I always see you swimming all alone out here at night and being so brave about it. But brave and lonely can live in the same pond. So here is a hello, swimming over to keep you company. — A Friend."*

Tai went very quiet, which for Tai was a small miracle. "I *do* swim alone," he admitted, his fin-tail drooping just a smidge. "I tell everyone it's because I'm brave. But mostly it's because I thought nobody wanted to come out this far." He looked up, eyes shining. "Somebody came out this far. In a *letter.*"

"That's the thing about a tegami," Dora said warmly, settling onto the canal wall and dangling his pancake-feet over the gleaming water. "A friend can't always be next to you. But a letter? A letter can swim *anywhere.* Up canals, over rooftops, all the way to the far end of a lonely pond. It's like a hug that learned to travel."

Tai turned this over in his bubbly head. "So even when I'm out here all by myself... someone could still reach me. Just by *writing.*"

"Just by caring enough to fold the paper," said Dora.

Tai laughed — a splashy, bubbly, full-fin laugh that sent happy ripples skittering across the whole canal. "Then I'm going to write one BACK. A whole SCHOOL of them! One to you, one to the letter-friend, one to everybody who ever swam somewhere alone!" He immediately tried to write on a lily pad with the tip of his tail, made a magnificent inky mess, accidentally dotted his own nose, and laughed so hard he did three loops in a row. Dora laughed too, until his beret slid sideways. He promised to bring proper paper tomorrow — and pens that smelled like strawberries, if Tai was lucky. He could already think of a shop that had plenty.

---

Now there was one letter left — and a problem.

Dora squinted at the last envelope. The address was smudged. All it said was a single, soggy word: *"...lonely."*

"Oh dear," Dora murmured. "Postcat, who is this one *for*?"

But Postcat was all the way back at the shop. There was no name, no street, no helpful little arrow. Just a letter, and the whole sleepy neighborhood, and a dorayaki with tired pancake-feet and absolutely no idea where to go.

A lesser snack might have given up. A lesser snack might have grumbled, "Well, no address, no delivery," and waddled home to bed. But Dora was a collector of facts, and he remembered an important one: *a letter without a name still belongs to* somebody. He sat on a curb under a humming streetlamp and thought, and thought, turning the little envelope this way and that. He checked it for hidden arrows. He held it up to the lamplight. He even asked a passing moth, very politely, if it knew anybody who was lonely — but the moth only bonked the lamp and flew off, which was no help at all. Dora's beret slowly slid over one eye, the way it always did when his brain was working hardest.

Then he did the only sensible thing. He opened the letter — just to peek for a clue.

Inside, the wobbly robot-printing said: *"Dear You. Yes, YOU, reading this. If you found this letter, it means you needed it. I get lonely too, you know — sitting on my dusty shelf, waiting. So here is a hello for whoever needs one tonight. You are not alone. Beep. — Postcat."*

Dora's filling went all warm and gooey. The last letter had no address... because it was for *anyone.* For everyone. For the next lonely heart who happened to walk by.

And then Dora realized something that made his beret pop right off his head.

He turned the letter over. On the very back, in even tinier printing, it said: *"P.S. — And if nobody else finds it, then it's for whoever is kind enough to be delivering my mail. Hello, you. Thank you, you. — P."*

It was for *Dora.*

---

Dora the Dorayaki sat under the lamplight, holding a letter that a rusty toy robot-cat had written for him before they'd even met, and he understood the best fact he would ever collect: *kindness travels farther than any gadget.* Faster than a wheel. Farther than a spring. A broken robot with a working heart had reached three corners of the neighborhood tonight — using nothing but paper, warmth, and a dorayaki with squishy feet who said *yes.*

He hurried back to Gizmo & Quill, where Postcat waited on its dusty shelf.

"All three delivered," Dora announced. "And I read the third one. The one for *anyone.*" He climbed up beside the little robot. "It found me. *You* found me."

Postcat's heart-eyes glowed soft and pink. "Then my tummy did its job. Beep."

"Tomorrow," said Dora, settling his beret over them both like a tiny blanket, "we fix your wheel. And the next night, and the night after, we deliver every lonely letter in town. Squeaky wheel and all." He grinned his red-bean grin. "A good story is the best filling. But a good *letter*? That might be the best ending."

But before sleep could catch him, Dora did one last thing. He borrowed a strawberry-scented pen and a sheet of nice paper — the very thing he'd come to buy — and wrote his first-ever letter to Postcat, folding it into a slightly lopsided paper pancake and tucking it gently into the robot's mail-tummy to be found in the morning. "Pen pals," he yawned happily. "From now on, a tegami every week — even after your wheel is fixed. *Especially* after."

And on the dustiest, snuggliest shelf in the coziest shop on Crumb Corner, a pancake and a robot-cat fell asleep together — one mail-tummy full, three hearts warmed, and one whole neighborhood a little less alone.

Snuggle down now, soft as a pancake, sweet as red bean. Goodnight.
