# Story 97: Maru Matcha and the Thank-You Tea

**Main Character:** Maru Matcha
**Guest Characters:** Kei the kei-truck, Momo Mochi, Niko Nigiri; a polite pond frog
**Setting:** A peaceful garden tea gathering to say thanks to everyone
**Theme:** The quietest helper deserves the loudest thank-you
**Japanese Spotlight:** **arigatou** (ah-ree-gah-toh) — thank you
**Cameo:** Violet Evergarden (Violet Evergarden) — marches up the lane to type the perfect thank-you, her dry ribbon prints a blank paper river that wraps Kei like a scarf, and she sits on the wall to watch the tea instead; walk-on: Fern (Frieren)

---

On the last morning of the journey, Maru Matcha carried her tea things into the old garden.

Her plan: one quiet **arigatou** (ah-ree-gah-toh) — thank you, for every friend. Nobody shouting SURPRISE.

She set twenty cups in a circle on the moss. The pond frog hopped into one.

Then she saw it: tire tracks in the dew, arriving at the gate and turning away. Again.

Maru set down her whisk. "Oh no. I forgot the one who carried us all."

---

Niko Nigiri was at the gate, counting cushions. "Niko," said Maru. "Who is missing?"

Niko counted twice. His cape sagged like a flag with no wind. "Kei," said Niko. "I forgot parking."

There was a silence so big you could have parked a truck in it.

"Today," said Maru, raising her whisk like a tiny green sword, "the quietest helper gets the loudest thank-you."

---

Kei was in the gravel lane, parked perfectly straight, polishing her own bumper with a mud-flap.

"Putt-putt, let's GO!" beeped Kei. "Mountain? Market? Full tank, fresh seat-cover!"

"Nowhere," said Maru. "We came to invite you to the tea."

Kei's wipers gave one confused swish. "Trucks wait outside," beeped Kei. "That's the rule."

Momo Mochi gasped and flung her taffy arms around the whole truck, hood to tailgate.

"Squish together, stay together!" said Momo into the windshield. "And that means TRUCKS."

"But how," beeped Kei shyly, headlights down, "does a truck even drink tea?"

---

Attempt one: the hood. The cup slid off like a tiny green sled, into Niko's arms.

Attempt two: Momo held the cup to Kei's grille. Steam fogged both headlights. "No pressure," whispered Momo.

Then boots clicked up the lane: a traveler with golden braids, shining metal arms, and a heavy case.

"I am Violet Evergarden, Auto Memory Doll," she said, bowing. "I will type the perfect thank-you."

CLACK-CLACK-CLACK. Her metal arms blurred over the keys. The paper curled out blank as fresh snow.

She typed harder. Out poured a long white paper river that wrapped Kei from hood to tailgate.

"Oh," beeped Kei from inside the paper. "Now I'm a truck in a scarf."

"My finest letter," said Violet, folding the blank page away, and sat on the wall to watch.

---

Momo unwound Kei like a present. Maru sat on the gravel. "Whisk slow," she murmured. "Feel calm."

Her calm eyes followed the tire tracks: to the gate, and away. To the gate, and away.

"Backward," said Maru, standing up. "Friends don't pass kindness over a fence. We bring Kei to the tea."

---

Momo gripped the old bamboo gateposts and s-t-r-e-t-c-h-e-d the opening wide, the way only taffy arms can.

"Kei," called Maru. "The garden is ready for you now."

Slower than ever, Kei rolled through the gate and over the moss.

---

Maru set the frothiest cup on a flat stone before Kei's grille. Steam curled up her hood.

"ARIGATOU, KEI!" roared everyone, and the frog, so loud the lily pond rippled.

Kei went misty in both headlights. Swish-swish went her wipers, the way a truck wipes her eyes.

"I always thought waiting outside WAS the job," beeped Kei, in the smallest, fullest voice.

"The job," said Maru, "was never outside. **Otsukaresama** (oh-tsoo-kah-reh-sah-mah) — thank you for your hard work."

A quiet stranger with long purple hair and a dark robe refilled Kei's cup, unasked.

"You are pouring too fast," Fern told the frog, deadpan. The frog slowed down.

---

When the sun leaned low, Maru found a cup set in front of HER. Frothy. Nearly perfect.

"I whisked it eleven times, to be safe," said Niko, standing very straight.

"Even the tea-maker forgets herself," she said. "Arigatou for counting me."

Kei's engine ticked as it cooled, the coziest lullaby a garden ever had. "Putt-putt," murmured Kei. "Let's stay."
