# Story 67: Momo Mochi and the New-Year Mochi Pounding

**Main Character:** Momo Mochi
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A village square at New Year, where mochi is pounded in a big wooden mortar
**Theme:** New beginnings are best started together
**Japanese Spotlight:** おもち (omochi) = pounded rice cake

---

On the very first morning of the New Year, the village square wore a hat of snow, and Momo Mochi wore a grin so wide her rosy peach cheeks nearly popped. She was a soft, pillowy snow-white rice cake, plump and squishable, dusted all over with potato starch like she'd rolled in powdered sugar — and at the moment she was bouncing in place so fast that little puffs of starch lifted off her like tiny clouds.

"It's TODAY!" she squealed, stretching one taffy arm all the way across the square to ring the festival bell. "Oh my squishness, it's finally today!"

In the middle of the square sat the biggest wooden bowl Momo had ever seen — a great round **臼** (usu) = mortar, carved from one whole tree trunk, smooth and honey-brown. Beside it leaned a mallet taller than Momo herself.

Today the whole village would make **おもち** (omochi) = pounded rice cake. Together. From scratch. And Momo had volunteered to be Chief Cheerer.

She had even made a sign. It said: *SQUISH TOGETHER, STAY TOGETHER!* The letters were a little wobbly. She'd written them with her arm.

---

The trouble was that nobody was pounding.

A small crowd had gathered around the usu, all bundled in scarves, all looking at the enormous mallet, and all very quietly hoping someone *else* would pick it up first.

"It's so heavy," whispered a little adzuki bean.

"And the rice is so hot," murmured a sesame seed, peering into the mortar where a great steaming pile of cooked sticky rice sat fat and glistening. Steam curled up in lazy ribbons, smelling of warm clouds and good mornings.

"And what if I do it wrong?" said the adzuki bean, going a shade pinker. "On the very first day of the year? What if I make a *lumpy* New Year for everybody?"

A hush fell. Even the snow seemed to hold its breath.

Momo's bouncing slowed. She blinked her gentle eyes. Then she did what Momo Mochi always did when a friend felt small — she stretched out her warm arms and gathered the whole worried crowd into a great, gentle, starchy squish.

"Ohhh, sweet beans and seeds," she said, her voice soft as a pillow. "Nobody pounds the New Year alone. That's the whole *point.*"

"It is?" sniffed the adzuki bean.

"It IS," said Momo. "Watch."

---

She marched right up to the usu, planted her squishy feet, and grabbed the mallet. It was indeed enormous. It was indeed heavy. Momo lifted it about four inches, wobbled like a jelly in an earthquake, and very gently tipped over backward into a powdery *pomf.*

A starch cloud went up. Somewhere in it, Momo's voice said, brightly: "See? Easy!"

The crowd burst out laughing — the warm kind of laugh, the kind that melts ice off rooftops.

"Okay, okay," Momo giggled, sitting up and dusting herself into an even bigger powder puff. "Maybe not *alone* alone. But that's GOOD news! Because the secret of **おもち** isn't one big strong arm." She paused, peach cheeks glowing. "It's two."

She held up two fingers — well, two little arm-tips.

"There's a pounder," she said, "and there's a turner. The pounder lifts the mallet and goes *BAM!* And the turner reaches into the hot rice between every BAM and folds it over, quick as a wink, so it gets squished even and smooth. One can't do it without the other. If the turner is too slow—"

"You squish their hands!" gasped the sesame seed.

"You squish their hands," Momo agreed cheerfully. "Which is why you have to *trust each other* and find a rhythm. Like a song. Like a heartbeat." She wiggled happily. "Like a hug that goes BAM."

The adzuki bean looked at the great mallet. Then at the steaming rice. Then at Momo's kind, certain face.

"...I'll turn," the bean said quietly, "if somebody pounds."

"I'll pound!" boomed a voice — and out stepped a sturdy roasted soybean with arms like little barrels. "Been wanting to swing that thing all morning!"

---

So it began.

The soybean lifted the mallet — *up, up* — and brought it down. **よいしょ!** (yoisho!) = heave-ho! the crowd chanted, the way you do when something needs a little extra muscle.

*BAM!*

And quick as a blink, the adzuki bean darted in and folded the rice. *Fold.*

*BAM!* — *fold.* — **よいしょ!**

*BAM!* — *fold.* — **よいしょ!**

The rhythm caught. It rolled through the whole square like a warm wave. The little ones clapped on every BAM. The grown-up grains stomped their feet. Steam billowed up and caught the winter sun and turned gold, and right in the middle of it all stood Momo Mochi, conducting like the world's squishiest orchestra leader, both taffy arms swinging.

"YES!" she cried. "Feel it? Feel the squish becoming smooth? That's a whole year — bumpy at first, then BAM, then *softer,* then BAM, then *softer still* — "

"Momo," panted the soybean between swings, "you're a rice cake. Aren't you, technically, made *out* of this?"

Momo froze mid-swing. Her cheeks went pink as plums. "...Oh," she said. "Oh my goodness. I *am.*" She looked down at her own pillowy self, then at the smooth, stretchy, glossy rice forming in the usu, and her eyes filled up — not sad, never sad — just full.

"That's where I come from," she whispered, wonderstruck. "Friends, pounding together. *That's* where mochi comes from. We're literally — we're made of teamwork."

For one tiny moment the whole square went tender and quiet.

Then Momo flung both arms wide. "Which means it's a FAMILY REUNION! Hello, cousins! Hello, future me!"

And the laughing started all over again.

---

By the time the sun climbed over the rooftops, the rice in the usu had become **おもち** — a great glossy mound, soft and warm and impossibly stretchy. Momo reached in and pulled, and it stretched, and stretched, and *stretched*, longer than the bell rope, until everyone gasped.

"That's how you know it's perfect," Momo said proudly, and she let it go *snap* back into a happy blob.

But there was one little problem left.

The adzuki bean — the very one who'd been afraid of making a lumpy New Year — was looking at the beautiful mochi with worried eyes again. "It's so perfect now," the bean said. "What if I shape my piece wrong? Everyone else's will be round and pretty and mine will be all... bean-fingered and weird."

Momo crouched down beside the little bean. Around them, friends were tearing off warm pieces and rolling them into round white cakes, dusting them with starch, stacking two together with a tangerine on top — the special New Year stack called **鏡餅** (kagamimochi) = mirror mochi, set out to welcome good fortune.

"Can I tell you the squishiest secret of all?" Momo whispered.

The bean nodded.

"There's no such thing as a wrong mochi." Momo pinched off a piece and pressed it into the bean's little hands. "Lumpy ones, lopsided ones, ones shaped like a potato or a cloud or a sneeze — every single one tastes exactly like *we made it together.* And THAT is the only flavor that matters on a new year." She beamed. "A new beginning isn't about getting it perfect. It's about getting it *started* — with the people you squish beside."

The adzuki bean looked at the warm mochi in its hands. Slowly, a smile spread across its little face. Then it rolled the piece — wonkily, joyfully, gloriously imperfect — into something almost-round-ish, and held it up like a tiny moon.

"Squish together," the bean said shyly, "stay together?"

"SQUISH TOGETHER, STAY TOGETHER!" the whole square roared, and Momo laughed so hard she rolled clean over backward into the snow.

---

That evening, when the lanterns came on and everyone's bellies were full of warm sweet **おもち**, they wrapped Momo in blankets at the center of the square, because she was the softest and best for snuggling against. The stars came out one by one, like starch dusting a dark blue cake.

"Best New Year ever," Momo murmured, her arms looped lazily around three sleepy friends at once.

"Even though you couldn't lift the mallet?" teased the soybean.

"*Especially* because I couldn't," Momo said, eyes already half-closed. "Best things always take more than one." And she gave one last, gentle, all-around squish — and the whole square sighed happily into the night.

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, may your new beginnings always be soft at the edges and warm in the middle, and may you never have to start anything all alone. May the people you love be your turners and your pounders, folding kindness back to you every time life goes *BAM.* If today was a bit lumpy, that's all right — lumpy mochi tastes just as sweet, and tomorrow is a fresh warm batch waiting to be made together. Snuggle in close now, soft as a rice cake under the snow, and let the lantern-light dim. Squish together, stay together, and rest. Goodnight.
