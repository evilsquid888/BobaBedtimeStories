# Story 67: Momo Mochi and the New-Year Mochi Pounding

**Main Character:** Momo Mochi
**Guest Characters:** Azu, a shy little adzuki bean; Mame, a sturdy roasted soybean
**Setting:** A village square at New Year, where mochi is pounded in a big wooden mortar
**Theme:** New beginnings are best started together
**Japanese Spotlight:** **omochi** (oh-moh-chee) — pounded rice cake

---

On the first morning of the New Year, the village square wore a hat of snow.

"It's TODAY!" squealed Momo Mochi, stretching one taffy arm clear across the square to ring the festival bell.

In the middle sat a wooden mortar carved from a whole tree, steaming with hot sticky rice.

Beside it leaned a mallet taller than Momo. Today the village would pound **omochi** (oh-moh-chee) — pounded rice cake.

"I want a whole square of squishing," said Momo, "before this rice goes cold!"

---

Treat-folk in scarves stood around the mortar. "It's so heavy," whispered Azu, a tiny adzuki bean.

"And the rice is so hot," said Mame, a sturdy roasted soybean, poking the steam and yelping.

"What if I make a lumpy New Year for everybody?" said Azu. Even the snow held its breath.

Momo stretched both arms around the whole worried huddle and squished. Gently. Starchily.

"Nobody pounds the New Year alone," said Momo. "Except me. Watch."

---

Momo grabbed the mallet, lifted it four inches, wobbled like jelly, and tipped over backwards.

POMF. A starch cloud went up. From inside it, Momo's voice said, "See? Easy!"

Try two. Momo wound both taffy arms around the handle, stretched back like a slingshot, and let go.

The mallet whizzed up, up, and hooked itself on the festival bell. BONG.

"I'll get it," said Momo, and stretched. And stretched. Her arms went up like two noodles.

She reached the mallet. Her arms snapped back like rubber bands and flung her into the mortar. SPLAT.

Hot, sticky, glossy rice. Momo sank in to her cheeks. "Oh," she said. "Oh, it's warm in here."

---

"Momo," said Mame, peering in, "you're a rice cake. Aren't you, technically, made of this?"

Momo went still. Her cheeks turned plum. "I AM," she whispered. "This is where I come from."

"Friends pounding together," said Momo, wonderstruck. "That's where mochi comes from. I'm made of teamwork!"

Then she flung her arms wide. "FAMILY REUNION! Hello, cousins! Hello, future me!"

She tried to climb out. The rice held her like loving glue. "Help?" said Momo. "Family is clingy."

---

Mame grabbed one arm and heaved. Momo stretched three feet and stayed exactly as stuck.

Azu grabbed the other arm. "**Yoisho!** (yoy-shoh) — heave-ho!" they chanted, and PLOP, out she popped.

Momo landed in the snow in a puff of starch and beamed at them.

"A pounder and a turner," said Momo. "One goes BAM, one folds. Like a hug that goes BAM."

"I'll turn," said Azu quietly, "if somebody pounds."

"I'll POUND!" boomed Mame, and yanked the bell rope until the mallet dropped. BONG.

---

Up went the mallet. "Yoisho!" chanted the square. BAM. Azu darted in and folded. Fold.

BAM. Fold. Yoisho! The rhythm rolled through the square, and the steam caught the sun and turned gold.

Momo conducted in the middle, both taffy arms swinging, the squishiest orchestra leader alive.

The rice went lumpy, then smooth, then glossy. Momo grabbed an end and pulled.

It stretched past the mortar, past the bell, past the end of the square. Everyone gasped.

SNAP. It sprang back into a blob. Mame plopped a tangerine on top: **kagamimochi** (kah-gah-mee-moh-chee) — mirror mochi!

---

Azu held up a piece shaped like a sneeze. "Mine came out wrong," whispered Azu.

Momo pressed it between her paws and Azu's. "No such thing as a wrong mochi," said Momo.

"A new beginning isn't about perfect," said Momo. "It's about starting, beside the friends you squish."

"Squish together," said Azu shyly, "stay together?"

"SQUISH TOGETHER, STAY TOGETHER!" roared the square, and Momo laughed herself over backwards into the snow.

---

That evening, under lanterns, they wrapped Momo in blankets, the softest thing to lean on.

"Even though you couldn't lift the mallet?" teased Mame, yawning.

"Especially because," murmured Momo, arms looped around two sleepy friends. "Best things take more than one."

---

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, may your new beginnings be soft at the edges and warm in the middle, and may you never start anything alone. Snuggle in close, soft as a rice cake under snow. Squish together, stay together, and rest. Goodnight.
