# Story 58: Kompei and the Indigo Dye Workshop

**Main Character:** Kompei the Konpeito
**Guest Characters:** Auntie Nasu, a round eggplant dumpling who keeps the dye studio
**Setting:** A small indigo-dyeing (aizome) craft studio down a cobbled Kyoto lane
**Theme:** A happy accident can become the most beautiful part of what you make
**Japanese Spotlight:** **ai** (eye) — indigo blue
**Cameo:** Soot Sprites (Studio Ghibli) — mistake the indigo-dripping sugar star for their star candy and parade him toward the boiler, speckling every cloth below; walk-on: Ditto (Pokémon)

---

Kompei the Konpeito was a tiny spiky star of sugar, and he had exactly one rule.

"Today," he told the mirror, "I make something PERFECT. No smudge. No wobble."

He held up a tiny brush, a tiny ruler, and an eraser bigger than his head.

Then he marched down the lane to the indigo studio, home of the messiest art there is.

---

Behind the deep-blue curtain stood wooden tubs of **ai** (eye) — indigo blue — so dark it glowed.

Auntie Nasu, a round eggplant dumpling in a splotchy apron, wobbled over.

"I would like," announced Kompei, "one perfect blue square. Even all over. No surprises."

"Mm," said Auntie Nasu. "We shall see what the indigo thinks."

"Indigo doesn't THINK," said Kompei. "The dye does what I say." Clack went his ruler.

---

He lowered his spotless cloth into the tub, careful as a cat on a shelf.

It came up GREEN. Frog-belly green.

"WRONG!" wailed Kompei, tipping off his stool. "I wanted BLUE!"

"Hold it up," said Auntie Nasu. "Let the air have a turn."

Slowly, like a sunset running backwards, the green bloomed into a breathing blue.

Kompei's mouth hung open. "It turned blue by itself. I didn't do anything."

---

But Kompei was stubborn. He climbed the stool to dip the corners exactly alike. It wobbled.

He measured the drips with his ruler. The ruler dripped. The stool wobbled more.

He leaned out for the far corner, spikes first. The stool went over. SPLOOSH.

Kompei went in pointy end first. The line snapped. Six cloths tumbled in after him.

Auntie Nasu fished him out by one spike, blue from ear to pointy ear.

---

BANG went the boiler door, and out poured the Soot Sprites, fuzzy black fluffballs with white eyes.

They saw a spiky blue star and chittered with joy. A giant sugar star. Snack time.

Fifty stick-thin arms hoisted Kompei overhead and paraded him toward the boiler.

"I am NOT a snack!" wailed Kompei. "I am an ARTIST! Put me DOWN!"

Every drip off his points speckled the fallen cloths with little blue star-prints. Plip. Plip.

Auntie Nasu rattled a candy jar. The sprites dropped Kompei, SPLAT, and sat munching on the boiler.

---

"I've ruined EVERYTHING," said Kompei, dripping. "Bunched. Blotchy. Nearly eaten. Ruined."

They hung the crumpled cloth up, and the air did its slow blue work.

Where the folds squeezed tight, the cloth stayed pale. Where they opened, deep indigo.

Starbursts. White rivers through midnight. Across the others, a scatter of tiny dripped star-prints.

A drop landed on his nose. "I didn't mean to do that," Kompei whispered.

"No," said Auntie Nasu. "The slip did. The cloth doesn't care HOW the wonder happened."

---

After that, Kompei made accidents on purpose, a delightful, silly thing to do.

He scrunched a cloth into a ball and tied it. Out came frozen lightning.

Then, daringly, he sat on a fresh square. Dipped, it came out dotted with a hundred tiny Kompeis.

"It's ME!" he shouted. "I made a me-pattern! By SITTING on it!"

Auntie Nasu wheezed onto a flour sack. "The first artist to paint with his own bottom."

In the corner, Ditto the purple blob squished itself into a lumpy Kompei, dot eyes and all.

"Close enough," said Kompei. "Perfect is just one of the patterns."

---

By lantern-time, six cloths swayed on the mended line, none even, none matching.

"I came for one perfect square," said Kompei, "and made six imperfect ones. Is that allowed?"

"Perfect is a cold word," said Auntie Nasu. "This is what living things make."

She folded the crumpled first cloth, the accident, into his spiky arms.

Kompei hugged it at the curtain, blue nose and all, and waved one point.

---

That night he pulled his indigo blanket over his points and dozed under handmade surprises.
