# Story 58: Kompei and the Indigo Dye Workshop

**Main Character:** Kompei the Konpeito
**Guest Characters:** Auntie Nasu, a round eggplant dumpling who keeps the dye studio
**Setting:** A small indigo-dyeing (aizome) craft studio down a cobbled Kyoto lane
**Theme:** A happy accident can become the most beautiful part of what you make
**Japanese Spotlight:** **ai** (eye) — indigo blue

---

Kompei the Konpeito was a tiny spiky star of sugar, and he had exactly one rule.

"Today," he told the mirror, "I will make something PERFECT. Not one smudge. Not one wobble."

He held up a tiny brush, a tiny ruler, and an eraser bigger than his head.

Konpeito have no pockets. He carried all three on his points, and dropped the eraser twice.

Then he marched down the cobbled lane to the indigo studio, home of the messiest art there is.

---

Behind the deep-blue curtain stood wooden tubs of **ai** (eye) — indigo blue — so dark it glowed.

Auntie Nasu, a round eggplant dumpling in a splotchy apron, wobbled over to greet him.

"I would like," announced Kompei, "to make one perfect blue square. Even all over. No surprises."

"Mm," said Auntie Nasu. "We shall see what the indigo thinks of that."

"Indigo doesn't THINK," said Kompei. "I think. The dye does what I say." Clack went his ruler.

---

He lowered his spotless white cloth into the tub, careful as a cat on a high shelf.

It came up GREEN. Frog-belly green.

"It's WRONG!" wailed Kompei, tipping off his stool. "I wanted blue and I've ruined it!"

"Hold it up," said Auntie Nasu. "Let the air have a turn."

Slowly, like a sunset running backwards, the green deepened and bloomed into a breathing blue.

Kompei's mouth hung open. "It turned blue by itself. I didn't do anything."

---

But Kompei was a stubborn candy. He wanted his square even, and even meant control.

He climbed higher on the stool to dip the corners exactly alike. The stool wobbled.

He measured the drips with his ruler. The ruler dripped. The stool wobbled more.

He leaned out for the far corner, spikes first. The stool went over. SPLOOSH.

The cloth went in crumpled. Kompei went in after it, pointy end first, like a dropped star.

The tub rocked, the drying line snapped, and six white cloths tumbled in on top of him.

Auntie Nasu fished him out by one spike, blue from ear to pointy ear.

"I've ruined EVERYTHING," said Kompei, dripping. "Bunched. Blotchy. Imperfect. Ruined."

---

They hung the crumpled cloth in the air, and the air did its slow blue work.

Where the folds had squeezed tight, the cloth stayed pale. Where it opened, the indigo went deep.

Starbursts. White rivers winding through midnight. A whole field of pale sparkles nobody had planned.

Kompei stared so long a drop of indigo landed on his nose, and he didn't wipe it.

"I didn't mean to do that," he whispered.

"No," said Auntie Nasu. "The slip did. The cloth doesn't care HOW the wonder happened."

---

After that, Kompei tried to make accidents on purpose, which is a delightful, silly thing to do.

He scrunched a cloth into a ball and tied it with string. Out came frozen lightning.

Then, very daringly, he sat on a fresh square with his whole spiky bottom.

Dipped, it came out dotted with a hundred tiny spiky shapes. A whole cloth of Kompeis.

"It's ME!" he shouted. "I made a me-pattern! By SITTING on it!"

Auntie Nasu wheezed onto a flour sack. "The first artist to paint with his own bottom."

---

By lantern-time, six cloths swayed on the mended line. Not one even. Not one matching.

"I came for one perfect square," said Kompei, "and made six imperfect ones. Is that allowed?"

"Perfect is a cold word," said Auntie Nasu. "This is what living things make."

She folded the crumpled first cloth, the accident, and tucked it into his spiky arms.

Kompei hugged it at the curtain, blue nose and all, and waved with one point.

---

That night he pulled his indigo blanket over his points and dozed off under handmade surprises.
