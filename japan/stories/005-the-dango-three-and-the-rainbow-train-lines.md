# Story 5: The Dango Three and the Rainbow Train Lines

**Main Character:** The Dango Three (Pinku, Shiro, Cha)
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** The colorful Tokyo subway map and station platforms
**Theme:** Three heads (and three colors) are better than one
**Japanese Spotlight:** **Eki** (eh-kee) — train station

---

The trouble began, as trouble so often does, on a perfectly lovely Tuesday — and it began the moment the Dango Three saw the map.

Now, if you have never met the Dango Three, picture three little dumplings stuck on one wooden skewer, like beads on a string who never quite agree on anything. Pinku was pink and bold and bouncy, always one inch ahead. Shiro was white and shy and worried, always one inch behind. And Cha was green and clever, squished thoughtfully in the middle, holding the whole wobbly trio together.

They had pivoted into the **eki** (eh-kee) — the train station — the way they did everything: all at once, like a tiny carousel, and they stopped so suddenly in front of the giant subway map that they nearly toppled off their own stick.

"Look!" gasped all three at once. The map glowed with train lines in every color of the rainbow, looping and crossing and curling like ribbons spilled across the wall.

And that is when the arguing started.

"PINK LINE!" shouted Pinku, leaning so hard the whole skewer tilted. "We ride the pink line! It matches me! It's clearly the best one!"

"N-no, the white line," whispered Shiro, leaning the opposite way to balance. "White is calm. White is safe. White doesn't have any scary loud tunnels, probably, maybe, I hope."

"Actually," said Cha, in the middle, calm as a cup of tea, "the green line is the smartest route. It connects to everything. Also it's green, which is the correct color, no offense to either of you."

"OFFENSE TAKEN," said Pinku and Shiro together, which was the only thing they agreed on all morning.

The three little dumplings wobbled and tugged and pivoted in three directions — but here's the silly trouble with being stuck on one stick: when you all pull at once, you go absolutely *nowhere*. The skewer spun in a slow, dizzy circle right there on the platform, like a tiny green-pink-white pinwheel, until they were so dizzy the whole map looked like soup.

"Okay," said Cha, planting their little dumpling-self firmly. "Stop. Spinning. We can't all go three ways. We're one stick."

"That," sighed Shiro, "is the saddest sentence anyone has ever said."

A station sign overhead gave a soft, friendly *ding-dong*, the way station signs do, as if it were clearing its throat to help.

And Cha — clever Cha — looked up at that gentle *ding-dong* and got an idea so good that the green swirl on top almost sparkled.

"What if," said Cha slowly, "we don't pick *one* line. What if we ride *all three* — one after another — and whoever's turn it is gets to lead? Pink line first, because Pinku is loudest. Then white, then green. A fair plan. A *neat* plan."

Pinku gasped with delight. Shiro gasped with relief. And for one beautiful moment, all three dumplings beamed in three different colors at the very same time.

"Three on a stick," they cheered together, "stuck together for good!"

So onto the **densha** (den-shah) — the train — they hopped, the pink one first, of course, because a deal is a deal.

The pink line whooshed them through bright bubblegum tunnels lit up like the inside of a strawberry. Pinku pressed all three of their faces to the window (which was easy, since their faces were all on the same stick) and narrated *everything*.

"Pink building! Pink sign! Pink lady with a pink umbrella — HELLO PINK LADY!"

"Pinku, she can't hear you," said Cha.

"She waved!"

"She was swatting a fly."

"She WAVED with the fly-swat. Same thing."

Shiro giggled so hard the whole skewer trembled, which made Pinku giggle, which made Cha pretend not to giggle while absolutely giggling. The pink train rocked them gently — *clackity-clack, clackity-clack* — and Pinku, full of joy and bounce, declared this the single greatest train in the entire universe.

Then the doors slid open at a busy eki, and it was time to switch.

This is where it got tricky.

Switching trains, you see, is hard enough when you have legs. It is *much* harder when you are three dumplings sharing exactly one wobbly skewer and a single sense of balance.

"Platform Two!" Pinku barreled forward.

"NO — Platform Two is the *other* way!" Shiro yanked back.

And once again the little trio spun — *wheee, oh no, wheee* — pinwheeling across the platform while travelers hopped politely out of the way, smiling at the dizzy little dumplings doing donuts on a stick.

It was Cha, of course, who saved them. "STOP. New rule. We *count*. On three, we ALL lean the same way. One stick, one direction. Ready? **Ichi** (ee-chee) — one... **ni** (nee) — two... **san** (sahn) — three — LEAN!"

And they leaned. All three. The very same way. The skewer slid smooth as butter right across the platform and landed neatly — *neatly!* — on the white line train, exactly on time.

"It worked," whispered Shiro, blinking. "We didn't spin. We didn't fall. We just... went."

"Three heads," said Cha, "leaning as one."

Now it was Shiro's turn to lead, and oh, how shy Shiro had dreaded this part. But the white line was the gentlest, quietest train of all — soft white seats, soft white lights, soft white hush like a snowfall you could ride. There were no scary tunnels at all. There was only a very old grandmother dumpling-fan reading a newspaper, and a sleepy melon-bread napping against a pole, snoring the tiniest *crackle-snore*.

"It's... nice," Shiro breathed. "It's so quiet I can hear myself not-worry."

"See?" said Pinku, surprisingly soft. "Your line is the cozy one. Mine's the loud one. We *needed* yours after mine."

Shiro, who almost never led, sat up just a tiny bit straighter, and pointed out the window with great care at each gentle thing they passed — a quiet park, a sleeping cat, a paper lantern swaying like it was dreaming. And the funny thing was, with Shiro leading, even bold Pinku and clever Cha slowed down enough to *notice* it all.

By the time the white train sighed into the next eki, all three were warm and giggly and ready for the last leg.

"My turn," said Cha. "Green line. The smart one. Watch and learn."

The green train looped up high — *up, up* — onto a track above the rooftops, and suddenly all of Tokyo unrolled below them like a glowing rainbow quilt. Pink lights here. White lights there. Green parks woven all between. And the trio pressed close, three little dumplings, three little colors, watching the city sparkle.

"Hey," said Pinku slowly, squinting at the route map glowing by the door. "Hey, wait. Cha. The green line... loops back. To the pink line. Which loops to the white line. Which..."

Cha grinned the grin of someone who had quietly known all along.

"Which loops right back here," Cha finished. "To our home eki. The whole rainbow is *one big circle*. Pink, white, green — they were never three separate things at all. They all hold hands."

Shiro's eyes went enormous. "Just... like us."

And there it was. The three of them looked down at their own little skewer — pink, white, green, stuck together, going round and round through the whole bright city and home again — and they understood the silliest, truest thing all at once:

They had spent the *entire morning* fighting over which line was best, when the lines were all part of the same loop, the way they were all part of the same stick. You couldn't pick a favorite color of a rainbow. You needed every single one, or it wasn't a rainbow at all.

"We're a loop," whispered Shiro.

"We're a *rainbow*," declared Pinku.

"We're," said Cha, with great dignity, "delicious. And also correct. Mostly correct. We're a correct rainbow."

The green train curved its big bright curve, and the city lights swept past pink and white and green and gold, and the three little dumplings leaned together — all the same way, all at once — as their home eki glowed up ahead like a warm lantern saying *welcome back, welcome back, you silly wonderful trio.*

They pivoted off the train as one tidy little carousel, not spinning, not fighting, just *together* — and they had ridden the whole rainbow and never once let go of each other, because of course they couldn't. They were stuck on the same stick.

Which, they decided, was the very best place to be stuck.

"Same time tomorrow?" asked Pinku.

"Same loop," said Cha.

"Same stick," sighed Shiro happily, "stuck together for good."
