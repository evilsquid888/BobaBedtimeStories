# Story 81: Pock Ramune and the Summer Cicada Chorus

**Main Character:** Pock Ramune (Ramune marble soda bottle)
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A green countryside hillside buzzing with summer cicadas
**Theme:** Every voice adds to the song
**Japanese Spotlight:** natsu (nah-tsoo) — summer

---

It was the buzziest, fizziest morning of the whole **natsu** (nah-tsoo) — summer, and Pock Ramune could not — *would* not — sit still. He zipped up the green countryside hillside leaving a trail of happy little bubbles behind him, *pop! pop! pop!*, like a tiny bottle-shaped firework that had wandered off its rocket.

"Listen, listen, LISTEN!" he fizzed, screeching to a stop so fast that his marble rattled in his neck — *clink-clink-POP!* "Do you hear that?"

The whole hillside was singing. From every tree, from every blade of grass, from somewhere up in the warm green leaves came a sound like a thousand tiny saws made of sunshine: *zzzeeee-zzzeeee-zzzeeee.* It was the song of the **semi** (seh-mee) — cicadas, and it wrapped around the hill like a buzzy summer blanket.

Pock Ramune's fizz-freckles sparkled. "I have decided," he announced to nobody, which is the very best person to announce things to, "that I am going to find the LOUDEST cicada of the entire summer. *Pop!*"

---

Now, you and I both know that finding the loudest anything is a slippery sort of mission. But Pock Ramune had never met a slippery mission he didn't want to bounce right into. He fizzed up to the very first tree and pressed his cool glass ear against the bark.

"Helloooo? *Pop!* Are you the loudest cicada? Sing for me!"

A small green cicada peeked out from behind a leaf. She was no bigger than a sesame seed with wings, and when she sang, her voice came out tiny and shy. "*Zee?*" she chirped. "*Zee-zee.*"

"Oh," said Pock Ramune, deflating just a fizzle. "That's very nice. Very... gentle. But I'm looking for the LOUD one. The BOOMER. The one that goes ZZZZZ so big it rattles the dewdrops!" He bounced away. "*Pop!* Thank you anyway, sweetie!"

The little green cicada watched him go, and her tiny *zee* got even tinier and shyer, until you could barely hear it at all.

Up the hill Pock Ramune zipped, asking every cicada he met. There was a plump brown one who sang in a wobbly *zzzwooo-zzzwooo*, like a cicada who'd just woken from a nap. There was a stripy one who only sang on the count of three and kept losing count. ("One, two, three — *zzz!* Wait. Was that three? Let me start again. One, two...") There was even a teeny baby cicada who didn't sing at all yet but practiced its buzzing by blowing the politest little raspberries you ever heard. *Pbbbbt.*

"Adorable," said Pock Ramune kindly, "but not loud."

He met a cicada who only sang underwater (he was visiting from a pond and was, frankly, very confused about which season it was). He met two cicadas who sang in perfect harmony and immediately got into an argument about who started it. He even met one extremely old cicada with a tiny cane who buzzed so slowly that by the time he finished one note, the **yuugata** (yoo-gah-tah) — evening was practically falling.

"Lovely!" Pock Ramune said to each of them, fizzing politely. "But still not the LOUDEST. *Pop!*" And on he bounced, because that's what bouncy bottles do.

By the time the sun was high and golden, Pock Ramune had a marvelous idea — which, if we're being honest, is exactly the moment in a story when you should brace yourself.

"I know how to find the loudest cicada!" he fizzed, doing three excited backflips and a barrel roll. "I'll have a CONTEST. A grand summer SING-OFF! Whoever is the loudest wins, and I shall declare them the Champion Cicada of all natsu! *POP!*"

---

This, it turned out, was the kind of idea that sounds wonderful in your head and gets complicated the moment it leaves your bottle.

Pock Ramune fizzed all over the hillside spreading the news. "Sing-off! Sing-off at the big crooked tree! Loudest cicada WINS!" And because cicadas are very fond of singing, every single one of them came buzzing over, wings shimmering in the sun, settling onto the branches of the big crooked tree like the world's smallest, most excitable audience.

He'd even made a trophy, sort of. It was a shiny acorn cap that he'd polished with a dewdrop until it gleamed, balanced on top of a twig. "The Champion Cup!" he announced proudly. A passing beetle tried to eat it. Pock Ramune shooed him away with a flurry of bubbles. "Not yet, sir! That's for the WINNER! *Pop!*"

Pock Ramune climbed onto a smooth round stone and held up one little arm. "Welcome, welcome! When I say *pop*, everyone shows me your LOUDEST voice. The biggest buzz wins! Ready? One, two — POP!"

What happened next was not a song.

It was a *kerfuffle.*

Every cicada buzzed its absolute loudest, all at the very same time, but each one trying to be louder than the others. The plump brown one cranked up its wobble. The stripy one buzzed without counting at all. A hundred cicadas all shoved their voices forward at once, elbowing and over-buzzing, until the sound wasn't a song anymore — it was a great big tangled *ZRRRRGGHZZZBRRRZZT* that made the leaves shiver and the dewdrops dive for cover.

"Hm," said Pock Ramune, his marble wobbling. "That's not... that's not very nice to listen to, is it? *Pop.*"

The cicadas thought so too. They stopped, all out of breath, and began to grumble. "*You* were too loud!" "No, *you* drowned me out!" "I couldn't even hear myself buzz!" The old cicada with the cane said he'd gotten exactly one note out before being completely flattened by the kerfuffle, thank you very much. The two harmony cicadas started arguing again. The stripy one was *still* trying to count to three.

One by one, in twos and threes, the cicadas huffed off the crooked tree and scattered across the hillside, each one buzzing crossly to itself in its own grumpy corner. The shiny acorn-cap trophy toppled off its twig and rolled away down the slope, and not a single cicada chased after it. Even the beetle didn't want it anymore.

And then — oh, my dears — the hillside went *quiet.*

Not cozy-quiet. Not bedtime-quiet. *Lonely*-quiet. The kind of quiet where the wind has nothing to hum along to.

Pock Ramune sat down on his round stone with a small, sad *fizz*. All the summer felt suddenly empty, like a Ramune bottle with no bubbles left at all.

---

"Oh no," he whispered. "I came to find the loudest voice... and I made all the voices stop."

He looked down at himself — at his cool blue glass, at his little marble that loved to *pop*, at the bubbles that only ever rose up when he was happy. And right then, very quietly, he heard something.

*Zee.*

It was so small he almost missed it. *Zee-zee.*

There, on the lowest branch of the crooked tree, was the very first cicada he'd ever met — the tiny green one no bigger than a sesame seed with wings. She wasn't grumpy. She wasn't sulking. She was just... singing. Quietly. To herself. Because singing made her happy, and that was reason enough.

Pock Ramune fizzed over softly, so as not to startle her. "You're still singing," he said. "Even after all that kerfuffle. Why?"

The little cicada thought about it. "*Zee,*" she said simply. Which, in cicada, means: *Because it's summer, and this is my part.*

And that's when the bubbles rose all the way up to the top of Pock Ramune's head, because he'd had a NEW idea — a soft one this time, the kind that doesn't need a contest at all.

"Sweetie," he said, "you're not the loudest cicada. *Pop!*" He grinned his fizzy grin. "You're something better. You're a *piece* of the song."

---

Off he zipped, but gently now, no zooming. He floated from grumpy corner to grumpy corner, and to each cicada he whispered the same little thing:

"I was wrong about the loudest. Loud isn't the prize. *Pop!* The hillside doesn't need ONE big voice. It needs ALL the little ones. Will you come back — not to win, but to sing your part?"

Some of them were tricky to convince. The two harmony cicadas only agreed to come back if Pock Ramune promised, marble-on-glass, that nobody would ask who started it. The old cicada with the cane needed a little boost up onto a low branch, which Pock Ramune gave him with a gentle puff of bubbles. The stripy one wanted to know if he still had to count to three. "Only if you want to," said Pock Ramune. "And if you lose count, that's allowed too." The stripy cicada looked so relieved that he nearly cried a tiny stripy tear.

The plump brown one with the wobble came back. The stripy one who couldn't count came back. The baby one with its polite raspberry *pbbbt* came back. The tiny green one led the way, *zee-zee-zee*, brave as anything.

Pock Ramune climbed onto his stone one more time. But this time he didn't say *be the loudest.* This time he said:

"Just sing your own little voice — soft or wobbly or tiny or stripy or any-which-way. And let's see what happens when we all sing *together.* Ready? One... two... *pop.*"

And oh.

*Oh.*

The shy green *zee* slipped in like a thread of silver. The brown wobble rolled underneath it, warm as a pillow. The stripy one buzzed — and missed the count, and it didn't matter even a little, because the baby's *pbbbt* came tumbling in afterward and made everyone giggle-buzz. A hundred little voices, each one small, each one different, each one absolutely *itself* — and somehow, when you put them all together, they wove into the biggest, roundest, most beautiful summer sound you ever did hear. It rolled down the hillside and up into the warm gold sky and made the dewdrops sparkle right back up onto the leaves.

It wasn't the loudest song. It was the *fullest* one. The shy green *zee* didn't disappear inside it — it shone all the brighter, because it had a hundred friends holding it up. Even the old cicada's slow, slow note found its perfect place, like the deep warm bottom of the whole sound. And the baby's polite raspberry? It turned out to be everyone's favorite part.

And Pock Ramune, bubbling with happy tears, added his own *pop! pop! pop!* right into the middle of it — because he understood now that he was part of the song too.

"Squish my marble," he sniffled joyfully, "if that isn't the best sound in all of natsu. *Pop!*"

---

From that day on, the cicadas of the green hillside never had another sing-off, because they didn't need one. Every summer morning, the tiniest voice and the wobbliest voice and the stripy-est voice all sang together, and the fizzy little soda bottle who started it all bounced happily among them, adding his bubbles to the chorus.

Because the loudest voice was never the point.

The *whole* song was.

---

## Goodnight Blessing
May your summer be full of small sounds that you learn to love — the hum of a sleepy fan, the whisper of leaves, the tiny *pop* of bubbles settling down for the night. Remember, little one, that your voice matters too, exactly as it is — not because it's the loudest, but because the song isn't finished without you. So sing your gentle part, then let your eyes grow soft and warm. The whole world is humming you a lullaby, and there is room in it just for you. Goodnight.
