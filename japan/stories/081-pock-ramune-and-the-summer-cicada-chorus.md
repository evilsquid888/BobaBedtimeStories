# Story 81: Pock Ramune and the Summer Cicada Chorus

**Main Character:** Pock Ramune (Ramune marble soda bottle)
**Guest Characters:** Zee, a tiny shy green cicada; Stripes, a stripy cicada who counts to three; Bibi, a baby cicada who blows raspberries; a hungry beetle
**Setting:** A green countryside hillside buzzing with summer cicadas
**Theme:** Every voice adds to the song
**Japanese Spotlight:** **natsu** (nah-tsoo) — summer

---

It was the fizziest morning of the whole **natsu** (nah-tsoo) — summer, and Pock Ramune could not sit still.

He zipped up the green hillside, pop pop pop, like a firework that had lost its rocket.

He stopped so fast his marble rattled. Clink-clink-POP. "Listen, listen, LISTEN!"

From every tree came a sound like a thousand tiny saws made of sunshine. Zzzeeee. Zzzeeee.

It was the song of the **semi** (seh-mee) — cicadas, wrapped around the hill like a buzzy blanket.

"I have decided," Pock announced to nobody, "to find the LOUDEST cicada of the entire summer. Pop!"

---

He pressed his cool glass ear against the first tree. "Helloooo? Are you the loudest? Sing!"

A cicada no bigger than a sesame seed with wings peeked out. "Zee?" said Zee. "Zee-zee."

"Very gentle," said Pock, deflating a fizzle. "But I want the BOOMER. The dewdrop-rattler."

Zee's tiny zee got even tinier, until you could barely hear it at all.

Next tree: Stripes, who only sang on three. "One, two, three... zzz! Wait. Was that three?"

Next: Bibi, a baby who couldn't buzz yet, so she blew polite raspberries. "Adorable," said Pock. "Not loud."

---

By noon Pock had a marvelous idea, which is the moment in a story to brace yourself.

"A SING-OFF!" he fizzed, doing three backflips. "Loudest cicada wins the Champion Cup! POP!"

The Champion Cup was a polished acorn cap on a twig. A beetle tried to eat it.

Pock shooed the beetle off in a flurry of bubbles. "Not yet, sir! That's for the WINNER!"

Every cicada on the hill came buzzing to the big crooked tree, the world's smallest audience.

Pock hopped onto a round stone and raised one arm. "Loudest buzz wins! One, two, POP!"

---

What happened next was not a song. It was a kerfuffle.

A hundred cicadas shoved their voices forward at once. ZRRRGGHZZZBRRRZZT. The leaves shivered.

"LOUDER!" fizzed Pock, and they went louder. The dewdrops dove for cover.

"LOUDEST!" and the crooked tree shook like a wet dog. Stripes stopped counting entirely.

The buzz rattled Pock's marble right out. POP! He fizzed over and sprayed the whole front row.

The Champion Cup toppled off its twig and bounced away down the slope. The beetle followed it.

The cicadas grumbled and huffed off, one by one, to sulk in their own corners.

And then, oh my dears, the hillside went quiet. Lonely-quiet.

---

Pock sat down. "I came to find the loudest voice," he whispered, "and I made every voice stop."

Then, from the lowest branch, something tiny. Zee. Zee-zee.

Zee was still singing. Not sulking. Just singing, because it was summer and that was her part.

"You're not the loudest, sweetie," said Pock slowly. "You're something better. You're a piece of the song."

---

Off he floated, gently now, from grumpy corner to grumpy corner.

"Loud isn't the prize," said Pock. "The hill needs ALL the little voices. Come sing your part."

Stripes wanted to know if he still had to count. "Only if you want to," said Pock.

Bibi came back. Zee led the way. The beetle came back too, bumping the Champion Cup uphill.

---

Pock climbed his stone one last time. "Sing your own voice, soft or wobbly or stripy. Together. Pop."

The shy zee slipped in like a silver thread. Stripes lost count, and it didn't matter at all.

Bibi's pbbbt tumbled in after, and the whole tree giggle-buzzed.

A hundred small voices wove into the fullest summer sound, and the dewdrops sparkled back up.

Pock added his own pop, pop, pop. "Squish my marble," he sniffled, "that's the best sound in natsu."

---

The beetle set the Champion Cup on the lowest branch, next to Zee, and nobody argued.

The whole hill hummed, warm and gold, and a fizzy bottle dozed among the cicadas.

Because the loudest voice was never the point. The whole song was.
