# Story 56: Aiyu & the Friendly Mist Spirits

**Main Character:** Aiyu (Aiyu Jelly)
**Guest Characters:** Ā-Lǐ (Alishan Oolong Tea Leaf)
**Setting:** A misty mountain stream high above the night market, where aiyu seeds are gently rubbed
**Theme:** Kindness clears away even the foggiest worries.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 山 (shān) = mountain

---

High up where the clouds liked to nap, there was a stream so cold and clear it giggled when it ran over the stones. And there, perched on a smooth pebble, sat the shyest little jelly cube on the whole **山** (shān) = mountain.

Her name was Aiyu.

She was a translucent golden-amber cube, wobbly as a bowl of sunshine, and you could see *right through* her — clean through to the other side, like looking through a window made of honey. Most of the time Aiyu wished she could turn cloudy and hide. But tonight, hiding was impossible, because tonight the mountain had gone *foop* — completely lost inside a fog so thick you couldn't see your own toes. If you had toes. Aiyu did not. She had a wobble, and right now her wobble was very, very nervous.

"Oh dear," whispered Aiyu. "I can't even see the stream."

And then something bumped her. *Boop.*

---

"Sorry! So sorry! Pardon me! Was that a rock? Are you a rock?"

A tiny voice, soft as a dandelion sneeze, was apologizing from somewhere in the fog. Aiyu squinted — and there, drifting in front of her, was the cutest thing she had ever seen: a wisp of mist no bigger than a teacup, round and silvery, with two cloud-puff cheeks and big worried eyes. It had bumped its little nose right into her.

"I'm not a rock," said Aiyu gently. "I'm a jelly. Are you alright?"

"I think so," said the mist spirit, rubbing its squishy nose. "I'm Wú. I'm a mist spirit. There are lots of us up here on the mountain, but tonight the fog is so thick that *we* can't even see *each other*, and I've bonked into a pine tree, a teapot, a startled frog, and now you. I can't find my way home." Its little cheeks drooped. "I always get lost. I'm made of fog and I still can't see *through* fog. Isn't that the silliest thing?"

Aiyu felt something warm bubble up inside her translucent middle. She knew exactly what it felt like to be small and worried in a great big world.

"Wú," she said, "would you believe it — I can see perfectly. I'm see-through, you see. The fog goes right *through* me and comes out the other side, so I can spot the path just fine. Climb onto my pebble. I'll guide you home."

Wú's eyes went as round as two dewdrops. "You'd do that? For a clumsy little fog-puff like me?"

"Of course," said Aiyu. "Kindness costs nothing — and besides, I think you're wonderful."

---

So off they wobbled. Aiyu bounced from stone to stone (she was very good at bouncing, being mostly squish), and behind her floated Wú, holding onto a little thread of Aiyu's jelly like the string of a balloon.

"Left here," said Aiyu, "and mind the slippery moss — ooh, *that's* slippery."

"Wheee — oof," said Wú, who slipped anyway and landed softly on a mushroom. The mushroom said nothing, because mushrooms rarely do, but it did look mildly surprised.

"You know," puffed Wú, climbing back up, "I once spent a *whole night* drifting in a circle because I kept following my own foggy bottom. I thought it was somebody *else's* bottom. I said 'excuse me' to it forty times."

Aiyu laughed so hard she jiggled clean off her pebble and had to bounce twice to get back on. "Forty times!"

"Forty-one," said Wú solemnly. "I'm very polite to my own bottom."

They wobbled on, giggling, past a sleepy pine that dripped dew on Aiyu's head — *plip* — and past a fat brown toad who blinked one eye at them and went right back to sleep. The path twisted up between the rocks, slick and silvery, and Aiyu found that the more she watched out for Wú, the less she worried about herself. *That's funny,* she thought. *Worrying about a friend is much cozier than worrying about me.*

They were laughing so hard about the bottom that they almost didn't notice the leaf.

It was a slender, curled jade-green tea leaf, sitting cross-legged (well, cross-*curled*) atop a granite boulder, wearing a tiny conical bamboo hat and breathing very, very slowly. Swirls of orchid-scented steam puffed up from its tip, and around it the fog had gone soft and pearly, almost glowing.

"Ah," said the tea leaf, opening one peaceful eye. "Travelers. On a night like this. The mountain breathes deep tonight." It tipped its little hat. "I am Ā-Lǐ. I grow up here, where the air is thin and the mist is thick. **早安** (zǎo ān) = good morning... though I suppose it is rather more like good *midnight*."

"Hello, Ā-Lǐ!" said Aiyu, bowing her whole wobbly self. "I'm Aiyu, and this is Wú. We're helping him find his way home, only the fog is *terribly* thick."

"It is," agreed Ā-Lǐ, slow as a sunrise. "Steep slow, breathe deep. The fog cannot be hurried, little ones. But it can be *helped*." The tea leaf unfurled a curl and pointed downstream. "Listen. Do you hear them?"

Aiyu held very still. And then she did hear them — tiny voices, drifting through the gray.

*"Help!" "Where's the path?" "I bonked a turtle!" "Is anyone there?"*

"More mist spirits," breathed Aiyu. "Lots and lots of them. All lost."

---

Now, a shy little jelly might have wobbled the other way. The old Aiyu probably would have. But the new Aiyu — the one with a fog-puff friend holding her string — squared her wobbly corners and said the bravest thing she had ever said:

"Then we'll guide *all* of them home."

Ā-Lǐ smiled the smallest, warmest smile. "A clear heart," the tea leaf murmured, "sees clearest of all." And the leaf released a long, slow breath of fragrant steam that drifted out across the whole valley like a soft silver blanket, calming everything it touched.

So began the kindest rescue the mountain had ever known.

One by one, the mist spirits found their way to Aiyu — who, being see-through, glowed faintly golden in the fog like a tiny lantern. They came bumping and bonking and apologizing:

*Boop.* "Sorry!" That was Mí, who was shaped like a sleepy comma.

*Boop boop.* "Excuse us!" That was the twins, Lù and Lù, who always traveled in a pair and finished each other's *"—sentences!"*

*BOOP.* "Whoopsie-doodle." That was Pàng, the roundest, biggest, bounciest mist spirit of all, who apologized so sweetly that nobody could ever be cross. Pàng was so soft and so large that when he bonked into things he *bounced*, and when he bounced he giggled, and his giggle was so contagious that pretty soon the whole foggy hillside was giggling along with him.

"I bonked a pinecone earlier," Pàng confessed, "and I said sorry, and *it rolled away from me*. A pinecone! Rolling away! Was I that frightening?"

"You are the *least* frightening thing I have ever met," Aiyu assured him, patting his cottony side. "You're like a marshmallow that learned to float."

Pàng beamed. It is hard to make a cloud beam, but he managed it.

Soon Aiyu had a whole parade of fog-puffs trailing behind her, each holding the next one's misty hand, like a string of cottony pearls floating down the mountain. There were big ones and small ones, comma-shaped ones and dumpling-shaped ones, and one extremely tiny one named Diǎn who was no bigger than a single breath and rode happily on top of Pàng like a feather on a pillow. And Aiyu — who used to wish she could disappear — led them all, calling out the path in her gentle voice.

"Step down here, friends! Mind the frog — hello again, frog — and follow my glow! Lù and Lù, please stop bonking each other; yes, I know it's funny, but save it for the bottom of the hill."

"We'll save it —"

"— for the bottom!" agreed the twins, and bonked once more for luck.

"You're like a little moon," sighed Wú happily.

"I'm like a little *jelly*," laughed Aiyu, "but I'll take it."

---

They wobbled past the **茶** (chá) = tea bushes, all silvery with dew. They wobbled past a rubbing-stone where, every dawn, gentle hands swished aiyu seeds in cool water until the magic jelly set — Aiyu's very own birthplace, and she felt proud to lead her new friends past it. They wobbled, at last, to a hollow tucked between two boulders, soft with moss and glowing with a warm, sleepy light.

But just before they reached it, the parade hit a snag. The path dropped away into a deep cold ravine, and the only way across was a slim mossy log, slick with dew and slippery as a noodle.

"Oh no," whispered Diǎn, the tiniest one. "If I float across and the wind puffs, I'll blow clean off the mountain. I'm only the size of a sneeze."

The mist spirits huddled together, worried all over again, their cottony edges trembling.

Aiyu thought hard. Then she did the most see-through thing of all: she stretched. Being jelly, she could go *very* wobbly when she tried, and she draped herself across the gap like a wobbly golden bridge, glowing soft and warm.

"Quick," she said, "walk across me. I'm sturdy as a jelly can be, which is to say — careful, but I'll hold."

One by one, the mist spirits tiptoed across the wobbling golden Aiyu-bridge, and one by one they made it safe to the other side. Pàng went last, very slowly, holding his breath, and when he reached the far side he turned around and cried, "You held *all of us*! And you're *jelly*!"

"I'm *stubborn* jelly," said Aiyu, springing back into a cube with a satisfying *boing*. "There's a difference."

And then, at last —

"Home!" cried all the mist spirits at once. "This is home!"

And oh, the *joy*. The little fog-puffs tumbled and twirled and did happy loop-the-loops, hugging each other and hugging Aiyu (which felt like being hugged by a warm cloud, which is to say: lovely). Pàng was so happy he hiccuped and turned a somersault and landed on his own face, and everyone laughed until the boulders nearly giggled too.

"We were so worried," said Wú, "and you cleared *all* of it away. Every foggy bit of it."

"I didn't clear the fog," said Aiyu honestly — for she was always honest, that was simply her way. "The fog's still here. I just... helped you *through* it. One kind step at a time."

Ā-Lǐ, who had drifted down behind them on a curl of steam, nodded its bamboo-hatted head. "And that," said the tea leaf softly, "is the secret of every mountain. The fog never truly lifts. But a kind heart? A kind heart is *see-through*. It lets the worry pass right through and out the other side — and then there is room to see the way home."

Aiyu glowed a little brighter at that. She didn't feel shy at all anymore. She felt clear, all the way through.

---

By the time the first pale light crept over the **山**, the mist spirits were yawning their cottony yawns and snuggling into their mossy hollow. Ā-Lǐ unfurled fully in the warming air, releasing one last gorgeous swirl of orchid-and-honey fragrance over the whole sleepy valley.

"Steep slow," yawned the tea leaf. "Breathe deep. And come visit again, little lantern."

"I will," promised Aiyu. "Goodnight, Ā-Lǐ. Goodnight, mist spirits. Goodnight, mountain."

Wú floated up and gave her one last soft, cloudy nuzzle on her wobbly cheek.

"Thank you for seeing us," he whispered, "when we couldn't even see ourselves."

And Aiyu — the shyest, see-through-est, kindest little jelly on the whole tall, misty, giggling mountain — wobbled home through the clearing dawn, glowing golden as a tiny sun, feeling, for the very first time, not invisible at all.

She felt seen. And she felt very, very loved.

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, the fog on your own mountain may come and go, and that is alright — you do not have to chase it away. You only have to be kind, and a kind heart is clear enough to see the path through any gray. Tonight, breathe slow like Ā-Lǐ, and let your worries drift right through you and out the other side. Somewhere up high, a golden jelly is glowing soft as a lantern, lighting the way for sleepy little fog-puffs all the way home. May your dreams be misty-soft and warm. Goodnight.
