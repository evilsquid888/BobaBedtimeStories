# Story 54: Moon Rabbit & the Mochi-Pounding Lullaby

**Main Character:** Yù Tù (Moon Rabbit)
**Guest Characters:** Mochi
**Setting:** The surface of the moon, glowing above sleeping Taiwan
**Theme:** Gentle rhythms carry everyone to sleep.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 月亮 (yuèliàng) = moon

---

High up above sleeping Taiwan, on the soft silvery curve of the **月亮** (yuèliàng) = moon, there lived a rabbit who refused to use an alarm clock.

She didn't need one. Yù Tù, the Moon Rabbit, was snow-white and fluffy as a freshly washed cloud, with long velvety ears that flopped when she thought hard and a tiny crescent-moon pendant that bonked her chin whenever she hopped. Her eyes were pink as the inside of a peach, and she carried a little wooden mallet she loved more than anything in the whole sky.

Every single night, while the island below pulled its blankets up to its chin, Yù Tù pounded mochi.

*Thump.* *Thump.* *Thump-a-thump.*

"Pound, pound," she whispered, "sweet dreams abound."

Tonight, though, something was wrong. Yù Tù lifted her mallet, brought it down — and her giant bowl of moon-rice went *ploop* instead of *thump*. Floppy. Soggy. Sad.

"Oh dear," she sighed, ears drooping like wet noodles. "My rhythm has gone all wobbly."

And down below, on the whole entire island, not one single child could fall asleep.

---

Now, here is a secret the grown-ups never tell you, but I will tell *you*, because it's bedtime and you can keep a secret.

The reason Taiwan sleeps so sweetly each night is *the Moon Rabbit's pounding*. Her mallet keeps a beat — a slow, soft, cozy beat — and that beat slides down the moonbeams like a marble down a slide, and it tucks itself into every pillow on the island. *Thump, thump, sleep.* *Thump, thump, deep.*

But tonight the beat had gone lopsided. And so, far below, in a snug little kitchen, a small pillowy fellow sat bolt upright in bed.

That was Mochi — pillowy, white, stretchy, and extremely huggable, the kind of friend who could wrap all the way around you twice and still have arms left over for a sandwich. Mochi was a champion sleeper. Mochi could nap through a typhoon, a drum parade, and his auntie's loudest karaoke. So when *Mochi* couldn't sleep, you knew the trouble was serious.

"Something's off," Mochi mumbled, bouncing gently with worry. "The night feels... un-sleepy."

He padded to the window. The moon hung huge and pearly and close — closer than he'd ever seen it, as if it had leaned down to whisper. And riding down from it came a single thin moonbeam, shimmering like a silver ribbon, landing *right* on Mochi's windowsill.

Mochi blinked. Mochi was stretchy. And stretchy things are very good at climbing ribbons.

"Stick together," he told himself bravely, which is what Mochi always said even when he was the only one there. "Let's bounce."

He grabbed the moonbeam and *boing* — up he went.

---

Climbing a moonbeam, it turns out, feels exactly like climbing a very long, very gentle escalator made of cool whipped light. Mochi stretched and sproinged, stretched and sproinged, past sleepy clouds who waved their floppy edges at him, past a flock of stars playing a slow game of tag.

"'Scuse me," Mochi said politely to a star. "Pardon. Coming through. Lovely twinkling, by the way."

At last he reached the top, rolled over the soft edge of the moon, and landed *poomf* in a drift of glittering moon-dust.

And there she was: a small white rabbit, ears down, staring glumly into an enormous stone bowl.

"Oh!" said Yù Tù, spinning round. Her pendant bonked her chin. "A visitor! On the **月亮**! I haven't had a visitor since the year a dragon got lost up here looking for the bathroom."

"I'm Mochi," said Mochi, brushing dust off his squishy self. "And — wow. You're soft."

"You're *softer*," gasped Yù Tù, and she reached out and gave his cheek the gentlest poke, and Mochi went *sproing* and bounced four times, and the Moon Rabbit laughed so hard her ears flapped right up over her eyes.

"Sorry — sorry," she giggled, peeking out from under one ear. "I haven't laughed all night. It's been a terrible night."

"I *know*," said Mochi. "Nobody on the whole island can sleep. I came up to see why."

Yù Tù's whiskers wilted. She pointed her little mallet at the giant bowl. "It's my mochi," she said. "I pound it every night to make the lullaby beat — the one that drifts down and tucks everyone in. But tonight my arms got tired and my rhythm went all *crooked*, and now look."

Mochi looked. The moon-rice in the bowl was a sad, soggy, lumpy mess, all stuck to one side.

"I keep losing the beat," Yù Tù said, very small. "Thump-thump — thumpa — *thwip* — and then it's all wrong, and the dreams won't roll. I'm just one little rabbit. Some nights one mallet isn't enough." She sniffled, but it was a soft, sweet sniffle, the kind that means *I'm a bit sad* and not *the world is ending*.

Mochi, who had once been pounded into existence in a bowl exactly like this one, felt something warm puff up inside his pillowy chest.

"Hey," he said. "I think I can help. I *am* mochi. I was *made* by pounding. I know this rhythm better than my own bedtime."

Yù Tù's pink eyes went round. "You'd really help?"

"Stick together," said Mochi. "That's the only way I know how to do anything."

---

So the two of them set to work, and here is where the magic truly began.

Yù Tù lifted her little wooden mallet. Mochi puffed himself big and round and stretched out two squishy arms like a champion. They stood on either side of the great stone bowl, and Yù Tù counted them in.

"One... two... and a sleepy-three—"

*Thump,* went the rabbit's mallet.

*Thump,* went Mochi's squashy arms.

*Thump-a-thump-a-thump.*

Oh, it was glorious. Two poured into one. When Yù Tù's arms got tired, Mochi swung. When Mochi needed a breath, Yù Tù picked up the beat. The moon-rice began to come together — smooth and glossy and stretchy and shining, the softest, dreamiest mochi the moon had ever seen.

But more than that: the *beat* came back. Steady. Slow. Cozy as a heartbeat heard through a blanket.

*Thump, thump — sleep.* *Thump, thump — deep.*

"It's working!" Yù Tù squealed, hopping in a circle. "Look! Look at the **月亮**!"

For with every thump, soft silvery dust puffed up out of the bowl and floated to the edge of the moon — and then *spilled over*, tumbling down the moonbeams toward the island like glittering snow, like a slow sweet waterfall of *shhhh*.

Mochi peeked over the edge. Far, far below, he could see Taiwan all curled up in the dark. And one by one, as the moon-dust drifted down, the lights in the windows blinked out. *Pip. Pip. Pip.* A whole island, sliding gently into sleep.

"That's everybody," Mochi whispered. "They're dreaming now. Because of us."

"Because of *you*," said Yù Tù warmly, bonking him with her pendant on purpose this time. "I couldn't keep the beat alone tonight. You floated all the way up here just to help a tired rabbit pound her rice. That's the kindest thing anyone's done in a thousand moons."

Mochi turned a little pink, which for a white mochi is mostly just glowing.

"Now," said Yù Tù, and she scooped up a fresh, warm, stretchy piece of the moon-mochi they had made together. It steamed gently in the cool moonlight, soft as a cloud and sweet as a secret. "There's only one proper way to thank a helper. You have to taste the mochi you made."

Mochi's eyes went huge. "I — but I — I'm *also* mochi," he stammered. "Isn't that a bit... strange?"

Yù Tù grinned the way only a moon creature can grin. "Not strange," she said. "*Wonderful.* It's like a song hearing itself hummed. Go on. It's the happiest bite in the sky."

And Mochi, who was brave even when he wobbled, popped the warm little piece into his mouth.

Oh, the *joy* of it! It tasted like clean snow and sweet dreams and the gentle hush right before you fall asleep. It tasted like *home*, somehow, even all the way up here. Mochi felt like a firework that had finally gotten to light the sky — proud and tingly and full to the brim with happy. He glowed so bright that, for one tiny moment, the people on the island below smiled in their sleep without knowing why.

"That," Mochi said, mouth full and grinning, "is the best mochi in the entire **月亮**."

"Of course it is," said Yù Tù, scooping a piece for herself. "*We* made it."

---

They sat together at the soft edge of the moon, two of the softest things in all the sky, sharing warm mochi and watching the dark island breathe slow below them.

"Will you come back?" Yù Tù asked. "Some nights one mallet really isn't enough. But two..." She bumped Mochi gently, shoulder to squishy shoulder. "Two is plenty."

"Every night I'm needed," Mochi promised. "Just send a moonbeam. I'm a *very* good climber."

So if you ever look up and see the moon glowing especially round and especially gold, you'll know: up there, a fluffy white rabbit and a pillowy white friend are pounding sweet mochi side by side, keeping the beat that keeps the whole world dreaming.

*Thump, thump — sleep.*

*Thump, thump — deep.*

And that, my sleepy darling, is the lullaby drifting down to you right now.
