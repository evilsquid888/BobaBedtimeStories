# Story 95: The Sweet-and-Savory Cook-Off Truce

**Main Character:** Niú Niú (Beef Noodle Soup)
**Guest Characters:** Shaved Ice, Tāng Yuán, Pái Pái
**Setting:** A grand pre-reunion cook-off in a giant covered market hall
**Theme:** The best meal has a little of everything.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 合作 (hézuò) = cooperation

---

The biggest covered market hall on the whole island had never, not once in a hundred sleepy years, been THIS loud.

Banners flapped. Steam rose in fat curly ribbons. A wooden sign over the doorway swung back and forth, painted in gold:

**THE GREAT PRE-REUNION COOK-OFF — Tonight!**

And right in the middle of it all stood Niú Niú, a big warm clay bowl with a friendly face on his rim, wrapped in a long wheat-noodle scarf that he tossed over one shoulder with a *whump* like a movie star. His beef-cube shoulders steamed. His bok-choy collar wobbled. A little cozy cloud floated above his head like a personal weather system.

"Slurp first, worry later," Niú Niú rumbled to nobody in particular — which was his way of saying good morning, good evening, and good luck all at once.

Then he noticed the line painted down the middle of the hall.

A line. Straight down the middle. Tape and everything.

On one side: a banner reading **SAVORY SQUAD**. On the other side, in glittery frosting letters: **SWEET CREW**.

"Uh oh," said Niú Niú. "That's a lot of *line* for a family that's supposed to be having a reunion."

---

It had started, like most silly things do, over something small.

Pái Pái — an enormous crispy golden chicken cutlet, bigger than her own face — had flexed her crunchy cape and announced, "The reunion feast needs a CHAMPION dish. Something bold! Something *savory*! Bigger than your face, bigger than your worries!"

And TOWERING over the dessert side, SHAVED ICE rattled all of his wobbly toppings and boomed, "WRONG! THE CHAMPION DISH MUST BE *SWEET*! AND TALL! AND DRAMATIC! LIKE ME!" (Shaved Ice spoke only in capital letters. It was just how he was built.)

So a cook-off was declared. Savory versus sweet. Winner gets the center of the reunion table. Loser gets the corner by the napkins.

Which is how a happy family ended up split right down the middle with TAPE.

Niú Niú scratched his rim with a noodle. "Kiddo," he said to a small round rice ball bouncing past, "what in the dumplings is going on?"

The rice ball stopped. He was Tāng Yuán — a jolly white sphere with a sweet sesame center, who always, always said *we*.

"We are *competing*," sighed Tāng Yuán, and he did not sound like he enjoyed it. "We used to be a family. Now we are two *teams*. I miss when we were just... one big round everybody."

Niú Niú's steam-cloud drooped a little. He did not like the sound of that one bit.

"And it's getting *worse*," Tāng Yuán went on, rolling closer and lowering his voice. "Yesterday the Savory Squad wouldn't share the good chopsticks. So the Sweet Crew hid all the spoons. Now nobody can eat ANYTHING without scooping it with their face."

As if to prove it, a small pepper bun hopped past trying to drink soup by dunking his whole head in. He came up with broth in his ears.

"See?" said Tāng Yuán mournfully. "Chaos. *Soggy* chaos."

Niú Niú let out a long warm sigh, and the steam from it fogged up a nearby tea egg's glasses. "Kiddo," he said, "a family that hides each other's spoons is a family that's forgotten how to set the table. We'll fix it. We always do." He gave his scarf a gentle, thoughtful flick. "But first... I need to watch this silly contest. A good big brother never solves a problem before he understands it. Slurp first, *think* later — that's the slow-cooked version."

---

The cook-off began.

On the Savory side, Pái Pái led her squad like a general. Pepper buns hopped. Scallion pancakes cartwheeled. A whole giggling crew of popcorn chicken nuggets reshuffled themselves into the shape of the number ONE.

"FLAVOR!" Pái Pái roared, sprinkling plum powder like confetti. "We bring the SALT! We bring the SPICE! We bring the CRUNCH!"

On the Sweet side, SHAVED ICE built himself taller and taller until his sunglasses scraped the ceiling lanterns. Brown sugar syrup drizzled artistic swirls. Mango did sporty little jumps. Tāng Yuán bobbed sadly in his bowl, not feeling very *we* at all.

"BEHOLD!" thundered Shaved Ice. "SWEETNESS! BEAUTY! AND HEIGHT! SO MUCH HEIGHT!"

The two sides cooked. And bragged. And cooked some more.

The Savory Squad cooked a feast so bold it made the lanterns flicker. There were braised aromas and peppery sizzles and a crackle so loud the floorboards hummed. Pái Pái stood on top of it all, cape billowing, shouting, "Bigger than your face, bigger than your WORRIES!"

The Sweet Crew answered with a tower so tall and beautiful that three baby tangyuan rolled over just to cry happy tears at the sight of it. Brown sugar painted golden tiger-stripes down the sides. It glittered. It sparkled. It had a little flag on top for no reason except DRAMA.

The judges — three very confused tea eggs — took one nibble of the savory dish and went, "Mmm! Bold!" Then one nibble of the sweet dish and went, "Mmm! Lovely!" Then they looked at each other and went, "...so which one *wins*?"

And nobody knew. Because they were exactly, perfectly, equally delicious.

The tea eggs whispered among themselves, their cracked shells crinkling. "If we pick savory, the sweets will sulk." "If we pick sweet, the savories will stomp." "If we pick *neither*, they'll BOTH chase us into the cooler." They huddled tighter. "We are eggs," one squeaked. "We were not built for this kind of pressure."

Pái Pái flexed harder. Shaved Ice grew taller. The tape line on the floor seemed to get wider and lonelier by the minute.

That's when a small voice piped up.

"Excuse me," said Tāng Yuán, rolling right up to the tape. "Could I — could *we* — try something? I have a feeling. A round little feeling."

---

Now, Niú Niú had been watching all of this with his warm, slow, winter-night eyes. And he'd been thinking. Big brothers do a lot of thinking, even when they're mostly steam.

He stepped right onto the tape line. One foot on Savory, one foot on Sweet. His noodle-scarf draped across both sides at once.

"Kiddos," he said, in that voice like a quilt fresh from the dryer, "gather round. I want to tell you something my grandmother — a very wise stock-pot — once told me."

Everyone leaned in. Even Shaved Ice bent down (which took a while).

"She said: *a soup is never just one thing.*" Niú Niú slurped thoughtfully. "It's the beef AND the noodle AND the broth AND the little green onion on top. Take any ONE away, and it's not the best version of itself. It's just... lonely flavor."

"So what are you saying, big bowl?" asked Pái Pái, lowering her crispy cape just a smidge.

Niú Niú grinned. "I'm saying I think you've all been asking the wrong question. You keep asking *who wins, sweet or savory?*" He tossed his scarf. "But the best meal in the world has never been sweet OR savory, kiddo."

He paused, the way good storytellers do.

"It's sweet *and* savory. The winning dish isn't on EITHER side of this tape." He pointed one steamy beef-cube finger straight down at the line. "It's right HERE. In the middle. Where you make it **合作** (hézuò) = *together*."

The whole hall went quiet. Even the lanterns stopped swinging.

"**合作**," Niú Niú said again, soft and warm. "Cooperation. It just means we cook the dish *as one big round everybody*."

Tāng Yuán's sesame center practically glowed. "That's MY round feeling!" he cried. "That's *exactly* it! *We!*"

---

What happened next was the best kind of beautiful chaos.

Pái Pái peeled the tape right off the floor and balled it up. "Fine!" she laughed. "But MY crunch goes in. Non-negotiable."

"OBVIOUSLY," boomed Shaved Ice. "AND MY HEIGHT. WE WILL BUILD UPWARD!"

They got to work — together. And oh, it was a marvelous mess.

Niú Niú poured a warm, savory beef broth into a great wide pot, his noodle-scarf swirling in like the world's first ingredient. Brown sugar dripped in a single shy ribbon — "JUST a touch," everyone agreed, and it made the broth glow amber and rich. Pái Pái crumbled her crispy, plum-dusted edges over the top for crunch. The popcorn chicken crew cannonballed in, giggling, "More crunch, more fun!"

Tāng Yuán bobbed gently into the center, his sweet sesame heart melting just so, until the broth tasted of comfort and surprise all at once. "Ooh," he gasped, going a little dizzy from the warmth, "I can feel the *we* happening! It tickles!" Mango added a sunny tang and a sporty little fist-pump. Scallion pancake flipped a crispy spiral over the top like a hat, calling out, "Let's flip the script — literally, I'm a hat now!"

Somebody nudged the missing spoons out from their hiding place. Somebody else slid the good chopsticks back into the cup. Nobody said sorry, exactly — but everybody quietly put things back where they belonged, which is the same thing in family language.

And SHAVED ICE — well, he couldn't melt INTO a hot soup, of course (that would just be sad and soggy). So instead he stacked himself proudly on the *side*, in his own little frosty bowl, as a cool sweet finish.

"BEHOLD," he said, but quieter this time, almost gentle. "DESSERT *AND* DINNER. SIDE BY SIDE. AT LAST."

The three confused tea-egg judges leaned over the new dish. They sniffed the savory steam. They spotted the sweet glow. They tasted.

For a long moment, nobody breathed.

Then all three tea eggs burst into happy cracks and shouted together: "THIS ONE! THIS IS THE CHAMPION! It has a little of EVERYTHING!"

---

The hall erupted. Pancakes cartwheeled. Pepper buns hopped so high they bonked the lanterns. Mango did a victory lap. And Tāng Yuán rolled in giddy circles squealing, "WE WON! WE *ALL* won! Because there's no *we* without *everybody*!"

Pái Pái and Shaved Ice — who an hour ago had glared across a strip of tape — bumped a crispy cape against a frosty shoulder.

"You're not so bad, tall guy," said Pái Pái.

"NEITHER ARE YOU, CRUNCHY," said Shaved Ice. "FOR A SAVORY PERSON."

The tea eggs, hugely relieved that nobody would chase them into the cooler, did a little wobbly conga line of joy.

Niú Niú watched it all with the warmest, steamiest, most big-brotherly smile a clay bowl ever wore. He tossed his noodle-scarf one final, glorious time.

"And THAT, kiddos," he rumbled, "is **合作**. Slurp first. Worry later. And always — *always* — leave room at the table for the other half of the flavor."

The dish they made that night sat right in the dead center of the reunion table, glowing sweet and savory and warm, with everybody's hands stirred into it.

It was the best meal anyone could remember.

Because the best meal always has a little of everything.
