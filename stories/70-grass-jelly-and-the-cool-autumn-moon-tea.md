# Story 70: Grass Jelly & the Cool Autumn Tea Hour

**Main Character:** Grass Jelly (仙草)
**Guest Characters:** Ā-Lǐ (Alishan Oolong Tea Leaf), Kǎ Kǎ (Popcorn Chicken)
**Setting:** A breezy teahouse balcony on a crisp autumn evening
**Theme:** The coolest thing you can do is help someone feel calm.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 秋天 (qiūtiān) = autumn

---

High above the loudest, blinkiest, most cymbal-crashiest corner of the night market, there was a balcony where everything went quiet.

It belonged to Grass Jelly, an inky black-green cube of cool, wobbly calm who wore a small black beanie at all hours and had, by general agreement, never once been seen to hurry. Down below, the festival roared — drums, sparklers, a man selling balloons shaped like alarmingly cheerful octopuses. But up here, on the teahouse balcony, the only sounds were the soft *clink* of cups and the **秋天** (qiūtiān) = autumn breeze sliding by like a yawning cat.

Grass Jelly set out three little cups, gave the night sky a slow nod, and said the coolest thing a cube can say:

"Welcome to the quiet hour."

Then a popcorn chicken hit the balcony like a bag of marbles dropped down a staircase.

---

It was Kǎ Kǎ — or rather, it was *Kǎ Kǎ*, plural, a whole giggling cluster of golden-crispy popcorn-chicken nuggets dusted with pepper and crowned with tiny fried-basil hats. They bounced in every direction at once, finishing each other's sentences and starting four new ones.

"WE HEARD there was a party up here—" "—a SECRET party—" "—are there snacks—" "—are WE the snacks—" "—MORE CRUNCH, MORE FUN!"

They ricocheted off a teapot, a railing, and a potted orchid before settling into a vibrating little pile that could not stop wiggling.

Grass Jelly blinked, slow as moonrise. "It's not a party, kiddo. It's a tea hour."

"A tea hour!" the nuggets chorused. "What do you DO at a tea hour?!"

"You sit," said Grass Jelly. "You sip. You let the autumn breeze do the rest."

The nuggets stared. One of them fell over. "We tried sitting once. It lasted half a second. Then we sneezed and rolled off a table."

From the doorway came a sound like wind through bamboo — a gentle, knowing chuckle.

It was Ā-Lǐ, the high-mountain oolong tea leaf: slender, curled, jade-green, with a tiny conical bamboo hat and a faint silvery mist that clung to her like she'd brought a piece of the mountain down for the evening. She smelled of orchids and cool fog. When she moved, the air seemed to slow down to keep her company.

"Steep slow," she said, settling beside the teapot. "Breathe deep."

"WHAT'S STEEPING?" asked Kǎ Kǎ, already vibrating again.

---

Now, here is a thing you should know about autumn in the night market.

Autumn is the trickiest season for the snack folk. Summer is easy — everyone melts a little and naps in the shade. Winter is cozy — everyone huddles up and slurps soup. But autumn? Autumn is *exciting*. The air gets crisp and electric, the moon swells fat and golden, and the festivals come one after another like fireworks: lantern parades, harvest dances, the great mooncake gatherings. The whole market gets jumpy with the thrill of it.

And nobody got jumpier than Kǎ Kǎ.

"The thing is," Grass Jelly explained, lowering himself onto a cushion with the dignity of a cube who has never spilled a drop of anything, "down there, the festival is *all go-go-go*. Folks forget how to stop. And when you forget how to stop, kiddo, your insides start to rattle."

"OUR insides ARE rattling," admitted Kǎ Kǎ. "All the time! We thought that was just being us!"

"It's a little bit being you," said Grass Jelly kindly. "But it can also feel nice to be still. Stillness is its own kind of cool."

Kǎ Kǎ gasped — all of them, at once, a small popcorn windstorm. "Cool? Cool like *you*? You're the coolest cube in the whole market! Everybody says so! Grass Jelly never sweats, Grass Jelly never panics, Grass Jelly wears a beanie *indoors*—"

"The beanie helps," Grass Jelly agreed.

"—so if YOU say stillness is cool, then we wanna be still TOO! Right now! Let's be SO still! Watch us be the STILLEST!"

And the nuggets clenched themselves into one trembling, basil-hatted ball and held perfectly still for nearly... three whole seconds.

Then a moth flew by and they exploded in every direction, knocking over Grass Jelly's beanie.

---

Ā-Lǐ poured the tea.

She did it the way she did everything — slowly, gracefully, with the patience of a peak that has watched a thousand autumns roll past and is in no rush to see the next. Warm water met curled leaf, and the leaf unfurled, releasing a swirling little cloud of fragrant orchid-mist that drifted out over the balcony and down toward the noise below.

"In **秋天**," Ā-Lǐ said softly, "the mountain grows quiet. The loud green of summer fades, and what's left is the still, gold thread of the world. The best tea is grown in this season. Do you know why?"

The nuggets shook their many heads, fascinated despite themselves.

"Because the cold makes the leaf slow down," she said. "And a leaf that slows down has time to gather its sweetness. Hurry can't make sweetness. Only stillness can."

Kǎ Kǎ went very, very quiet. (For about four seconds. A record.)

"...So if we slow down," one nugget whispered, "we get *sweeter*?"

"Try it," said Grass Jelly, "and find out."

---

But just then — *of course* — there was a commotion below.

A great wail rose up from the festival: not a hurt wail, a *worried* one. Down in the square, a whole troupe of little lantern-bearers had gotten themselves wound up into a frantic knot. The harvest dance was about to begin, the moon was rising, the drums were thundering, and the lantern-bearers had worked themselves into such a jittery panic that none of them could remember which way to march. They were spinning in circles, bonking lanterns, on the very edge of a full-blown festival meltdown.

"OH NO," said Kǎ Kǎ, instantly buzzing again. "WE SHOULD GO DOWN THERE AND— and— RUN AROUND? And— BOUNCE AT THEM? And tell them to HURRY—"

"No, kiddo." Grass Jelly stood. He picked up his beanie, settled it, and looked out at the spinning, panicking crowd with the calmest expression in the entire night market.

"They're already hurrying. That's the problem. What they need," he said, "is someone to help them feel *calm*."

He turned to Kǎ Kǎ. "And here's the secret nobody tells you. The coolest thing you can ever do — cooler than the fastest bounce, cooler than the loudest crunch — is to walk into a panic and make it *quiet*."

Kǎ Kǎ's nuggets looked at each other. "We don't... we don't really *do* quiet."

"You learned a little tonight," said Grass Jelly. "Want to try the real thing?"

---

Here is what they did, and it was the most beautiful thing that happened in the market all autumn.

Grass Jelly went down first — wobbling slowly, slowly down the balcony stairs, so unhurried that just *watching* him made shoulders unclench. Behind him drifted Ā-Lǐ, trailing her cool orchid-mist, and the moment that mist reached the panicking lantern-bearers, they breathed it in and felt their hearts go *ahhh*, like a hot forehead meeting a cool cloth.

And then came Kǎ Kǎ — but not bouncing.

Kǎ Kǎ remembered the leaf. *Hurry can't make sweetness.* So the nuggets did the hardest thing they had ever done: they slowed down. They formed one calm, gentle cluster and walked — actually *walked* — into the middle of the spinning crowd, and instead of shouting "MORE CRUNCH, MORE FUN," they said, very softly:

"Hey. Hey. It's okay. Slow down with us. Sip slow."

And the lantern-bearers, seeing the *jitteriest* snacks in the whole market choosing to be still, felt something loosen in their chests. If Kǎ Kǎ could be calm, anyone could. One by one they stopped spinning. They breathed in Ā-Lǐ's mist. They watched Grass Jelly wobble, gentle and unbothered, and they remembered which way to march.

The drums softened to a heartbeat. The moon rose round and gold. And the harvest dance began — not frantic, but flowing, like syrup off a spoon.

---

Afterward, back up on the balcony, the three friends sat in the cool **秋天** breeze and drank their tea.

Kǎ Kǎ sat in one calm pile, basil hats tilted toward the moon, vibrating only the tiniest, happiest amount.

"That felt..." the nuggets began, and for once they didn't all talk at the same time. They let one little voice say it. "That felt as good as bouncing. Maybe better. We helped everybody feel okay. That's the coolest we've ever felt."

Grass Jelly raised his cup. "Told you, kiddo. Stillness is its own kind of cool."

Ā-Lǐ smiled into her mist. "Steep slow," she said. "Breathe deep."

And the nuggets — the giggling, rattling, never-stopping nuggets — closed their many eyes, leaned into the autumn quiet, and felt, for the very first time, sweet.

The moon is round and watching over you. Goodnight.
