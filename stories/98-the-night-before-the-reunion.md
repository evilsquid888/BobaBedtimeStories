# Story 98: The Night Before the Reunion

**Main Character:** Douhua (Tofu Pudding)
**Guest Characters:** Pudding, Mochi, Niú Niú
**Setting:** A hushed, cozy guesthouse the night before the grand gathering
**Theme:** Calm before the joy is a gift too.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 明天 (míngtiān) = tomorrow

---

The little guesthouse on Lantern Lane had never, in all its sleepy years, held this much fizzing excitement under one roof. Every room was stuffed full of friends, and every friend was full of one enormous, glittering thought: **明天** (míngtiān) = tomorrow was the Hundredth-Story Reunion.

One hundred stories! Imagine. Every dessert, dumpling, and dawn-time breakfast snack from across the whole island had come to gather for the biggest, warmest party ever thrown.

And not a single one of them could sleep.

Doors creaked. Floorboards giggled. Somebody was practicing a cartwheel in the hallway and bonking into the umbrella stand — *thump, thump, sorry, thump.* Pillows were being fluffed with the fury of a thousand worried hands. Down in the kitchen, three teacups were arguing about who got to sit closest to the teapot. Up on the roof, two friends who could not sleep had started a game of cloud-spotting, even though it was midnight and there were no clouds, only stars.

It was, the guesthouse decided with a sleepy creak, the most wide-awake night it had ever known.

Down at the end of the hall, in a quiet little room with a round window, Douhua woke up gently — because Douhua always did everything gently — and listened to the happy chaos.

"Oh dear," wobbled Douhua softly. "Nobody's resting at all."

---

Now, you should know about Douhua. She was a silky-smooth wobble of snow-white tofu pudding, swimming in a warm bowl of sweet ginger syrup that smelled of honey and faraway mountains. When she moved, she shimmered like moonlight on a pond. She was the calmest soul in the entire collection — so calm that flustered friends would simply look at her and feel their worries melt like sugar in tea.

She slid out of her bowl-bed, gathered a little ladle of warm ginger syrup just in case, and padded into the hallway on her wobbly bottom. She did not hurry. Douhua had never hurried in her life. Hurrying, she liked to say, was just worrying with its shoes on.

The first door she peeked through belonged to Pudding.

Poor Pudding. The wobbly caramel flan was sitting bolt upright in bed, jiggling so hard his whole little dish rattled against the nightstand — *clatter-clatter-clatter* — like a tiny earthquake made entirely of nerves.

"Douhua!" Pudding gasped. "I can't sleep! What if I trip at the reunion? What if I wobble into the snack table? What if I wobble into the snack table and knock over the pyramid of moon cakes and they roll down the stairs and out the door and into the harbor and a fish eats them and—"

"Breathe," said Douhua, with a smile soft as steam. "**慢慢來** (màn man lái) = take it slow."

She floated to his bedside and gave a single, slow, gentle wobble — *wooble, wooooble* — like ripples spreading across a still pond.

"Watch my wobble," she whispered. "In. And out. In... and out."

Pudding watched. And — funny thing — his frantic *clatter-clatter* began to slow. *Clatter... clatter... cla... tter...* until his jiggle matched her wobble exactly, smooth and easy and round, the two of them swaying together like reflections in a pond.

"Ooh," said Pudding, surprised. "I'm doing it. I'm being calm. I've never been calm before. Is this what calm feels like? It's a little boring." He paused. "I think I like a little boring."

"There," murmured Douhua. "You see? Even your wobble can be a calm thing. **明天** will come whether you fret or not. So why not arrive at it rested?"

"But what if I—"

"If you wobble into the snack table tomorrow," said Douhua kindly, "then you will be the bravest, bounciest, most magnificent wobble anyone has ever seen. And we will all cheer." She tucked his little blanket up to his caramel chin. "Sleep now, brave one."

Pudding yawned an enormous flan-yawn. "I am... pretty brave..." And before he could finish, he was snoring the gentle snore of a dish of custard at peace.

*One down,* thought Douhua warmly. *Many to go.*

---

The next door rattled with a different sort of sound — a soft, squishy *boing, boing, boing.*

Inside, Mochi was bouncing off the walls. Literally. The pillowy white stretchy blob was ricocheting from bed to ceiling to wall to floor, leaving little dusty handprints everywhere, squealing with pure joy.

"DOUHUA!" Mochi cried, bouncing past her face. "We're all going to be together! ALL of us! A hundred stories' worth of friends, all in one place! I'm too happy to sleep! My happy is too BIG!"

*Boing.* He stuck to the ceiling for a moment, beaming down at her.

"Your happy is a wonderful thing," Douhua agreed, gazing up. "But a happy that's all bounce and no rest gets tired and grumpy halfway through the party. And we don't want a grumpy Mochi at the reunion, do we?"

Mochi peeled off the ceiling and *floomped* onto his bed, thinking very hard. "...No. Grumpy Mochi is the worst Mochi. One time I was grumpy and I stuck to a chair for an hour and wouldn't let anyone sit down. It was not my finest moment."

"Here is a secret," Douhua said, settling beside him. "Tonight's quiet is part of tomorrow's joy. The stillness now is like... like the deep breath before you blow out birthday candles. The calm makes the wish even bigger."

Mochi blinked his squishy eyes. "The calm makes the wish bigger?"

"Mm-hmm. So let's save up all your bounce. Every single boing. And tomorrow, when everyone arrives, you'll have a *whole night's worth* of bounce stored up to give them."

Mochi gasped. This was the best idea he had ever heard. "A whole NIGHT'S worth!" He stretched himself out long and flat and soft, like dough rolled smooth. "I'm saving it. I'm saving ALL my bounce."

He held very still. Then he wiggled one corner. "...Can I save it lying down? Saving is hard when you want to boing." 

"Lying down is the very best way to save bounce," Douhua assured him. "Champions do it. Ask the moon."

He squeezed his eyes shut with great determination. "Stick together... tomorrow..." he mumbled, and melted into sleep, one happy little snore-bubble rising and falling on his squishy cheek.

Douhua smoothed his pillowy edge and floated out.

---

The hardest room was the last one.

It glowed with a warm orange light and breathed out clouds of the coziest smell in the world — beef and ginger and slow-simmered comfort. From inside came a low, restless sloshing, like a pot stirring itself in worry.

Niú Niú.

The big steaming clay bowl sat by the window, his noodle-scarf draped around his shoulders, staring out at the dark with his kind face all crinkled up. His cozy steam-halo, usually so puffy and bright, drifted thin and gray.

"Kiddo," he rumbled when he saw her, his voice a slow warm winter. "Didn't expect anybody else awake."

"You're worried," Douhua said. It wasn't a question. She wobbled in and settled beside the big bowl, tiny next to his hearty shoulders.

Niú Niú let out a long, steamy sigh that fogged up the windowpane. "One hundred stories. That's a lot of friends countin' on tomorrow bein' perfect. I keep thinkin'... what if I'm not warm enough? What if my soup's gone cold by the time everyone's hungry? What if my noodle-scarf gets tangled and I trip during the welcome toast? A reunion's only as good as the welcome, kiddo, and I'm the welcomin' bowl. Always have been. If I fail 'em..."

He stirred his own broth glumly. A single sad bok choy leaf bobbed up and sank again.

Douhua looked at her gentle, kind, big-brotherly friend — the one who comforted *everyone*, who always said *slurp first, worry later* — and realized that even the warmest soul sometimes needs warming back.

So she did the gentlest thing she knew. She tipped her little ladle and let one slow drop of warm honey-ginger syrup fall — *plip* — into Niú Niú's broth.

A sweet, golden ribbon swirled through his soup. The thin gray steam thickened, curled, and puffed up bright and silver again.

"Niú Niú," she said softly. "Do you know why everyone trusts you to welcome them? It isn't because you're never cold. It's because you let yourself be warmed, and then you pass it on. That's all warmth is. It's a thing we hand to each other."

Niú Niú's face crinkled — but this time into a smile. The honey-ginger glow spread all through his bowl, warming him from the bottom up, and a deep, comfortable steam-cloud bloomed around them both like a hug made of mist. The thin gray worry blew clean away.

"...Slurp first," he murmured, "worry later. I tell everybody that. Forgot to tell myself." He chuckled, low and warm, and the whole room felt softer, the way a kitchen feels when something good is simmering. "Thank you, kiddo. You came to soothe a soup, and you went and soothed the soother. That's a clever bit of cookin'."

"It isn't cooking," Douhua said with a quiet smile. "It's just remembering, together, that we don't have to be warm all by ourselves."

"That's what tonight is for," said Douhua. "The calm before the joy is a gift too. Maybe the best one."

Niú Niú's big eyes were already drooping. His steam-halo settled into a slow, sleepy drift. "Stay a minute?" he asked.

"Of course," she whispered.

---

And so Douhua sat with him until his breathing turned to the gentle bubble of a pot at perfect peace.

Then she floated back down the hall, past Mochi storing up all his bounce, past Pudding wobbling softly in his dreams, past every door now hushed and dim. The cartwheeling friend had finally curled up beside the umbrella stand. The fluffed pillows lay still. The whole guesthouse breathed slow and even, like one big sleepy creature.

She paused at the kitchen doorway, where the three arguing teacups had finally tipped together into one drowsy stack, snoring tiny porcelain snores. On the roof, the two cloud-spotters had given up and curled into a single warm bundle beneath the stars. The umbrella stand had a sleeping cartwheeler draped across it like a scarf.

Douhua wobbled to the round window and looked up at the moon, which looked back at her like an old, kind friend.

She had soothed everyone in the house. Everyone except, perhaps, herself — for she felt a flutter too, small and bright, behind her silky surface. **明天.** Tomorrow. One hundred stories' worth of friends, all together at last.

But Douhua only smiled, and let the flutter settle, the way ginger settles sweet to the bottom of a warm bowl.

She slid back into her own little bed of syrup, pulled the moonlight up like a blanket, and let the quiet hold her.

The calm before the joy. A gift, too.

And outside, the lanterns dimmed one by one, saving their glow for the brightest day the island had ever known.

## Goodnight Blessing
Sweet dreamer, the busiest, happiest days are always nicer after a good, deep rest — so let tonight be soft and slow, just for you. May your worries melt like sugar in warm tea, and may your wobbles turn calm and round. The big, bright tomorrow can wait; it will keep, like honey in a jar. Snuggle down, breathe in, breathe out, and let the quiet hold you the way it held a whole guesthouse full of friends. Goodnight.
