# Story 36: Oyster Omelet & the Tide-Pool Song

**Main Character:** Wō Wō (Oyster Omelet)
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A seaside night market in Tainan by the tidal flats
**Theme:** Being soft is its own kind of strong.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 蚵仔煎 (ô-á-tsian) = oyster omelet

---

On the softest, saltiest edge of Tainan, where the night market spilled right down to the tidal flats, there lived the wobbliest little snack you ever did meet. Her name was Wō Wō, and she was a **蚵仔煎** (ô-á-tsian) = oyster omelet — a glossy, jiggly pancake of egg and sweet-potato-starch, draped in a glistening sweet-pink sauce cape, with frilly crispy edges and three plump pearly oysters peeking out like shy little moons.

The trouble was, Wō Wō could barely talk above a whisper.

"H-hello," she breathed, when the lanterns blinked awake. It came out so quiet that a passing moth said "Bless you" and flew off, certain she had sneezed.

Wō Wō sighed, which for her was the loudest sound she made all evening, and hugged her tiny seashell. "Soft and saucy, that's me," she whispered. "Maybe a little too soft."

---

Now, the seaside market was the noisiest, jolliest place on the whole island. Sizzle-pans crackled. Vendors hollered. Coconut Jelly surfed by on a wave of dishwater shouting "Looking saucy, Wō Wō, bro!" and a cluster of giggling popcorn-chicken nuggets bounced past chanting "More crunch, more fun!" so loudly the lanterns rattled.

In all that wonderful racket, a whisper like Wō Wō's simply vanished. When customers came near, she would try to say "Try one, I'm fresh tonight!" — but it floated out thinner than steam, and the customers, hearing nothing, wandered off to the louder, bolder stalls.

"Don't fret, little one," hummed Ā-Lǐ, the oolong tea leaf who unfurled her misty clouds nearby. "Steep slow, breathe deep. The quiet ones notice the most."

"But nobody notices *me*," Wō Wō whispered, and her sauce cape drooped so low it nearly dragged in the sand.

She watched the bold snacks all evening and tried, very politely, to learn their tricks. She practiced a big booming "**多謝** (to-siā) = thank you so much for coming!" in front of a tide pool, but her reflection only rippled and wobbled, looking just as shy as ever. She tried a confident flip the way the scallion pancakes did, but a soft jiggly omelet does not flip so much as flop, and she landed sauce-side-up in a very dignified, very quiet *splat*.

"It's hopeless," she breathed to her seashell. "A market this loud has no room for a voice this small."

What nobody knew — not even Wō Wō, not quite yet — was that the three little oysters tucked inside her omelet were not just decorations. They were *real, tiny, breathing oysters*, and they had names: Pearl, Plink, and Bubbles. They had been scooped that very morning from the silvery tidal flats just beyond the market, where the mudskippers danced and the moon laid a path of light across the wet sand.

And tonight, the three little oysters were *homesick*.

---

It started small. Around the time the moon climbed high, Pearl gave a tiny hiccup. Then Plink stopped wiggling. Then Bubbles — who usually blew the most delightful saucy bubbles all evening — went still and quiet and would not blow even one.

"Oysters?" whispered Wō Wō, feeling them go cold and shivery inside her. "Pearl? Bubbles? What's wrong, my dears?"

"We miss the *flats*," Pearl said in a voice even tinier than Wō Wō's own. "We miss the cool mud and the wash of the tide. We miss the mudskippers who hop like little commas, and the crabs who tip their hats, and the way the moon lays a silver road right across the wet sand. Out here it's all sizzle and shout, and we... we can't sleep without our lullaby."

"Not even one tiny wink of sleep," sniffled Plink. "We've tried counting bubbles, but it only makes us thirsty for the sea."

And the three little oysters began to tremble so hard that Wō Wō's whole soft body rippled like a pond in the wind.

This, you must understand, was a problem. Because an oyster omelet whose oysters lose heart goes flat and dull and grey. Wō Wō felt her lovely pink sauce growing pale. She felt her crispy frills wilting. She felt herself, bit by bit, going as quiet on the *inside* as she had always been on the outside.

"Oh no, oh no," she whispered. "Hold on, little ones. I'll find someone loud to help. I'll find someone *strong*."

---

She wobbled across the market as fast as a wobbly thing can go, which is not very fast, leaving a trail of sweet pink sauce like a tiny comet's tail.

First she found Pái Pái, the enormous fried chicken cutlet, flexing her crunchy cape for a crowd. "PÁI PÁI!" Wō Wō tried to shout, but it came out *pái pái* like a butterfly landing.

"Bigger than your face, bigger than your worries!" Pái Pái boomed to her fans, not hearing the gentle voice at her feet. A whole gust of cheering swallowed Wō Wō's words whole. She wobbled on, sauce trembling.

Next she found Chòu Chòu, the stinky tofu, who turned with his big puppy eyes. "Did somebody whisper my name? Bold on the outside, gold on the inside — at your service!" But just then a gust of friendly purple aroma-sparkles swirled up, and a crowd swooped in, and tiny Wō Wō was gently swept aside like a leaf in a stream.

She even tried the steady, dependable Luóbo Gāo, the radish cake, who was busy organizing the breakfast-snack roster for dawn. "Crispy edges, soft heart — what can I do for you, dear?" he asked kindly, leaning his square golden self down low. But the moment Wō Wō opened her mouth, a fishmonger nearby clanged a great brass bell to announce the midnight catch, and her whisper drowned beneath it like a pebble in the surf. Luóbo Gāo only saw her lips move, smiled an apologetic smile, and was swept off to settle a squabble between two very loud dumplings.

Wō Wō found a quiet spot behind a coil of fishing rope and looked down at her three trembling oysters, who were now so pale they were nearly see-through.

"I can't make anyone hear me," she whispered, and a sauce-tear rolled down her frilly edge. "I'm too soft. I've always been too soft. If I were loud like Pái Pái, or bold like Chòu Chòu, I could save you. But I'm just... me."

"That's all we want," Bubbles murmured, the smallest oyster of all. "Just you. You smell like home, Wō Wō. You smell like the flats and the tide and warm sand. Could you... could you sing us our song? The one the sea hums? It's the only thing that lets us sleep."

Wō Wō blinked her gentle eyes. "But I don't know the sea's song."

"Yes you do," said Pearl softly. "You were made of it. The egg, the starch, the sauce — and *us*. You've carried the tide inside you all along. Just close your eyes and let it out slow."

---

So Wō Wō closed her eyes.

She thought of the silvery tidal flats. She thought of the cool, kind mud and the wash of saltwater coming in, *shhh*, and going out, *shhh*. She thought of the moon laying its quiet path across the wet sand, and the mudskippers hopping home, and the crabs tucking sideways into their little doorways. She thought of the way everything out there was *soft* — soft mud, soft tide, soft fog — and yet the soft, patient sea had carved the whole coastline, gentle wave by gentle wave, over a thousand thousand nights. No mountain had ever stood against the sea and won. And the sea never once had to shout.

*Soft is not the same as weak,* Wō Wō thought, and something in her gooey heart sat up a little straighter. *Soft is just strong that knows how to be gentle.*

And from somewhere deep in her gooey, glistening heart, a song came up.

It was not loud. It started as the tiniest hum, soft as a bubble rising.

*"Hush now, little oyster, the moon is on the bay,*
*the tide will come and rock you, then carry you away.*
*Soft is how the water sleeps, soft is how it sings —*
*soft is how a quiet thing can do the biggest things."*

The three little oysters stopped trembling.

And here is the marvelous part, the part the whole market would talk about for seasons to come. Wō Wō's song was so *true*, so full of the real and salty sea, that it did something a shout could never do. A shout bounces off the noise. But a song like Wō Wō's slipped *underneath* it — under the sizzle, under the holler, into the cracks and the quiet corners — and once it got in, nobody could stop listening.

The sizzle-pans simmered down. The hollering vendors trailed off mid-holler. Pái Pái lowered her crunchy cape. Chòu Chòu's aroma-sparkles drifted gently to the ground. Even the bouncing popcorn-chicken nuggets settled into a soft golden pile, blinking sleepily. The whole roaring seaside market went *still* — and then, all together, leaned in to hear the wobbly little omelet sing her tide-pool song.

"Hài-iô," sighed the gentle vendors, a soft Taiwanese sound that means something close to *oh, my heart*, and old fishermen at the market's edge wiped their eyes and remembered being small.

Wō Wō sang her whole sea-lullaby, soft verse by soft verse. She sang of the tide that always comes back, and the moon that always returns, and the warm wide flats that would always, always be home no matter how far three little oysters traveled. And as she sang, her sauce blushed pink again, her frills crisped up golden, and the steam-cloud halo around her glowed the gentle silver of moonlight on water.

Pearl, Plink, and Bubbles snuggled deeper into her glistening heart. "We can hear the tide," murmured Bubbles, drowsily. "It's *inside* you, Wō Wō. It was inside you all along." And the three little oysters drifted off into the coziest, most homesick-cured sleep three little oysters ever slept, dreaming of moonlit mud and the *shhh* of the tide.

---

When the last note faded, the whole market was quiet for one whole, glittering heartbeat.

Then it *cheered*. Not a loud, crashing cheer — a warm one, a misty-eyed one, the kind that wraps around you like a blanket fresh from the line. They cheered for the softest snack who'd hushed the loudest market with nothing but a true small song.

Pái Pái lumbered over, her enormous crunchy cape suddenly looking a little bashful. "Kiddo," she said — and for once she said it softly — "I can be loud all night and never reach somebody the way you just did. That was the bravest thing I've heard at this market in a hundred moons." Chòu Chòu wiped a happy tear with the corner of his cabbage cape. Even the popcorn-chicken nuggets, who had never once in their lives finished a sentence quietly, whispered all together, "Encore... please?"

"You didn't need to be loud," said Ā-Lǐ, unfurling a proud little cloud. "You needed to be *you*. Soft, you see, is its own kind of strong."

And from that night on, every customer in the seaside market knew that when the moon climbed high, the wobbly little **蚵仔煎** sang the sea to sleep — and if you were very lucky, and very quiet, and leaned in close, she might just sing the tide-pool song for you, too.

"Soft and saucy, that's me," whispered Wō Wō, hugging her seashell. And for the first time, she said it like it was the best thing in the whole wide world to be.

Because it was.

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, the day was loud, but now the noise is going *shhh*, like the tide slipping out across the soft, silver sand. You don't have to be the biggest or the boldest to matter — your quiet, gentle heart is its own kind of strong, and tonight it can simply rest. Let the sea hum its lullaby just for you: soft is how the water sleeps, soft is how it sings. Close your eyes, and drift out on the gentle moonlit tide. Goodnight.
