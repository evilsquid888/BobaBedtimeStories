# Story 34: Soup Dumpling & the Eighteen Folds

**Main Character:** Xiǎo Lóng (Soup Dumpling)
**Guest Characters:** Boba
**Setting:** A tower-top dumpling restaurant overlooking Taipei 101
**Theme:** Perfection wobbles; friendship steadies.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 小籠包 (xiǎolóngbāo) = soup dumpling

---

High above the city, where the clouds liked to nap and the lights of Taipei twinkled like spilled sugar, there was a restaurant on the very tippy-top floor of a tower. And in that restaurant, on a tiny bamboo steamer that hovered like a tiny hovercraft, sat the most carefully folded dumpling in all of Taiwan.

His name was Xiǎo Lóng. He was a plump, pearly-white **小籠包** (xiǎolóngbāo) = soup dumpling, glistening and jiggly, with a warm puddle of soup sloshing gently inside his round little belly. On top of his head he wore a tiny steamer-lid beret, tilted at exactly the angle he believed a respectable dumpling should tilt it.

And he had eighteen pleats. Eighteen! He counted them every single morning, the way some people count their toes.

"One, two, three..." he whispered, peering down at himself. "...sixteen, seventeen, eight—"

He stopped.

He gasped.

He counted again, slower, with the dread of a dumpling who suspects something terrible.

"...eighteen." Pause. "But number eleven is... crooked."

Outside the window, Taipei 101 winked at him as if to say, *oh dear.*

---

It was the worst possible morning for a crooked pleat. Because tonight — *tonight!* — was the Grand Dumpling Parade.

Every dumpling in the tower had been buzzing about it for a week. There would be lanterns. There would be a marching band of clinking chopsticks. There would be a float shaped like a giant bamboo steamer, rolling slowly past the windows while the whole city looked up to admire the dumplings riding proudly on top.

And Xiǎo Lóng — careful, precise, eighteen-pleated Xiǎo Lóng — had been chosen to ride at the very front.

"This is a disaster," he said, very politely, because he was always polite even mid-disaster. "A crooked pleat means an uneven fold. An uneven fold means a weak seam. A weak seam means..." He shuddered so hard his soup went *gloop.* "...a *spill.*"

You see, a soup dumpling's greatest fear is the spill. All that warm, savory soup, carried so carefully inside — if even one little fold gave way, *sploosh,* there it would go, all over the parade float, in front of everyone.

"Careful," he murmured to himself, the way he always did. "I'm full of feelings... and soup."

He counted his pleats again. One through eighteen. Number eleven was *definitely* leaning a little to the left, like a tower that hadn't read the instructions.

"I cannot ride in a parade like *this,*" he declared. "I am imperfect. I am lopsided. I am a *catastrophe in a beret.*"

And that is exactly when a glossy black-brown sphere came bouncing in through the open window.

---

*Boing. Boing. BOING.*

Boba landed on the steamer cart with a cheerful little wobble, his round body catching the morning light. Of all the friends in all the night markets, Boba was the bounciest — a curious, chewy tapioca pearl who never met a problem he didn't want to bounce around for a while.

"Morning, Xiǎo Lóng!" Boba said. "Big day! Parade day! I bounced all the way up forty-one floors. My record is forty-two but I got distracted by a dumpling shaped like a teapot — long story." He paused and looked closer. "Hey. Why do you look like you swallowed a worried lemon?"

"Because," said Xiǎo Lóng, in the wobbly voice of someone trying very hard not to wobble, "my eleventh pleat is *crooked.*"

Boba squinted. "...Your what is what now?"

"My *pleat.*" Xiǎo Lóng turned slowly to show him. "Look. A proper xiǎolóngbāo has eighteen perfect pleats, each one identical, spiraling up to a neat little point. Mine are perfect. Except for number eleven. Which is..." He could barely say it. "...*slanted.*"

Boba leaned in. He looked at pleat number ten. He looked at pleat number twelve. He looked at pleat number eleven, the slanted one, for a very long time.

"Huh," said Boba.

"You see it," whispered Xiǎo Lóng. "It's *horrible,* isn't it."

"I gotta be honest with you, buddy," said Boba. "I cannot tell which one is number eleven."

"It's the *crooked* one!"

"They all look great to me!"

Xiǎo Lóng nearly fainted into his own soup. "That's because you're not *looking* properly! A trained eye would spot it instantly! Tonight, in front of the *entire city,* every dumpling expert in Taipei will gaze up at the parade float and say, *Oh look, there's the famously imperfect dumpling with the famously crooked eleventh pleat,* and I shall be *ruined,* and probably I'll spill, and—" *gloop* went his soup, "—oh, you see? Even *talking* about it makes me slosh!"

Boba sat down — which for Boba meant flattening into a slightly squishier sphere — and thought about it.

"Okay," he said. "Let's bounce through this together. First question. Does the crooked pleat hurt?"

"No."

"Does it leak?"

Xiǎo Lóng checked himself carefully. "...No. It holds. It's just... not *centered.*"

"Does the soup taste worse out of a crooked dumpling?"

Xiǎo Lóng opened his mouth. Then closed it. Then opened it again. "...That is not the *point.*"

"It's kind of the whole point, though," said Boba gently.

Xiǎo Lóng huffed a tiny, polite huff. "You don't understand. I have spent my *whole life* getting these folds right. When I was just a little ball of dough, the chef who made me pinched and pleated, pinched and pleated, around and around, and she always said the same thing: *eighteen folds, little one — eighteen exactly, no more and no less, or you are not a true xiǎolóngbāo.* I *have* eighteen. But if one of them is *wrong,* then am I really..." His voice went very small. "...really a true one at all?"

Boba stopped bouncing.

He looked at his polite, perfect, worried little friend, and something in his chewy heart squished a bit.

"Buddy," he said softly. "Can I tell you about the day I learned to bounce?"

Xiǎo Lóng sniffled. "...Alright."

"When I was new, I couldn't bounce straight to save my life. I'd push off and go sideways. I'd push off and go *backwards.* Once I bounced clean into a bucket of grass jelly, and Grass Jelly just looked at me, real calm, and said, *'You found me. Now find your way out.'*" Boba chuckled. "Took me an hour. But every wonky bounce taught me something. And you know what I figured out? The pearls who bounced *perfectly* never went anywhere interesting. They just went straight up and straight down, same spot, forever. It was the wobble in my bounce that carried me all over Taiwan. The wobble is what gave me a *story,* Xiǎo Lóng."

The dumpling considered this very carefully, the way he considered everything. "But a *bounce* is meant to go places," he said. "A *pleat* is meant to *stay put.*"

"And yours did stay put," said Boba. "It just stayed put with a little *style.*"

---

Now, the thing about Boba is that he had bounced all over Taiwan, and a pearl that bounces that much sees a *lot.* He had bounced past wonky teapots that poured the best tea. He had bounced past lopsided lanterns that glowed the brightest. He had bounced past a thousand things that weren't perfect and were loved anyway.

"Let me tell you a secret about us boba pearls," he said. "You know how we're supposed to be perfectly round? Well — *psst* — look at me." He turned. There, on his side, was a tiny dimple, a little flat spot where he wasn't quite a perfect circle. "That's my dent. Got it years ago. And you know what? It's how my friends pick me out of a whole cup of pearls. *There's Boba — the one with the dimple.* Hundreds of round pearls, and they find *me,* every time, because of the one part of me that isn't perfect."

Xiǎo Lóng stared at the little dimple. "But... doesn't it bother you?"

"Used to," Boba admitted. "Then I figured it out. Perfect things are easy to forget, because they all look the same. The wobble is the part that's *you.* It's the part people fall in love with."

Outside, the sun was sinking, and the city lights were beginning to wink on, one by one, all the way up Taipei 101. The tall tower glowed softly, blue and silver, like a friendly giant standing guard over the whole twinkling city. Xiǎo Lóng had looked at it a thousand times. But tonight, for the first time, he noticed that even Taipei 101 wasn't a perfectly smooth tower — it had little ledges and steps and bumps all the way up, like a stack of lucky boxes. And it was still the most beloved building in the city. Maybe *because* of the bumps. They made it *Taipei 101* and not just any old tower.

"But I'm supposed to be *precise,*" said Xiǎo Lóng softly. "I'm supposed to be the careful one."

"You *are* the careful one," said Boba. "Eighteen pleats, kiddo! Seventeen of 'em laser-straight! Nobody else in this whole tower can fold like you. One little lean on number eleven doesn't undo all that. It just means a real dumpling made you, with real little dumpling hands, on a real morning when the kitchen was warm and busy and full of love." He bounced once, twice. "That crooked pleat isn't a *mistake.* It's a *signature.*"

Xiǎo Lóng looked down at pleat number eleven.

For the very first time, he didn't see a catastrophe.

He saw... a little curl that leaned to the left, like it was *winking.* Like it had its own opinion. Like it was the one pleat brave enough to do its own thing.

"...A signature," he repeated.

"Now you're slurpin'," said Boba, who had picked up that phrase from a certain noodle-soup bowl and used it slightly wrong, but everyone knew what he meant.

---

That night, the Grand Dumpling Parade was the most beautiful anyone could remember.

The lanterns glowed like little orange moons. The chopstick band went *clink-clink-clink* in perfect rhythm. And the giant steamer float rolled slowly past the tower windows with the whole twinkling city spread out below — and right at the very front, riding tall and proud, was Xiǎo Lóng.

He did not hide his eleventh pleat.

He *showed* it.

He stood up straight on his tiny bamboo steamer, soup steady and snug inside him, his beret at exactly the right angle, and he turned slowly so the crooked pleat caught the lantern light.

And down below, a tiny dumpling pointed up and cried, "Look! Look at *that* one! He's got a little curl that leans! He's *my* favorite!"

And another voice called, "Which one's Xiǎo Lóng?"

"The one with the *winking pleat,*" came the answer. "You can't miss him."

Xiǎo Lóng felt his whole soup-filled belly go warm — and not from the steamer. From the inside. From the good kind of warm.

Beside the float, bouncing along to keep up, Boba grinned. "What'd I tell ya," he called. "They found you. Every single time. Because of the part that wobbles."

"Boba," said Xiǎo Lóng, very precisely and very happily, "I think eleven might be my *favorite* pleat now."

"Atta dumpling," said Boba.

And not once — not on a single bounce of that whole glorious parade — did Xiǎo Lóng spill a single drop. Because it turns out a heart that isn't worried holds its soup just fine.

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, you have your own pleat that leans — a freckle, a giggle, a way of saying things that's only yours — and that is not a flaw, that is your signature. The people who love you will always find you in any crowd, by the very part of you that doesn't quite match the others. So tonight, let your worries unfold, gentle and slow, like steam rising off a warm little dumpling. You are held. You are steady. You are exactly, perfectly, wonderfully you. Goodnight.
