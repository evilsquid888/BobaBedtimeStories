# Story 45: Pepper Bun & the Clay-Oven Jump

**Main Character:** Hú Jiāo (Pepper Bun)
**Guest Characters:** Kǎ Kǎ (Popcorn Chicken)
**Setting:** A famous Raohe Night Market clay-oven stall
**Theme:** Courage is a leap, even when you're scared and warm.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 胡椒餅 (hújiāo bǐng) = pepper bun

---

High on the inside wall of the hottest oven at **饒河夜市** (Ráohé yèshì) = Raohe Night Market, a round little bun was stuck. Not stuck like glued — stuck like scared.

His name was Hú Jiāo, and he was a **胡椒餅** (hújiāo bǐng) = pepper bun: a plump dome of dough with a crackly sesame-freckled crust, rosy warm cheeks, and a juicy peppery pork-and-scallion heart that made him hum when he was happy. Right now he was not humming. He was clinging to the curved clay wall by his toasty bottom, peeking down at the floor far, far below — okay, fine, it was about as far below as a soup spoon is tall, but to a freshly baked bun it looked like the bottom of the sea.

"You can do it!" called the baker's tongs.

Hú Jiāo squeezed his eyes shut. "I most certainly cannot," he whispered. Then, because he was a hot-headed softie who got dramatic when nervous, he sneezed a tiny puff of pepper. "*ACHEW.* Excuse me. Fear makes me spicy."

---

You see, every pepper bun's big proud moment is the Clay-Oven Jump. When a hújiāo bǐng is perfectly baked — crust crackly, sesame golden, belly bubbling — he is supposed to leap off the blazing oven wall and tumble triumphantly into the basket, *fresh from the fire, full of heart!* It's tradition. It's glory. The whole night market cheers.

Hú Jiāo had practiced the cheer. He had practiced the landing pose. He had even practiced the humble little wave for after.

He had simply never practiced the part where you let go of the wall.

"It's so high," he said to no one. "And I'm so... round. Round things roll, and rolling things bonk, and bonked buns leak their pepper soup, and a leaking pepper bun is just a sad puddle with sesame seeds." He shivered, which made another sneeze sneak out. "*ACHEW.*"

From below came a sound like rain on a tin roof — except the rain was giggling.

"Did somebody say *pepper*? We LOVE pepper!"

Hú Jiāo cracked one eye open. Down at the bottom of the stall, bouncing and reshuffling like a fistful of golden popcorn that had learned to laugh, was a whole giggling crowd of nuggets. Each one wore a tiny fried-basil-leaf hat, each one was dusted with salt and pepper, and all of them were talking at once.

It was Kǎ Kǎ — the popcorn chicken crew — who never arrived as one but always as *many*.

"Hello up there!" said one nugget.
"We heard a sneeze!" said another.
"We came to investigate the sneeze!" said a third.
"Sneezes are basically invitations!" they all finished together, and then high-fived themselves, which for popcorn chicken means gently bonking and bouncing apart.

"Oh," said Hú Jiāo politely, the way his grandmother the Sesame Bun had taught him. "Hello. I'm Hú Jiāo. I'm stuck."

"Stuck is just resting with extra commitment!" chirped the nearest nugget.

"More crunch, more fun!" cheered the whole crew, which was, Hú Jiāo was learning, the thing they said about absolutely everything.

"That's very kind," Hú Jiāo said, "but you don't understand. I'm not resting. I'm *trapped* by my own fear. There's a difference, and the difference is that one of them is dignified."

The popcorn chicken crew considered this for a tenth of a second.

"Nope!" said one.
"Still sounds like resting!" said another.
"Want a snack?" offered a third, holding up a crumb of itself, then thinking better of it. "Wait. No. We *are* the snacks. Forget I said that."

Hú Jiāo couldn't help it — he giggled. The giggle jiggled his pepper soup, and a tiny curl of fragrant steam puffed out of his seam. For one whole second he forgot to be afraid.

Then he remembered, looked down, and went stiff as a day-old crust all over again. "Oh no. Oh no no no. I looked at the floor. Why do I keep looking at the floor?"

"Classic mistake," sighed the wisest-sounding nugget, who was wise for approximately one sentence at a time. "Looking at the scary thing makes the scary thing bigger. It's basic snack science."

---

A new voice rumbled in, warm as a winter blanket. Niú Niú the beef noodle bowl steamed over, tossing his long wheat-noodle scarf dramatically over one shoulder. His cozy steam-halo drifted up toward the oven like a friendly little fog.

"What's all this, kiddo?" he asked, peering up at the trembling bun. "Slurp first, worry later. Now — what's the worry?"

"I have to do the Jump," Hú Jiāo said miserably. "Tonight is my baking day. I'm done — see how golden I am? — and a proper pepper bun leaps off the wall when he's done. Everyone leaps. My father leaped. My grandmother leaped. There is a *plaque*." He sniffled. "But the floor is far, and I am full of soup, and I'm so scared my crust is shaking." He sneezed again. "*ACHEW.* And I keep doing that."

"Ah," said Niú Niú, nodding slowly the way he did everything. "Scared and warm at the same time. That's the trickiest kind of scared. The warm part wants to stay cozy on the wall. The scared part is sure the floor is a monster." He smiled, and the steam curled up around his bok-choy collar. "But here's a secret, kiddo. Courage isn't *not* being scared. Courage is the little jump you take *while* you're scared. The two ride along together."

"I can't," whispered Hú Jiāo.

"You don't have to yet," said Niú Niú gently. "First, we make the floor friendly."

"You can do that?" Hú Jiāo asked. "Make a floor friendly?"

"Kiddo," said Niú Niú, with the slow warm smile of someone who has watched a thousand night markets come alive, "you'd be amazed what a little company can do to a scary place. A dark alley with a friend in it is just a quiet street. A tall jump with a soft pile under it is just a hop. The floor doesn't change. *You* do, because you stop being alone with it." He tossed his noodle-scarf for emphasis, and a single noodle flopped right onto his own face. He left it there with great dignity. "Now. Watch this."

---

He turned to the popcorn chicken crew, who were already vibrating with ideas.

"Kǎ Kǎ," said Niú Niú, "how would you fellows feel about being the softest, bounciest, giggliest landing pile in all of Taipei?"

There was a pause exactly one heartbeat long.

Then the entire crew *exploded* with joy.

"A PILE?!"
"We're EXCELLENT at piles!"
"Piles are our second-favorite thing after snacks!"
"Make way, make way, professional cushioning coming through!"

And with a great clattering, bouncing, giggling whoosh, the popcorn chicken nuggets hopped together right beneath Hú Jiāo, stacking and reshuffling and snuggling into one big, golden, fragrant cushion — round nuggets on the bottom, smaller crispy bits filling the gaps, fried basil leaves fluttering on top like a sprinkle of tiny green pillows.

"Behold," announced one nugget from somewhere in the middle of the pile, slightly muffled, "the Crunch Cushion!"

"It even *smells* welcoming," Hú Jiāo admitted. The peppery, salty, basil-y aroma drifted up and mingled with his own. It smelled, he thought, a lot like home.

But the floor still looked awfully far. His crust gave a worried little crackle. "What if I miss the pile? What if I bounce off and roll into the soy-sauce puddle? What if I land upside down and everyone sees my bottom?"

"Then we catch you again!" called a nugget cheerfully. "We're a *pile*, not a one-time offer!"

"And bottoms are funny, not shameful," added another, very solemnly. "We have looked into this extensively. We are all bottom and no shame."

"That," said Hú Jiāo, "is the bravest thing I've ever heard."

"Now then," said Niú Niú. "Whenever you're ready. No rush. The night market isn't going anywhere, and neither are we."

---

Hú Jiāo looked down at the giggling golden pile. He looked at the kind steaming bowl. He took a deep breath, which is a brave thing for a bun to do, and his crust crackled bravely.

"Okay," he said. "I'll try. But I'm still scared."

"Good!" called Niú Niú. "That means it counts."

Hú Jiāo loosened one toasty edge from the wall. The scared part of him squealed. The warm part of him sighed. *Both at once,* he reminded himself. *Riding along together.*

"You've got this!" sang the popcorn chicken.
"Aim for the giggly part!"
"It's all the giggly part!"
"Then you can't miss!"

Hú Jiāo wobbled. He teetered. His belly sloshed with hot pepper soup. And right at the very last brave second, a tiny ticklish bit of fear shot up his crust and —

"*ACHEW!*"

The sneeze did it. The mighty pepper sneeze popped him clean off the wall, and Hú Jiāo went tumbling through the warm oven air, sesame seeds twinkling, crust crackling, cape of steam streaming behind him like a little comet.

"WHEEEEE—" he started.

"*OOMPH!*" went the Crunch Cushion.

He landed in the middle of the popcorn chicken pile with the softest, bounciest, most ridiculous *poomf* you ever heard — like a pillow hugging another pillow. The nuggets caught him completely. Not one bit of pepper soup spilled. Not one sesame seed went astray.

For a moment everyone held their breath.

Then Hú Jiāo started to laugh. A big, warm, soup-jiggling, crust-crackling laugh. "I did it! I jumped! I'm whole! I'm *delicious!*"

The popcorn chicken crew bounced him gently up and down in celebration, all of them giggling so hard their basil hats fell off. "More crunch, more fun! MORE CRUNCH, MORE FUN!"

Niú Niú slurped happily. "See, kiddo? The floor was never a monster. It was just friends, waiting for you to come down and say hello."

"I was scared the whole time," Hú Jiāo confessed, still bouncing gently on the Crunch Cushion. "Even at the very last second. *Especially* at the very last second."

"I know," said Niú Niú warmly. "That's how I know it was real courage and not just showing off. You took your scared along for the ride, and you jumped anyway. That's the kind of brave that sticks to your crust forever." He winked. "Some of us are very good at things sticking to us. Trust me, I'm a soup."

A few of the popcorn chicken crew climbed up onto Hú Jiāo's shoulders to admire the view from the floor, which was, they all agreed, much less terrifying than the view from the wall had looked. From down here the great glowing oven was just a cozy warm cave. The far-off ceiling was just a friendly dark sky. The whole stall smelled of toasted sesame, five-spice, and a hundred happy dinners.

"Funny thing," said Hú Jiāo softly. "From up there it looked like falling. From down here it just looks like... arriving."

---

That night, when a hungry kid pointed and said, "*That* one — the brave crispy one on top," Hú Jiāo didn't feel scared at all. He felt proud, all the way through his peppery heart. Being chosen, being shared, being the very best part of someone's warm and happy night — that was the whole point of being baked. That was the firework finally getting to light the sky.

"Fresh from the fire, full of heart!" he announced, and hopped right into the paper bag like a champion, with the popcorn chicken cheering him on the entire way.

He even gave them the humble little wave he'd practiced.

It was perfect.

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, the bravest jumps are the small ones you take while your heart is still a-flutter — and there is almost always something soft waiting below to catch you. Let your worries hop off the wall and tumble away while friendly, giggling warmth holds you close. You don't have to be unafraid to be brave; you only have to lean, and trust, and let go just a little. Tonight you are toasty and golden and exactly enough, freckled with sesame stars and full of quiet courage. Snuggle down into your own soft pile of blankets, sweet and warm and caught. Goodnight.
