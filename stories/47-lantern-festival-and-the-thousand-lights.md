# Story 47: Tangyuan & the Thousand Sky Lanterns

**Main Character:** Tāng Yuán (Tangyuan)
**Guest Characters:** Starfruit
**Setting:** Pingxi Sky Lantern Festival in the mountains
**Theme:** A wish shared lifts higher than a wish kept.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 天燈 (tiāndēng) = sky lantern

---

Up in the cool mountain town of Pingxi, on the kind of night where the stars look freshly washed, Tāng Yuán came bouncing down the old railway tracks singing a song he had made up exactly four seconds ago.

"We rooooll, we rooooll, the whole gang in a bowl!" he warbled, wobbling his soft white rice-ball body so that his little sesame heart jiggled inside. Tāng Yuán was a perfectly round dumpling, smooth as a soap bubble and just as squishy, and tonight he was so excited his wobble had its own wobble.

Because tonight was the festival of the **天燈** (tiāndēng) = sky lantern! Tonight, all over Pingxi, friends would write their dearest wishes on glowing paper lanterns and send them whooshing up into the dark like slow, polite shooting stars.

"We're going to send up the biggest, brightest lantern the sky has ever seen!" Tāng Yuán announced to nobody, which is the best audience, because nobody never argues.

---

The little station was packed with happy snacks. Egg Waffle was selling glow-sticks two for one and shouting about it through a paper-cone megaphone. Coconut Jelly was teaching a noodle to surf on a puddle. ("Hang ten, bro. Or, like, hang however many toes a noodle has. Which is none. Surf anyway, dude.") Red Bean had set up a little stall ladling warm syrup, fussing that *everyone* must have something hot in their bellies before the cold mountain night set in. Pudding was wobbling between them all, apologizing in advance to anyone he might trip over later.

And there, on the edge of the platform, sitting all by herself with her five points drooping like a tired little star that had stayed up too late, was Starfruit.

Tāng Yuán recognized her at once — that pale yellow star shape, those dreamy eyes that always looked halfway into a wish. But tonight Starfruit didn't look dreamy. She looked nervous. She was holding a brand-new paper lantern, still flat and folded, and turning it over and over in her points.

"Hi-hi!" Tāng Yuán bounced up beside her, landing with a soft, friendly *flump*. "You're Starfruit, right? I'd know those five points anywhere. You always look like you're about to wish on something."

"I am," Starfruit said softly. "I'm always wishing. That's sort of the problem." She held up her flat little lantern, the way you'd hold a baby bird that you weren't quite sure how to hold. "This is my very first **天燈**. I've been saving my best wish my whole life — folding it up smaller and smaller, keeping it tucked away where nothing could bump it. And tonight I'm finally supposed to send it up into the sky for all the stars to read. But I—" Her points curled inward. "I can't make my hands do it. They just won't."

Tāng Yuán plopped down beside her, all warmth and squish. "Hey now. We've got this. And when I say *we*, I mean it — a tangyuan is never just one. We always come as a bowlful. So tonight, you're part of my bowl. Okay?"

Starfruit blinked. A tiny bit of the dreamy came back into her eyes. "Okay," she whispered.

---

"First," said Tāng Yuán, "we have to build the lantern. Watch this — I've done this a *bunch* of times." He had done it zero times. But confidence is its own kind of sticky rice; it holds everything together.

They unfolded the lantern carefully, point by gentle point. It bloomed open into a tall paper balloon, crinkly and orange-warm, taller than Tāng Yuán himself, with four blank paper sides waiting like four blank pages. It smelled of fresh paper and far-off woodsmoke, the way the whole valley did tonight.

"Now we write the wish on the sides," Tāng Yuán said, plucking up a writing brush and a little pot of ink that a passing Sesame Ball had left behind. "That's the whole magic of it. You write your wish where the wind can reach it. So — what's your best wish? The one you saved your whole life?"

Starfruit looked down at her points. "That's just it," she admitted. "I don't actually... have one. I have a *thousand* tiny wishes. I wish for sunny mornings, and ripe fruit, and that the train always comes on time, and that Pudding stops tripping over his own caramel. But I don't have one big, perfect, important wish. Everyone else writes such grand things. I'm afraid mine are too small to lift."

A nearby gua bao was writing *I WISH TO BECOME A WORLD-FAMOUS HUG.* A passing zongzi had written *I WISH TO WIN THE DRAGON BOAT RACE (AGAIN)(LOUDLY).* Shaved Ice had written something in letters so enormous they used up three sides of his lantern and read simply *I WISH TO BE EVEN TALLER.* Even the puddle-surfing noodle had scrawled *I WISH FOR MORE PUDDLE.*

Starfruit's points sagged again. "See? Big wishes. Brave wishes. Mine are just... little leftover wishes."

---

Tāng Yuán scratched the top of his smooth round head, which made a soft *squick* sound. He thought hard. And while he was thinking, a tiny breeze rolled down the mountain and made the lantern bob in his hands, and that's when the idea arrived, the way ideas usually do — sideways, smelling faintly of sesame.

"Starfruit," he said slowly, "can I tell you something my grandma-bean once told my whole bowl? She said: a wish you keep just for yourself is a heavy little stone. But a wish you make *for someone else* — that one's got wings."

Starfruit tilted a point. "What do you mean?"

"I *mean*," said Tāng Yuán, bouncing up so fast his sesame heart did a flip, "let's not put *your* wish on the lantern. Let's put everyone *else's* on it. All those little wishes you've got for other people? The sunny mornings? Pudding not tripping? Let's send up a lantern full of those!"

For a second Starfruit just stared at him. Then, very slowly, she picked up the writing brush.

She wrote: *I wish the train always comes on time for the tired travelers.*

She wrote: *I wish Pudding never trips, and laughs even when he does.*

She wrote: *I wish Red Bean's ladle never runs empty, so everyone stays warm.*

She wrote: *I wish every snack in this valley sleeps warm tonight, and dreams of mornings.*

And the more wishes she wrote — little, soft, leftover wishes, but all of them aimed at someone she loved — the straighter her points stood up, and the brighter her pale yellow glow grew, until she was practically humming light.

"There's no more room!" she laughed. The lantern was covered, top to bottom, in tiny generous wishes, packed as snug as red beans in a steamer.

"Perfect," said Tāng Yuán proudly. "Now we light it."

---

They carried the lantern out together — Starfruit holding one side, Tāng Yuán balancing it with his squishy round arms — out to the wide open field where hundreds of snacks stood waiting, faces turned up, lanterns glowing in their hands like captured fireflies. The mountain air was crisp and sweet, and somewhere down the valley a temple bell rang once, soft and round.

"Hold steady, kiddo," Tāng Yuán murmured, and he lit the little fuel pad at the bottom. *Whoomph* — a soft golden flame caught, and warm air began to fill the paper balloon, swelling it gently from the inside until it stood up tall and glowing and tugged at their hands, eager, alive, ready, the way a balloon tugs when it has somewhere very important to be.

"It's pulling!" Starfruit gasped. "It wants to go!"

"Of course it does," Tāng Yuán said softly. "It's full of wings now, remember? On three. One... two..."

But on *three*, Starfruit froze. Her points trembled. The old fear came rushing back, cold as mountain mist. "What if it's not good enough?" she whispered. "What if a lantern full of *other people's* wishes just... falls?"

Tāng Yuán leaned his round, warm self gently against her side, the way only a tangyuan can — soft and sure and impossible to be sad next to. "Kiddo," he said (he'd been saving that word; it felt right), "a wish shared lifts higher than a wish kept. Trust the bowl. Trust the wings. Now — let *go*."

And together, they let go.

---

The lantern lifted.

Oh, how it lifted! It rose slow and gold and steady, turning gently as it climbed, and every little wish written on its sides glowed warm against the dark. Up it went, past the treetops, past the railway lights, joining a thousand — no, *thousands* — of other sky lanterns, until the whole night above Pingxi shimmered like an upside-down sea full of glowing jellyfish, or a sky that had decided, just for one night, to grow its own gentle stars.

Starfruit watched her lantern climb, and climb, and *keep* climbing — higher than the grand wishes, higher than the loud wishes, higher than any lantern in the valley.

"It's the highest one," she breathed. "Tāng Yuán, mine is the *highest one of all.*"

"Told you," he said, sniffling just a little, because lanterns make everybody's eyes leak. "The light ones always fly best. And nothing's lighter than a wish you made for somebody else."

Starfruit didn't say anything for a long moment. She just watched her thousand little wishes drift up into forever, carrying sunny mornings and on-time trains and warm sleeping snacks up to wherever wishes go. Then she leaned her star-point against Tāng Yuán's squishy shoulder.

"Thank you," she whispered, "for putting me in your bowl."

"Always room for one more," Tāng Yuán said. "That's the whole point of a bowl."

---

Far below, in the glowing field, the snacks began to drift home, sleepy and happy, their faces still tilted upward. Egg Waffle had stopped selling glow-sticks and was just watching, top hat over his golden heart, for once with nothing at all to sell. Coconut Jelly floated on his back in the puddle, murmuring "whoa... so many, dude... so many" at the sky. Red Bean ladled out one last cup of warm syrup, tucked her ladle away, and wiped her eye with a corner of her stripe. Even Pudding stood perfectly still and didn't trip over a single thing — which, for Pudding, was its own quiet miracle.

And high, high above them all, one small lantern full of borrowed wishes glowed on, a tiny golden full-moon of kindness, lighting the way for everyone's dreams to follow.

"Slurp first, worry later," Tāng Yuán hummed, even though there was nothing to slurp, because some catchphrases just belong to the night.

And Starfruit, for the very first time in her whole wishing life, didn't make a single new wish.

She'd already sent up the best one.

## Goodnight Blessing

And may your own dreams float up gentle and gold, lit by all the kindness you've tucked into the dark. Snuggle down now, soft as sticky rice, safe as a bowl that always has room for one more. The lanterns are watching over you. Goodnight.
