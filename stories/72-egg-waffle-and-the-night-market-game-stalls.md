# Story 72: Egg Waffle & the Ring-Toss Champion

**Main Character:** Egg Waffle (雞蛋糕)
**Guest Characters:** Wō Wō (Oyster Omelet)
**Setting:** The classic carnival game-stall row of a night market
**Theme:** Winning is fun; cheering others on is funner.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 遊戲 (yóuxì) = game

---

"LADIES! GENTLEMEN! GLORIOUS GOLDEN SNACKS OF EVERY SHAPE!" boomed Egg Waffle, twirling his top hat so fast it whistled. "Tonight, I, the magnificent **雞蛋糕** (jīdàngāo) = egg waffle, will conquer EVERY game stall on the row! Watch me sweep the night market like a broom made of pure talent!"

Nobody had asked him to do this. That was the wonderful thing about Egg Waffle — he announced things to himself and then applauded.

He stood at the top of the game-stall row, golden and grid-shaped, each puffy little bubble of him gleaming under the strings of red lanterns. He flexed a waffle-bubble like a tiny muscle. Somewhere a balloon popped, and he took a bow as if it had been for him.

"Step right up," he whispered to no one, "to the greatest show in dessert history."

And off he marched, top hat first, into a row of stalls so bright and beepy it looked like a rainbow had sneezed.

---

The first stall was the dart-the-balloons stall, run by a sleepy old radish cake named Luóbo Gāo who organized his darts by size, smallest to biggest, because that was simply correct.

"One **遊戲**, please!" announced Egg Waffle, slapping down a shiny coin. (That's *yóuxì* — game. Egg Waffle said it like it was the name of a beloved pet.)

He threw a dart. He missed everything, including the wall.

"WARM-UP THROW!" he cried. "Doesn't count!"

He threw again. The dart bounced off a balloon, spun in a circle, and popped one entirely by accident. A bell rang.

"YOU SEE?" Egg Waffle roared, holding up the small rubber-duck prize like an Olympic medal. "Skill! Precision! The grid never misses — *eventually*!"

He pinned the duck to his hat band and strutted to the next stall, glowing with the kind of confidence that comes from not noticing you got lucky.

The second stall was the ring-toss, run by an egg waffle's worst nightmare: a stall where you had to be calm. Egg Waffle was many wonderful things, but calm was not on the list. He hurled rings the way a fountain hurls water — everywhere, dramatically, and with great enthusiasm.

"OBSERVE THE ARC!" he announced, flinging a ring. It sailed over the bottles, over the stall, over a passing sweet potato, and landed neatly around the horn of a startled clay tiger named Xiǎo Hǔ Yé who'd been napping three stalls down.

"Rawr means hello!" called the tiger, wearing the ring like a hat. "Nice toss!"

"THAT WAS A GIFT!" Egg Waffle shouted back, because a true showman never admits a miss. "A FREE GIFT FOR MY ADORING PUBLIC!"

The third stall was a whack-the-mochi game, where soft white mochi heads popped up and you bopped them gently with a foam mallet (they giggled every time; it was the politest game on the row). Egg Waffle bopped seventeen mochi in a row, mostly because his waffle-arms were flailing so wildly that bopping happened by sheer accident.

"GRID OF GLORY!" he sang, taking a bow so deep his hat fell off and bopped one final mochi. "Even my HAT scores points!"

By the fourth stall, his hat was a small zoo. A rubber duck. A bouncy ball. A glow-stick bracelet. A tiny plush turnip he didn't remember winning. He clinked when he walked. He looked, frankly, ridiculous, and he had never been happier.

That's when he heard the smallest sound on the whole row. A sigh.

It came from the goldfish-scoop stall.

---

There, at the edge of a wide shallow tank, stood the gentlest, wobbliest snack Egg Waffle had ever seen.

It was Wō Wō, the oyster omelet — a glossy, jiggly little fellow draped in a sweet-pink sauce cape, with plump pearly oysters peeking out and frilly sweet-potato-starch edges that trembled when he was nervous. And right now, he was *very* nervous. In one careful hand he held a tiny paper scoop. The scoop already had a hole soaking through the middle.

"Soft and saucy, that's me," Wō Wō murmured to himself, the way you whisper your own name to feel braver. He dipped the paper scoop toward the water, where dozens of cheerful goldfish flicked and darted like little orange commas.

The paper touched the water. It went soggy. The goldfish swam right through it. The scoop tore.

Wō Wō's edges drooped.

"That's... the eleventh one," said the goldfish-scoop lady, a kindly red bean grandma with a ladle she clearly wished she could lend out. "You sure you don't want to try the ring toss, dear? Easier on the heart."

"I want a goldfish," Wō Wō said very softly, hugging his tiny seashell. "I've wanted one since I was a little egg. I just... I always go too fast, or too slow, and the paper always—" His voice did the gulpy thing voices do. "I've never won. Not once. Not a single **遊戲** in my whole life."

Egg Waffle, eavesdropping from behind a stack of plush turnips, felt something strange happen inside his grid.

His prizes suddenly felt heavy.

---

"GREETINGS!" Egg Waffle burst out, because he did not have a quiet setting. Wō Wō jumped a foot in the air and landed with a *splat-wobble*.

"S-sorry," Wō Wō squeaked. "I'm — I'm in your way. You probably want to win this one too. You've won everything."

Egg Waffle opened his mouth to say *Yes! Yes I have! Step aside, jiggly friend, and witness greatness!*

But the words wouldn't come out.

Because Wō Wō was looking at the goldfish the way Egg Waffle looked at applause. Like it was the whole entire world. And it suddenly seemed like a very silly thing, to have a hat full of prizes you didn't even care about, when someone right next to you wanted *one* thing so badly his sauce-cape was shaking.

"...No," said Egg Waffle slowly, surprising himself. "No, I don't want to win this one."

"You don't?"

"I want *you* to win it." He puffed up his chest, but gentler this time, like a balloon filling halfway. "And the great Egg Waffle is going to coach you. Stick with me, kiddo — wait, that's Niú Niú's word. Stick with me, *little oyster*. We're going to get you a goldfish if it takes all night."

Wō Wō's pearly eyes went huge. "Really? But — what about your sweep? Every stall in one night?"

Egg Waffle waved a flappy waffle-hand. "Eh. I conquered four. Four's a sweep if you say it loudly enough."

---

Here is the thing about Egg Waffle that nobody expected, possibly including Egg Waffle: under all the showing-off, he was actually a *fantastic* coach.

"First," he declared, peering at the soggy paper scoops, "we study the enemy."

"The goldfish?" whispered Wō Wō.

"The PAPER." Egg Waffle held a scoop to the lantern light dramatically. "See how it weakens in the water? You've been pushing *down*, like you're stamping a letter. But the paper hates that. You have to be soft. You have to be — " he searched for the word — "*saucy*."

Wō Wō blinked. "Saucy?"

"It's your whole thing! Slide in sideways, smooth and gentle, like sauce sliding off an omelet. Let the fish *come to you*. Don't chase. Welcome."

Wō Wō, who knew an awful lot about being soft and saucy, suddenly looked like a snack who'd been handed his own native language.

They practiced on a dry napkin. Slide, lift, slide, lift. The kindly red bean grandma leaned on her ladle and pretended she wasn't grinning.

The first real try, Wō Wō went too fast, and the goldfish scooted away with an offended little flick. Egg Waffle did not boom. He did not roar. He simply leaned in and said, very kindly, "That wasn't a fail. That was *research*."

The second try, Wō Wō's edges shook so hard he nearly folded himself in half. "I can't," he whispered. "Everyone's watching. What if I lose again in front of all of them?"

Egg Waffle crouched down to oyster-level, and for the first time all night, his voice was as soft as steam. "Listen, kiddo — little oyster. I've spent my whole life loving the crowd. But here's a secret a showman knows: the crowd isn't here to watch you fall. They're here, the whole lot of them, *hoping* you'll fly. Every single one of them wants you to win. So do it for them. Give them a reason to cheer."

Wō Wō looked at the crowd. The popcorn-chicken nuggets gave him a thumbs-up with about nine thumbs. The soup dumpling whispered, "Careful — you've got this," counting his own pleats for luck.

"Now breathe," said Egg Waffle, lowering his big carnival voice to almost a hush. "In through the oysters. Out through the cape."

"That's not how breathing works."

"It is TONIGHT."

---

Wō Wō dipped the scoop.

He didn't push. He slid it in sideways, smooth and slow, the way warm sauce settles over rice. A round little goldfish drifted near, curious, flicking its tail. Wō Wō's edges trembled — and then went still.

"Welcome," Wō Wō breathed.

He tilted. He lifted. The water ran off the paper in a soft silver sheet —

— and there, wriggling and gleaming and very surprised, sat one small orange goldfish, safe in the scoop.

For one whole second, nobody moved.

Then Egg Waffle EXPLODED.

"WINNNNNNERRRRR!" he bellowed, leaping onto the stall counter, both waffle-arms in the air, top hat flying off and raining rubber ducks everywhere. "LADIES AND GENTLEMEN, WITNESS THE CHAMPION OF THE NIGHT MARKET! THE GOLDFISH WHISPERER! THE SAUCIEST SCOOPER WHO EVER SCOOPED! WŌ WŌ THE MAGNIFICENT!"

A crowd had gathered without anyone noticing — popcorn-chicken nuggets, a passing soup dumpling, two youtiao who clapped in perfect unison. They cheered. They actually *cheered*. For Wō Wō.

And Wō Wō — shy, wobbly, never-won-anything Wō Wō — stood there holding his very first goldfish in a little water-filled bag, and started to cry the happiest, gooiest tears in the history of omelets.

"I won," he whispered. "I won a **遊戲**."

"You won THE **遊戲**," Egg Waffle corrected, hopping down. "*The* important one. The only one that mattered all night."

---

Later, they sat on the curb at the quiet end of the row, the noise softening behind them, the goldfish circling its bag in slow lazy loops. Egg Waffle had given his entire hat-zoo of prizes away to passing kids, one by one, and somehow felt *more* full, not less.

"I thought winning everything would feel amazing," he admitted, hat empty, voice unusually small. "But this feels better. Watching you win. Is that weird?"

Wō Wō held up his goldfish so it caught the lantern glow. "It's the least weird thing I ever heard," he said softly. "Soft and saucy, that's me. And... cheering, that's you."

Egg Waffle thought about that.

Then he stood up, threw his arms wide, and announced to the whole sleepy market: "ATTENTION, EVERYONE! I, THE GREAT EGG WAFFLE, HAVE DISCOVERED THE GREATEST GAME OF ALL!"

"What is it?" called a tired tea egg from the cooker across the way.

Egg Waffle grinned, golden and warm under the lanterns.

"Helping somebody else light up like the moon," he said. "Best **遊戲** there is."

And he meant it so loudly that the goldfish did a happy little spin.

## Goodnight Blessing
May you win the small sweet things you wish for, slow and gentle, the way warm sauce settles over rice. And may you find, like Egg Waffle did, that the loudest cheer in your heart is the one you give to someone else. Tonight, be the rubber-duck winner and the duck-giver both. Hold your one good goldfish close, and let the lanterns dim soft and golden around you. Sleep now, little champion — the night market hums you a lullaby. Goodnight.
