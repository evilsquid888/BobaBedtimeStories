# Story 61: Sweet Potato & the East Coast Cliffs

**Main Character:** Sweet Potato (地瓜)
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** The dramatic cliffs and blue Pacific of Taroko and the east coast
**Theme:** Humble roots can climb the highest cliffs.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 東部 (dōngbù) = the east

---

Sweet Potato hopped off the train with a *clunk-clunk-clunk* of his tiny yellow hardhat, took one look at the wild east coast, and said, "Whoa. Somebody turned the volume up on Taiwan."

He wasn't wrong. The cliffs went *up*. The ocean went *way* down. And in between, the marble gorge of Taroko twisted like a dragon doing yoga. Sweet Potato was a humble fellow — rough brown skin, golden inside, street-smart from a hundred night markets — and he had come all the way to the **東部** (dōngbù) = the east just to see what all the fuss was about.

"Pretty," he decided, tipping his hardhat. "Loud, but pretty."

Then he heard a small voice go, "Ohhh no, ohhh no, ohhh dear." A wobbly little Pudding was clinging to the trailhead sign, jiggling so hard his caramel top sloshed like a tiny brown wave.

"Easy there, wobble-buddy," said Sweet Potato. "You're gonna shake yourself into a milkshake."

---

The Pudding's name was Bù-Bù, and Bù-Bù was a klutz with a capital *KLUTZ*. He had traveled to the cliffs to mail a postcard to his grandma that simply said *I climbed a mountain!* The only problem, he explained between jiggles, was that he had not, in fact, climbed a mountain. He hadn't climbed so much as a curb. He was terrified of heights, terrified of slipping, and *especially* terrified of the gorge trail, which clung to the marble cliffs like a shoelace on a giant's boot.

"I can't do it," Bù-Bù whispered. "Everyone else is so... shiny. Look at them."

Sweet Potato looked. Down the platform, a crew of glossy travelers were posing for photos: Lychee in pearl-white perfection ("*Darling*, get my good angle"), Mochi striking flexing poses, and a Mango bouncing on his sporty little feet doing warm-up squats. They glittered. They sparkled. They looked like they belonged on a postcard *and* in a museum.

And then there was Sweet Potato. Brown. Lumpy. A little dusty. With a hardhat one size too small.

"Buddy," said Sweet Potato, leaning close, "let me tell you a secret about shiny. Shiny is just light bouncing off the *outside*." He thumped his rough little chest. "Me? I'm golden on the inside. You can't see it from here. You gotta get close. You gotta crack me open." He grinned. "And the cliffs don't care if you sparkle, kiddo. The cliffs only care if you *climb*."

Bù-Bù blinked. "But what if I fall?"

"Then you'll be the bounciest faller on the whole mountain," said Sweet Potato. "Now come on. We're doing this together."

---

The trail into the gorge was called the **太魯閣** (Tài-lǔ-gé) = Taroko gorge path, and it was carved straight through marble — swirly white-and-gray stone that looked like someone had stirred cream into rock. Above them the cliffs leaned in close, almost touching, leaving just a ribbon of sky. The river roared turquoise far below.

"OKAY," squeaked Bù-Bù, "THIS IS VERY TALL."

"It's *grand*," Sweet Potato corrected. "There's a difference. Tall just stands there. Grand says *hello*." He pressed a flappy little hand to the cool marble. "Touch it. Go on."

Bù-Bù touched it. The stone was smooth and ancient and somehow... calm. His jiggling slowed to a tiny shimmy.

"See?" said Sweet Potato. "The mountain's been here longer than night markets. Longer than postcards. Longer than grandmas, even. It's not in a hurry, so neither are we. One step. Then one more. That's the whole trick. That's the *only* trick."

They went. One step. Then one more.

When the path got narrow, Sweet Potato went first, planting his stubby feet and saying, "Solid as a root." When water dripped off the cliff overhead, Bù-Bù shrieked and Sweet Potato held out a leaf like an umbrella and announced, "Tableside service, very fancy." When a butterfly the size of a dinner plate flapped past, even Bù-Bù forgot to be scared for a whole three seconds.

"You're doing it," Sweet Potato pointed out. "You've been climbing this whole time and you didn't even notice. That's the sneaky thing about brave. It doesn't show up like a parade. It shows up like a *step*."

---

They were nearly to the famous Swallow Grotto — a stretch where hundreds of little birds nest in pockets of the marble cliff — when disaster did its disaster thing.

A loose stone slid under Bù-Bù's wobbly bottom. He gasped. He tipped. He went sliding toward the edge of the trail with a *wheeeee-NO-NO-NO!*

Sweet Potato did not think. Sweet Potato *moved*. He flung himself flat, jammed his sturdy little body against a marble knob, and grabbed Bù-Bù's smooth caramel hand. For one heart-thumping second the pudding dangled, jiggling like jelly in an earthquake.

"I'VE GOT YOU," Sweet Potato grunted, his hardhat sliding over one eye. "I have *literally* got you, now stop wiggling, you slippery dessert!"

He dug in his heels. His rough brown skin scraped the stone — but underneath, where it mattered, he was solid as the mountain itself. With one mighty heave, he hauled Bù-Bù back onto the trail, and they both flopped down in a heap, breathing hard.

"You," panted Bù-Bù, "are lumpy. And dusty. And you have a silly hat."

"Yep," said Sweet Potato.

"And you just saved my whole pudding life."

"Also yep." Sweet Potato pushed his hardhat back up and grinned. "Shiny couldn't've caught you, kiddo. Shiny would've been worried about getting scratched. *Rough* doesn't mind a scrape. Rough just holds on."

Bù-Bù looked at him for a long moment. Then, very quietly, he said, "I want to be golden on the inside too."

"You already are," said Sweet Potato. "You just climbed half a marble mountain after a tumble like that. You think a not-golden pudding could do that? No way. That's solid-gold custard in there. Trust me — I know gold when I see it."

---

The last stretch went easier, the way last stretches do when you've decided to be brave. The cliffs opened up. The sky widened. And then — all at once — there was the **太平洋** (Tài-píng-yáng) = the Pacific Ocean, spread out blue and enormous and shimmering all the way to the curve of the world.

Bù-Bù stood at the lookout, his caramel top glowing in the late sun, and he did not jiggle. Not even a little.

"I climbed a mountain," he whispered.

"You climbed *the* mountain," said Sweet Potato. "Now write that grandma her postcard."

Bù-Bù pulled out his soggy little postcard, scratched out *I climbed a mountain!*, and wrote underneath in wobbly letters: *I climbed a mountain — with my friend. He's lumpy but he's the best.*

Then he paused. "What should I send her? A picture of the view?"

Sweet Potato thought about it. "Nah. Send her a sweet potato. There's a fella down at the night market who roasts 'em right." He winked. "Best thing in the **東部**, and you can't tell by looking. You gotta crack it open. Just like everybody worth knowing."

Far below, the Pacific kept rolling in, patient and blue. The swallows swooped home to their marble pockets. And a lumpy little hero in a too-small hardhat sat beside a brave pudding at the top of the world, both of them golden — one on the outside, one on the in, and the very best ones, somewhere in between.

"Hey, Sweet Potato?" said Bù-Bù sleepily, as the sun dipped low.

"Yeah, kiddo?"

"Thanks for not being shiny."

Sweet Potato laughed, a warm crumbly laugh, and tipped his hardhat over his eyes. "Anytime," he said. "Anytime at all."
