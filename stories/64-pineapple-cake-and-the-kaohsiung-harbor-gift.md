# Story 64: Pineapple Cake & the Harbor Gift

**Main Character:** Pineapple Cake (鳳梨酥)
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** The colorful harbor and ferry docks of Kaohsiung
**Theme:** The best gift is the one wrapped with thought.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 禮物 (lǐwù) = gift

---

Pineapple Cake stood at the edge of the Kaohsiung harbor with seventeen ribbons fanned out in front of him, frowning at every single one.

"Too shiny," he announced to the gold ribbon. "Too plain," he told the white one. "And YOU," he said sternly to a polka-dot ribbon, "are far too cheerful for a serious occasion."

He was a proud golden rectangle, neat as a freshly pressed envelope, with crisp pastry corners he kept very, very square. Today those corners were trembling — not from the sea breeze, but from the heaviest responsibility of his life. His dear friend Coconut Jelly was sailing away at sunset on the big blue ferry, off to surf the southern islands for a whole year.

And Pineapple Cake intended to send him off with the most magnificent **禮物** (lǐwù) = gift the harbor had ever seen.

"It must be perfect," he murmured. "Absolutely, completely, ribbon-tied PERFECT."

A seagull landed beside him, eyed the seventeen ribbons, and squawked something that sounded a lot like *good luck with that.*

---

The harbor of Kaohsiung was the most colorful place you could imagine. Ferries the color of mango and sky bobbed at the docks. Container cranes stretched like enormous friendly giraffes against the clouds. Fishing boats wore strings of flapping flags, and the water sparkled so brightly it looked like someone had spilled a whole jar of sequins into the sea.

Pineapple Cake had taken the long road trip all the way down the island just for this — rattling south on the train past green rice paddies and silver rivers, past temples and mango groves, clutching his empty gift box the entire way and refusing to let the conductor so much as glance at it. He carried that box now tucked under one crisp arm, guarding it like treasure, even though there was, as yet, no treasure inside.

"What's inside?" asked a passing **小販** (xiǎofàn) = street vendor, a cheerful mango stand on wheels.

Pineapple Cake puffed his pastry chest. "That, my friend, is the problem. The box is empty. I cannot decide what is grand enough to put inside."

"Empty?" The vendor blinked. "The boat leaves at sunset, you know."

Pineapple Cake checked the great harbor clock. It was already afternoon. The sun had begun its slow slide toward the water, painting the sky in syrupy orange.

"Plenty of time," he said, with the confidence of someone who absolutely did not have plenty of time. "A gift this important cannot be rushed."

He marched along the docks, inspecting every shop, growing fussier with each step.

At the souvenir stall, he found a glass dolphin leaping on a little wooden wave. "Too breakable," he tutted. "Coconut Jelly is a surfer — he'd shatter it on day one and feel dreadful about it. No, no."

At the tea shop, he found a beautiful tin of oolong with golden mountains painted on the side. "Too ordinary," he sniffed. "Anyone can buy tea. A gift should be *special.*"

At the lantern stand, he found a paper lantern shaped like a plump red fish. He held it up, considered it from every angle, and shook his head. "Too floppy. It has no *dignity.* A gift must have dignity."

The lantern-stall owner, a tired-looking clam, muttered, "And yet, here you are with an empty box."

Pineapple Cake pretended not to hear.

By now the seagull was following him from stall to stall, hopping along the dock railing, clearly enjoying the show more than any seagull has ever enjoyed anything.

Pineapple Cake stopped, set down his box, and sighed a long, crumbly sigh. "A proper gift," he explained to the seagull, who was not listening, "must look impressive. It must SHINE. It must make everyone gasp. *Ooh,* they will say, *what a gift!*"

The seagull yawned.

---

Determined, Pineapple Cake threw himself into a frenzy of wrapping.

He bought the most expensive paper — silver, with tiny embossed pineapples (a touch he was very pleased with). He bought ribbon the color of the sunset sea. He bought a bow so enormous it was nearly bigger than the box itself.

The trouble was, he still hadn't decided what went *inside.*

So he put in the glass dolphin after all. Then took it out. Then put in the tin of tea. Then took it out. Then added the paper fish lantern, frowned, and removed it too.

"Careful, careful," he muttered, folding the silver paper into perfect creases. "A wrinkle here would be a catastrophe."

He measured the ribbon with a piece of string. He tied the bow, untied it, and tied it again because the loops weren't *symmetrical.* He measured it a third time. He tied a *fourth* bow because the third one had leaned ever so slightly to the left, which simply would not do. He polished the silver paper with the edge of his apron until it gleamed so brightly that the seagull squinted and the squid had to look away.

"Perfect," Pineapple Cake breathed, holding the box up to the golden light. "Almost. Nearly. Just one more bow, I think."

A friendly old **燈塔** (dēngtǎ) = lighthouse keeper — actually a stout little squid in a raincoat — watched from a bench.

"That's a fine-looking parcel," the squid said. "What did you put in it?"

Pineapple Cake paused, ribbon halfway tied. "I... well... I'm still deciding."

"Ah," said the squid, puffing a tiny bubble. "You've spent two hours wrapping a box of air."

"It is not *air,*" said Pineapple Cake, deeply offended. "It is *potential.*"

The squid chuckled and went back to watching the boats.

---

The sun dropped lower. The sky turned the color of brown-sugar syrup. Out on the water, the big blue ferry gave a long, low *HOOOONK* — the half-hour warning.

Pineapple Cake's perfect corners went stiff with panic.

"Half an hour!" he gasped. "And my box is — is —" He looked inside. It was empty. Beautifully, magnificently, expensively wrapped on the outside. And utterly empty within.

He scrambled. He shoved in the dolphin, the tea, AND the lantern, all at once, slamming the lid. The lid would not close. He pushed harder. The gorgeous silver paper *tore* straight down the middle with a sound like a tiny scream.

"NO!" Pineapple Cake wailed. He sat down hard on the dock, crumbs scattering, his enormous bow drooping over one eye like a sad sea creature.

The seagull, the squid, and the mango vendor all gathered round, looking very sorry for him.

"All that work," Pineapple Cake whispered, "and I have nothing. No gift. No time. Coconut Jelly is leaving for a whole year, and I'll never even say a proper goodbye."

It was the lighthouse-keeper squid who spoke gently. "Lad," he said, "in all your fussing — did you ever once think about *Coconut Jelly?* About what he'd actually like? About what you wanted to *tell* him?"

Pineapple Cake went very still.

He had thought about silver paper. He had thought about symmetrical bows. He had thought about gasps and *ooh*s and shining.

But he had not thought, even once, about his friend.

---

The ferry honked again. Fifteen minutes.

Pineapple Cake looked at the torn box, the tangled ribbons, the seventeen rejected colors. Then he did something a proud, formal, perfectly-square pineapple cake almost never does.

He gave up on perfect.

He tossed aside the silver paper. He scooped up a plain brown paper bag from the mango vendor — humble, soft, smelling faintly of fruit. And then, from inside his own pastry self, he pulled out the only thing he had truly carried all the way down the island: a small, flaky, golden piece of himself. A real **鳳梨酥** (fènglí sū) = pineapple cake — sweet, sunny, and made with love.

He set it gently in the brown bag.

Then he borrowed a pen from the squid and, on the side of the bag, he wrote — quickly, messily, with his heart instead of a ruler:

*Dear Coconut Jelly. When the southern sun feels too far from home, take one bite of this. It tastes like Taiwan, and it tastes like your friend who will be waiting right here. Surf brave. Come back soon. The island misses you already.*

No ribbon. No silver. No symmetrical bow. Just a brown bag, a sweet little cake, and the truest words he'd ever written.

He ran.

---

Pineapple Cake reached the dock just as the gangplank began to lift. There, leaning over the ferry rail in his tiny sunglasses, was Coconut Jelly — translucent, chill, and grinning his easygoing grin.

"BRO!" Coconut Jelly called. "You made it! I thought you got lost in a ribbon shop or something."

"I nearly did," Pineapple Cake panted. He hurled the brown bag up, and Coconut Jelly caught it with a casual flick.

"For me?" Coconut Jelly opened it, read the words on the side, and went quiet. His sunglasses fogged up just a little. "Dude," he said softly. "This is... this is the best thing anyone's ever given me."

"It's only a brown bag," said Pineapple Cake, suddenly shy. "I ran out of time for the wrapping. There's no bow. No silver. It isn't grand at all."

"Are you kidding?" Coconut Jelly held up the bag like a trophy. "You put *you* in it. You wrote me a note that made my whole heart wobble. That's the realest **禮物** I've ever gotten." He grinned. "Trust me, the weird humble ones are always the best ones."

The ferry's engines rumbled. The water churned mango-gold.

"Come back soon!" Pineapple Cake shouted, waving with both crisp arms.

"Always!" Coconut Jelly called back, already taking a happy bite of the little cake. "Mmm — tastes like home, bro!"

And the big blue ferry slid out across the glittering harbor, into a sunset so warm and orange it looked like the whole sea had turned to syrup.

---

Pineapple Cake stood on the dock long after the boat became a speck. The seagull settled beside him. The squid puffed a contented bubble. The mango vendor offered him a slice on the house.

"You know," said the squid, "you wrapped a hundred boxes today. But the only one that mattered was the one with your heart inside."

Pineapple Cake smiled — a soft, slightly crumbly, perfectly *imperfect* smile.

He looked down at his pile of seventeen ribbons and silver paper, then gathered them up and handed them, one by one, to everyone on the dock. A ribbon for the squid's hat. Silver paper to wrap the vendor's mangoes. A bow for the seagull, who wore it with great dignity.

The grandest gift, he finally understood, was never the one that shone the brightest. It was the one wrapped with *thought* — and with a little flaky piece of your own warm self.

And as the harbor lights blinked on across the water, gold and gentle, Pineapple Cake began his long road trip home, lighter than he'd been all day.

## Goodnight Blessing
May your gifts always carry a little piece of your heart, dear one, tucked sweet inside like a cake in a brown paper bag. May you fuss less over how things look and treasure more how things *feel.* When someone you love sails far away, may you trust that the truest words travel farther than any ribbon ever could — all the way across the glittering, syrup-gold sea. Rest now, soft as a sunset on a quiet harbor. You are loved, just as you are, no wrapping required. Goodnight.
