# Story 88: Peanut Brittle & the Stuck-Together Day

**Main Character:** Huāshēng Táng (Peanut Brittle) — a crunchy, golden, glued-together slab of candied peanuts, loud and busy
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A sunny candy-maker's workshop on a humid summer afternoon
**Theme:** Slowing down lets you notice who's right beside you
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 慢慢來 (mànman lái) = take it slow / no rush

---

Huāshēng Táng was the fastest thing in the whole candy-maker's workshop, and he wanted everybody to know it.

"Out of the way, out of the way, COMING THROUGH!" he hollered, skidding across the wooden table on his crunchy golden belly. He was a slab of candied peanuts, you see — a glittering, glued-together raft of toasty brown peanut halves all baked into one sheet of shiny amber sugar. When the afternoon sun hit him, he sparkled like a treasure chest that had learned to shout.

"Done with the flour shelf!" he announced, zooming past. "Done with the sprinkle jars! Done, done, DONE!" He left a tiny trail of sugar dust behind him like the tail of a comet in a very big hurry.

The sesame cookies watched him zip by and sighed. The little soft mochi yawned. And one shy, quiet peanut — stuck right at the corner of Huāshēng Táng's own crunchy edge — opened her mouth to say hello.

But by then he was already gone.

(He was, between you and me, the only candy in the whole workshop who could leave a *room* while still sitting in it.)

---

Huāshēng Táng had a system, and his system was simple: do everything as fast as possible, then do it faster than that.

"Slow is for puddings!" he liked to say, which made the puddings very cross. "I have things to do! Places to skid! Shelves to organize at the speed of light!"

He had a chore list as long as a dragon's tail, and he was *proud* of it. Each morning he rolled out of his shelf the instant the sun cracked the window, and he didn't stop rolling until the moon came up. Tidy the almond bins! Stack the sesame cookies! Polish the sugar jars until you could see your reflection — then polish your reflection too, just to be safe! Huāshēng Táng believed, deep in his crunchy heart, that a thing not done quickly was a thing not done at all.

"If I slowed down," he'd say, shuddering, "why, the whole *world* might catch up with me. And then where would I be? Standing in a line, that's where. Waiting. *Ugh.*"

The trouble was, that humid summer afternoon, the air in the workshop had gone thick and warm and sticky as a yawn. You could practically swim through it. The candy-maker had propped the door open, but the breeze just sort of flopped inside, fanned itself once, and gave up.

And in all that gooey heat, something began to happen.

Huāshēng Táng tried to zoom toward the cinnamon jars — and stuck. His golden bottom went *gloop* against the warm tabletop. He pulled. He grunted. He yanked one peanut corner up with a sound like *thwuck*.

"What — is — HAPPENING," he puffed.

"It's the wet heat," murmured the candy-maker, wiping her brow. She set a fresh tray of bubbling caramel beside him to cool. "**潮濕** (cháoshī) = humid," she explained to nobody in particular, the way busy grown-ups do. "Everything softens up. The candy won't set fast today. We'll just have to wait."

"WAIT?" Huāshēng Táng nearly fell off his peanuts. "I don't *wait*. I'm the fastest brittle in the —"

*Gloop.* He stuck again. This time, good and proper. He pulled with all his crunchy might and barely wiggled.

"Oh no," he whispered. "Oh no no no."

---

For the first time in his entire shiny life, Huāshēng Táng had absolutely nothing to do and nowhere to go.

He fidgeted. He counted the ceiling beams. He counted them again. He tried to skid one inch to the left and made a sad little *squik* noise that fooled nobody. He hummed every song he knew, including three he made up on the spot, and one of those was just the word "hurry" sixty-two times.

He tried to organize the sugar jars *with his mind*, which did not work and only made him dizzy. He tried holding very still and pretending he was already finished with everything, which lasted about four seconds. He even tried bossing a passing ant, but the ant — wise creature — simply walked around him.

"This is the worst," he groaned, flopping flat. "How does anybody *stand* it? Sitting still! Doing nothing! Just — waiting!"

"It's not so bad," said a small voice. "Once you get used to it."

Huāshēng Táng froze. (He was already frozen, technically, being stuck, but now he froze on purpose.)

"Who said that?"

"I did," said the voice. "Down here. To your left. On your edge, actually. I've, um. I've kind of always been here."

He swiveled his whole crunchy self as far as he could — which was about a quarter of an inch — and there, tucked into the very corner of his golden slab, was a little peanut. A round, freckly, cinnamon-brown peanut with a soft worried smile and shy little eyes, baked right into the same sheet of sugar he was.

"You're — wait." Huāshēng Táng squinted. "You're attached to me?"

"Since the day the candy-maker poured the syrup," the little peanut said. "We set together. You've been my neighbor this whole time." She wiggled bashfully. "I'm Xiǎo Dòu. It means *little bean*. I tried to say hi about a hundred times, but you were always —" she made a tiny whooshing gesture — "*zoom*."

Huāshēng Táng's golden cheeks went a little pink, which on peanut brittle looks like extra-warm caramel. He had skidded right past this peanut a thousand times. Ten thousand. And he had never once stopped long enough to hear her.

"I... didn't know you were there," he admitted. "I'm sorry. I'm always in such a rush."

"That's okay," said Xiǎo Dòu kindly. "**慢慢來** (mànman lái) = take it slow, no rush. My grandmother peanut used to say it. She said the best things in life don't come fast — they come *close*."

---

And so, with the whole workshop too warm and gooey to move, Huāshēng Táng did something he had never done before.

He stayed.

"So," he said, a little awkwardly. "Um. Tell me about you, Xiǎo Dòu. What do you... like?"

And the little peanut lit up like a lantern.

She told him about the freckle on her left side that was shaped, she was *certain*, like a tiny boba pearl. She told him she could feel the morning sun before anyone else, because she sat closest to the window. She told him about the secret game she played counting the candy-maker's footsteps — *eleven from the stove to the door, fourteen back* — and how the number changed when the candy-maker was sleepy.

She told him she'd been quietly inventing names for every cloud that drifted past the workshop glass — *that one's Dumpling, that fat one's Grandpa Bao, that wispy one is Noodle* — and she'd always, always wished she had someone to share them with.

"You named the *clouds*?" Huāshēng Táng gasped. "That is the silliest, most wonderful thing I have ever heard. Show me. Show me right now. Which one's Noodle?"

"That long stretchy one," Xiǎo Dòu said proudly, pointing with a wiggle. "And see the puffy little one chasing it? That's Wonton. Wonton always follows Noodle. They're a set."

"Of course they are," said Huāshēng Táng, utterly delighted. "Noodle and Wonton. *Obviously.*" And he laughed — not his big show-off honking laugh, the one he used for an audience, but a soft real one he didn't even know he had. It surprised him so much he laughed again.

So they watched the clouds together. And Huāshēng Táng — who had spent his entire life going *zoom* — discovered something astonishing.

When you slow down, the world gets *bigger*.

He noticed the way the afternoon light turned the caramel into liquid honey. He noticed the cozy *plip... plip* of cooling syrup. He noticed that the workshop smelled like toasted sugar and warm rain and home. He noticed a fat bumblebee bumbling along the windowsill, in no hurry at all, and for once he didn't think *hurry up, bee* — he just watched it, and it was lovely.

And mostly, *mostly*, he noticed Xiǎo Dòu — who had a laugh like a tiny hiccup, and a freckle shaped like a boba pearl, and who had been right beside him all along, waiting so patiently to be his friend.

And here is the funny, tender truth of it: all the rushing in the world had never once helped Huāshēng Táng feel like *this*. Not warm. Not seen. Not snug down to his last crunchy peanut. Speed had carried him past a thousand shelves and a thousand chores — and right past the one thing he'd been missing all along, which had been sitting quietly at his elbow the whole time, naming clouds and waiting to be noticed.

"I missed all of this," he said softly. "All this time, I was so busy being *fast*, I never even saw who was sitting next to me."

"You see me now," said Xiǎo Dòu. And she gave a happy little wiggle. "**朋友** (péngyǒu) = friend. That's what we are now. Right?"

"The very best kind," said Huāshēng Táng. And he meant it more than he'd ever meant anything in his speedy little life.

---

By late afternoon, the air finally cooled. A real breeze tiptoed in through the door, ruffled the flour, and seemed almost embarrassed to have kept everyone waiting. The candy was setting at last — crisp, golden, and perfect.

Huāshēng Táng felt the stickiness let go of him with a gentle *crack*. He could feel his old speedy self come zinging back, ready to skid and holler and conquer every last shelf before sundown. He was free to zoom again!

But he didn't.

Instead, he scooched — slowly, carefully, on purpose — just close enough to bump shoulders with Xiǎo Dòu. "Mànman lái," he said with a grin. "No rush. I've got everything I need right here."

"You're sure?" she teased. "No shelves to conquer? No reflections to polish?"

"The shelves can wait," said Huāshēng Táng grandly. "I have a whole sky full of clouds to learn the names of. Now — which one's Grandpa Bao again?"

When the candy-maker came to wrap the day's treats, she lifted their golden slab and beamed.

"Oh, this is a *beautiful* one," she said. "Look how they set together! I'll keep these two side by side. Wouldn't dream of splitting up a pair like that."

And she tucked them into the same crinkly paper, snug as two friends sharing one blanket.

"Together?" whispered Xiǎo Dòu.

"Together," said Huāshēng Táng. "Slow and crunchy and together. The very best way to be."

And do you know — when a child finally unwrapped them the next morning and crunched them up with a joyful giggle, the two friends went into the very same happy bite. Side by side, the whole way down. Which, if you ask any peanut, is exactly how the best friends were always meant to go.

---

## Goodnight Blessing

So slow down, little one, the way the warm day slowed.
There is no shelf you must reach tonight, no race to run, no place to be but here.
The sweetest things aren't fast — they're close.
And someone who loves you is right beside you, snug as candy in the dark.
Mànman lái. Take your time. The morning will wait.
Goodnight.
