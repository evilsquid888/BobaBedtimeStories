# Story 37: Stinky Tofu & the Lights-Out Hide-and-Seek

**Main Character:** Chòu Chòu (Stinky Tofu) — the bold, pungent fried-tofu cube
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A maze-like old shophouse alley during a power-out game of hide-and-seek
**Theme:** The very thing you'd hide can be what helps everyone find each other
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 捉迷藏 (zhuō mícáng) = hide-and-seek

---

Chòu Chòu was, without question, the smelliest snack in the entire night market — and he was deeply, dreadfully embarrassed about it.

He was a plump golden cube of fried tofu, crackly-crisp on the outside, soft and steamy in the middle, with four little toothpick legs and a face that scrunched up apologetically every time he walked past anybody. His crust crackled when he hopped. Tiny droplets of frying oil still glistened on his corners like little jewels, and when the breeze caught him just right, faint wisps of steam curled off his top like the world's smelliest little crown.

And oh, the smell. Chòu Chòu's smell arrived a full thirty seconds before he did, knocked politely, then barged right in. Cats fainted. Lanterns flickered. Once a passing dumpling sneezed so hard he turned himself inside out, which is how everyone learned that dumplings are the same on both sides. A nearby clothesline of socks once smelled Chòu Chòu coming, decided enough was enough, and simply walked off the line and into a new career. (We do not know what career. Socks are mysterious.)

"Sorry! Sorry!" Chòu Chòu whispered, scooting along the cobblestones with a wooden clothespin pinched over his own nose. He spent most evenings tucked behind the pickled-cabbage barrels, breathing as shallowly as a tofu cube possibly can, hoping nobody would notice him.

Which was a shame. Because tonight, the alley was about to need him very, very badly.

"Everyone! Gather up!" shouted Mochi, bouncing onto an overturned bucket. "Tonight we play **捉迷藏** (zhuō mícáng) = hide-and-seek! Lao Wang's Alley is the twistiest in all of Taiwan — perfect for hiding!"

The little snacks cheered. Lao Wang's Alley was indeed a glorious tangle: forty crooked shophouses leaning on each other like sleepy old friends, doorways that doubled back, staircases that went nowhere, and laundry lines crisscrossing overhead like a spider's daydream. There were red lanterns and dripping air-conditioners and one very confused chicken statue that had been pointing the wrong way since 1962. You could hide a water buffalo in there and lose it for a week. You could hide *yourself* in there and have a hard time finding *you* again.

Which, if you are a snack who has spent his whole life trying to be invisible, sounds less like a game and more like a dream come true.

"I'll be the seeker!" Mochi declared, slapping a soft squishy hand over her eyes. "Yī... èr... sān..."

The snacks scattered, giggling.

Pearl the tapioca bubble rolled into a teapot spout and went perfectly still, looking enormously pleased with herself. Taro the dumpling flattened himself against a purple wall, where he blended in so perfectly that you could only spot him by his nervous little eyes darting left and right. Shaved Ice melted halfway down a drainpipe, decided that was close enough to a hiding spot, and re-froze into a lumpy icicle. And Chòu Chòu — oh, Chòu Chòu — squeezed into the deepest, darkest corner he could find, three alleys back, behind a teetering stack of bamboo steamers, pulling that clothespin tight over his nose until his cheeks went wobbly.

*If I just stay tiny and quiet and smell-less,* he thought, *nobody will ever find me. That's the whole point of hiding. To disappear completely. To be the very best at vanishing.*

He was extremely good at it. That was rather the problem with his whole life.

"... jiǔ... shí! Ready or not, here I come!" called Mochi.

And that was the exact moment the lights went out.

Not just a flicker. *Everything.* Every lantern, every shop bulb, every glowing sign — *pop* — gone. A blackout had swallowed the whole block, the way blackouts sometimes do on a hot summer night when too many fans are spinning at once. The alley went darker than the inside of a sesame ball.

For one heartbeat, nobody made a sound.

Then: "Um," said a small voice. "Guys? I can't see my own bubble."

That was Pearl, somewhere off to the left. Or maybe the right. In the dark, directions get shy and refuse to say which way they are.

"Pearl? Where are you?" called Taro, and his voice came from somewhere unexpected, because Taro had wandered while hiding and now had absolutely no idea which purple wall he was leaning on.

"I'm by the teapot!" said Pearl.

"*Which* teapot? There are nine teapots!"

"There are *nine teapots?!*"

It turned out that Lao Wang's Alley, which was a delightful playground when you could see, became something else entirely when you couldn't. The twists twisted. The turns turned. The staircases that went nowhere suddenly went *somewhere*, and that somewhere was always wherever you didn't want to be. Even the chicken statue, who had at least been a reliable landmark, was now just a lump of darkness that everybody kept apologizing to. One by one, the snacks realized they were not hiding anymore.

They were *lost.*

"I bumped into a wall," wailed Shaved Ice, who had re-frozen in a panic. "I bumped into the *same* wall. Three times. I think the wall is following me!"

"Walls don't follow people," said Taro, not very confidently.

"Then why does it keep being *there?!*"

Mochi, the seeker, bounced bravely toward the voices — and promptly got herself tangled in a laundry line and ended up dangling upside down from somebody's bloomers, which would have been hilarious if everyone could have seen it. (It was. We're just telling you now so you can enjoy it later.)

Way back in his deep dark corner, Chòu Chòu listened to his friends' worried voices echoing through the maze, getting smaller and more scattered, and his soft little tofu heart squeezed tight.

*They sound scared,* he thought. *I should help.*

So he did what he always did. He shrank down small. He clamped the clothespin tighter over his nose. He made himself as quiet and tucked-away and *invisible* as possible — because that was how Chòu Chòu had learned to move through the world. Smell less. Take up less. Be less. Apologize, and disappear.

He scooted out from behind the steamers, holding his breath, trying to creep toward the voices without anyone catching even a whiff of him. *Don't smell, don't smell, don't smell,* he chanted to himself, which is a very silly thing for a stinky tofu to chant, like a thundercloud whispering *don't rain.*

And he got lost too.

Of course he did. In the pitch dark, with his nose pinched shut and his smell tucked away and himself made as small as a sneeze, Chòu Chòu was just another lost snack bumping into walls. He couldn't see his friends. They couldn't see him. He could have been an inch away from Pearl and neither would have known.

He sat down on a cold cobblestone, and for a moment he felt very alone.

Then a strange thought floated up out of the dark, soft and surprising, like a bubble rising in milk tea.

*Wait. They can't SEE me. But maybe... maybe seeing was never my best part.*

Chòu Chòu reached up. Very slowly, with his two little toothpick hands, he unclipped the clothespin from his nose.

And he let himself *smell.*

Oh, friends. He did not hold back. He did not apologize. For the very first time in his whole shy life, Chòu Chòu took a deep, proud breath, puffed out his crispy golden chest, and let his magnificent, eye-watering, world-famous aroma roll out into the dark in great glorious waves — funky and bold and absolutely, unmistakably *him.*

The smell rolled down the alley. It curled around the nine teapots. It drifted up the staircases that went nowhere and the ones that went somewhere. It found Pearl in her spout, and Taro on his wall, and Shaved Ice cowering by the following wall, and Mochi still swinging gently from the bloomers.

And every single one of them lifted their nose and said, at exactly the same time:

"...*Chòu Chòu?*"

Because there was no mistaking that smell. Not in a hundred dark alleys. It was the one thing in the whole blackout that you could find with your eyes shut tight — *especially* with your eyes shut tight.

"He's THIS way!" cried Pearl, rolling toward the wonderful stink.

"No, the smell's stronger over HERE!" laughed Taro, peeling off his wall and following his nose.

"FOLLOW THE STINK!" hollered Shaved Ice, and it became the official rule of the night.

Through the twisty dark they came, all of them, navigating not by sight but by scent — Chòu Chòu's bold beautiful smell like a lighthouse beam, a warm pungent thread pulling every lost player home. The stronger it got, the closer they knew they were. Around the nine teapots, past the wrong-way chicken, under the laundry lines, down the staircases that went somewhere — and for once *somewhere* was exactly where they wanted to be. Nobody bumped a wall. Nobody got lost. They just breathed in, said "*ooh, getting warmer!*", and trusted their noses to do what eyes could not. Pearl bonked gently into Taro in the dark, and instead of yelping, they both laughed, because they could tell by the smell that home was only a few hops away.

When they all tumbled into Chòu Chòu's corner — Pearl first, then Taro, then a slightly damp Shaved Ice, and finally Mochi, who had freed herself from the bloomers and was very ready to never mention it again — they piled together in a giggling, relieved heap.

"You found us," said Pearl, hugging his crispy little corner. "You found *all* of us. In the dark!"

"You're the only thing in this whole alley you can't lose," said Taro, wonderingly. "Chòu Chòu, your smell is the best smell I have ever smelled in my life."

Chòu Chòu blinked his little tofu eyes. He waited for the embarrassment to come, the way it always did.

It didn't come.

Instead, something warm and golden and crispy bloomed right in the middle of him — the feeling of being, for the first time ever, exactly the right amount of *himself.*

"I spent my whole life hiding the thing that helped you find me," he said softly. And then, because he was still Chòu Chòu, he added, "Also, can someone get Mochi down from those bloomers — oh. You did. Good."

The lights flickered back on a few minutes later, all at once, the whole alley glowing gold again. But by then nobody really needed them. They'd already found their way.

And from that night on, whenever the snacks of Lao Wang's Alley played 捉迷藏 in the dark, there was one player nobody ever bothered hiding from — because hiding from Chòu Chòu was completely, gloriously, deliciously impossible.

He never wore the clothespin again.

## Goodnight Blessing

Little one, the thing you keep tucked away — the loud laugh, the big feeling, the part of you that takes up room — that might just be the very thing someone needs to find their way back to you. You don't have to be smaller tonight. You don't have to disappear. The world finds the people it loves by following exactly what makes them *them.* So breathe deep, take up all the room you need, and let the soft dark wrap around you like a warm steamer lid. You are not too much. You are just enough, and then a little more, in the best possible way. Sleep now, brave and bold and wholly yourself. Goodnight.
