# Story 82: Pudding & the Wobbliest Bridge

**Main Character:** Pudding (caramel flan — wobbly, anxious, lovable klutz)
**Guest Characters:** Douhua (silky tofu pudding in ginger syrup)
**Setting:** A swaying suspension footbridge over a green mountain gorge
**Theme:** Being wobbly doesn't mean you can't cross.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 橋 (qiáo) = bridge

---

Pudding had a job to do, and Pudding did not love jobs that involved standing up.

He was a glossy golden flan, the kind that jiggles if you so much as look at him sternly, crowned with a slow river of caramel that dripped down his sides like melted sunshine. When he was calm, he wobbled. When he was nervous, he wobbled *faster*. And right now he was holding a tiny acorn-cup of warm caramel medicine — the very best medicine in the mountains for a tummy ache — and across the green gorge, on the far misty cliff, his friend Hú Jiāo the pepper bun was sniffling and sneezing little puffs of pepper.

"Achoo! *Pepper!* Achoo!" came the faint, fiery sneezes drifting across the canyon.

Between Pudding and his sneezy friend stretched a long rope **橋** (qiáo) = bridge, swaying gently in the morning breeze like a hammock that had decided to become terrifying.

Pudding looked at it.

The bridge looked back.

Pudding wobbled.

---

"Oh dear oh dear oh dear," Pudding whispered, his caramel sloshing. "I can't cross *that*. It moves! Bridges aren't supposed to move! I move enough for *both* of us!"

The footbridge was made of woven vine-rope and smooth wooden planks, strung between two cliffs over a gorge so green and deep it looked like a bowl of grass-jelly soup. Far below, a silver river giggled over stones. Mist curled up from it like steam off a breakfast griddle. It was, honestly, beautiful — which Pudding would have happily told you, from very far away, with both feet planted firmly on solid ground.

"Going across, are we?" said a soft, silky voice.

Pudding nearly leapt out of his caramel. Beside him sat Douhua, the silky tofu pudding, wobbling in her little bowl of golden ginger syrup. She was the gentlest, most poetic wobble in all the mountains, and she had a way of appearing exactly when a worried friend needed her.

"I *can't*," Pudding moaned. "Look at me, Douhua. I'm a flan. One gust of wind and I'll jiggle right off the edge and become caramel *splat*. And the bridge wobbles, and *I* wobble, and two wobbles make a — a — a *double wobble*, and double wobbles are how disasters happen!"

Douhua tilted her smooth, silky head and considered this. A curl of ginger steam rose off her.

"You know," she said dreamily, "the river down there has been wobbling its way through this gorge for ten thousand years. It never once stood still. And yet — it always, always arrives at the sea."

Pudding blinked. "...Is that a yes or a no?"

"It's a river," said Douhua, smiling. "Rivers don't do yes or no. They do *forward*."

Pudding chewed on this, which is a thing flans can do even though they have no teeth. It did not make him feel braver, exactly, but it did make him feel slightly more *poetic* about being scared, which is a very Douhua sort of feeling to have.

"That's lovely," he said. "Truly. But the river doesn't have to carry a cup of medicine without spilling it. The river doesn't have arms. The river doesn't *wobble.*"

"The river is *nothing but* wobble," Douhua said, with a quiet little laugh that sounded like syrup pouring. "It's the wobbliest thing on the whole mountain, and it has never once fallen off a cliff. Think about that, Pudding. The wobbliest thing in the gorge is also the surest."

---

"Achoo! Pepper! Somebody — achoo! — bring me my medicine!" cried Hú Jiāo from across the gorge, a little puff of pepper-smoke blooming over the far cliff like a tiny gray cloud.

That did it. Pudding's friend needed him. And medicine doesn't deliver itself, no matter how nicely you ask it.

Pudding took a deep, trembling breath, held his acorn-cup of caramel medicine very, very level, and put one wobbly foot on the first plank.

The bridge said *creeeeak*.

Pudding said *"EEP."*

"Don't look down," Douhua advised softly, floating along beside him in her bowl, gliding like a little ginger-scented boat. "Look at the next plank. Only ever the next plank, **慢慢來** (màn màn lái) = take it slow."

"Màn màn lái," Pudding repeated, squeezing his eyes mostly shut. "Slow. Slow I can do. I am *extremely* good at slow." He shuffled forward. *Wobble.* The bridge swayed left. Pudding swayed right. The cup of medicine swished — but did not spill. "I — I'm doing it! I'm on the bridge! I'm a bridge-crosser! I'm a — *WHOOP* —"

A breeze rolled up the gorge and the whole bridge tipped like a seesaw.

Pudding wobbled so hard he nearly turned inside out. His caramel slid up one side and down the other. He flailed his little flan arms. He squeaked. He jiggled in a complete, dizzying circle.

And then — somehow — he was still standing. Still holding the cup. Still on the bridge.

"I MOVED," Pudding gasped, eyes wide as tea-saucers, "AND I'M STILL HERE."

"That," said Douhua warmly, "is the whole secret."

"That cannot possibly be the whole secret," Pudding panted. "Secrets are supposed to be *helpful*. That one was just terrifying with a happy ending."

"Most of the good ones are," said Douhua.

A small brown bird landed on the bridge-rope and chirped, as if to say *carry on, you're doing fine,* and then flew off into the mist as if to say *but I'm not staying to watch.* Pudding watched it go with deep sympathy. He understood that bird completely.

---

But the middle of the bridge was the worst. That's a rule of bridges, you should know — the very middle is always the wobbliest part, because it is the farthest from anything solid on either side. The planks bounced. The ropes hummed. And below, the river giggled on, not unkindly, just busy being a river.

Pudding froze. His caramel went very still, which for Pudding is the scariest thing of all, because a still flan is a *worried* flan.

"I can't go forward," he whispered. "And I can't go back. I'm stuck in the middle of the wobble. This is it. This is where I become a permanent mountain decoration. 'Behold the Frozen Flan, who tried.'"

"Pudding," said Douhua gently. "May I tell you a secret about wobbling?"

"...Will it make the bridge stop?"

"No," she admitted. "Nothing makes the bridge stop. The bridge is *supposed* to wobble — that's how it bends instead of breaking. A bridge that couldn't wobble would snap in the first big wind." She drifted closer, her silky surface catching the morning light. "You, my friend, have spent your whole life thinking your wobble was a flaw. But the bridge wobbles. The river wobbles. The mountain mist wobbles. The whole world is a little bit jiggly, Pudding. You're not *broken*. You're just *honest about it.*"

Pudding stared at her. A single drop of caramel rolled thoughtfully down his cheek.

"So I don't have to stop wobbling," he said slowly. "I just have to wobble... *toward* something?"

"Wobble forward," Douhua said, "and it still counts as crossing."

Far below, the river giggled over its stones, wobbling its silver way toward a sea it had never seen but somehow always trusted was there. A breeze pushed up the gorge, and the bridge swayed, and Douhua's ginger syrup rippled into tiny golden rings, and Pudding's caramel slid gently from one side of him to the other — and for one quiet moment the whole mountain was wobbling together, all at once, in a kind of slow and secret dance.

It was, Pudding realized, sort of beautiful.

"Okay," he whispered. "Okay. One wobble at a time."

---

So Pudding wobbled.

He didn't stop being scared — that would be a tall order for a flan over a gorge. He just stopped *waiting* to be unscared. He let his wobble be a wobble, and he pointed it at the far cliff, and he went.

*Wobble-step. Wobble-step. Sway. Squeak. Wobble-step.*

The bridge swung, and Pudding swung with it, and for the first time he noticed something marvelous: when he stopped fighting the sway and just *moved with it*, the wobbling didn't tip him over at all. It was almost like... dancing. A clumsy, jiggly, caramel-flavored dance, three thousand feet over a giggling river.

The trick, he discovered, was to let the bridge wobble *him* instead of trying to hold perfectly still. Whenever it tipped, he tipped with it, and somehow that kept him centered, the way a leaf rides a wave instead of fighting it. Each plank that had looked so frightening from the cliff turned out to be just one plank — wide enough, sturdy enough, exactly enough. *Màn màn lái*, he hummed to himself. The next plank. Only ever the next plank.

"I'm a dancer!" Pudding announced to the entire gorge. "I'm a wobble-dancer! Watch this!" He did a little spin. The bridge bounced. He bounced. The caramel medicine sloshed right up to the rim of the acorn-cup — and settled back down, not a single drop lost.

"Careful, show-off," laughed Douhua.

"Bold on the wobble, gold on the *cross!*" Pudding crowed, borrowing somebody else's catchphrase entirely, because he was too happy to remember his own.

And then, before he even realized it, his front foot touched something that did not wobble at all.

Solid cliff. The far side. He'd done it.

"Achoo!" sneezed Hú Jiāo, popping up from behind a rock, rosy-cheeked and stuffy-nosed and absolutely delighted. "Pudding! You — achoo! — you *crossed!* On the wobbliest **橋** in the mountains! How?!"

Pudding held out the acorn-cup of warm caramel medicine, beaming so wide his glaze nearly cracked.

"I wobbled," he said proudly. "The whole entire way. And it turns out — wobbling forward still counts."

Hú Jiāo drank the caramel medicine in one happy gulp. Warmth spread through his peppery little heart, his sneezes softened to sleepy sniffles, and within a minute his nose had stopped puffing pepper-clouds entirely. "*Xièxie*," he sighed gratefully — **謝謝** (xièxie) = thank you. "You saved my whole afternoon, you brave, jiggly hero."

---

That evening, the three friends sat together on the warm, solid cliff and watched the sun melt down behind the mountains, turning the mist gold and rose and the color of caramel.

Douhua wobbled contentedly in her ginger syrup. Hú Jiāo, all better now, toasted everyone with a fresh round of sneeze-free hugs. And Pudding — Pudding looked back across the gorge at the long swaying bridge, glowing softly in the sunset, and felt something he had never, ever felt about a wobbly thing before.

He felt proud of it.

"You know," Pudding said quietly, "I think I'll always wobble. I'm a flan. It's sort of my whole situation." He gave a happy little jiggle. "But I crossed anyway. So maybe wobbling isn't the opposite of brave. Maybe wobbling *is* what brave looks like, when you're made of caramel."

"Màn màn lái," murmured Douhua, smiling at the last of the light. "Slowly, slowly. The river always arrives."

And high above them, the mountain bridge swayed gently in the night breeze — wobbling, always wobbling, holding everyone safely as it always had, and always would.

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, the world is full of wobbly bridges, and you do not have to cross them perfectly — you only have to cross them *forward*. So when your knees shake and your heart sways like a rope bridge in the wind, remember sweet jiggly Pudding, who wobbled the whole way and arrived all the same. Be soft. Be honest about your wobble. And know that even the steadiest mountain is held up by ten thousand gentle sways. Sleep now, brave and jiggly and golden as caramel in the lamplight. Goodnight.
