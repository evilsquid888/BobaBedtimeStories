# Story 33: Beef Noodle & the Steamy Stadium

**Main Character:** Niú Niú (Beef Noodle Soup)
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A bustling beef-noodle festival arena in Taipei
**Theme:** Warmth shared is warmth doubled.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 牛肉麵 (niúròu miàn) = beef noodle soup

---

On the chilliest night of the whole Taipei winter, the grandest stadium in the city was glowing like a lantern stuffed with sunshine — because tonight was the Festival of **牛肉麵** (niúròu miàn) = beef noodle soup, and that meant steam. Oh, so much steam! It rose off a hundred simmering pots and curled up into the cold air, and if you squinted, the whole stadium looked like a giant bowl of soup wearing a hat made of clouds.

In the very middle of it all, hiding behind a stack of bamboo chopsticks taller than himself, was Niú Niú.

Now, Niú Niú was a warm steaming clay bowl with a friendly face right on his rim, and he wore a long wheat-noodle scarf wrapped twice around his neck for good luck. He had tender beef-cube shoulders, a swirl of bok choy for a collar, and a cozy little steam-cloud halo that floated over his head like a thought he hadn't quite finished thinking.

And right now, his thought was: *Maybe I shouldn't have come.*

"Slurp first, worry later," he whispered to himself. But his slurp came out as a nervous little *sip.*

---

It was Niú Niú's very first festival. He had traveled all the way from a quiet little corner of Tainan, where he'd spent his whole life making one bowl of soup at a time for sleepy travelers and stray cats. He was good at warm. He was a *champion* at cozy. But this — this was the SLURP-OFF, the biggest beef-noodle contest in all of Taiwan, and every bowl here looked enormous.

Across the arena, a towering bowl named Big Ol' Brawny was bellowing through a megaphone. "STEP RIGHT UP! My bowl is so BIG, I have my OWN postal code!" The crowd roared. Brawny flexed his beef cubes, which were each the size of a small refrigerator.

Niú Niú looked down at his own beef cubes. They were the size of, well, beef cubes.

"Oh dear," he murmured, and his steam-halo drooped like a balloon two days after a birthday party. "Everyone here is so big and bold. I'm just... warm." He tugged his noodle scarf up over his face. "I'll just watch from back here. Behind the chopsticks. Where it's safe."

A little sign by the entrance read: **SLURP-OFF SIGN-UPS — ALL BOWLS WELCOME!** But Niú Niú's ladle-hand wouldn't reach for the pen. It just hovered there, shivering a little, the way your foot hovers over a cold swimming pool.

---

That was when he heard it.

*Chatter-chatter-shiver-shiver.*

Niú Niú peeked around the chopsticks. There, by the stadium's back gate, where the cold wind whistled in, stood a wobbly little line of latecomers — and they were FREEZING.

There was a thin rice noodle named Mǐ Fěn, so cold her noodles had gone stiff and straight as drinking straws. There was a shy half-portion of bok choy who'd lost her bowl in the crowd and now just rolled around like a lonely green tumbleweed. And there was the tiniest dumpling Niú Niú had ever seen — a baby wonton named **餛飩** (húntún) = wonton, no bigger than a thumbprint, whose teeny soup had gone completely, sadly cold.

"W-we got here l-late," chattered Mǐ Fěn, her words bouncing like hiccups. "All the warm spots by the s-s-stoves are taken. The big bowls don't have any r-room to spare."

The baby wonton sniffled. "I'm s-so cold my soup turned into a t-tiny ice rink." He gave it a little tap. *Tink.* "See?"

Niú Niú's friendly clay face did a thing it always did when somebody was cold and sad. It got *warmer.* His steam-halo, which had been drooping, suddenly puffed right up like a startled cat.

"Oh, you poor sleepy noodles," he said, his voice slow and warm as a winter night. "Come here, kiddos. Come stand by me."

---

He didn't even think about it. He just opened up his cozy steam-cloud halo wide, wide, WIDE, and let it billow out around the shivering latecomers like a warm fuzzy blanket made of *air.*

"Ahhhhh," sighed Mǐ Fěn, as her stiff noodles went soft and wiggly again.

"Ohhhhh," wobbled the bok choy, perking right up.

"Eeeeee!" squeaked the baby wonton, as his little ice rink melted back into proper hot soup. "It's WARM again! Mister Bowl, you're like a hug that you can *breathe!*"

Niú Niú chuckled, and when he chuckled, he slurped a little — *shloorp* — which made all the cold latecomers giggle so hard the bok choy fell over.

"Stick close now," Niú Niú said, gathering them under his steam. "There's plenty of warm to go around. That's the funny thing about warm, kiddo. It doesn't run out when you share it. It *grows.*"

And here's the wink, dear reader: he was exactly right, and he didn't even know how right he was yet.

---

Because the more Niú Niú warmed up his shivering little friends, the bigger his steam-halo got. Mǐ Fěn's gentle relief added a soft curl of steam. The bok choy's happy sigh puffed up another. And the baby wonton was so overjoyed that he started doing happy backflips in his bowl, splashing tiny droplets that turned into a whole fountain of sparkling steam.

Their warmth bounced into Niú Niú's warmth. His warmth bounced back into theirs. Round and round it went, doubling and doubling, until — *FWOOMP!* — Niú Niú's steam-cloud halo grew so enormous it lifted right up off his head and spread across the WHOLE stadium ceiling like a cozy indoor sky.

The freezing-cold spectators in the cheap seats stopped shivering.
The judges, who'd been blowing on their frozen ladle-fingers, sighed in relief.
Even Big Ol' Brawny stopped flexing and went, "Ohhh, that feels nice."

A hush fell over the arena. Then the Head Judge — a wise old marbled tea egg named Dàn Dàn, who read fortunes in cracked shells — climbed up onto her podium and squinted at the giant warm cloud filling the stadium.

"Who," she said into the microphone, "is doing *that?*"

Every face in the stadium turned toward the stack of chopsticks. And there stood Niú Niú, his noodle scarf slipped down off his face, surrounded by three of the happiest, warmest, no-longer-shivering little snacks in all of Taipei.

"Oh," squeaked Niú Niú, turning the color of a radish. "Um. Hello. I was just — they were cold, so I —"

"Young bowl," said Dàn Dàn gently, "do you know what this contest is really about?"

Niú Niú shook his head, jingling his bok-choy collar. "The... biggest bowl?"

Dàn Dàn laughed, a soft crackly laugh like a fire settling. "Oh no, kiddo. We don't crown the biggest bowl of **牛肉麵**. Any pot can be big. We crown the *warmest* one. Every crack tells a story, and your story just warmed an entire stadium full of cold strangers — and you weren't even trying to win." She raised her ladle high. "THAT is the warmest bowl I have ever seen."

---

The stadium ERUPTED.

Confetti made of crispy scallion flakes rained down. The crowd chanted "NIÚ NIÚ! NIÚ NIÚ!" The baby wonton was so proud he fainted with joy and had to be revived with an extra-warm puff of steam. Big Ol' Brawny lumbered over, sniffling into his enormous broth.

"Kid," Brawny said, "I've been trying to win this thing for eleven years by being the biggest. And you won it by being kind. Can I... can I stand under your cloud too? It's chilly out by the postal-code section."

"Of course!" beamed Niú Niú, and he opened his halo wider. "Everybody come on in! There's no such thing as too much warm."

So the whole stadium scootched together under Niú Niú's giant steam-cloud sky. The big bowls and the little bowls, the rice noodles and the wontons, the judges and the show-offs, all snuggled into one enormous shared warmth. And do you know what? When everybody shared, the warm didn't get thinner.

It got *bigger.*

---

They gave Niú Niú a golden ladle trophy that night, but honestly, his favorite prize was the way the baby wonton fell fast asleep on his rim, snoring tiny bubbly snores, safe and toasty at last.

As the festival lights dimmed low and the steam settled into a soft cozy fog, Niú Niú looked around at all his brand-new sleepy friends and smiled his warmest clay-bowl smile.

"Slurp first," he whispered, "and worry never."

Then he wrapped his noodle scarf around as many little ones as it would reach, and let his steam tuck the whole stadium into the gentlest, warmest, most well-shared goodnight in all of Taipei.

## Goodnight Blessing

Breathe slow, like soup-steam rising. You are cozy, you are loved, and there is plenty of warm to go around. Goodnight.
