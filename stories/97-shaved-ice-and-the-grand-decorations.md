# Story 97: Shaved Ice & the Grandest Decorations

**Main Character:** Shaved Ice (剉冰)
**Guest Characters:** Egg Waffle, Pineapple Cake
**Setting:** A vast festival hall being decorated for the hundredth-story reunion
**Theme:** More is more — but the most is everyone pitching in.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** **裝飾** (zhuāngshì) = decoration

---

The biggest, fluffiest, most DRAMATIC dessert on the whole island swept through the doors of the festival hall, flung out both syrupy arms, and declared at the top of her wobbly voice:

"BEHOLD! THE GRANDEST HALL THAT EVER GRANDED!"

This was Shaved Ice, of course. You could not mistake her for anyone else. She was a towering snowy mountain of fluff, crowned with red beans and mango and a teetering scoop of taro, and she wore tiny sunglasses indoors — even at night, even in a dim hall, even when there was absolutely no sun to be glamorous about. Especially then.

Today the festival hall was empty and echoey, a cavern as big as a cloud. Tomorrow it would hold the most important party in dessert history: the **百** (bǎi) = hundredth story reunion, when every single friend from every single bedtime adventure would gather under one roof.

And Shaved Ice had appointed herself — with a flourish nobody had asked for — Director of **裝飾** (zhuāngshì) = decoration.

"This hall," she announced to the empty room, "shall be DECORATED. And not a little. Not a medium. I am thinking MORE. I am thinking MOST."

The echo answered: *most... most... most...*

"See? Even the room agrees."

---

Shaved Ice got to work the way a thunderstorm gets to work — all at once and everywhere. She unrolled streamers as long as rivers. She hung paper lanterns until the ceiling looked like a sky full of glowing dumplings. She built a tower of pineapple-shaped balloons, then a second tower to keep the first tower company, then a third tower because two towers looked lonely.

She did not so much *decorate* as *erupt*. Glitter went up. Confetti came down. Somewhere in the middle, she got tangled in her own ribbon, spun three full circles to escape, and decided the spin had been "an artistic choice."

"More lanterns!" she cried, melting just a tiny bit from the excitement. "More ribbons! More sparkle! More! MORE! A party this big needs decorations you can see from the MOON!"

Somewhere far above, the Moon Rabbit probably waved. (She did, in fact. She also leaned over to her tiny mochi mallet and whispered, "Goodness, that one has *flair*.")

By the time the front doors creaked open again, the hall had become a jungle. A glittery, swirling, gloriously bonkers jungle.

In walked Egg Waffle — the golden grid-shaped showman — twirling his little top hat. Behind him strolled Pineapple Cake, a neat golden rectangle who carried himself like a very polite envelope that had learned to walk.

Egg Waffle stopped. His eyes went wide as waffle-holes.

"Ladies and gentle-snacks," he gasped, "what a SPECTACLE! Folks, you gotta see this! It's bigger than my biggest carnival! It's — wait." He squinted. "Is that a fourth balloon tower?"

"Fifth," said Shaved Ice proudly, sliding down a ribbon. "I lost count, which means it's working."

Pineapple Cake adjusted his crumbly little bow tie and looked around with great formality. "It is most... abundant," he said carefully, because he was too polite to say *too much*. "I confess I cannot find the door anymore."

"You don't need the door, darling," said Shaved Ice. "You're already in."

"But how will the hundred guests get in?"

There was a small pause.

"...Through the abundance," Shaved Ice declared, and struck a pose so dramatic that a balloon popped loose and bonked Egg Waffle gently on the brim of his hat.

"Ladies and snacks," he said, catching it, "I have been UPSTAGED by a balloon. I respect it."

---

Now, here is a thing about Shaved Ice that you should know, the way you know a secret about a friend: she was not being selfish. Not even a little. She wanted this party to be perfect because she loved her friends so enormously that "perfect" felt like the only way to say so. Her heart was as big as her hairdo, which is to say: ENORMOUS, and slightly tipping over.

So she kept going.

She strung a chandelier of sugar crystals so heavy it groaned. She built an archway of stacked tea eggs (Dàn Dàn, the marbled little tea egg, was *in* the archway, blinking politely and offering everyone a fortune as they walked under). She hung so many lanterns that the room got warm — and warmth, for a tower of ice, is a problem.

*Drip.*

A bead of melt slid down Shaved Ice's snowy slope.

"I'm FINE," she announced, before anyone asked. "That's just sparkle-sweat. Champions sparkle-sweat."

*Drip. Drip.*

"More streamers!" she cried, a little wobblier now. "If I stop, I'll have to think about how warm it is in here, and I refuse to think, I only DECORATE—"

And then the great sugar-crystal chandelier gave a tired groan, the topmost balloon tower leaned like a sleepy giraffe, and a whole curtain of streamers came whooshing down in a glittery avalanche — *fwoomph* — right on top of the Director of Decoration herself.

For a moment, the hall was very quiet.

Then a muffled voice from under the pile said: "...I meant to do that."

---

Egg Waffle and Pineapple Cake rushed over and dug her out. Shaved Ice emerged with a paper lantern stuck on one corner like a hat and a streamer draped over her sunglasses. A little smaller than before. A little melty. A little — though she'd never say it — sad.

"It's not enough," she whispered. "I added everything. Lanterns, towers, ribbons, the works. And it still feels... empty."

Egg Waffle took off his top hat and held it to his chest, which is what showmen do when they get sincere. "Y'know," he said gently, "I once threw a carnival with a thousand lights and three brass bands. Brightest night you ever saw. And you know what folks remembered after?"

"The lights?" said Shaved Ice hopefully.

"Nope. They remembered who they stood next to."

Pineapple Cake nodded his crumbly nod. "In my family," he said, formal as ever, "we say a gift is not the wrapping. It is the giving. You have wrapped this hall most beautifully, my friend. But you forgot to put the gift inside."

"What gift?" asked Shaved Ice.

Pineapple Cake gestured, very politely, at the empty space where one hundred friends were going to stand.

"Them," he said.

---

Shaved Ice looked at the towers. She looked at the lanterns. She looked at the great groaning chandelier she'd been so proud of. And then she did the bravest thing a show-off can do.

She took some of it *down*.

"Okay," she said, blinking back a melty tear. "Okay. New plan. Less stuff. More room. Egg Waffle — clear me a big open middle. Pineapple Cake — you're good at neat. Make a path. A welcome path, right to the heart of the hall."

"A path to where?" Egg Waffle asked.

"To everybody," said Shaved Ice. "The decoration isn't the streamers. The decoration is the *crowd*. A hundred friends shoulder to shoulder, glowing like — like a sky full of stars that came down for snacks." She gave a wobbly little gasp at her own idea. "Oh. OH. That's the most *zhuāngshì* of all, isn't it?"

"That," said Egg Waffle, putting his hat back on, "is showmanship, kid."

So the three of them got to work — but differently this time. They didn't pile up. They opened up.

It was, Shaved Ice admitted, much harder to take *down* a tower than to build one. Each time she unhung a lantern she felt a tiny pang, the way you feel when you give away a toy you secretly still like. But every time she cleared a space, the hall seemed to take a breath. The echoes turned friendly. The room felt less like a closet stuffed with sparkle and more like a warm pair of open arms.

"You know," said Egg Waffle, hauling a balloon tower aside, "less stuff means folks can actually *see* each other. And seeing each other — kid, that's the whole show."

Egg Waffle, who knew a thing or two about a grand entrance, built a stage of light at the center. Pineapple Cake laid a tidy golden path, gift-wrapped at the edges, leading every guest gently inward, and pressed each ribbon flat with the loving care of someone wrapping a present for a grandmother. And Shaved Ice — well.

Shaved Ice kept just *enough* sparkle. A river of lanterns overhead, soft now, not blinding. One balloon tower (the friendliest one). And in the very middle, a great open ring of warm light, waiting.

Waiting for friends.

---

The next night, they came. All of them.

Boba bounced in first, naturally — *boing, boing, boing* — followed by Taro adjusting his reading glasses, and Mochi sticking to absolutely everyone in the kindest way. Niú Niú swept his noodle-scarf through the doors with a cozy *slurrrp*. Tangyuan rolled in singing about family. The popcorn-chicken crew arrived as one giggling cloud, bouncing off the friendly balloon tower and squealing "MORE CRUNCH, MORE FUN!" Tǔdì Gōng, tiny and jolly, handed out lucky candies at the door, and Xiǎo Hǔ Yé padded under the snack tables to claim the comfiest spot for a nap.

Chòu Chòu strolled in with his heart-shaped cape, trailing his friendly purple sparkle-cloud, and even *that* somehow made the hall feel cozier — like a campfire that smelled, well, *bold*. Mango did a victory lap just for fun. Xiǎo Lóng floated in on his bamboo steamer, very carefully keeping level, counting his eighteen pleats so as not to spill a single drop of feeling.

One by one by one, they filled the great open middle, until the hall was packed shoulder to shoulder, glowing, laughing, warm.

And from her perch above it all, Shaved Ice looked down — and gasped.

Because Pineapple Cake had been right. The decorations had been pretty. But *this* — a hundred friends, every color and shape and silly voice, all crowded together under the lanterns — *this* was the most beautiful thing the hall had ever held.

"Slurp first, worry later, kiddo," called Niú Niú from below, raising a cheer.

"Look at us all!" cried Shaved Ice, flinging out her arms. "It's the grandest **裝飾** that ever DECORATED! And not a single bit of it is mine — it's ALL OF US!"

The whole hall roared back, a hundred voices at once, so loud and so warm that one tiny lantern swung from the happiness of it.

And Shaved Ice — towering, dramatic, glittery, melty, and loved — decided that being a little smaller, with everyone gathered close, felt about a hundred times bigger than any tower of balloons.

She didn't even mind the dripping. She was, after all, simply too cozy to stay frozen.

## Goodnight Blessing

May your room be just full enough — a soft blanket, a sleepy lamp, and the people you love close by. You don't need a thousand sparkles to feel grand; you only need to know you belong, right in the warm middle of things. Tonight, imagine a hall glowing gently with friends, and you tucked happily among them. The lanterns are dimming. The party is winding down into dreams. Goodnight.
