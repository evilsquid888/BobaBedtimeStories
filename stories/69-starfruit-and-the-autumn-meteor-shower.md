# Story 69: Starfruit & the Autumn Meteor Shower

**Main Character:** Starfruit (Starfruit) (existing)
**Guest Characters:** Tangyuan
**Setting:** A dark countryside hilltop on a clear autumn night
**Theme:** Every wish counts, even the ones that fall.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 流星 (liúxīng) = shooting star

---

On the one night each autumn when the sky took off its cloudy pajamas and showed off all its sparkles, Starfruit packed a picnic basket entirely with snacks shaped like stars — star crackers, star jellies, and one accidental triangle she insisted was "a star resting on its side." Then she marched up the darkest, quietest hilltop in all the countryside, plopped down on a checkered blanket, and announced to absolutely nobody, "Tonight, I shall make one hundred wishes."

She had counted them out in advance. She had written them in a tiny notebook with a cover she'd decorated herself, which is to say she'd drawn stars on it, because Starfruit drew stars on everything — her lunchbox, her shoes, her arm, and once, by accident, the cat. Wish number forty-seven was simply, "to grow one inch taller, but a *glamorous* inch." Wish number eighty-one was "to finally win at hide-and-seek against Coconut Jelly, who hides in the ocean, which is *cheating.*" Wish number thirteen had been scribbled out and rewritten four times and now just said "something dramatic — decide later."

Starfruit, you should know, was a five-pointed golden star of a girl, glowing faintly at each tip like she'd been dipped in lamplight. When she tilted her head, all five points tilted with her, and she had a habit of staring up so dreamily that she sometimes walked into trees, lampposts, and once, memorably, a cow. The cow had not been pleased. Starfruit had apologized to it in poetry, which only confused the cow further.

---

She lay back on the blanket and waited.

The sky was enormous tonight, blacker than grass jelly and freckled with a million stars. Somewhere far below, the night market glowed like a spilled jar of fireflies, and if she listened hard, Starfruit could just barely hear the faraway *clink* of Niú Niú's ladle and the giggle-chorus of the popcorn-chicken crew bouncing between stalls. Up here, though, it was just Starfruit and the hush of crickets practicing their lullabies, and the cool autumn air that smelled of dry grass and far-off woodsmoke.

"Any minute now," she whispered, hugging her knees. "Any minute now, the **流星** (liúxīng) = shooting stars will fall, and I will catch every single one with a wish." She patted her notebook. "I have prepared. I am a professional wisher. This is my craft."

A voice behind her said, "Ooh! Are we wishing? We love wishing!"

Starfruit yelped and nearly rolled off her own blanket. Up the hill, round and white and beaming, came Tangyuan — a plump little rice ball with a sweet sesame center, bouncing along like a happy moon that had decided to take a stroll.

"It's *we*," Tangyuan corrected herself before Starfruit could ask. "Family always says *we*. Where one tangyuan goes, the whole bowl follows in spirit. May we share your hill?"

"I suppose," said Starfruit, scooting over. "But I warn you, I have very important wishing to do. One hundred wishes. I made a list."

"A *list*!" Tangyuan gasped, as though Starfruit had said she'd invented dessert. "How organized. We forget our wishes the moment we think them. They roll right out of us, like, well — like a tangyuan down a hill." She demonstrated by rolling a little way down the slope. "Whoops. See?" She rolled back up, slightly out of breath. "Once we wished for a snack and then immediately forgot, so we just... ate ourselves a little. It was a confusing afternoon for the whole family."

"That sounds like a problem," said Starfruit.

"It built character," said Tangyuan proudly. "Now budge over, dreamy one, and let us watch the sky together. The more eyes, the more wishes caught. That's mathematics, that is."

---

They settled in to wait together, two soft shapes on a dark hill, the picnic basket between them.

And then it happened.

A thin silver thread tore across the sky — *fwip!* — bright and quick and gone.

"A **流星**!" both of them shouted at once.

"Quick, Starfruit, wish!" Tangyuan bounced so hard she briefly left the blanket.

But Starfruit hesitated, her little notebook open, her pencil hovering. Because just then, drifting up the hill on the cool autumn air, came a sound. A small, sniffly, wobbly sound.

It was Pudding.

The caramel flan came wobbling out of the dark, jiggling with every nervous step, a tiny lantern bobbing in one trembling hand. "Oh — oh, I didn't know anyone was up here," he hiccuped, and the lantern light shivered with him. "I just — I climbed up to wish I'd stop being such a klutz. I dropped the whole tray at the bakery today. *Again.* Forty-two egg tarts. They rolled everywhere. One landed in the Earth God's hat. He said it was 'a blessing' but I think he was just being kind."

There was a pause.

"...To be fair," said Tangyuan gently, "that does sound a little funny."

"It was *hilarious*," Pudding admitted, sniffling and almost laughing. "But I missed the shooting star. I was too busy crying about tarts."

Starfruit looked at her notebook. She looked at her one hundred carefully numbered wishes. Then she looked at Pudding's wobbly, hopeful face.

"Here," she said, tearing the silver thread of the wish out of the air with her pointiest point and pressing it gently into Pudding's hands. "Take that one. It was going to be mine, but I have ninety-nine more. Wish to be a *graceful* klutz. The kind that drops things in slow motion and makes it look like dancing."

Pudding stared at the little glimmer in his hands. "You'd... give me your wish? Your *actual* wish?"

"It's just one," said Starfruit, waving a point breezily. "I have a whole list."

Pudding squeezed his eyes shut, held the glimmer to his wobbly chest, and wished so hard that he jiggled like a bowl of soup in an earthquake. When he opened his eyes, the glimmer had melted into him like sugar into warm milk, and he looked, somehow, just a tiny bit steadier.

"I feel..." he whispered. "I feel like maybe the next thing I drop, I'll drop *beautifully.*"

"That's the spirit," said Starfruit warmly. "Now shoo — go practice falling with style."

Pudding wobbled off down the hill, and — would you believe it — tripped on a root, tumbled head over heels, and somehow landed in a perfect, graceful, arms-out pose at the bottom, as though he'd meant to do it all along.

"DID YOU SEE THAT?" he hollered up into the dark.

"FIVE STARS," Starfruit hollered back, because she was a star, and she would know.

---

But the night, it turned out, had other plans for the list.

Because once you give one wish away, word travels fast on a hilltop, and the autumn sky was *generous* — soon the **流星** were falling in twos and threes, silver threads stitching across the dark. And one by one, snacks came climbing up the hill, drawn by the glow at the tips of a five-pointed star who, it seemed, was simply *giving wishes away.*

Mochi came puffing up, breathless. "Starfruit! I heard — could I — there's a friend who moved far away and I miss them so, so much—" and before he'd finished, Starfruit caught a falling **流星** and tucked it into Mochi's stretchy hands.

"Wish them close," she said. "Wishes are very good at carrying hugs across long distances. Better than the mail."

Wish number sixty-three, gone.

Then came Sweet Potato in his tiny hardhat, scuffing his feet. "Don't need much," he mumbled. "Just — my little garden patch. The frost's coming early this year. I'd like it to hold on one more week so the last sprouts make it."

Catch — *fwip!* — and a wish landed warm in Sweet Potato's rough little hands. "For the sprouts," said Starfruit. "Tell them to hang in there."

Wish number twenty-eight, gone.

Tangyuan watched all of this with her round face glowing brighter and brighter. "Starfruit," she whispered. "Your list..."

"Shh," said Starfruit, scanning the sky. "I'm working."

---

They came all night, one after another, a whole sleepy parade of snacks climbing the dark hill toward the glow of a giving star. Lychee swept up in a flutter and wished — *darling* — for her stage fright to vanish before the autumn talent show. "I have a *number*," she explained breathlessly. "It involves interpretive dance and three costume changes. I cannot do three costume changes while *terrified.*" Starfruit caught a **流星** and handed it over with a flourish, and Lychee practiced one tiny brave bow right there on the hilltop, and then another, and then a third for luck.

Coconut Jelly came drifting up, hands in his pockets, and wished, *bro, for real,* that the tide pools wouldn't freeze over so the little crabs would have somewhere warm to ride out the cold snap. "They're my homies," he said solemnly. "Can't let the homies freeze." Catch — and gone. Egg Waffle arrived twirling his top hat and wished, with surprising softness under all the showmanship, that his old carnival lights would flicker back to life for one more season, "for the kids, you understand — every kid deserves a Ferris wheel." Even a single, very small cricket hopped up and wished only for "a slightly larger leaf to sing from," which Starfruit declared "the most reasonable wish she'd heard all night," and granted at once.

And with every silver streak that fell, Starfruit caught it, and gave it away.

By the time the sky began to soften at its eastern edge — the faint blush that meant Dòu Jiāng would soon be waking the world — Starfruit looked down at her tiny notebook.

One hundred wishes. Every single one crossed out. Given away.

She had not made a single wish for herself.

"Oh," she said softly, and for a moment her glow dimmed, just a little, the way a star does when a cloud thinks about passing. "I... gave them all away. I climbed all this way to make a hundred wishes, and now I have none left."

---

Tangyuan rolled close and leaned her round warm self against Starfruit's side.

"May we tell you something?" she said. "Something our grandmother tangyuan always says when the bowl gets quiet?"

Starfruit nodded.

"A wish you keep is a wish that stays small," Tangyuan said gently. "A wish you give away gets to *grow up*. It gets legs. It goes and lives in somebody else and becomes real. You didn't lose a hundred wishes tonight, Starfruit. You sent a hundred wishes out into the world to find homes." She gave a soft bounce. "That's the biggest wish of all. You just made it without writing it down."

Starfruit was quiet.

Tangyuan gave her a gentle nudge. "Besides," she added, "the sky isn't *done* with you. Look down, dreamy one. Look down instead of up, just this once."

So Starfruit looked down. And then she felt it — the strangest, warmest thing. All across the dark countryside below, tiny lights were beginning to glow. Sweet Potato's garden patch, safe from frost. Egg Waffle's carnival, flickering awake. A window where Mochi sat writing a letter that felt, suddenly, like it would arrive. Lychee practicing her bow. Pudding, dropping a spoon — *and catching it in slow motion, like a dance.*

A hundred little lights. A hundred wishes, all glowing back up at her from the valley.

"Oh," Starfruit breathed. "*Oh.* They look just like..."

"Like stars," Tangyuan finished, smiling. "Like a whole sky of them. Down on the ground, where you put them."

---

And just then, as the very last **流星** of the season streaked across the paling sky, Tangyuan caught it — *fwip!* — and pressed it into Starfruit's five-pointed hands.

"This one's yours," she said firmly. "We saved it. Family always saves the last bite for someone they love. Go on. Make a wish for *you.*"

Starfruit held the little silver glimmer. She thought, and thought.

Then she smiled, closed her glowing eyes, and whispered her wish.

She never told anyone what it was. But the next morning, when the snacks gathered to thank her, Starfruit was standing exactly one inch taller.

A glamorous inch.

And she swore, hand on her pointiest point, that she hadn't wished for it at all — that the wish had simply *come true on its own*, the way the best ones do, when you spend a whole night giving the rest away.

## Goodnight Blessing
May your wishes be soft tonight, little one, and may you never be afraid to give one away — for a shared wish only shines brighter. May the lights in your window be a sky of their own, and may someone, somewhere, be quietly glowing because of something kind you did today. Tuck in your dreams like stars into the dark, and let the night hold every one. Pound, pound goes the sleepy moon; rest, rest goes your tired head. Sleep now, dear heart — there are a hundred wishes watching over you. Goodnight.
