# Story 60: Mango & the Mango Capital Race

**Main Character:** Mango (芒果)
**Guest Characters:** Passion Fruit
**Setting:** The mango orchards and sunny roads of Yujing, Tainan
**Theme:** Home is the sweetest finish line.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 芒果 (mángguǒ) = mango

---

Now, if you have never seen a mango run, you are in for an absolute treat — and I do mean treat, because Mango is exactly that. She was a round, sun-blushed bundle of orange and gold, with one rosy cheek that always looked like she'd just been laughing at her own joke. She wore a stretchy green sweatband across her stem, and she did not so much walk as *bounce-sprint*, which is what happens when a fruit has too much sunshine packed inside and absolutely refuses to keep it still.

"FINISH LINE!" she hollered, blasting down a warm road in **玉井** (Yùjǐng) — the town in Tainan they call Taiwan's **芒果** (mángguǒ) = mango capital. "Out of my way, slowpokes, I am SPEED in a peel!"

She zoomed past a sleeping cat. The cat did not wake up. She zoomed past a stack of fruit crates. The crates wobbled, gasped, and then thought better of falling.

It was harvest season, and Mango had entered the famous Yujing Sweet-Sprint.

---

Here is the thing about the Sweet-Sprint, in case you'd like to enter one yourself someday. It was the silliest race in all of Tainan. Racers had to dash from the bottom of the orchard hill, weave through the **果園** (guǒyuán) = orchard, loop the old well, and burst across the finish line at the market square — all without dropping a single drop of their precious juice. Mangoes are very juicy. This is a serious sporting challenge.

"On your marks," called the starter, a plump old papaya in a referee's cap. "Get set..."

Mango crouched. Her rosy cheek glowed. Her sweatband tightened all by itself.

"...GO!"

And she was *gone* — a streak of gold through the green, leaving behind only the smell of sunshine and the faint sound of her own bragging fading into the distance. "Eat my dust! Wait — fruit don't have dust — eat my LEAVES!"

The other racers were nowhere close. A dragonfruit tripped over its own spikes. A guava rolled the wrong way entirely and ended up cheering for a fence post. Mango bounced up the hill, around a gnarled old tree, and skidded onto the high orchard path where the sun sat fattest and hottest in the sky.

She was winning. She was *so* winning. She practiced her victory wave for when she crossed the line.

And that's when she heard it.

A tiny, wilty, wobbly little voice.

"...help?"

---

Mango screeched to a stop. Her sweatband slid down over one eye. She pushed it back up and squinted.

There, slumped against a fence post in a puddle of his own shadow, was the smallest mango she had ever seen. He was barely bigger than a marble, green-going-gold, and so sun-wilted that his stem drooped like a tired noodle. His little face was flushed and dizzy.

"Oh no," he mumbled. "I rolled out of my tree to watch the race... and now the sun is so big and I'm so small and I can't find any **樹蔭** (shùyìn) = shade... and everything is spinny..."

Mango glanced down the path. The finish line was *right there*. She could practically taste her victory wave.

She glanced back at the little mango. He had hiccuped, which is what very small wilty mangoes do instead of crying, and somehow it was a thousand times sadder.

Mango sighed the great heroic sigh of someone whose competitive heart has just been outvoted by her kind one.

"Hang on, little dude," she said, scooping him up against her cool, juicy cheek. "Champions don't leave teammates baking on the road. New plan: we find you shade, *then* I win. Probably. Mostly. We'll see."

---

But finding shade in the mango capital at the hottest hour is harder than it sounds, because — well — it's the mango capital, and every tree was very busy being a mango tree and dropping mangoes, which is not relaxing when you ARE a mango.

"What about under that tree?" the little one squeaked.

*PLONK.* A ripe mango fell from it and nearly bonked them both.

"Too plonk-y," said Mango, dashing on.

"What about that big leaf?"

A goat was eating that big leaf. The goat looked at them. They looked at the goat. They did not stay to debate the leaf.

"What about—"

"Okay, new newer plan," Mango said, plopping the little mango safely in the crook of her arm. "When you don't know the road, you ask someone who knows ALL the roads. And I know exactly who that is. Hold on to your stem."

---

She found him by the irrigation canal, where the cool water gurgled between the orchards — a wrinkly, purple, lumpy fellow lounging in an inner tube made of a leaf, sunglasses on, sipping something through a straw made of a hollow reed.

"Passion Fruit!" Mango called. "I need your help!"

Passion Fruit lifted one sunglass lens. Inside his purple shell, his orange seedy pulp sparkled with mischief. "Mango! In the middle of a race! Either you're cheating, or you're up to something noble. Which is *way* more fun. What's the dare?"

"No dare. This little guy is overheating, and I can't find shade fast enough."

Passion Fruit sat up so quickly his inner tube squirted him forward a foot. He peered at the tiny wilty mango, and for one moment all his prankster sparkle went soft and gentle.

"Oof. Yeah. Poor little **芒果**. Okay." He cracked his knuckles, which a passion fruit does not have, but he did it anyway for drama. "Here's a dare for the both of us: get him cool in under one minute. Ready? The canal! Cool water, cool air right above it, and"— he pointed a seedy finger —"the big banyan tree leans right over the bend. Best shade in all Yujing. C'mon, *splash race!*"

---

And oh, what a splash race it was.

Passion Fruit flopped into the canal and floated like a happy purple boat. Mango set the little mango gently onto a broad lily pad and gave it a *push* — and the lily pad sailed down the cool, gurgling water like the world's tiniest, leafiest dragon boat.

"WHEEE!" cried the little mango, his droop already lifting. The cool air kissed his cheeks. His green began blushing back to gold. "It's so... so *cool* down here! And shady! And the water sounds like a lullaby!"

Under the great banyan tree, the three of them came to rest in a pool of perfect, dappled **樹蔭**. Sunlight made coin-shapes on the water. A breeze carried the smell of a hundred ripening mangoes. It was, quite simply, the coziest spot in all of Tainan.

"Better?" Mango asked.

The little mango, no longer wilty, no longer spinny, beamed up at her with the brightest little face. "Way better. You're the best racer ever. You stopped winning to help *me*."

Mango opened her mouth to say something modest, which was not a thing she was good at, when Passion Fruit gasped dramatically and pointed back up the hill.

"Uh, Mango? The race?"

Far in the distance, a determined guava — having finally found its way back from cheering at the fence post — was rolling toward the finish line. The crowd at the market square was roaring.

Mango watched it. Her sweatband twitched. The old competitive fire flickered in her rosy cheek...

...and then she just laughed, big and warm.

"Let 'em have it," she said. "I already won the only race that matters. Look at this little guy's face."

---

But here's the thing about kind hearts in a mango capital: the town *sees*.

Old Auntie Lychee had been picking fruit on the high path and had watched the whole thing — the stopping, the scooping, the splash race, the shade. By the time Mango carried the little mango back toward the market square (in absolutely no hurry now, taking the cool canal road), the whole town had heard.

The papaya referee was waiting at the finish line. So was the guava, looking very confused about being in first place.

"Mango," the referee announced, "you did not finish the Sweet-Sprint."

"Yep," said Mango cheerfully. "Worth it."

"Which is why," the referee went on, lifting a leaf-woven crown, "the town of Yujing is awarding you something we've never given before. The Sweetest Finish Medal. Because in the mango capital, we decided long ago — the sweetest finish line isn't the fastest one. It's the one where everybody gets home safe."

The crowd cheered. The little mango cheered loudest of all, perched on Mango's shoulder, fully un-wilted and glowing gold as a sunset.

Passion Fruit leaned in. "So... does this mean *I* get a medal for the splash-race dare?"

"You get the next mango off the tree," said the referee.

*PLONK.* One landed neatly in Passion Fruit's lap. "Best race ever," he sighed happily.

---

That evening, as the orchards turned pink and gold, Mango and Passion Fruit and the little mango sat together by the banyan tree, juice cooling, hearts full, watching the lanterns of Yujing flicker on one by one.

"You know," said the little mango sleepily, "I always thought being the fastest was the whole point."

Mango pulled him a little closer into the shade. "Nah, kiddo. The point's the people you slow down for. Home's not a place you race *to*. Home's wherever your friends are safe and cozy and full of sunshine." She yawned. "Now *that's* a finish line."

And the three of them watched the stars come out over the sweetest little town in all of Tainan.
