# Story 96: Mochi & the Moonbeam Invitations

**Main Character:** Mochi (Mochi)
**Guest Characters:** Yù Tù (Moon Rabbit), Willa Wobble
**Setting:** A moonlit rooftop where moonbeams carry messages across Taiwan
**Theme:** Stick together, no matter how far apart.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 月光 (yuèguāng) = moonlight

---

Mochi had a problem, and it was a sticky one. (Most of Mochi's problems were sticky ones, on account of Mochi being a pillowy white blob of stretchy rice who left a little smudge of himself on everything he touched, including doorknobs, friends, and once — memorably — a passing pigeon.)

Tonight the problem sat in his lap as a tall stack of invitations to the Grand Dessert Reunion, the biggest, cuddliest, most enormous get-together the whole island had ever planned. Boba was coming. Taro was coming. Even Shaved Ice was coming, which meant SOMEONE would be shouting in ALL CAPS by dessert.

But three invitations had no place to go.

"The post office says they can't reach them," Mochi whispered, his soft cheeks drooping. "Too far. Too high. Too... away."

He read the three names again. And his whole stretchy body went *floomp* with worry.

Because the rule of Mochi — the only rule he had ever truly believed — was simple: *Stick together.* And how do you stick together with a friend you can't even reach?

So Mochi did what any squishy hero does when the post office gives up.

He climbed onto the roof.

---

The rooftop was cold and quiet, and the whole sleepy town spread out below like a blanket dotted with night-market lanterns. Above him, the moon hung round and creamy as a giant cousin he'd never met.

Mochi sat down with a soft *splat* and sighed up at it. "I just want everyone to come. Even the faraway ones. *Especially* the faraway ones."

"Then you have come to exactly the right roof," said a voice as soft as a closing eyelid.

Mochi spun around — well, he tried to spin, but being a blob, he mostly just sort of jiggled in a circle — and there she was.

A snow-white rabbit with long velvety ears, sitting on a beam of pale light as if it were a comfy bench. Her eyes glowed gentle pink, and around her neck hung a little crescent-moon pendant. In her paws she held a tiny wooden mallet.

"Yù Tù," she whispered, which is the **月兔** (Yuè Tù) = Moon Rabbit, the one who pounds mochi up on the moon every single night. She gave a sleepy little bow. "Pound, pound — sweet dreams abound."

Mochi gasped so hard he nearly inhaled his own collar. "You pound *mochi*? On the *moon*? So we're... we're practically *cousins*?"

"Practically family," Yù Tù agreed warmly. "Now. Why does my faraway cousin look so droopy tonight?"

Mochi held up the three lonely invitations. "I can't reach them. The post can't reach them. And a reunion isn't a reunion if even one friend is left out." His voice wobbled. "Sticking together is the only thing I'm actually good at. But how do you stick to someone who's a whole island away?"

Yù Tù's pink eyes twinkled like dew. She tilted her mallet toward the sky.

"With **月光** (yuèguāng) = moonlight, of course," she said. "Did you not know? Moonbeams are the oldest mail carriers in the world. They touch every rooftop on the island at the very same moment. A message that rides the **月光** can reach anywhere the moon can see."

Mochi's mouth fell open. "Anywhere?"

"Anywhere," said Yù Tù. "But you'll have to stretch."

---

Now, if there was one thing Mochi could do better than stick, it was *stretch.* He could stretch like warm taffy, like a yawn that won't quit, like the very last bit of cheese clinging to a slice of pizza.

"Tell me what to do," he said, rolling up sleeves he did not have.

Yù Tù tapped a moonbeam with her tiny mallet, and it rang out a soft *bong* like a sleepy bell. The beam of light shivered, then went stiff and shimmery — like a slide made of frozen starlight.

"Write your invitation," she said. "Then press it to the beam. The **月光** will carry your words wherever they need to go. But moonbeams are shy creatures. They only carry a message wrapped in something *truly* sticky. Something that won't let go." She winked. "Something rather like you."

Mochi looked down at his own gloopy, squishy, marvelously sticky self.

"Oh," he said. "OH. I am *perfect* for this job."

He stretched out one long pillowy arm, scribbled the first name with a flourish, wrapped the invitation in a thin glistening layer of himself, and *PRESSED* it onto the moonbeam.

*FWOOMP.*

The little glowing parcel zipped up the beam of light and vanished toward the horizon, leaving a faint silver trail and a faint smell of toasted rice.

"It worked!" Mochi bounced so high he bonked the moon. (Gently. The moon did not mind.) "It actually worked!"

"One down," said Yù Tù, pounding her mallet in cheerful rhythm. "Pound, pound."

---

But the second invitation was trickier.

The moment Mochi pressed it to the beam, the moonbeam *fizzled*, sputtered, and went dark — like a candle in a sneeze.

"A cloud," Yù Tù murmured, peering up. "A big sleepy cloud has rolled over the moon. No moonlight, no mail."

Mochi's heart sank into his squishy toes. "So we just... wait?"

"We could," said a brand-new voice, "or we could ask somebody who's *really* good at getting into the sky."

A small round penguin came waddle-wobbling across the rooftop, flippers out for balance, tipping left, tipping right, tipping so far she ought to have fallen over six times but somehow never did. She had a scarf, a determined little face, and the unmistakable bounce of someone who falls down a lot and has decided to enjoy it.

"Willa Wobble!" Mochi cried, delighted. "What are *you* doing all the way up here?"

"Practicing my balance," said Willa, immediately losing her balance and catching it again with a triumphant *wobble-wobble-WHOA-ha!* "I figured the highest roof had the best view. Turns out it also has a moon cousin and a worried dumpling. What's the trouble?"

Mochi explained: the cloud, the dark beam, the friend still waiting for an invitation.

Willa narrowed her eyes at the fat sleepy cloud. "You know what clouds are? Cowards. They scatter if you tickle 'em right." She flapped her stubby flippers. "Mochi, can you stretch me up there?"

"I can stretch *anybody* anywhere," Mochi said proudly, which was almost true.

He grabbed Willa, looped a long stretchy mochi-arm around and around until she was wrapped like a present, and — with a mighty *SPROING* — flung her toward the cloud. Willa wobbled through the air spinning like a fuzzy little comet, flippers windmilling, shouting "WOBBLE FOR VICTORYYY—" and plunged straight into the cloud.

For a moment, nothing.

Then — *poof, poof, fwip!* — the cloud burst apart into a dozen embarrassed little puffs that drifted away to find a quieter sky. And down floated Willa, riding a fresh beam of **月光** like a tiny penguin on a slide, giggling the whole way.

"NAILED the landing," she announced, landing on her bottom. "Eventually."

The moonbeam glowed bright and strong again. Mochi pressed the second invitation to it, and — *FWOOMP* — off it flew.

"Two down," Yù Tù hummed. "Pound, pound."

---

The third invitation, though. The third was for the friend who lived farthest of all — so far that even the moonbeam seemed to squint trying to see that part of the island.

"This one needs the longest stretch of your life," Yù Tù said softly. "Farther than you've ever reached, little cousin. Are you afraid?"

Mochi looked at the impossibly distant dark, where one small friend waited, not knowing they were wanted, not knowing the reunion would feel incomplete without them.

And Mochi smiled, because being afraid and doing it anyway is the bravest, stickiest thing of all.

"Stick together," he said. "No matter how far apart."

He gripped the moonbeam. He took a deep breath that puffed him up like dough. And he *stretched.*

He stretched past the rooftops. Past the tea hills. Past the place where the lanterns turned to fireflies and the fireflies turned to stars. He stretched until he was thin as a moonbeam himself, a silver thread of pure sticky love reaching all the way across the whole sleeping island —

— and he pressed the last invitation gently into the dark.

Far, far away, a tiny window lit up. And a tiny faraway voice — too far to hear, but somehow felt right in the middle of Mochi's gooey heart — said: *Me? You want ME to come?*

Yes, the moonbeam answered. Always. You were never far at all.

Mochi snapped back to the rooftop with a happy *FWUMP*, slightly stretched out of shape, grinning so wide his face nearly went all the way around.

"Three down," whispered Yù Tù, and she pounded her mallet one final time. "Pound, pound — sweet dreams abound."

---

They sat together on the roof, the three of them, watching three silver trails of moonlight curl off across the island like ribbons on a gift.

"Nobody left out," Mochi sighed happily, leaning a sticky shoulder against Willa, who leaned a wobbly shoulder right back. "Everybody coming. Everybody together."

"That," said Yù Tù, gathering her moonbeam to go, "is the whole point of the moon, you know. It's the one thing every faraway friend can look at, at the very same time. Apart, but never alone."

Mochi looked up at her — his moon cousin, his pounding-partner, his proof that family stretches farther than you'd ever think.

"See you at the reunion?" he asked.

"I'll be the bright round face in the sky," she promised, "smiling down on all of you at once."

And she hopped onto a beam of **月光** and slid gently home to the moon.
