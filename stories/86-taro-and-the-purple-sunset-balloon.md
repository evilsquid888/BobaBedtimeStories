# Story 86: Taro & the Purple Sunset Balloon

**Main Character:** Taro (芋頭)
**Guest Characters:** Aiyu
**Setting:** A hot-air-balloon festival over the Taitung valley at sunset
**Theme:** Rise slowly, and you'll see how beautiful patience is.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 熱氣球 (rè qìqiú) = hot-air balloon

---

High in the green hills of Taitung, where the grass rolls like a sleepy cat stretching, the whole valley smelled of warm sugar and burning lamp-oil. It was the evening of the famous **熱氣球** (rè qìqiú) = hot-air balloon festival, and the meadow was speckled with giant puffed balloons in every color, bobbing like a basket of fruit that had decided to float away.

Taro, the chunky dusty-purple fellow with the little round reading glasses, stood at the front of a long line, calmly polishing the lenses on his lavender sleeve. He had waited eleven months for tonight. He did not mind. Taro never minded waiting.

"A proverb," he announced to no one in particular, "says that the slowest river still reaches the sea."

"Did the river TELL you that?" asked a small, wobbly voice behind him. "Or did you just make it up?"

Taro smiled. "Both, usually."

---

Behind him stood Aiyu — the translucent golden-amber jelly cube — shimmering like a square of bottled sunlight, except that she was trembling so hard she was practically a square of bottled earthquake.

"This is my first time," Aiyu whispered. "First time on a — a — I can't even say the big word."

"**熱氣球**," Taro said gently. "Hot-air balloon. Say it slow. The words are like the balloon. They go up better when you don't rush."

"Rè... qìqiú," Aiyu managed, and a tiny proud bubble of confidence rose in her see-through middle. Then she looked up at the basket waiting at the top of the line, and the bubble popped. "It's SO high, Taro. What if I wobble out? What if I melt from nervousness and drip on a sheep? There are sheep down there! I counted nine!"

"Then nine sheep will have a very sweet evening," Taro said, and adjusted his glasses. "Come, kiddo. Up we go. But slowly."

The balloon assigned to them was magnificent — a great round dome the deep purple of a ripe plum, stitched with golden swirls, with a wicker basket cozy enough for two friends and a small picnic. A friendly burner crackled overhead like a campfire that had learned to fly.

Aiyu climbed in. Or rather, she sort of poured herself in, jiggling, and then clamped both little hands onto the basket rim so tightly she left two clear handprints.

"I will not look down," she announced. "I have decided. I am a jelly who only looks UP. Forever."

"That sounds tiring," said Taro.

"It is ALREADY tiring," Aiyu admitted.

---

The balloon-keeper — a calm old fellow with a peanut-dust apron — tugged a rope, and the burner gave a soft, golden *whoomph*. The great purple dome filled and swelled like a yawning belly.

And then, ever so gently, the basket lifted off the grass.

Aiyu screamed. It was a very small scream, the kind a teacup might make, but a scream all the same. "WE'RE LEAVING THE GROUND THE GROUND IS LEAVING US GOODBYE GROUND—"

"Aiyu," said Taro, in his slow proverb-voice, the one that sounded like warm soup poured over a cold day. "Look at my finger."

She peeked. He was holding up one chunky purple finger, pointing at the grass below.

"Watch the grass," he said. "Is it rushing away?"

Aiyu forced herself to look. The meadow was sinking beneath them, yes — but slowly. So slowly. A blade of grass slid out of view the way a thought slides out of your head right before a nap. Gently. Without any fuss at all.

"It's... not rushing," she said.

"That's the secret of a **熱氣球**," Taro said. "An airplane shoves you into the sky. A balloon *invites* you. You don't fall up. You float up. Like a thought rising in a quiet bowl of soup."

"Why is everything soup with you?"

"Because soup is patient," Taro said, with great dignity. "And so am I."

---

Up they rose, slow as honey. And here is the thing Aiyu had not expected: the slowness was the best part.

Because the sun was setting over the Taitung valley, and it was not doing it quickly. The sky began to blush — first a shy pink at the edges, like a mochi who'd been complimented. Then orange, the warm crackly orange of fried things. Then, at last, a deep and glorious purple — the exact same purple as Taro himself, so that for one magical moment it looked as though Taro had simply unrolled across the whole sky.

"Taro," Aiyu breathed. "The sky matches your *face*."

"The sky," said Taro, "has excellent taste."

Other balloons drifted around them like a slow parade of glowing fruit — a red one, a striped one, one shaped like a chubby panda, and one that was very clearly just an enormous floating dumpling with a face. Each balloon glowed from within whenever its burner flared, so the whole purple sky filled with floating golden lanterns.

Aiyu had completely forgotten to hold the rim. Both hands were now pressed to her cheeks instead. "I didn't know patience looked like *this*," she whispered.

"That's the whole proverb, kiddo," Taro said softly. "Rise slowly, and you'll see how beautiful patience is. Rush, and you'd already be at the top, with nothing left to look at."

Far below, the nine sheep had become nine tiny white dots. The night market at the valley's edge had begun to twinkle on, stall by stall, like someone gently waking up a string of fireflies. A river caught the last purple light and lay across the land like a ribbon of plum syrup.

"**美** (měi) = beautiful," Aiyu murmured, the word she'd been saving. "It's so **美**."

"It is," Taro agreed. "And we only saw it because we didn't hurry."

---

Now, every good balloon ride has one small wobble, and theirs came just then.

A gust of mountain wind huffed across the valley — a sleepy, harmless huff, but enough to tilt the basket, *just so*. The picnic basket slid. A single mooncake — Taro had packed snacks, naturally — rolled toward the edge.

"THE MOONCAKE," Aiyu yelped, and lunged.

"Slow," said Taro, calm as ever. He simply leaned the other way, shifting his chunky purple weight, and the basket settled back to level like a seesaw finding its balance. The mooncake rolled right back into his open hand.

"How did you DO that?" Aiyu gasped.

"Did I rush?"

"...No."

"Did I panic?"

"...No."

"Did I keep my soup level?"

"You don't HAVE soup, Taro."

"I have *inner* soup," Taro said serenely, biting the mooncake in half and offering her the other piece. "Everyone does. The trick is not to spill it."

Aiyu took the mooncake. And — this is true — she laughed, a real laugh, bright as a struck bell, the kind of laugh that only comes out once your fear has wandered off to nap somewhere. She had been so busy being scared that she'd nearly missed being brave. And being brave, it turned out, was much more delicious. Especially with mooncake.

---

At the very top of their climb, the balloon paused. It simply hung there in the violet hush, neither rising nor falling, as if even the wind had stopped to admire the view.

The whole valley lay below in soft purple folds. The lanterns of the other balloons floated all around like patient little moons. And the real moon — pearly and new — had just lifted its chin over the eastern ridge, as if it, too, had taken its time getting dressed.

Aiyu leaned her shimmering golden cheek on the rim, the very rim she'd been so afraid of, and sighed the happiest sigh a jelly has ever sighed.

"I was scared of the whole sky," she said. "And it turned out the whole sky was just... waiting to be gentle with me."

"The sky usually is," said Taro. "If you let it take its time."

They drifted down again, slow as a falling leaf, slow as a closing eye, while the purple deepened to indigo and the first true stars pricked through. The burner gave its soft *whoomph-whoomph*, a heartbeat made of warmth, and the meadow rose up to meet them as kindly as it had let them go.

When the basket finally kissed the grass, Aiyu didn't want to get out.

"Can we do it again?" she asked.

"Tomorrow," Taro promised. "Slowly."

And Aiyu, the jelly who had decided to look only up forever, found that she now liked looking everywhere — up at the lanterns, down at the nine sheep, out at the plum-syrup river, and right beside her, at one patient purple friend who had known all along that the best things in the whole wide sky never, ever rush.

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, the day does not need to hurry, and neither do you. Let your worries float off slow, like balloons rising into a plum-colored sky, growing smaller and softer until you can hardly see them at all. Snuggle deep, breathe slow, and watch the gentle dark fill up with quiet stars. There is nothing to chase tonight — only this warm, drifting, beautiful slowness, carrying you up and up into sleep. Rise slow, dream sweet, and let the whole soft sky be gentle with you. Goodnight.
