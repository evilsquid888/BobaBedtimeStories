# Story 40: Braised Pork Rice & the Humble Bowl

**Main Character:** Lǔ Ròu (滷肉飯) — Braised Pork Rice
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A tiny family rice shop in old Tainan
**Theme:** Simple, done with love, outshines fancy.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 滷肉飯 (lǔ ròu fàn) = braised pork rice

---

Lǔ Ròu had a job, and the job was this: be warm.

Every single night, in a tiny family rice shop on a crooked little lane in old **台南** (Táinán) = Tainan, Lǔ Ròu sat in his cozy bowl on the counter and waited to make somebody's whole day a little softer. He was a humble fellow — a steaming heap of fluffy rice crowned with glossy, glistening minced pork that shimmered like wet brown velvet, and right on top, balanced like a fancy hat, one perfect **茶葉蛋** (chá yè dàn) = tea egg.

He smelled of five-spice and soy sauce and home.

"Simple things, done with love," Lǔ Ròu liked to say, patting his own warm rim. "That's the whole secret, kiddo. That's the whole show."

And for forty cozy years, that had been the whole show. Until the night the cart arrived.

It rolled up across the lane with a *toot-toot-TOOOOT*, blasting bubblegum music and flashing a thousand tiny lights, and everybody on the street turned to stare.

---

It was a dessert cart. But not a normal one.

This was the **GLITTERAMA DELUXE 3000**, and it was the most ridiculous, dazzling, over-the-top thing Lǔ Ròu had ever seen in his quiet little life.

It had spinning rainbow umbrellas. It had a fountain that squirted whipped cream in the shape of swans. It sold towering sundaes wrapped in edible gold, jellies that changed color when you poked them, and a "Mega Mochi Volcano" that actually went *FWOOSH* and shot harmless pink sparkles into the night sky.

The whole street oooohed.

"STEP RIGHT UP!" bellowed the cart, in a voice like a carnival on roller skates. "WHY EAT BORING WHEN YOU CAN EAT *SPARKLY*?!"

Lǔ Ròu blinked from his little counter.

Boring.

He looked down at himself. Brown rice. Brown pork. A brown egg in a brown sauce. No umbrellas. No swans. No *FWOOSH*. Just... brown.

"Huh," said Lǔ Ròu quietly, and for the first time in forty years, he felt a tiny, unfamiliar crinkle of doubt right in the middle of his rice.

All night, the crowds flocked to the Glitterama. They snapped photos. They squealed. They held up jellies that giggled and sundaes that sang. A little boy got an ice cream so tall he needed a stepladder to reach the top, and a grandpa won a "Spin the Sparkle Wheel" and got showered in candy confetti that smelled faintly of fireworks and joy.

"WHO'S NEXT?!" roared the cart. "DON'T BE *BROWN*! BE *BRILLIANT*!"

Lǔ Ròu winced at the word *brown* as though somebody had flicked him.

Nobody crossed the lane to the tiny rice shop.

Not one single body. Not even the stray cat who usually came to sniff him hopefully at midnight. Tonight the cat had wandered off to bat at the cart's whipped-cream swans instead, and that, somehow, stung most of all.

Lǔ Ròu sat very still in his bowl, his steam curling smaller and smaller, until it was barely a wisp. The tea egg on his head — a sleepy old friend named Dàn Dàn who read fortunes in his own shell-cracks — peered down at him.

"You've gone quiet, friend," said Dàn Dàn. "And you are never quiet. Every crack tells a story. What does yours say tonight?"

"It says I'm dull," mumbled Lǔ Ròu. "It says I'm a brown bowl in a sparkly world. Maybe I should get some umbrellas. Or learn to go *FWOOSH*."

Dàn Dàn tilted thoughtfully. "Hmm. You? Go *fwoosh*?"

Lǔ Ròu tried. He scrunched up everything he had, held his breath, and went—

"...fwoosh," he said. A single grain of rice fell off his shoulder and plopped onto the counter.

"That," said Dàn Dàn gently, "was the saddest fwoosh I have ever heard."

---

So Lǔ Ròu decided to fancy himself up.

If sparkle was what the world wanted, then *sparkle* he would attempt. He had watched the shop's old grandmother cook for years; surely he could improve himself.

First he tried glitter. There wasn't any, so he sprinkled himself with a pinch of white pepper and a confetti of chopped scallion and announced, "Behold! I am now FESTIVE." He mostly looked like a bowl of rice that had rolled through a tiny garden. He sneezed. The scallions flew everywhere, and one landed squarely on Dàn Dàn's forehead like a tiny green hat.

"Festive," said Dàn Dàn flatly, peeling it off. "Truly. I have never felt such a party."

Then he tried *colorful*. The Glitterama had jellies that changed color, so Lǔ Ròu wobbled over to a forgotten bottle of beet juice the grandmother used for pickles, gave himself a brave little splash, and turned a startling, blotchy pink.

"DAZZLING!" he declared.

"You look," said Dàn Dàn, "like a bowl of rice that lost a fight with a radish."

He tried *musical* — humming a jingle and rattling his chopsticks like a drum kit. He tried *mysterious* — hiding under a napkin and going "ooooOOOoo." He even tried having a *catchphrase with more pizzazz*, practicing "Simple things... done with EXTREME love!!" until his voice cracked.

None of it felt right. None of it felt like *him*.

Next he tried *tall*. The Glitterama had towers, so Lǔ Ròu carefully, wobblingly stacked himself higher and higher — rice on rice on rice — until he was a teetering brown skyscraper with a tea egg dangerously near the ceiling.

"Look at me, kiddo!" he wheezed. "I'm MAJESTIC! I'm—"

He toppled. *Splat.* Rice avalanche. Dàn Dàn rolled clean across the counter and came to rest in the soy-sauce dish, looking deeply unimpressed.

"Every crack tells a story," Dàn Dàn said, dripping. "And this story is called *Why We Do Not Stack Ourselves.*"

Lǔ Ròu gathered his rice back into his bowl, one warm grain at a time. He felt sillier than ever. And tired. And, somehow, even more brown than before.

"I just don't know how to be exciting," he whispered.

And that was the exact moment the door creaked open.

---

In stumbled a traveler.

She was a young woman with a heavy backpack and rain on her shoulders and the kind of tiredness that lives deep in the bones after a very, very long day. She had walked all the way across the city. Her shoes were soaked. Her stomach was rumbling like distant thunder. Her face was the face of someone who has had Too Much Day.

She had passed the Glitterama Deluxe 3000 on her way in. Lǔ Ròu had seen her pause there, under all those swirling lights.

But she hadn't stopped.

She'd come *here*, to the tiny crooked shop with the steamed-up window and the single flickering lantern. She slid onto the worn wooden stool, set down her heavy bag with a sigh that seemed to empty her all the way out, and looked up.

"One bowl of **滷肉飯** (lǔ ròu fàn) = braised pork rice, please," she said softly. "I've been dreaming about it all day."

Lǔ Ròu froze.

Her? But there were *swan fountains* across the street. There was a singing sundae. There was actual edible gold!

"Are — are you sure?" he found himself asking. "I'm not very... sparkly. I don't go *fwoosh*. I'm just rice and pork and one slightly cracked egg."

The traveler smiled, and it was the tiredest, warmest smile in the world.

"That," she said, "is exactly what I want."

So the grandmother scooped him fresh and hot. And Lǔ Ròu, even though he felt small and brown and ordinary, did the only thing he knew how to do.

He was warm.

He let his steam rise up and curl gently around the traveler's cold cheeks like a soft hello. He let his five-spice fragrance wrap around her like a blanket fresh from the dryer. He let the soft rice be soft, and the glossy pork be rich and savory and deeply, achingly comforting, and the tea egg be a small perfect surprise. He didn't sparkle. He didn't go *fwoosh*. He just gave her everything he had, which was warmth, and patience, and forty quiet years of being made the same loving way every single night.

He didn't even have his pepper-confetti or his pink beet blotches anymore — the grandmother had scooped him fresh and plain and exactly, ordinarily himself.

And that, it turned out, was the most important part.

The traveler took one bite.

And she closed her eyes.

And a tear — a happy one, the warmest kind — slid right down her cheek and went *plip* into the bowl.

"Oh," she whispered. "Oh, this tastes like *home*."

---

Lǔ Ròu didn't move. He almost didn't breathe.

"It's been such a long day," the traveler said quietly, eating slow, savoring every spoonful. "I took the wrong bus. Twice. I lost my umbrella. I got rained on. I missed my train and I had to walk and walk and walk, and the whole time my feet hurt and my heart felt a little bit lost." She took another warm bite and sighed. "Everything out there is so loud. So bright. So much." She glanced at the window, where the Glitterama's lights still flashed and spun. "But I didn't want loud. I wanted... this. Something simple. Something warm. Something that feels like somebody, somewhere, made it just for me."

Lǔ Ròu felt his whole self glow, right down to the bottom grain.

Up on his head, Dàn Dàn — back from the soy dish and only slightly salty about it — chuckled softly. "Every crack tells a story, friend. And do you know what yours just told? It said the world has plenty of *fwoosh*. What the world runs out of, at the end of a long day, is comfort."

The traveler ate the whole bowl. She scraped up the very last shiny grain. Then she sat for a while with her hands wrapped around the warm empty bowl, the way you'd warm them on a winter morning, and she let out a long, slow breath — and this time it wasn't an empty sigh. This time it was a *full* one.

"Thank you," she said to the little bowl, the way nobody had said thank you to a sundae all night. "I needed you exactly the way you are."

Across the lane, the Glitterama Deluxe 3000 went *FWOOSH* one last time, ran out of sparkles, and rolled away to find a louder street.

And Lǔ Ròu? Lǔ Ròu sat in his cozy shop, steam curling happy and high again, and he understood something he had almost forgotten on a doubtful, glittery night.

Anyone can be loud. Anyone can sparkle for a moment.

But to be the warm, simple thing that someone dreams about all day — the thing that tastes like home — *that* was a kind of magic no umbrella, no swan, no *fwoosh* could ever beat.

"Simple things, done with love," Lǔ Ròu murmured, settling cozily into his bowl as the rain pattered soft on the window. "That's the whole secret, kiddo."

And it was.

## Goodnight Blessing
May your day end the way the traveler's did — soft, and slow, and warm right down to your toes. You don't have to sparkle to be wonderful, little one; you only have to be exactly, beautifully yourself. Somewhere out there, the world is hungry for the gentle, honest comfort that only you can give. So rest now, snug as rice in a warm bowl, blessed and whole and quietly, perfectly enough. Let your steam settle, let your worries cool, and let the lantern light guide you down into dreams. Goodnight.
