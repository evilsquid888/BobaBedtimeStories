# Story 66: Red Bean & the First Warm Day of Spring

**Main Character:** Red Bean (紅豆)
**Guest Characters:** Mochi
**Setting:** A countryside cherry-blossom hillside in Wuling as winter ends
**Theme:** After every cold spell, warmth comes cooking back.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 春天 (chūntiān) = spring

---

On the very last cold morning of winter, high up on the Wuling hillside, a small burgundy bean with a single white stripe down her middle climbed out of bed, put on her apron, lit her stove, and announced to the entire frosty valley:

"Right then. Everybody UP. It's nearly **春天** (chūntiān) = spring, and I refuse — *refuse* — to let it arrive before breakfast."

This was Red Bean, the warmest, nurturing-est, ladle-wavingest grandma-bean on the whole mountain. She had a ladle as long as her arm and a voice as cozy as a quilt fresh from the dryer.

The trouble was, nobody answered.

Not the crickets (asleep). Not the frogs (snoring). Not even the cherry trees, which stood along the hill in long sleepy rows, bare and grey, wrapped in the last thin shawl of frost like a hundred old men who had stayed up far too late.

Red Bean planted both hands on her round little hips.

"Hmph," she said. "Sleepyheads, the lot of you."

So she did what she always did when the world needed a nudge. She started to cook.

She filled her big copper pot with mountain spring water, so clear you could see clouds floating in it. She poured in a stream of glossy red beans — her cousins, her aunties, her countless rolling relatives — and a knob of brown sugar that melted with a contented sigh. Then she stirred, slow and round and patient, the way you stir a thing you love.

*Glug. Bubble. Glug.*

And the most wonderful smell began to rise off the pot — sweet and toasty and round, a smell like a hug you can breathe. It drifted down the frosty rows of cherry trees. It curled under doors. It tiptoed into burrows.

And one by one, all over the hillside, the sleeping snacks of winter began to twitch their noses.

---

The first to stir was a soft white blob who had been buried in a snowbank since November, stretched flat as a pancake and frozen stiff as a doorknob.

"Mmmmnnnff," said the blob.

It was Mochi.

He had gone to sleep back when the first snow fell — "I'll just have a little winter nap," he had said, which is exactly the sort of thing that turns into a four-month nap if nobody stops you — and now here he was, half an icicle, blinking his sleepy black-dot eyes at the frosty sky.

"Is it morning?" Mochi mumbled. "Is it Tuesday? Did I miss snacks?"

"You missed *winter*, dear," said Red Bean, hurrying over with her ladle. "All of it. Now come on, up you get."

But Mochi could not get up. He was frozen so solid that when Red Bean tugged his arm, it went *boing* and stretched all the way across the hill and back, and Mochi did not move one inch.

"Oh dear," he said, still flat in the snow. "I think my insides are still asleep."

Red Bean nodded wisely. Beans understand this. Beans know all about being hard and cold and needing time and warmth to soften into your best, sweetest self.

"Don't you worry," she said. "I know exactly the cure. **慢慢來** (màn man lái) = take it slow."

She fetched a bowlful of her warm red bean soup and set it steaming beside Mochi's frozen ear.

*Sniff,* went Mochi.

*Sniiiiiff,* went Mochi.

And then — *gloop* — the most marvelous thing happened. The sweet warm steam settled over him like a blanket, and slowly, slowly, from his middle outward, Mochi began to thaw. His corners went from icicle-stiff to pillow-soft. His frozen frown melted into a gummy grin. And with a happy stretch he sat up, gave a tremendous yawn, and bounced — *boing!* — a full three feet into the air.

"WOO!" cried Mochi. "I'm SOFT again! Stick together, everybody — it's spring!"

"Not yet it isn't," said Red Bean, dabbing his chin like a fussing grandma. "Half the hill's still snoring. We've got coaxing to do, you and I."

---

So off they went, the warm bean and the bouncy blob, the copper pot of soup wobbling between them, up and down the frosty rows of cherry trees, knocking gently on every burrow and hollow and frost-crusted door.

It was, Red Bean explained, a very particular sort of work.

"You can't *yell* a snack awake," she said. "That just gives everybody a fright and a grumpy face. You have to wake them the way the sun does. Soft. Warm. From the inside out."

"From the inside out," Mochi repeated thoughtfully, bouncing along beside her. "Like soup. Like me. Like everything warm." He chewed on this for a moment. "Red Bean, were you ever frozen stiff like I was?"

"Oh, kiddo," said Red Bean with a soft laugh, "every bean starts out hard as a pebble. You could knock me on a table and I'd go *clack*. It's only the long slow warming that turns a bean sweet. That's the whole secret of us. Never rush a bean. Never rush a spring."

Their first stop was a curled-up cluster of crickets, frozen mid-snore inside a hollow log, looking like a tangle of brown commas.

Mochi, being new to all this, leaned right into the log and bellowed, "GOOD MORNING!"

The crickets did not wake. But the log fell over, and Mochi tumbled head over heels down the hill and landed — *splork!* — in a snowdrift shaped exactly like himself.

"Less yelling," called Red Bean kindly. "More soup."

She ladled a little warmth into the log instead, and the toasty steam drifted in, and the crickets uncurled one leg at a time, blinking, and began — very quietly, very politely — to tune their tiny fiddles for spring.

Next they found a family of sleepy frogs frozen at the bottom of a pond, stacked like green dumplings in a steamer. Mochi very nearly bellowed again, caught Red Bean's eye, and instead whispered, "...good morning?"

Red Bean warmed the pond's edge with her pot. The ice went soft, then slushy, then gone — and up popped the frogs, one *blorp* at a time, rubbing their eyes and croaking the first slow notes of the year.

On they went. They warmed a hedgehog who had rolled himself into such a tight ball he'd forgotten which end was his nose; he uncurled with a tiny *pop*, sniffed the soup, and curled right back up — but this time with a happy smile and one paw sticking out for seconds. They warmed a whole orchestra of bees, who came buzzing out of their hive in fuzzy striped pajamas, yawning honey-yawns and bumping gently into each other like fuzzy little bumper cars. They warmed a tortoise who took the news that winter was over with great calm, blinked twice, and announced — very, very slowly — that he would be ready to celebrate in roughly an hour, possibly two, and would somebody kindly save him a bowl.

"He always says that," Red Bean whispered to Mochi with a fond chuckle. "Every single spring. And every single spring he turns up just in time for the last ladleful."

By now Mochi had gotten the hang of gentle waking, and he was rather proud of himself. He tucked the crickets back into their log when they shivered. He bounced softly — *boing, boing, boing* — to fan the warm steam toward the burrows that were hardest to reach. He even hugged a very grumpy mole who didn't *want* to wake up, holding on softly and warmly until the mole stopped grumbling and started, despite himself, to hum.

"You're a natural, kiddo," said Red Bean, and Mochi glowed pinker than a blossom.

And everywhere Red Bean went, she said the same warm thing.

"After every cold spell, kiddo, the warmth comes cooking right back. It always does. That's a promise the mountain keeps."

---

By late afternoon, the whole hillside was awake — crickets fiddling, frogs croaking, bees humming, the tortoise still getting ready — and yet, Red Bean noticed with a small frown, the cherry trees themselves had not stirred at all.

They stood in their long grey rows, still bare, still wrapped in frost, fast asleep on their feet.

"They're the biggest sleepyheads of all," Mochi whispered. "How do we warm a whole *tree*?"

Red Bean looked at her copper pot. It was nearly empty. One ladleful left.

She looked at the long, long rows of trees marching up the hill. Far too many for one little pot.

And then Red Bean smiled the smile of a grandma who has solved harder problems than this before breakfast.

"We don't warm them one at a time, dear," she said. "We warm them *together*. **團圓** (tuányuán) = gather as one."

She called out across the hillside — to the crickets, the frogs, the fuzzy bees, the not-quite-ready tortoise, the bouncing Mochi — and asked every newly woken creature to come close and lend their warmth. The crickets fiddled a quick warm tune. The frogs piled up like green hot-water bottles around the roots. The bees fanned their fuzzy wings. Mochi hugged each trunk, soft and warm, the way only a thawed mochi can. And Red Bean tipped her very last ladle of sweet steam high into the branches.

All that gathered warmth rose up together — one big cozy breath of spring.

And the cherry trees *woke up*.

It started as a single pink dot on the lowest branch. Then another. Then a hundred. Then a thousand, thousand more — *pop, pop, poppoppop!* — until the whole grey hillside burst into clouds of soft pink blossom, the way a smile spreads across a sleepy face. Petals drifted down like the gentlest pink snow, the warm kind, the kind that lands on your nose and makes you giggle instead of shiver.

"It's SPRING!" cried Mochi, bouncing so high he disappeared into the blossoms and came down wearing a pink-petal hat. "We did it! 春天 春天 SPRING!"

Red Bean stood in the falling petals, her copper pot empty, her apron dusted pink, her warm little heart fuller than any pot of soup.

"See, kiddo?" she said softly, watching the whole woken hillside fiddle and croak and hum and bloom. "Simple things, done with love. That's all it ever takes. After every cold spell — *every* one — the warmth comes cooking back."

And then, because that is exactly what grandmas do, she refilled her pot, set it bubbling under the biggest cherry tree, and served warm red bean soup to the entire hillside until the sun went down pink and golden behind the mountains. The crickets fiddled. The bees hummed lullabies. And Mochi, soft and happy and full of soup, fell fast asleep in Red Bean's lap under the blossoms — for a nap this time, just a *little* one.

"Sweet dreams, all my sleepyheads," whispered Red Bean. "Spring will still be here when you wake."

## Goodnight Blessing
