# Story 100: The Grand Island Reunion

**Main Character:** Boba (波霸 bōbà) — Tapioca Pearl
**Guest Characters:** The entire cast — desserts, savory squad, folklore friends, and the penguin and Piggle-Wiggle crossover guests
**Setting:** An island-wide festival joining every night market in Taiwan into one glowing chain of light
**Theme:** A hundred stories, one big delicious family.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 團圓 (tuányuán) = reunion / coming together

---

Boba woke up bouncing.

She didn't mean to. She simply opened her glossy black eyes, remembered what day it was, and *boing* — she shot straight off her little syrup pillow and bonked her round head on the lantern hanging above her bed.

"Ow," she said happily. "Worth it."

Because today was THE day. Not a day. *The* day. The day every single friend from every single story — all one hundred of them — would finally come together in one place at one time for the biggest, brightest, most enormous feast the island of **台灣** (Táiwān) = Taiwan had ever seen.

They were calling it the Grand Island Reunion. The **團圓** (tuányuán) = reunion. The coming-together.

And somebody had to make sure it actually happened.

Boba looked at the long, long list rolled out across her floor. It was so long it went out the door, down the stairs, and — she was fairly sure — into next week.

"Okay," she said, cracking her knuckles, which is impressive for a sphere with no fingers. "Let's bounce."

---

The plan was bonkers, which is the best kind of plan.

Every night market in Taiwan — Shilin, Raohe, Liuhe, Fengjia, the little ones with no names and the big ones with too many — would light up at the very same moment, and a glowing ribbon of lanterns would connect them all, market to market, market to market, until the whole island wore a necklace of light you could probably see from the moon.

Speaking of the moon — that was Boba's first problem.

"We can't have a reunion without **玉兔** (Yù Tù) = the Moon Rabbit," she said, hopping up onto the roof of Diàn Diàn's all-night convenience store. "But she lives on the *moon.* That's, like, very far. Even for me."

"Open all night, here for you!" beeped Diàn Diàn cheerfully, the little mochi clerk glowing softly in his mini vest. "I sent the invitation up on a moonbeam three nights ago. Beep. Delivery confirmed."

And right on cue, a silvery beam slid down out of the dark like a slide at a playground, and down it hopped the snow-white rabbit herself, mallet over her shoulder, crescent pendant swinging.

"Pound, pound — sweet dreams abound," Yù Tù whispered, landing soft as a snowflake. "I brought enough fresh mochi for everyone. I pounded all afternoon. My arms are *very* tired and *very* proud."

Behind her, sliding down the same beam with significantly less grace, came four penguins on snowboards.

"WIPEOUT!" hollered Piper Paddlefoot, crashing into a tower of paper cups.

"Technically," said Barnaby Beaksworth, adjusting absolutely nothing because penguins don't wear glasses, "a moonbeam is not a regulation halfpipe."

"Wheeee," said Willa Wobble, who had ridden the whole way down backward and upside-down and was thrilled about it.

"I'm okay! I'm okay!" announced Finnegan Flipsworth from inside the cup tower. "I am, in fact, *cups* now. But I'm okay."

"Beep. Welcome to the island," said Diàn Diàn. "We have hand-warmers in aisle two."

Boba checked her list and crossed off *Moon Rabbit (arrived)* and *Penguins (arrived, slightly crashed)*. Then she frowned at the next line.

"Zongzi is bringing everyone from the mountains and the coast by river," she said. "But the rivers run *down* to the sea, not *across* the island. How is a sticky rice dumpling going to get a mountain tea leaf and a beach coconut to the *middle?*"

As if to answer, there came a tremendous splash from the canal, and up popped Zòng Zi the rice dumpling, bobbing proudly in his green bamboo leaves, towing a whole flotilla of friends behind him on lily-pad rafts.

"Wrapped tight, ready to ride!" Zòng Zi sang out. Behind him floated Ā-Lǐ the curled jade tea leaf in her little bamboo hat ("Steep slow, breathe deep," she murmured, releasing a swirl of orchid-scented mist), Coconut Jelly hanging ten on a coconut shell ("Sweet ride, bro"), and Sweet Potato in his tiny hardhat, looking faintly seasick.

"Next time," groaned Sweet Potato, "we take the train."

"There IS no train across water, dude," said Coconut Jelly.

"That's my *point.*"

---

By sundown, the markets were *roaring* to life.

At the savory end of things, Niú Niú the beef noodle bowl was directing traffic with great sweeping tosses of his noodle scarf, his steam-halo puffing like a friendly little weather system.

"Easy now, kiddo, easy," he rumbled to a frantic Pudding, who was carrying a tray of teacups and wobbling so hard the tea was practically applauding. "Slurp first, worry later. The night's young and the broth is warm."

"I'm trying to be calm!" wailed Pudding. "But there are SO many people and I'm made of *jiggle!*"

"Come on in — I'll fold you a hug," said Guā Bāo, the soft pork-belly bun, gently folding her pillowy self right around the trembling flan until only his caramel top peeked out. "There. Snug as a peanut."

Across the way, the popcorn chicken crew — Kǎ Kǎ, all forty of them — were supposed to be setting tables but had instead organized themselves into a giggling, bouncing pyramid.

"More crunch—"
"—more fun—"
"—more PYRAMID!" they chorused, before the whole tower collapsed into a heap of basil-hatted laughter.

Xiǎo Lóng the soup dumpling floated past on his bamboo steamer, holding very, *very* level. "Careful — I'm full of feelings... and soup," he announced to no one in particular. "Eighteen pleats. I counted them twice. We are going to have a *lovely* evening provided nobody jostles me."

A penguin immediately jostled him.

"...Seventeen, eighteen," Xiǎo Lóng breathed, recounting his pleats with the focus of a tightrope walker. "We're fine. Everything's fine."

---

The sweet end of the markets was, frankly, chaos — the good kind.

SHAVED ICE had appointed himself Director of Decorations and was bellowing orders from atop a fruit cart. "MORE LANTERNS! TALLER! TALLER! I WANT THIS VISIBLE FROM SPACE! ALSO I'M MELTING, SOMEONE BRING A FAN, THIS IS BOTH AN ARTISTIC AND A PERSONAL EMERGENCY!"

Hēi Táng the brown-sugar syrup swirled across an enormous paper banner, painting golden tiger-stripe loops that spelled out, in glistening caramel, a single word: **團圓**.

"I make everything sweeter," Hēi Táng sighed, admiring his own dripping handiwork. "Including spelling."

Taro adjusted his reading glasses and read the banner aloud. "Reunion. A fine word. As the old saying goes: *a single pearl is a snack, but a hundred pearls are a family.*"

"You just made that up," said Mango, jogging in place with her sweatband on.

"All the best proverbs are made up by someone," said Taro serenely. "That's how they start."

And then — because no reunion is complete without him — there came the soft scent of incense and tangerines, and out from behind the rice stand toddled a tiny grandfather no taller than a teapot, in a little red temple hat.

"**土地公**," everyone breathed. The Earth God.

"Bless this little corner of the island," chuckled Tǔdì Gōng, scattering lucky candies as he walked. "And this corner. And that one. And — oh my, what a *lot* of corners we have tonight." Behind his golden robe, the plump painted clay tiger Xiǎo Hǔ Yé padded along, yawning enormously.

"Rawr means hello!" yawned the tiger, flopping belly-up in the warm lantern light. "Rawr also means I would like a nap and possibly some pork."

"After the feast, little one," laughed the Earth God. "After the feast."

Meanwhile, Dàn Dàn the tea egg had wandered to the very center of the markets and was studying the cracks on his own marbled shell, turning slowly in the lantern light.

"Every crack tells a story," he murmured. "And look — tonight my shell has exactly one hundred lines on it. One for each tale. They all began separately... but see how they branch and cross and tangle together until you can't tell where one ends and the next begins?" He smiled his gentle, philosophical smile. "That's not a crack anymore. That's a *family tree.*"

Nearby, the Lucky Cat waved her little paw, the radish cake Luóbo Gāo organized the seating chart with crisp efficiency ("Crispy edges, soft heart — and everyone gets a chair"), and Hú Jiāo the pepper bun hopped out of a clay oven with such excitement that he sneezed a cloud of pepper directly into the air.

"Fresh from the fire, full of—*ACHOO!*—heart!" he beamed, while everyone within three tables sneezed in sympathy.

"Bless you," said the Earth God.
"Bless *you,*" said Hú Jiāo.
"That's my job," chuckled the Earth God, "but thank you."

---

But as the sky went purple, then deep blue, then velvet black, Boba began to worry.

She bounced to the top of the tallest lantern pole and looked out over the whole island. From up here she could see it all: every market, every friend, every glowing thread of light waiting to be switched on. Beef noodle and bubble tea. Mooncake and mochi. Tea eggs and tiger gods. Penguins and pork buns and one slightly damp pudding.

A hundred stories' worth of friends. All here. All real.

And Boba — the little pearl who'd once been too scared to leave her cup — felt her throat go all tight and sweet, like syrup left out in the sun.

"What's the matter, darling?" drawled Lychee, draping herself dramatically over the railing beside her. "You look like someone melted your dumpling."

"It's just..." Boba sniffled. "It's just that I started all alone. At the bottom of a cup. And now look. *Look.*" She gestured at the whole shining island. "How did this *happen?*"

Lychee was quiet for once. Then Douhua wobbled gently up the pole, silky and calm. "It happened," the tofu pudding said softly, "one story at a time. You didn't make a family in a single night, little pearl. You made one a hundred nights in a row. **團圓** isn't a thing that happens. It's a thing you *practice* — until one evening you look up, and there everyone is."

Mochi stretched all the way up the lantern pole to join them, wrapping his pillowy white arms around both Boba and Lychee at once. "Stick together!" he said warmly, which, being mochi, he was extremely good at. "That's the whole secret. There isn't a fancier one. I checked."

"Darling, you're getting me *sticky,*" complained Lychee, who did not pull away even a little.

Tangyuan the round rice ball rolled up next, jolly as ever. "We did it," he said — because Tangyuan always said *we.* "We started small, and we got round, and now we're a whole family soup. That's what we round folk do best."

From far below, a familiar warm voice called up: "Boba! Kiddo! It's time! Everyone's waiting on you to give the word!"

Boba wiped her eyes, took a deep breath, and looked out at every face turned up toward her — sweet and savory, fruit and folklore, old friend and new, penguin and pig and rabbit and god.

"Together?" she called down.

"TOGETHER!" the whole island roared back.

"Then let's bounce!"

She slapped the great lantern switch.

---

And oh, the *light.*

It started at Shilin and rippled outward — market to market to market — a wave of gold and red and rainbow racing across the dark island until the whole of Taiwan glowed like one enormous, joyful cup of milk tea lit from within. The lanterns rose. The steam curled up. Boba's hundred friends threw back their heads and *cheered*, a sound so big and so warm it probably tickled the toes of the Moon Rabbit's cousins up on the moon.

Then they feasted.

They feasted until Pái Pái declared the food "bigger than your face *and* bigger than your worries." Until Cōng Cōng cartwheeled across forty tables yelling "Let's flip the script!" and landed, miraculously, in a bowl of soy milk beside a delighted Yóu Tiáo ("Dunk me, I'm ready!"). Until Chòu Chòu won the "Bold on the Outside, Gold on the Inside" award by a landslide, his friendly purple sparkle-cloud puffing with pride. Until the Earth God blessed every single corner twice, just to be safe, and Mooncake glowed softly and whispered, "Round like the moon, full of family."

And Boba — small, round, glossy Boba — sat in the very middle of it all, in the warm sweet broth of the family she'd somehow, story by story, helped to make.

A hundred stories.

One big, delicious family.

**團圓.**

## Goodnight Blessing
Sweet dreamer, you've come all the way to the hundredth story — and look how far you've traveled with us, from the bottom of one little cup to the top of a whole glowing island. Tonight, every friend you've ever met in these pages is together at last: the brave and the shy, the sweet and the savory, the silly penguins and the sleepy tiger and the kindly old Earth God scattering candy in the dark. They are all holding hands in the lantern light, and they are all whispering the same soft thing to you. You belong at this table. You always did. So close your eyes now, breathe slow, and let the warm island glow fold around you like a hug made of mist. The lanterns are dimming, the friends are yawning, and somewhere a hundred happy hearts are beating gently in time. Rest now, dear one — you are part of the family too. Goodnight.
