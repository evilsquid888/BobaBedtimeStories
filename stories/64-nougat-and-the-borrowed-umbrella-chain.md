# Story 64: Nougat & the Borrowed-Umbrella Chain

**Main Character:** Niúgá Táng (Milk Nougat) — a chewy, milky-white candy studded with nuts, cheerful and a bit forgetful
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A rainy shopping street with a long covered arcade in Jiufen
**Theme:** A small kindness passed on travels farther than one kept
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 雨傘 (yǔsǎn) = umbrella

---

It was raining so hard on Jiufen's old stone street that the puddles were having a meeting about whether to become a lake. The vote was leaning toward *yes*.

And right in the middle of it stood Nougat — a soft, chewy, milky-white little candy bar dotted all over with toasted almonds and cashews, so that he looked rather like a snowman who had rolled through a bowl of nuts and decided he liked it. Normally he was springy and bouncy, the sort of candy who boinged when he walked and left little nut crumbs wherever he went. But rain makes nougat go *sticky*, and right now he was so soggy he made a sound like *splort* every time he took a step. *Splort. Splort. Splort.*

"I knew I forgot something," Nougat sighed, peering up at the gray sky. "I think it was... a roof? No. A hat? No. Oh dear. Was it pants? Do I wear pants?" (He does not. He is a nougat.)

It was, of course, an umbrella. Nougat forgot many things. That was simply Nougat. He once forgot his own birthday and had to be reminded by his own cake. He once went to buy milk and came home with a chair, very pleased, having entirely forgotten the milk.

A raindrop landed on his nose with a tiny *plip*. Then another. Then a hundred of their friends.

"Right," he said, shivering until his almonds rattled. "Time to be brave and soggy."

---

He had not gone three squelchy steps when a voice said, "You'll dissolve out here, you know."

Nougat turned. An old, kindly Pineapple Cake stood beneath the dripping eaves, holding out a small green **雨傘** (yǔsǎn) = umbrella. It was nothing fancy — a little bent, one rib slightly crooked — but it was *dry* underneath, gloriously dry, the kind of dry that feels like a hug.

"For me?" Nougat blinked. "But it's yours!"

"I'm nearly home," said the Pineapple Cake, pressing the handle into Nougat's sticky paw. "Take it. Only one rule." Her crumbly face crinkled into a smile. "**傳下去** (chuán xiàqù) = pass it on — when you can."

"Pass it on? You don't want it back?"

"It's already done its job for me," she said, and she tottered off into a doorway with a little wave, leaving Nougat standing under a dome of dryness, warm and astonished, listening to the rain go *patter-patter* on a roof that was suddenly, wonderfully, his.

"Wait!" Nougat called after her. "How will I find you to give it back?"

"You won't!" she laughed from the doorway, and her voice was already soft with sleep and warmth. "That's not how it works, dear. You don't give it *back*. You give it *forward*." And then she was gone, and the rain swallowed her up, and Nougat was left blinking at a little green umbrella that had been pressed into his life by a stranger who didn't even want a thank-you.

(Between you and me, dear reader, that is the best sort of gift — the kind that comes with a tiny job attached. The job is the gift, really. But Nougat hadn't figured that out yet.)

---

Now here is where Nougat met his Great Temptation.

The umbrella was *so* cozy. The arcade stretched ahead of him, long and lantern-lit and stone-cobbled, the famous covered street of Jiufen with its red **燈籠** (dēnglóng) = lanterns glowing like warm soup against the storm. Tea houses breathed steam into the rainy air. Stalls glistened. And Nougat was finally, deliciously dry. The almonds in his tummy stopped shivering. His sticky toes un-stuck themselves one by one with little *pop, pop, pop* sounds, like the rain was letting go of him.

"I'll pass it on," he told himself firmly. "Definitely. Absolutely. *Eventually.*" He gave the handle a little squeeze. It was warm where the Pineapple Cake's paw had held it. "Maybe once I've dried out completely. Maybe once I've enjoyed it for just a *teeny* tiny —"

The trouble with keeping a kindness, you see, is that it always seems perfectly reasonable. *Just for a little longer. Just until I'm sure I don't need it. Just until a better moment comes along.* And the moments line up and line up, and somehow the better one never quite arrives, and the kindness sits in your pocket going stale.

That was when he heard the sneeze.

It came from a doorway up ahead — a *huge* sneeze, the kind that shakes loose buttons and startles passing pigeons. *AH-CHOO!* Nougat squelched over and found a little Pineapple Bun huddled in the rain, so drenched that her golden crust had gone the sad gray of wet cardboard. She was shivering so hard her crumbs were jiggling, and a small sad puddle was spreading out from underneath her like she was slowly turning into soup.

"Oh no," said Nougat. "You're much wetter than me. You're practically a smoothie."

"I'm f-f-fine," chattered the Bun, in the voice of someone who is extremely not fine. "I'm just w-waiting for the rain to t-take a break." She tried to smile, which is a very hard thing to do when your teeth are clattering like castanets.

Nougat looked at his lovely dry umbrella. He looked at the soggy Bun. And his warm marshmallowy heart did a little flip.

"Here," he said, and held out the **雨傘**. "Pass it on when you can. That's the only rule."

The Bun's eyes went wide as twin egg tarts. "But — you'll get wet again!"

"I'm a nougat," he said cheerfully. "I'm basically a sticky raincoat for *myself*." He tapped his chewy chest, which made a delightful *boing*. "Go on. Stay dry."

And as the Bun took the umbrella, something funny happened to Nougat. He stepped back out into the rain — and he didn't feel cold at all. He felt *toasty*. Warm right down to his almonds. It turns out that giving something away can keep you warmer than holding it. Nobody knows quite how. It just does.

---

Nougat could have walked home then. But he was curious. So he followed, splashing happily, just to see where his little green umbrella would go.

He didn't have to wait long.

The Pineapple Bun, now snug and dry and no longer making smoothie noises, had gone only a little way down the arcade when she came across a Shaved Ice fellow who was melting into a sad sweet puddle at the edge of the road. He was getting smaller by the second, his little syrup hat sliding right off his head.

"Oh, you poor slushy thing!" she cried, and — *just like that*, without a moment's pause — she handed him the umbrella. "Pass it on when you can!"

"But what about you?" the Shaved Ice gasped.

"I've had my dry," she said simply, and skipped off home, her crust crisping right back to gold.

The Shaved Ice, who had been about to become a small flavored pond, gasped with joy and scooped himself back together, hat and all. And a few doorways down, *he* found a Stinky Tofu shivering by a shuttered stall, looking thoroughly miserable, and pressed the handle into his hands. "Pass it on!"

(Even the Stinky Tofu, I am pleased to report, smelled slightly less alarming once he was dry. Slightly. Let's not get carried away.)

Nougat trailed behind the whole parade, hugging himself with glee, watching one small bent **雨傘** hop from snack to snack down the glowing arcade like a stone skipping across a pond. From Tofu to a sniffly Egg Tart whose crust had gone soggy at the edges. From the Egg Tart to a Taro Ball who'd lost his bobble in a gutter and was very sad about it. From the Taro Ball to a family of tiny Tapioca Pearls who all crowded under it together, six of them at once, and squeaked with delight like a basket of happy hiccups.

And every single time, the rule went with it. *Pass it on when you can.* Nobody kept it. Nobody even *wanted* to keep it once they heard those words — because the words turned out to be the best part of the gift, the bit that made you feel like you were part of something long and warm and bigger than one rainy night.

By the time the lanterns were burning their brightest and the sky had gone the deep blue-purple of nighttime, that single umbrella had kept a *dozen* soggy snacks dry. Maybe more. Nougat lost count, which — let's be honest — Nougat does.

"One umbrella," he whispered, watching it dance away into the lantern light. "All those snacks. From one tiny *pass it on.*"

He thought about the old Pineapple Cake, tucked up warm in her doorway somewhere, having no idea that her one small kindness had grown into a whole rainy-night parade. She would never even know. And somehow that made it even nicer — a kindness so big it had outgrown the person who started it, rolling on down the street under its own happy steam.

He felt so full of warmth that he quite forgot he himself was, by now, thoroughly soaked again, dripping from every almond and squelching with every step. He didn't mind even a tiny bit. There are kinds of wet that feel worse than they are, and kinds of warm that have nothing to do with being dry, and Nougat had just discovered the second sort.

---

And here, dear reader, is the part nobody saw coming. Not even Nougat.

He had just turned to squelch homeward through the downpour when a voice behind him said:

"Hey! You look like you could use this."

Nougat spun around.

There stood a small Boba Pearl, round and glossy and dark, beaming up at him, holding out a little green umbrella — *bent in the middle, one rib slightly crooked.* The very same one. It had traveled the whole long arcade, snack to snack to snack to snack, all the way down the street and all the way back up the other side, through a dozen grateful paws and one slightly stinky one... and now it had circled right back to where it began.

To Nougat. The very nougat who had let it go.

"Pass it on when you can!" said the Boba Pearl proudly, clearly thrilled to be saying it.

Nougat's mouth fell open. A little raindrop hopped right in.

"You..." he laughed, taking the handle, "you have *no idea* where this umbrella has been."

"A doorway, I think," said the Boba Pearl helpfully. "A Taro Ball gave it to me. He said a Pineapple Bun gave it to him. Or — no, maybe that was somebody else. There were a *lot* of somebodies." She scrunched up her glossy little face, trying to count them, and gave up, because there had simply been too many to hold in one small pearly head.

"There were," Nougat agreed, his eyes shining. "Oh, there were."

The kindness had gone all the way around the world — well, all the way around the *street*, which to a small nougat is very nearly the same thing — and come home. He had let it go, and it had come back warmer, having kept a dozen friends dry on the way. And the funny thing, the *wonderful* thing, was that if he had simply kept the umbrella for himself at the very start, snug under his cozy little dome, it would have kept exactly one snack dry. Just one. Just him. Instead, by giving it away, he had somehow kept the whole entire street dry — *including himself, twice.* That is the strange and lovely math of kindness, where two minus one somehow equals everybody.

Nougat tipped the little umbrella up over his head. The rain went *patter-patter* on the roof that was, once again, his. And he stood there in the lantern light, dry and chewy and grinning, thinking that the old Pineapple Cake had been exactly right.

A kindness you keep stays small and stops with you.

A kindness you pass on grows legs, and travels the whole rainy street, and finds its own sweet way back.

"Goodnight, little umbrella," Nougat murmured, hugging it close. "Tomorrow... I'll pass you on again."

Then — because he was Nougat — he immediately forgot where he was going, stood happily in the rain a while longer just enjoying being dry, and finally squelched off home humming a little tune, trailing nut crumbs and pure contentment all the way.

## Goodnight Blessing

Sleepy one, the rain is soft tonight, *patter-patter* on the roof above you. Somewhere a little green umbrella is being passed from hand to hand, keeping the whole world dry one kindness at a time. May the small kind thing you do tomorrow grow legs and wander far and find its way back to you, warmer than when it left. You are dry. You are cozy. You are loved. Let the rain hum you to sleep. Goodnight.
