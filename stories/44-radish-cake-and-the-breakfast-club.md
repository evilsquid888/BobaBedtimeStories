# Story 44: Radish Cake & the Breakfast Club

**Main Character:** Luóbo Gāo (Radish Cake)
**Guest Characters:** Dàn Dàn (Tea Egg)
**Setting:** A neighborhood morning griddle stall
**Theme:** Crispy edges, soft heart — be both.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 早餐 (zǎocān) = breakfast

---

Long before the sun rose over the sleepy little island, before even the roosters had finished yawning, a square golden fellow flipped himself off the breakfast griddle with a satisfied *tssss*.

"Morning, everybody!" called Luóbo Gāo, landing neatly on his crispy corners. He was a tidy pan-seared square of soft white radish cake, golden and crackly on the outside, gently jiggly within, speckled with little freckles of dried shrimp and scallion. He smelled exactly like a warm griddle on a cool morning, which is to say: like home.

He looked around the dark, quiet stall and rubbed his crispy hands together.

"This," he announced, "is going to be the smoothest **早餐** (zǎocān) = breakfast rush this neighborhood has *ever* seen. I made a chart."

He had, in fact, made a chart. It was enormous. It had arrows.

"Crispy edges, soft heart," Luóbo Gāo said proudly, patting his chart. "That's our motto. Crispy means *organized*. Soft means *kind*. Today we'll be both."

What he did not know was that the morning had other plans. The morning, you see, was feeling a little silly.

---

The breakfast crew began trickling in, blinking and sleepy.

First came Dòu Jiāng, the warm mug of soy milk, drifting in on a soft curl of steam. "Good morning," she hummed gently. "Sip slow, everyone."

Then bounced Yóu Tiáo, the long golden dough stick, who came as two inseparable strands and spoke, as always, to himself. "We're here! We're ready!" he said. "Dunk me, I'm ready! *Both* of me!"

"Stations, please," said Luóbo Gāo, tapping his chart. "Dòu Jiāng, you're by the warmers. Yóu Tiáo, you're right beside her — dipping duty."

"Beside Dòu Jiāng? Perfect! That's where I dive!" Yóu Tiáo cheered, and immediately did a practice dive into absolutely nothing, landing with a *clonk*. "Nailed it! *Both* of me nailed it!"

"There is no soy milk *yet*, dear," Dòu Jiāng hummed gently. "Wait for the warm."

"I waited! I'm done waiting! I'm diving again!" said Yóu Tiáo, and *clonk*, down he went a second time.

Luóbo Gāo made a tiny note on his chart. The note said: *Keep Yóu Tiáo away from things until breakfast is actually open.* It was, he felt, very good advice.

But here is where the morning's silliness began.

Because it was *so* early, and everyone was *so* sleepy, the breakfast crew couldn't quite read Luóbo Gāo's enormous chart. The arrows, it turned out, were very confusing when your eyes were still half-closed. One arrow pointed up. Another pointed sideways. A third seemed to point at the moon.

So the crew wandered off to find their stations by *guessing*.

And they guessed wrong. Every single one of them.

---

The first sign of trouble was the smell.

A friendly purple sparkle-cloud came puffing into the breakfast stall, followed by an adorable golden cube wearing a heart-shaped cabbage cape.

"Reporting for duty!" said Chòu Chòu, the stinky tofu, beaming with his big puppy eyes. "Bold on the outside, gold on the inside!"

Luóbo Gāo blinked. "Chòu Chòu? Buddy. You're a *night-market* snack. You belong at the *evening* stall. With the sizzly fried things."

Chòu Chòu's purple sparkles drooped. "Oh. The chart said... well, the chart was upside down when I read it."

"It's fine, it's fine," said Luóbo Gāo, keeping his voice steady and his edges crisp. *Soft heart*, he reminded himself. "Just hop on over to—"

But before he could finish, the stall filled up *fast*.

In tumbled Kǎ Kǎ, the giggling crew of popcorn-chicken nuggets, bouncing and reshuffling and finishing each other's sentences. "We heard there's a rush! More crunch—" "—more fun!" "—are we late?" "—are we early?" "—what time is it?"

"You're at the *wrong stall*," said Luóbo Gāo, a little louder now.

Then Pái Pái, the enormous fried chicken cutlet, came striding in, flexing her crunchy edges. "Did somebody say breakfast champion? Bigger than your face, bigger than your worries!"

"Pái Pái, you're *lunch*—"

And then — *boop* — a tiny round mochi clerk in a little vest rolled in, glowing softly. "Open all night, here for you! Wait. Is it day? Beep-boop. I think I took a wrong turn at the rice balls."

That was Diàn Diàn, the convenience-store mochi, who was supposed to be back at the all-night corner store and was *very* much not.

Luóbo Gāo looked around at his beautiful, organized breakfast stall, which was now full of entirely the wrong snacks. Night-market friends everywhere. Lunchtime heroes underfoot. And not a single breakfast crew member where they were supposed to be.

His crispy edges began to feel just a *tiny* bit soggy.

---

"Okay," he said, taking a slow breath that smelled of griddle and morning. "Okay. Don't panic. A radish cake never panics."

He climbed up onto an overturned bowl so everyone could see him.

"FRIENDS!" he called.

Forty-seven snacks turned to look at him. (He counted later. There were forty-seven.)

"There has been," he said carefully, "a small... sleepy... mix-up. You are all at the wrong stalls. And somewhere out there, my *actual* breakfast crew is wandering around looking for *their* stalls — probably at the night market, eating chart arrows by mistake."

He looked at his beautiful chart. The chart looked back, full of arrows, helping no one.

"It's only a mix-up," he told himself, taking another slow griddle-warm breath. "Mix-ups can be un-mixed. A radish cake is *dependable*. A radish cake stays crispy."

But somewhere in the back of his soft jiggly middle, a worry wobbled. *What if the whole street wakes up hungry, and there's no breakfast, and it's all because of my upside-down chart?*

A small, calm voice spoke up from the doorway.

"Every crack tells a story," it said. "And this story is *very* cracked."

It was Dàn Dàn, the tea egg — a marbled brown egg covered in beautiful cracked-tea patterns, like a tiny roadmap of the world. He rolled forward slowly, peering at his own shell.

"Dàn Dàn!" said Luóbo Gāo, brightening. "You! You read crackle-lines like maps! Can you read where everyone's supposed to be?"

Dàn Dàn tilted, studying the brown lines swirling across his shell. "Hmm. This crack here looks like the soy-milk warmers. And *this* little crack... ah. This one looks like the morning sun coming up in about ten minutes." He looked up with his gentle eyes. "Which means we don't have long, friend."

"Ten minutes," Luóbo Gāo repeated. The whole neighborhood would wake up hungry in *ten minutes*, and breakfast was a disaster.

His edges threatened to wilt again.

But then he remembered his motto. *Crispy edges, soft heart.* Crispy meant *steady*. Soft meant *kind*. And right now, he needed every bit of both.

---

"New plan," Luóbo Gāo announced, and his voice was warm but firm, like a good crust. "We don't have time to send everyone home. So we *help each other instead*."

He pointed his crispy hand. "Dàn Dàn — read the cracks. Call out where each breakfast crew member is hiding."

Dàn Dàn squinted at his shell. "I see... Cōng Cōng the scallion pancake doing cartwheels by the fish-ball cart. And Luóbo... wait, that's *you*. And Hú Jiāo the pepper bun is asleep inside a tea kettle."

"Perfect. Kǎ Kǎ crew!" Luóbo Gāo called. "You're fast and you're many. Go *bounce* and fetch our lost breakfast friends! Bring them home!"

"More crunch—" "—more *rescue*!" cheered the nuggets, and off they bounced in forty different directions, giggling the whole way.

"Pái Pái! You're big and strong. Stand at the door and wave that magnificent crunchy cape so everyone can find the stall!"

"Bigger than your face, bigger than your *waving*!" Pái Pái boomed, flapping her golden edges like a lighthouse.

"Diàn Diàn — you glow. Be our morning light until the sun gets here!"

"*Beep-boop!* Open all night — or, I guess, open all *morning*!" said Diàn Diàn, glowing brighter.

"And Chòu Chòu—" Luóbo Gāo paused. "Chòu Chòu, your wonderful smell wakes up the *whole* street. We need everyone awake. Stand outside and just... be your bold, beautiful self."

Chòu Chòu's puppy eyes went wide and shiny. "You mean — my smell is *helping*?"

"Bold on the outside, gold on the inside," Luóbo Gāo said, grinning. "Go be golden."

Chòu Chòu puffed out the friendliest purple sparkle-cloud you ever saw and trotted proudly to the curb, and all up and down the street, sleepy windows began to open. *Mmm. Is that breakfast?* (It was, in a way. Chòu Chòu's wonderful smell is bold enough to wake even the sleepiest grandmother three streets over, which is exactly the sort of thing the world ought to appreciate more.)

"And *me*?" asked Dàn Dàn quietly, rolling close.

Luóbo Gāo grinned at the little egg. "You, my friend, are the most important of all. You're the map. Keep reading the cracks. Keep us pointed the right way." He patted Dàn Dàn's marbled shell. "Every crack tells a story — and today, every crack brings somebody home."

Dàn Dàn glowed at that, in the quiet way tea eggs glow, which is mostly on the inside.

---

What happened next was the most wonderful, ridiculous, beautifully crispy ten minutes the neighborhood had ever seen.

The Kǎ Kǎ crew bounced back hauling Cōng Cōng, who cartwheeled in shouting "Let's flip the script!" and flipped right onto the griddle. They carried in a still-snoozing Hú Jiāo, who woke up sneezing pepper everywhere — *"AH — AH — PEPPER-CHOO! Fresh from the fire!"* — which made everyone laugh so hard that Niú Niú, the beef-noodle bowl, slurped soup right out of his own face.

Dàn Dàn read crack after crack, guiding each friend to the right spot. Yóu Tiáo dove into the soy milk with a triumphant "Dunk me!" and a splash that misted everyone like morning dew. Dòu Jiāng hummed her sunrise song. And Luóbo Gāo stood in the middle of it all, square and golden and steady, directing traffic with both crispy hands.

By the time the sun finally peeked over the rooftops — right on schedule, just as Dàn Dàn's crack had promised — the breakfast stall was *singing*. Every snack was in the right place. Plates were filling. Bellies were rumbling happily.

And the night-market friends, instead of going home, had simply *stayed to help*, handing out plates and waving hellos.

"You know," said Dàn Dàn, rolling up beside Luóbo Gāo, "your chart was a disaster."

"My chart was a *masterpiece*," sniffed Luóbo Gāo.

"Your chart pointed at the moon."

"...The chart had *vision*."

They both laughed, and the steam from the griddle curled up to meet the morning sun.

---

When the rush was over and the last happy customer had wandered off, full and warm, Luóbo Gāo looked around at his crew — breakfast friends and night-market friends all tangled together, tired and giggling and golden in the morning light.

He hadn't kept his *plan*. The plan had fallen apart before the sun came up.

But he'd kept his *crew*. He'd kept his cool. And he'd kept both his crispy edges *and* his soft heart, right up until the very end.

"Simple things," murmured Lǔ Ròu the braised-pork bowl, who had wandered in late, "done with love."

"Crispy edges," agreed Luóbo Gāo, settling down at last with a contented little *tssss*, "soft heart. Both."

And outside, Chòu Chòu was still on the curb, sparkling proudly, having woken up the entire happy, hungry, well-fed street.
