# Story 35: Scallion Pancake & the Great Flip-Off

**Main Character:** Cōng Cōng (Scallion Pancake)
**Guest Characters:** Pudding
**Setting:** A sizzling griddle-cart corner in Taichung
**Theme:** Showing off is more fun when you bring a friend along.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 蔥油餅 (cōng yóu bǐng) = scallion pancake

---

On the steamiest corner in all of Taichung, where six griddle-carts huddled together like gossiping aunties, a flaky golden disc was doing something no pancake should ever do indoors: a triple-spinning, edge-flapping, ceiling-grazing CARTWHEEL.

"BEHOLD!" shouted **蔥油餅** (cōng yóu bǐng) = scallion pancake, landing with a crispy *crackle* and striking a pose. His green scallion freckles glittered. His flaky layers fanned out like a peacock. He smelled of toasted sesame oil and pure, undiluted confidence.

"They call me Cōng Cōng," he announced to absolutely no one, because it was barely dawn and the only listener was a sleepy mug of soy milk. "And tonight — TONIGHT — I challenge this entire street to a Flip-Off! Loser buys the sesame oil!"

The mug blinked. "Cōng Cōng, you haven't even had breakfast."

"Breakfast is for pancakes who don't FLIP," said Cōng Cōng. He flipped anyway, just to prove it, and flattened a passing dumpling.

"Sorry!"

The Flip-Off was on.

---

By evening, word had cartwheeled all over the night market. A wobbly rope had been strung between two carts to mark the Flipping Ring, and a crowd of snacks gathered, slurping and giggling and placing bets in lucky candies.

Cōng Cōng strutted into the ring flapping his crispy pancake hands at the crowd. "Let's flip the script!" he crowed. That was his catchphrase, and he used it roughly forty times a day.

"First challenger!" called Luóbo Gāo, the square radish cake who'd been made referee because he was the only one dependable enough to keep score. "Cōng Cōng versus... the Popcorn Chicken Crew!"

A cluster of golden nuggets bounced into the ring, finishing each other's sentences. "We flip—" "—as ONE—" "—and we never—" "—stop giggling!"

They flipped together — a glorious popping fountain of crispy chicken, basil hats flying. The crowd roared.

"Cute," said Cōng Cōng. "Watch THIS."

He launched himself off the griddle's edge, spun *four times*, did a little spin-the-pancake move that should be illegal, and aimed for a perfect landing —

— and landed *folded completely in half*, like a taco that had given up.

"I MEANT to do that," he wheezed, muffled, from inside himself.

The crowd politely pretended to believe him. A small egg waffle in a top hat leaned over and whispered, "Is the pancake supposed to be that... bendy?" and his neighbor whispered back, "I think that's the *advanced* level," which was very generous of her.

Cōng Cōng unfolded himself with a wounded little *crackle*, fluffed his flaky layers back into shape, and pretended he had simply been *resting* down there. "Just stretching," he said. "Champions stretch."

---

Round two went worse.

Cōng Cōng challenged Pái Pái, the enormous fried chicken cutlet bigger than her own face. She flexed her crunchy edges like a superhero cape. "Bigger than your face, bigger than your worries!" she boomed, and did one single, majestic, slow-motion flip that landed with a *whump* so cool the crowd actually applauded the floor.

Cōng Cōng decided to one-up her with a *quintuple* flip.

He got dizzy on flip number three.

He overshot on flip number four.

And on flip number five he sailed clean out of the ring, bounced off Hú Jiāo the pepper bun (who sneezed a startled puff of pepper — *"AH-CHOO! Watch the cheeks!"*), skidded across a tray of tea eggs, and ended up draped over the soy-sauce bottle like a damp golden cape.

"...That was the warm-up," he announced.

"Slurp first, worry later, kiddo," rumbled Niú Niú the beef noodle bowl kindly, tossing his noodle scarf. "Maybe slow down?"

But Cōng Cōng did not want to slow down. Slowing down was for snacks who weren't *spectacular*. He wanted to be the most spectacular pancake on the entire island, and spectacular pancakes did NOT get folded in half in front of everybody.

Here is a small secret about Cōng Cōng — one he kept hidden under all those crispy layers, tucked away where even he didn't like to look. The truth was, when he was a fresh-flipped little pancake, nobody had paid him much attention at all. He was just one griddle disc among hundreds, golden and flaky and *ordinary*. So he had taught himself to cartwheel and spin and flap, louder and bolder every day, because flipping was the one way he knew to make people *look*. And once they looked, he was terrified of the moment they might stop.

So he stood up, brushed off his crispy edges, took a breath that smelled of sesame oil and stubbornness, and challenged the whole rest of the street at once.

---

That was when a tiny, trembling voice piped up from the very back of the crowd.

"Um. Excuse me. Is — is this the line for the bathroom?"

Everyone turned.

A small caramel-colored flan was teetering at the edge of the ring, jiggling so hard he was practically a blur. This was Pudding — wobbly, anxious, and a lovable klutz who had simply taken a wrong turn on the way to literally anywhere.

"I think I'm lost," Pudding whispered, wobbling. "I get nervous and then I wobble and then I get *more* nervous about the wobbling, and then—"

He stepped forward to apologize and tripped over the wobbly rope.

What happened next, the night market would talk about for *years*.

Pudding launched into the air. But he didn't flip like a pancake or pop like popcorn chicken. He *wobbled*. He wobbled so hard and so fast that he became a shimmering, jiggling, golden tornado of pure caramel motion — up, around, over, *boing-boing-BOING* — and bounced off the griddle, off the rope, off Pái Pái's cape, off the soy-sauce bottle, and landed *perfectly upright* in the dead center of the ring with a tiny, terrified, *"...sorry?"*

The crowd went absolutely BANANAS.

"THE WOBBLE!" shrieked Shaved Ice from the back, in all caps as usual. "I HAVE NEVER SEEN SUCH A WOBBLE!"

Luóbo Gāo blinked at his scorecard. "I — I don't even have a number high enough."

Pudding looked around, horrified. "Did I break something? I'm so sorry. I'll pay for it. I don't have any money. I'll pay in wobbles."

---

Cōng Cōng stood very still.

This was, technically, the worst moment of his evening. A *random nervous flan* had just out-flipped him without even trying — without even *wanting* to. The crowd was chanting *"WOB-BLE! WOB-BLE!"* and nobody was looking at the most spectacular pancake on the island anymore.

He felt his crispy layers droop.

And then Cōng Cōng noticed something.

Pudding wasn't enjoying any of it. The little flan had scrunched himself into the smallest jiggle possible and was shaking — not from wobbling now, but from being *looked at* by so many faces. His caramel top trembled. He looked like he might melt from sheer nervousness.

Cōng Cōng knew that feeling, deep in his flaky middle. It was the same fluttery fear that made him show off in the first place. He just hid his behind cartwheels.

So instead of grabbing the spotlight back, Cōng Cōng did something he'd never done in his whole spinning life.

He shared it.

"LADIES AND GENTLE-SNACKS," he boomed, flapping a pancake hand toward Pudding, "may I present my SECRET WEAPON — the one, the only, the WOBBLE WIZARD!"

Pudding squeaked. "Your what?"

"Just follow me, kiddo," Cōng Cōng whispered, sliding up beside him. "I flip, you wobble. I catch you, you bounce off me. We do it together — and nobody falls. Deal?"

"I — I always fall," Pudding whispered back.

"Not tonight," said Cōng Cōng, and for the first time all evening, he wasn't bragging. He was *promising*.

---

What they did next had no name yet, so they invented one on the spot: the **Flip-and-Wobble**.

Cōng Cōng cartwheeled — and at the exact top of his spin, he scooped Pudding up on his flat crispy back. Pudding wobbled — and the wobble *launched* Cōng Cōng higher than he'd ever flown. Cōng Cōng spun and caught Pudding mid-air with a flappy pancake hand. Pudding's jiggle softened the landing so Cōng Cōng didn't fold in half. And every time Pudding started to panic, Cōng Cōng called out, "I've got you! Wobble away!" — and somehow, knowing someone would catch him, Pudding wasn't scared at all.

They flipped. They wobbled. They spun into a double-decker pancake-pudding pinwheel that scattered sesame seeds like fireworks. They bounced off all six griddle-carts in a row, ringing each one like a little gong — *bong, bong, bong* — while the popcorn chicken crew drummed along and Niú Niú swung his noodle scarf in time like a conductor. Hēi Táng the brown-sugar syrup got so excited he painted a swirly golden spotlight on the ground beneath them, just so they'd have somewhere grand to land.

And land they did — back-to-back, perfectly balanced, both of them grinning so wide their faces nearly cracked.

The crowd didn't just cheer. The crowd *stood up*. Even deadpan Grass Jelly tipped his black beanie.

Luóbo Gāo looked at his scorecard, looked at the two of them, and threw the card in the air. "Forget scores," he said, beaming. "WINNERS. Both of them. Obviously."

---

Later, when the carts had cooled and the steam had thinned to lazy little curls, Cōng Cōng and Pudding sat on the edge of the warm griddle, sharing a saucer of sesame oil.

"You didn't have to share your big show with me," Pudding said softly, still a little wobbly, but happy-wobbly now. "It was your contest."

Cōng Cōng flapped a crispy hand. "Yeah, but here's the thing I figured out, kiddo." He leaned back. "Showing off all by yourself? You land folded in half and everyone pretends not to notice. Showing off *with a friend*?" He grinned. "You fly."

Pudding wobbled thoughtfully. "So... bringing a friend is the trick?"

"Bringing a friend is *better* than the trick," said Cōng Cōng. "Let's flip the script — I'm retiring the solo act."

Just then, a hungry night-owl auntie wandered up to the cart. "One scallion pancake, please. And — ooh, one pudding too."

Cōng Cōng and Pudding looked at each other.

And here is the part of the story you've been waiting for, the part that is not scary AT ALL but is in fact the proudest moment of a snack's whole life — because for a Taiwanese street food, being chosen, being shared, being the warm delicious thing that makes a tired auntie smile at midnight, is the greatest honor there is.

"Together?" said Pudding.

"Together," said Cōng Cōng, flipping one final, flawless flip — and landing, for the very first time all night, *exactly* where he meant to.

The auntie took her first bite, sighed the happiest sigh, and said, "Now THIS is worth staying up for."

Two snacks, one perfect flip, zero falling.

The best show in Taichung had finally found its grand finale.
