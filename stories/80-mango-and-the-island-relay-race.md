# Story 80: Mango & the Round-the-Island Relay

**Main Character:** Mango (芒果)
**Guest Characters:** Pudding, Sesame Ball, Cōng Cōng (Scallion Pancake)
**Setting:** A relay race passing a baton from Taiwan's north tip to south tip
**Theme:** We carry each other across the finish.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 接力 (jiēlì) = relay

---

At the very tippy-top of Taiwan, where the island pokes its nose into the morning sea, Mango stood on a windy cliff and bounced on her toes so fast she nearly took off like a tangerine helicopter.

"FINISH LINE, HERE WE COME!" she hollered, orange skin glowing, rosy cheeks blushing rosier, sweatband already soaked even though the race had not started. Mango did not jog. Mango did not stroll. Mango *sproinged*. She was the sportiest fruit on the whole island, and today she was captain of the Great Round-the-Island Relay — a **接力** (jiēlì) = relay race — where teams carried a baton all the way from the north tip to the south tip, one runner per leg, snack after snack after snack.

"The rules are simple, team!" Mango announced, waving a baton shaped like a little bamboo bubble-tea straw. "Each of us runs one piece of the journey, then *plonk* — we pass the baton to the next runner! We carry each other across the finish!"

Behind her stood her team. And, well. Her team needed a *little* work.

There was Cōng Cōng the scallion pancake, flaky and golden and grinning, doing warm-up cartwheels off the cliff edge and catching himself on his crispy hands. "Let's flip the script!" he whooped, landing in a flour-puff.

There was Sesame Ball, round and bold and crusted in toasty seeds, rolling in nervous little circles. "I roll FAST," he boasted, "but I cannot, technically, *steer*."

And there, at the very back, was Pudding.

Pudding was a wobbly caramel flan. Pudding wobbled when he stood still. Pudding wobbled when he sat down. Pudding wobbled when he *thought about* wobbling, which was always. He gave a tiny anxious jiggle that sent his caramel-syrup hat sliding over one eye.

"Captain Mango?" Pudding squeaked. "What if — what if I'm too slow? What if I wobble off a bridge? What if I wobble into the *sea* and a friendly fish thinks I'm breakfast?"

"Then you'll be the tastiest thing that fish ever met!" said Mango, which did not help, but she meant it warmly. "Don't worry, Pudding. I'll run the LAST leg myself. The big finish! You just run an easy middle bit. Quick and quiet. Nothing to it."

Now, you might be wondering why a fruit, a pancake, a sesame ball, and a flan would all decide to run a race together in the first place. The answer is the same as it always is on this island: somebody put up a sign at the night market. The sign said WHO IS BRAVE ENOUGH TO CARRY THE BATON FROM TIP TO TIP? in big drippy brown-sugar letters, and Mango had read exactly one and a half words of it before signing all four of their names. Mango signed up for things. It was simply what Mango did. She had once signed the team up for a swimming contest before remembering that Sesame Ball sinks.

"This time it'll be different," Mango promised, which is also what Mango always said.

Pudding wobbled in a way that clearly meant *everything to it*. But he nodded.

A seagull blew a whistle. (Where the seagull got a whistle, nobody asked.) And the relay began.

Cōng Cōng went first, and oh, he was a sight — cartwheeling down the coastal road, flipping over puddles, somersaulting past startled crabs, his scallion freckles flashing green. "Flip! Flap! FLOOM!" He didn't so much run as turn the entire coastline into a gymnastics floor. He cartwheeled over a sleeping fisherman's hat. He flipped clean across a stream and high-fived a passing fish with one flappy pancake hand. "Let's flip the script!" he crowed, doing a spin so fast he toasted himself a little crispier. He passed three other teams and a confused snail before skidding to the first handoff in a spray of sesame oil. "Catch, big guy!" he called, tossing the baton.

Sesame Ball caught it on his nose, wobbled, and then — *whoosh* — took off rolling. Down hills! Around bends! Through a night market that was just opening for the day! He rolled so fast he picked up speed, picked up wind, and picked up a small parade of cheering dumplings who jogged behind him for fun.

"I CAN'T STEEEER!" Sesame Ball announced joyfully as he ricocheted off a lamppost, bonked gently off a mailbox, bounced off a sleeping cat (who said "mrrp" and went back to sleep), and rolled — by pure luck and good seeds — *exactly* onto the handoff line.

"Your turn, Pudding!" he panted.

Pudding took the baton in his little caramel hands. He looked at the long, long road ahead, curving down the green spine of the island toward the faraway south.

And then Pudding did his best.

He wobbled forward. *Sploosh, sploosh, sploosh.* He was, it must be said, not fast. A passing turtle gave him a polite nod, the kind of nod that says *take your time, friend, the road is long.* Other teams' runners zipped past — a sprinting sweet potato in a tiny hardhat, a blur of giggling popcorn chicken, a soup dumpling carefully not spilling.

Pudding's chin began to wobble in a way that had nothing to do with running.

"I'm losing it for everybody," he whispered. "I'm the slow one. I always have been."

A dragonfly overtook him. A leaf blew past him. A second, *slower* leaf also blew past him, which felt particularly unfair. Somewhere far behind, he was fairly sure he heard the polite turtle catching up. His little caramel legs ached, and worst of all, every time he tried to hurry, he only wobbled *wider*, like jelly trying to sprint, which is a thing that has never once worked in the history of jelly.

But here is the thing about a relay, and about teams, and maybe about islands. You are never quite as alone as you feel.

Because up ahead, Mango had been watching the standings, and she made a decision that surprised even her.

"Change of plan, team!" she called, sproinging over to Cōng Cōng and Sesame Ball, who had caught up to cheer. "I'm not running the last leg. *Pudding* is. Pudding finishes the race."

Pudding nearly turned upside-down. "WHAT? But — but I'm slow! But I'll lose! But —"

"But nothing," said Mango, planting her sweatband-y self right beside him. "You won't run it alone. We'll run *with* you. Every step."

And so they did.

Cōng Cōng cartwheeled in slow, silly loops around Pudding, just fast enough to keep pace, flipping upside down to make funny faces. "Look, Pudding, I'm a pancake-shaped helicopter! Wobble with me, baby!"

Sesame Ball rolled gently alongside, matching Pudding's wobble exactly, *splish-splosh, splish-splosh*, like two friends in a three-legged race that had somehow become a four-legged-no-legs race. "If I roll slow enough," Sesame Ball announced proudly, "I don't crash into things! Pudding, you've made me a SAFE driver! You're a hero!" He bonked very gently into a flowerpot to celebrate, just to keep his streak alive.

And Mango — fast, sporty, competitive Mango, who loved to win more than almost anything — slowed all the way down to Pudding's pace and stayed there, bouncing softly, cheering every single step like it was an Olympic final.

"That's it, Pudding! Look at that wobble! That's a CHAMPION wobble! Best wobble on the island!"

"I'm still slow," Pudding huffed, but he was smiling now.

"Slow and *finishing*," said Mango. "That beats fast and quitting every single time."

Word spread the way good news does at a night market — quick and warm and a little bit delicious. Soon the roadsides filled with snacks who'd already finished their own races, all leaning out to cheer the wobbliest runner of the day. Tea eggs rattled their cooker like a drum. A pepper bun popped out of an oven wall just to yell "FRESH FROM THE FIRE, GO PUDDING GO!" and sneezed pepper everywhere. Even the polite turtle had caught up — or rather, Pudding had slowed to *its* pace — and the two of them nodded at each other like old pals.

And the strangest, warmest thing happened. The cheering didn't make Pudding faster. He stayed exactly as slow as he had always been. But somewhere along that long green road, with his friends wobbling beside him and a whole island shouting his name, Pudding stopped *minding* that he was slow. Slow was just his speed. Slow was the speed at which Pudding got to see the prettiest parts of the coast, and notice the way the light came golden through the bamboo, and have a nice long chat with a turtle. There are, it turns out, worse things to be than slow. There are runners who go very fast and arrive at the finish with nobody beside them at all.

The south tip of the island came into view at last, where the land dipped its toes into the warm southern sea and the **加油** (jiāyóu) = "add oil," or "go go go!" chants rose up like a tide.

The finish line shimmered ahead, a ribbon of golden brown-sugar syrup stretched between two bamboo poles.

"I can't make it," Pudding whispered, wobbling so hard now his caramel jiggled like a tiny earthquake. His little legs were tired. The line was *so close* and *so far*.

"You can," said Mango, "because you don't have to make it alone. That's the whole point of a 接力, kiddo. We carry each other across the finish."

And then — gently, carefully, the way you'd carry a sleepy baby or a bowl of soup you mustn't spill — Cōng Cōng folded his crispy hands into a little seat, Sesame Ball became a soft rolling cushion, and Mango boosted from behind, and together the three of them carried Pudding the last few wobbly steps...

...so that Pudding — *Pudding!* — was the one who broke the brown-sugar ribbon. The baton crossed the line in his very own caramel hands.

"WE WIN!" Mango roared, leaping so high she briefly became a small orange sun. "PUDDING WINS IT FOR US!"

The whole south tip of the island erupted. Confetti made of sesame seeds and scallion flecks rained down. Sweet brown-sugar syrup looped victory swirls in the air. And Pudding, wobbliest runner in all of Taiwan, stood at the finish line with the baton held high and happy tears (which were really just a little extra syrup) sliding down his glossy cheeks.

"I finished," he said, amazed. "I actually finished."

"You didn't just finish," said Mango, throwing a sweaty, joyful arm around him. "You carried the whole team home. The slow ones aren't the weak ones, Pudding. Sometimes they're the bravest of all — because they keep going when everybody's already passed them."

That night, the team curled up together on the warm southern sand under a sky thick with stars, the baton resting in the middle of them like a little campfire. Cōng Cōng snored in flaky puffs. Sesame Ball rolled in his sleep and bonked nobody, for once. And Pudding, hugged warm and safe between his friends, finally wobbled into the softest, proudest sleep of his life.

Because he had learned the very best thing a small, slow, wobbly heart can learn:

You are never running alone.

## Goodnight Blessing
Goodnight, little one, and rest your tired toes. You do not have to be the fastest, or the boldest, or the bounciest — you only have to keep going, one soft wobble at a time, and to let the people who love you run beside you. Somewhere a turtle is nodding at the long quiet road, and somewhere your friends are saving you a spot at the finish line. So close your eyes and let the world carry you for a while, the way good friends carry each other home. You crossed today's whole long day, and that is a victory worth a thousand cheers. Sleep proud, sleep slow, sleep safe and snug. Goodnight.
