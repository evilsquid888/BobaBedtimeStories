# Story 74: Popcorn Chicken & the Claw Machine Rescue

**Main Character:** Kǎ Kǎ (Popcorn Chicken)
**Guest Characters:** Pudding
**Setting:** A glowing claw-machine arcade alley in Ximending
**Theme:** Teamwork can reach what one alone cannot.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 夾娃娃機 (jiá wáwa jī) = claw machine

---

There is no sound in all of Taipei quite like a crew of popcorn chicken nuggets giggling at once. It sounds like rain made of laughter. It sounds like a popcorn machine that just heard a really good joke. And on this particular night, in a narrow neon alley in **西門町** (Xīméndīng) = Ximending — the bright, blinky, music-stuffed corner of the city — that sound came bouncing down the street like a hundred happy ping-pong balls.

"More crunch—" hopped one nugget.

"—more FUN!" finished the rest, all twelve of them, dusted with pepper and crowned with crispy basil leaves like tiny green party hats.

That's Kǎ Kǎ for you. Not one chicken — a whole giggling chorus of them, hopping and reshuffling and finishing each other's sentences, traveling together the way bubbles travel in soda. Where one went, eleven followed, usually sideways, usually laughing. They were impossible to count, because every time you tried, they shuffled, and you had to start over, and they thought this was the funniest thing in the entire universe.

And tonight, the whole crunchy crew had come to play the games.

---

The alley glowed pink and gold and electric blue. Rows and rows of glass boxes hummed and twinkled, each one stuffed with plushies, each one waving a shiny metal arm. These, my dear reader, are **夾娃娃機** (jiá wáwa jī) = claw machines — the kind where you drop a coin, wiggle a joystick, and a wobbly claw swoops down to *almost* grab a prize before changing its mind at the very last second. (Claw machines are famous for changing their minds. It is their hobby. Some people believe claw machines have feelings. The claw machines have never confirmed this, because they are very mysterious, and also because they are machines.)

The music in the alley was a happy jingly jumble — one machine played a sleepy lullaby, the next played a peppy marching tune, and somewhere a tiny robot voice kept chirping "Try again! Try again!" in the most encouraging way. It smelled of fried snacks and bubble tea and warm summer night.

"Look at this one!" squeaked a nugget named Pi.

"It's got a fluffy moon-bunny plush!" squeaked Po.

"It's got snacks!" squeaked the twins, Crunch and Munch, who noticed snacks everywhere, because they were snacks themselves and felt a deep kinship.

The Kǎ Kǎ crew swarmed up onto the little stool in front of the brightest machine of all — a tall glass tower stuffed with soft toys and shiny candy. Wobbling and tittering, they pooled their coins and dropped one in.

*Clink. Bing-bong-BLOOP!*

The claw came alive. It swung. It dangled. It looked very confident, the way claw machines do right before they let you down.

"Steer left!" cried Pi.

"No, RIGHT!" cried Po.

"Steer with your HEART!" cried Crunch, who did not understand joysticks but had a lot of feelings.

In all the wiggling and giggling and hopping, one little nugget — the smallest one, a shy bouncy crumb named **Dīng Dīng** (Dīng Dīng), no bigger than a thumbnail — bounced one bounce too many. Up he sprang, off the stool, *boing* off the joystick, *boing* off the coin slot, and *FWOOMP* — straight through the prize flap and down, down, down into the belly of the **夾娃娃機**.

The flap snapped shut behind him.

For one whole second, there was silence in the alley. Even the neon seemed to hold its breath.

Then, from inside the glass, came the smallest, bravest, most muffled voice in the world: "...Um. Hello? It's cozy in here? But also? I would like to come out now, please?"

---

Eleven nuggets pressed their crispy faces to the glass.

"DĪNG DĪNG!"

"Don't worry, buddy, we'll get you out!"

"More rescue, more fun!"

But here was the trouble, and it was a wobbly one. The prize chute was *high*. Dīng Dīng had fallen down into the bottom, into a soft nest of plushies, and the only way out was up through the flap — far, FAR above his little chicken head. He hopped. He hopped his very best hop. He bounced off a plush moon-bunny and got nowhere near the top.

"I can't reach!" he called. "It's too high! And the bunny plush keeps hugging me back, which is nice, but unhelpful!"

The crew tried the claw. They dropped their last coin and steered with all their hearts, but the claw — being a claw — swooped, dangled, gripped a plush *next* to Dīng Dīng, lifted it half an inch, looked deeply unbothered, and let it go.

"It HAD him!" wailed Po. "It had a plush RIGHT next to him and it just — it just *shrugged*!"

"Claws don't have shoulders," said Pi miserably. "How does it shrug?"

"With its WHOLE PERSONALITY," said Crunch.

*Bloop.* Out of coins.

"Oh no," whispered Pi.

And just then, a kindly hand-painted sign above the alley flickered. The arcade was closing soon. When the arcade closed, the big shutters rolled down, the lights blinked off, and the machines went to sleep till morning. Dīng Dīng would be stuck inside all night long, alone in the dark with a hugging bunny.

That, dear reader, is the closest this story ever comes to scary — and even that is mostly just *snuggly*.

"We need a plan," said Po, very seriously, which is hard to do when you are a nugget the size of a marble.

---

That's when a soft, wobbly, anxious voice spoke up from down the alley.

"Excuse me? Are you — oops — are you all okay? You look like you're having a *worry*."

It was Pudding. Sweet, jiggly, golden caramel Pudding, wobbling along on his little plate, looking like a tiny earthquake every time he took a step. He was a lovable klutz and an even more lovable worrier, and he had a heart as soft as, well, pudding.

The crew explained everything in a tumbling rush — eleven voices at once, which sounded like someone shaking a box of crunchy snacks.

Pudding listened, trembling thoughtfully. "Okay. Okay okay okay. So Dīng Dīng can't reach the top because he's too little, and you can't reach him because the glass is in the way..." He wobbled. "But there are *so many* of you."

The whole crew blinked. Eleven little nuggets blinked, all at the same time. *Plink-plink-plink.*

"Pudding," said Pi slowly, "are you saying...?"

"I'm saying," said Pudding, standing as tall as a flan can stand (which is not very tall, but very brave), "that one of you can't reach the top. But what if you were a *tower*?"

---

Oh, the giggling that broke out then! It rolled down the whole alley.

"A TOWER OF NUGGETS!"

"Stack-attack!"

"More stacking, more—"

"—FUN!"

Here is how they did it, and it was magnificent. The flap that had swallowed Dīng Dīng was the same flap that opened to drop prizes out — so if Dīng Dīng could climb high enough *inside* to push it open, he could pop right out the front.

But the climb was too high for one. So the crew, on the *inside*, had a different idea — except they were on the outside. Hmm. Reader, do not worry, they figured it out, the way teams do.

"Pudding!" called Pi. "We need to get in there with him to build the tower. Can you wobble onto the joystick and jiggle it juuuust right to bonk the flap open from outside, while we sneak in?"

"I can try," gulped Pudding. "I am extremely bad at most things. But jiggling is my whole entire personality."

Pudding wobbled up onto the machine. He looked down at the joystick. He looked at the long, scary climb. His little caramel knees knocked together like castanets.

"What if I jiggle wrong?" he whispered. "What if I jiggle us all into a bigger mess? I'm a klutz. I once tripped over my own plate."

"Pudding," said Pi gently, "you don't have to be perfect. You just have to wobble. And nobody in the whole night market wobbles like you."

Pudding gulped. Then something brave bloomed inside his soft caramel heart. He took a deep, brave breath. And then he did the thing pudding does best — he *jiggled*. He jiggled so hard and so wobbly that his whole caramel body became a quivering golden blur, *boing-boing-boing*, knocking the joystick this way and that. Left, right, left-left, a little hop, until — *clunk* — the claw swung over and smacked the prize flap and held it open a sliver.

"GO GO GO!" yelled Pudding, jiggling for dear life. "I can't hold it forever! I am ALL wobble and NO muscle!"

One by one, the nuggets squeezed through the propped flap and tumbled inside to join Dīng Dīng. *Bonk, bonk, bonk, bonk.* Eleven crunchy crumbs piled up beside their littlest friend.

"Tower time," said Pi. "Biggest on the bottom, smallest on top — Dīng Dīng, you're the climber!"

So they stacked. Crunch and Munch made the base, planting their crispy feet wide and locking their little crunchy arms together. "We are the foundation," announced Crunch proudly. "Foundations don't get to see the view, but foundations get all the glory." Pi and Po climbed up and balanced on their shoulders. Then more, and more, wobbling and giggling and shouting "to the LEFT, no MY left, no the OTHER left," each nugget bracing the one below, swaying like a noodle in the wind and somehow holding on, a teetering, towering, basil-crowned ladder of popcorn chicken reaching higher than any single one of them could ever dream of reaching alone.

The tower swayed. It wobbled. A plush moon-bunny bonked the side of it and the whole thing went "WHOA" in eleven voices at once.

"Steady, crew!" called Pi from somewhere in the middle. "Breathe together! In... and out... and crunch!"

And up the top scrambled Dīng Dīng, lighter than a feather, braver than a tiger, until — *stretch* — his tiny crispy hands touched the flap.

"You can do it, buddy!"

"Push with your HEART!" yelled Crunch, who was now at the very bottom and out of breath but never out of feelings.

Dīng Dīng pushed. The flap creaked open. And then the whole glorious tower gave one enormous bouncy *BOING* — and all twelve nuggets sprang out the flap together in a fountain of crunch, tumbling into the soft, waiting, jiggly arms of Pudding, who caught every single one of them and only fell over twice.

---

The alley filled with cheering — the nuggets, Pudding, even a passing **Tǔdì Gōng** (the tiny jolly Earth God) who happened by and chuckled, "Bless this little corner of the island," and handed out lucky candies all around.

Dīng Dīng, safe and sound and slightly hugged-flat from the moon-bunny, looked up at his whole crew. "I couldn't reach the top alone," he said softly. "Not even close."

"Nope," said Pi, hopping happily.

"But twelve of us, plus one wobbly hero," said Po, nudging Pudding, "could reach the moon."

Pudding turned the color of an even more bashful flan. "I just jiggled," he mumbled. "Jiggling isn't really a skill." But he was beaming so hard his caramel top glowed.

"Jiggling," said Dīng Dīng firmly, "is the most important skill there is. You held the door for all of us. A hero doesn't have to be the strongest. A hero just has to hold on."

And right at closing time, the kind arcade auntie who ran the alley scooped the whole crew up in a paper cup — and you know what comes next, dear reader, and it is the happiest thing of all. Because a popcorn chicken's greatest glory, the thing they bounce and giggle and dream toward, is to be shared and snacked and adored. They were popped into the cup together, all twelve, crunch by crunch, the way they did everything — *together* — and it was the proudest, crunchiest, most joyful moment of their whole bouncy lives. Like fireworks finally getting to light the sky.

"More crunch," they cheered, one last giggling time, "MORE FUN!"

And Pudding wobbled home under the neon, smiling all the way.

## Goodnight Blessing
Little dreamer, the world is full of things too high to reach all by yourself — and that is perfectly all right. When you cannot climb something alone, you simply find your crew, and you stack your kindness, and you boost each other up until someone touches the sky. Tonight, let your worries tumble out like nuggets from a claw machine, and let sleep catch you the way Pudding caught his friends — softly, surely, and with a little happy jiggle. You are held. You are loved. You are part of a tower of people who would reach the moon for you. Goodnight.
