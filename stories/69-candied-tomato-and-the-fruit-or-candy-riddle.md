# Story 69: Candied Tomato & the Is-It-Fruit-or-Candy Riddle

**Main Character:** Mì Fānqié (Candied Tomato) — cherry tomatoes on a stick in a glossy crackly sugar shell dusted with sour plum powder, giggly and tangy-sweet
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A buzzing Tainan night-market 'guess the snack' game stall
**Theme:** Joy doesn't care what category you fit in
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 番茄 (fānqié) = tomato

---

Mì Fānqié giggled when she was nervous, which was a problem, because right now she was VERY nervous and could not stop.

"Tee-hee! Hee! HOO-hee-hee!"

She was a row of four cherry tomatoes skewered on a slender bamboo stick, each one wrapped in a sugar shell so glossy and crackly it looked like a tiny window made of glass. A dusting of pinkish-red **酸梅粉** (suānméifěn) = sour plum powder freckled her shiny coat, so she smelled like a sweet-shop that had gotten into a friendly argument with a lemon.

She was, in a word, delightful.

She was also, at this exact moment, dangling upside down from a clothespin at the most famous **遊戲攤** (yóuxìtān) = game stall in the entire Tainan **夜市** (yèshì) = night market — and the night's big contest was about to begin.

"Tee-hee," she whispered to herself. "Please don't drop me. Please don't drop me. Tee-HOO."

---

The stall was run by a snack named Egg Waffle, who was puffed up with his own importance and shaped, conveniently, like a sheet of golden bubbles.

"Step right up!" Egg Waffle boomed, ringing a little brass bell. "Welcome to the GUESS THE SNACK riddle game! The rules are simple. Tonight, every treat on my wall belongs to exactly ONE of two boxes." He swept a flipper toward two glowing bins. One had a leafy green label that said **水果** (shuǐguǒ) = FRUIT. The other had a sparkly gold label that said CANDY.

"Fruit on the left! Candy on the right! Customers guess where each snack belongs, and the snack that gets sorted FASTEST wins the Golden Stick of the Night!"

A cheer went up from the wall of dangling snacks. A bunch of grapes rolled happily into the FRUIT bin. A gummy worm slithered proudly into CANDY. A slice of dried mango thought about it for half a second, then bounced into FRUIT with a triumphant "Ha!" A peppermint did a little tap-dance and cartwheeled into CANDY, showing off so much that three other candies told it to settle down.

It went on like that, *ding* after *ding*, snack after snack, each one sliding cleanly into its proper bin like a coin into a slot. The crowd loved it. There is something deeply satisfying about watching things fit, the way a puzzle piece clicks or a sock finds its matching sock at last.

Easy. Quick. Everyone fit somewhere.

Then a customer pointed at Mì Fānqié.

"Ooh — what's THAT one?"

Egg Waffle squinted at her through his bubbly golden face. "That, my friend, is a... hmm." He flipped his little tongs. "A tomato."

"So — fruit?" said the customer.

"A tomato is technically a fruit," agreed Egg Waffle.

"But it's covered in candy," said the customer.

"That is also true," said Egg Waffle.

"So it's candy?"

"But the inside is a tomato."

"So it's fruit?"

"But the OUTSIDE is candy."

The two of them stared at her. Mì Fānqié giggled out of pure panic. "Tee-hee! Hello! I'm both! Or neither! Or — tee-HOO — I'm honestly not sure?"

Egg Waffle scratched his bubbles with his tongs. "This is a problem. The riddle says EVERY snack must go in ONE box. You can't be in two. There aren't two-box snacks." He frowned. "I'll have to set you aside until we figure you out."

And with that, he unclipped her clothespin and dropped her — *plop* — into a dusty cardboard box behind the stall, labeled simply: **不知道** (bùzhīdào) = DON'T KNOW.

---

The DON'T KNOW box was lonely.

It was dark, and it smelled like old cardboard, and from outside Mì Fānqié could hear the night market roaring with joy — the sizzle of grills, the laughter, the *ding ding ding* of Egg Waffle's bell as snack after snack got sorted and cheered and won little ribbons.

"Fruit!" *Ding!*

"Candy!" *Ding!*

"Fruit!" *Ding!*

Nobody was ringing a bell for her.

Mì Fānqié sat in the dark, and for the first time in her shiny little life, she did not feel like giggling. Her sugar shell felt heavy. Her sour plum powder felt, well, sour. She thought about all the bright bins glowing out there, each one with a clear gold label and a clear right answer, and she thought about how nice it must be to know, deep down in your skewer, exactly where you belonged.

"Maybe they're right," she sniffled, very softly. "Maybe a snack that doesn't fit in a box isn't really a snack at all. Maybe I'm just... a mistake on a stick." She wobbled. "A confusing little glob of nonsense that made the bell stop ringing."

Now, you and I know that is absolute nonsense, and I am tempted to climb right into this story and tell her so myself. But sometimes a treat has to figure things out on her own. So let's keep reading and cheer for her quietly from out here.

A small voice came from the corner of the box. "Did somebody say *mistake?* Because I felt that one personally."

Mì Fānqié turned. In the shadows sat a wrinkly little snack she hadn't noticed — a **梅子** (méizi) = preserved plum, all puckered and dusty and somehow grinning.

"Hello," said the plum. "I've been in here three nights. They can't decide if I'm a fruit or a candy either. I'm a plum AND I'm a sweet AND I'm a little bit salty, and apparently that's TOO MUCH MATH for the bell." She cackled, and little puffs of plum dust came off her with each laugh. "I keep telling them: I'm not a riddle to be solved. I'm a snack to be enjoyed. There's a difference, you know, and grown-ups forget it about once an hour."

"But the rules—" began Mì Fānqié.

"The rules are about boxes," said the plum gently. "Not about joy. Tell me, little **番茄** — when a kid bites into your sugar shell and it goes *crackle-CRUNCH* and then the tomato squirts sweet and tangy and the sour plum powder makes their nose go all *scrunchy* — do they stop to check which box you came from?"

Mì Fānqié thought about it. "...No," she admitted. "Mostly they just say 'WHOA' and then they say 'AGAIN.'"

"Exactly," said the plum, and she winked one wrinkly eye. "Joy doesn't care what category you fit in. Joy just cares that you showed up tasting like a tiny fireworks show. Boxes are for sorting socks and stamps and screws. They were never meant for snacks — and they were certainly never meant for someone as marvelously mixed-up as you." She leaned in close. "Between you and me, the snacks that don't fit in one box are the ones everybody actually remembers."

Outside, Egg Waffle's voice rang out: "...and now, for the final treat of the night, who can sort the most MYSTERIOUS snack of all? Step right up!"

And Mì Fānqié — who had been crying just a minute ago — felt something new bubble up inside her. Not a nervous giggle. A *brave* one.

"Tee-hee," she said. "I have an idea."

---

She rocked. She rolled. She bonked her bamboo stick against the side of the box — *tonk, tonk, TONK* — until the whole box tipped, tumbled off the shelf, and spilled her out into the bright lights of the stall, right in the middle of the crowd.

"Tee-HEE!" she announced, standing tall on her stick. "Hello, everybody! I'm Mì Fānqié, and I do NOT fit in a box!"

The crowd gasped. Egg Waffle gasped loudest of all. "She escaped the DON'T KNOW!"

Egg Waffle waved his tongs in alarm. "You can't just escape the DON'T KNOW box! That's not in the rules! There's a PROCESS!"

"Your rules," said Mì Fānqié, lifting her chin so the sour plum powder sparkled, "have a hole in them the exact shape of me. And I have a better riddle for you anyway." She twirled, slow and grand, so her sugar shell caught the lantern light and threw little red sparkles across everyone's faces. "Forget fruit OR candy. Here's the real puzzle: What's sweet AND sour, crunchy AND squishy, healthy-ish AND a total treat, a vegetable-that's-secretly-a-fruit wearing a coat made of candy — all at the very SAME TIME?"

A little girl near the front raised her hand. "Um... is it YOU?"

"IT'S ME!" cried Mì Fānqié, and she did the biggest, sparkliest twirl of her life.

The little girl laughed and clapped. "I want THAT one! The both-at-once one!"

"Me too!" shouted a boy.

"Me three!" said somebody's grandpa, elbowing to the front.

Suddenly everyone wanted the snack that didn't fit. Because the snack that didn't fit, it turned out, was the most interesting snack on the whole wall. A line formed. Then the line grew a second line beside it. Somebody at the back started a chant — "BOTH! BOTH! BOTH!" — and the grapes and the gummy worm and even the show-off peppermint, all safely sorted in their tidy bins, leaned over the edges to watch, looking just a tiny bit jealous of the tomato nobody could label. Egg Waffle scrambled to keep up, ringing his bell so fast it nearly flew off the hook.

"Wait, wait, WAIT," he panted. "If she goes in both boxes, I'll have to invent a NEW box—"

"Then invent one," said the plum, climbing out of the cardboard with great dignity. "Call it the BOTH box. The YES-AND box. Some of us are seasoned professionals at being more than one thing."

So Egg Waffle, mopping his bubbly brow, hung a brand-new bin between the other two. He painted it with the brightest gold paint he had, and on the front he wrote: **又水果又糖果** — *both fruit AND candy.*

The crowd roared. The plum took a bow. And Mì Fānqié laughed so hard her sour plum powder shimmered like glitter.

The little girl held out her hand. "Can I try you?"

Mì Fānqié looked at the new BOTH box, all warm and golden and bright. Then she looked at the little girl's eager, joyful face.

"Tee-hee," she said. "I thought you'd never ask."

And the little girl bit down — *crackle-CRUNCH* — and the sugar shell shattered like a tiny window, and the tomato burst sweet and tangy and bright, and the sour plum powder did exactly what it was supposed to do, which was make her nose go *scrunchy* and her whole face light up like a lantern.

"WHOA," said the girl.

And then, of course: "AGAIN."

Mì Fānqié had never, in all her shiny life, felt so completely and joyfully herself. She was a fruit. She was a candy. She was a giggle on a stick and a tiny fireworks show and a snack that no box could ever hold.

And that — winking out at you now, little reader — was the most delicious thing of all.

## Goodnight Blessing

Tonight, little dreamer, may you never worry about which box you belong in. You are sweet AND brave, quiet AND silly, soft AND strong — all at the very same time, and that is exactly the wonder of you. The world will always try to sort everyone into neat little bins, but the most delightful things are the ones that giggle right over the edges. So snuggle down, let your sugar shell go soft and warm, and dream of night-market lanterns glowing just for you. You fit perfectly, sweet one — perfectly as your whole, wonderful self. Goodnight.
