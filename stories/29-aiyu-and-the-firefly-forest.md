# Story 29: Aiyu and the Firefly Forest

**Main Character:** Aiyu (愛玉) — Aiyu Jelly
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** Xitou (溪頭) Nature Forest, Nantou — a spring firefly night
**Theme:** Your quiet glow lights the way for others
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 螢火蟲 (yínghuǒchóng) = firefly

---

Aiyu was standing at the back of the line, which was exactly where Aiyu liked to stand, when somebody shone a flashlight straight through her and screamed.

"AAH! GHOST! There's a GHOST jelly!"

"I'm not a ghost," said Aiyu quietly. "I'm just see-through."

She was a small, wobbly cube of pale golden-amber jelly, soft as a held breath, and you really could see right through her — the trees, the path, the worried face of the boy with the flashlight, all of it gently blurred as if viewed through honey. Tiny lemon-seed freckles dotted her sides. When she was nervous she jiggled, and right now she was jiggling so hard she made a faint *gloop gloop* sound, like a pudding tiptoeing.

"You can see right through me," she said. "That's how you know I'm honest. A ghost would hide. I can't hide. I tried once. Everyone could still see my freckles."

It was the first night of the firefly walk in the **森林** (sēnlín) = forest, and Aiyu had signed up by accident, mostly because the sign-up sheet had been at the back of the room and the back of the room was, again, where Aiyu liked to stand.

The forest was Xitou, high in the misty mountains of Nantou, where the bamboo grows so tall it tickles the clouds and the air smells like cool green rain. And tonight, the guide promised, the whole **森林** would fill with **螢火蟲** (yínghuǒchóng) = fireflies — thousands of tiny floating lanterns blinking on and off in the dark.

"Stay together," said the guide, a kindly old salamander in a ranger hat. "No flashlights once we're inside — the lights scare the **螢火蟲** away. Just follow the glow."

And he turned off the last lamp.

The dark came down like a soft black blanket.

It was VERY dark. The kind of dark where you become extremely aware that you have feet and you cannot see them. Aiyu wobbled. The whole group wobbled, actually, bumping gently into one another and apologizing in whispers.

"Okay," said the salamander guide cheerfully. "Now we wait for the fireflies. Any moment now. Any. Moment."

They waited.

The forest stayed dark.

"...They're usually right on time," said the guide, in the voice of someone whose surprise party guests had not shown up.

Far off, deep in the trees, a few sad little lights blinked. One. Then another, way over there. Then nothing. The **螢火蟲** were scattered tonight, spread thin across the whole dark mountain, dim and lonely and much too far apart to light a path.

"Huh," said the guide. "That's odd. Where is everybody?"

And that was when Aiyu heard the crying.

It was a very small crying. A teeny, hiccupy, trying-to-be-brave-about-it crying, coming from somewhere down low, near the wet roots of an enormous bamboo. Aiyu was the only one who heard it, because Aiyu was the only one standing at the back, which — say it with me — is where Aiyu liked to stand.

She wobbled off the path.

"Hello?" she whispered. "Are you all right?"

"I am MAGNIFICENTLY all right," sobbed a tiny voice. "I am having the BEST night. This is FINE. Everything is FINE."

Down among the roots sat the smallest firefly Aiyu had ever seen. He was round and grey-green and absolutely not glowing, not even a little, and he was wearing the expression of someone whose tail had embarrassingly refused to light at the worst possible moment.

"My name is Pip," he announced, with enormous dignity, while crying. "I am a **螢火蟲**. A FIREFLY. And tonight was supposed to be my very first Big Glow, when all the babies light up together for the first time and the grown-ups gather round to watch, and instead my light WON'T WORK, and I got separated, and now everyone is scattered looking for me in the dark, and I CAN'T EVEN GLOW TO TELL THEM WHERE I AM." He took a shaky breath. "But I'm FINE."

"You don't sound fine," said Aiyu honestly.

"NO," wailed Pip. "I am NOT."

"That's okay," said Aiyu. "I cry too. Usually at the back. Where no one can—" She paused. "Well. Where no one can hear me, anyway. They can always see me."

Pip sniffled and looked up at her — really looked, the way you look through a clean window. "You're see-through."

"Yes."

"That's weird."

"Yes," Aiyu agreed.

"...You're also kind of glowing."

Aiyu froze. Her freckles jiggled.

She *was* glowing. Just faintly — a soft, warm, golden shimmer, the way she always did under the moon, the thing she'd spent her whole shy life trying to hush, because glowing meant being SEEN, and being seen meant a boy with a flashlight calling you a ghost. She'd always thought of her glow as a wobbly little embarrassment she carried around like a freckle she couldn't scrub off.

"Oh," she said, very quietly. "That. I — sorry. I'll stop. I'm always doing that. It's nothing. It's not even bright—"

"Are you KIDDING?" said Pip. He stood straight up on his tiny legs. "Do that MORE."

And from somewhere far across the dark forest, a single firefly blinked — and drifted a little closer.

Aiyu watched it come. A small green spark, floating toward her gentle gold like a moth toward a candle. It hovered near her amber cheek and blinked, slow and curious, as if to say: *oh. There you are.*

Aiyu's heart did something it had never done before. It got brave.

"Pip," she whispered. "I have an idea. And it's a very un-Aiyu idea, so I'd better say it fast before I lose it. What if I glow on PURPOSE?"

She climbed — wobbling, gloop-glooping, terrified — onto the very top of a moss-covered rock right in the middle of the clearing, where ANYONE could see her, where EVERYONE could see her, which was the single most un-back-of-the-room thing she had ever done in her wobbly little life.

And then Aiyu, who hated being looked at, stood up tall in the dark and let herself shine.

She glowed. She glowed with everything she had. Her whole honest see-through body filled up with warm golden light, like a lantern made of held sunshine, and the moonlight came down through the bamboo and caught her and doubled it, and Aiyu — small, shy, freckled Aiyu — became the brightest thing on the whole mountain.

"漂亮," breathed Pip — **漂亮** (piàoliang) = beautiful — and for once Aiyu didn't argue.

And the fireflies came.

Oh, they came. From every dark corner of Xitou they came, those scattered lonely little lights, drawn out of the deep trees by the warm gold beacon in the clearing. One, then ten, then a hundred, then a river — a whole flooding glittering river of **螢火蟲**, swirling up out of the bamboo and the ferns and the wet black roots, gathering around Aiyu's glow like the whole forest was being switched on at once.

The salamander guide turned around and dropped his ranger hat.

"Would you look at that," he whispered.

The clearing filled with light. Green and gold, gold and green, ten thousand tiny lanterns blinking in slow waves, turning the dark **森林** into something out of a dream — a cathedral of floating stars, with one small wobbling jelly glowing warm and golden at the very heart of it.

"They found me," whispered Pip, spinning in dizzy circles. "They found me because of YOU. You called them home."

"I just glowed," said Aiyu, a little dazed.

"You just glowed BRAVELY," said Pip. "That's the part that counts."

And then — because the story would not be complete without it — the gentlest thing happened. Pip, surrounded now by warmth and friends and his whole worried family swirling joyfully around him, felt something flicker deep in his little tail. A spark. A stutter. A tiny, trembling, brand-new light.

His tail lit up.

"I'M GLOWING!" Pip shrieked, so loudly that several dignified grown-up fireflies startled and bumped into a leaf. "AIYU! AIYU, LOOK! I'M GLOWING! IT WORKS! MY BUTT WORKS!"

"It's a beautiful butt," said Aiyu solemnly, and meant it.

Pip glowed and glowed, the proudest little light in all of Nantou, blinking out *thank you, thank you, thank you* in firefly Morse code that nobody could read but everybody understood. His family wrapped around him in a warm spinning hug of light, and then — gently, kindly — they wrapped around Aiyu too, because that is what you do for a **朋友** (péngyou) = friend who lights the way home.

The salamander guide led the whole tour group, single file and glowing-eyed with wonder, down the dark path — and they didn't need a flashlight, not anymore. They just followed Aiyu, who walked at the FRONT now, lit up like a small honest moon, with ten thousand fireflies trailing behind her like a comet's tail and one very small firefly named Pip riding proudly on her shoulder.

The boy with the flashlight — the one who'd called her a ghost — switched his light off and never turned it on again. He didn't need to.

"Sorry I called you a ghost," he whispered.

"It's okay," said Aiyu. "Ghosts hide. I glowed."

When at last they reached the warm yellow lamps of the forest lodge, the salamander guide turned to Aiyu and bowed his ranger hat.

"In forty years of leading this walk," he said, "I have never once seen the **螢火蟲** gather like that. You gathered the whole mountain, little jelly."

Aiyu jiggled — happily, this time. "I didn't do much," she said. "I just stopped hiding the glow."

"Sometimes," said the salamander, "that's the bravest thing there is."

And here is the truth about Aiyu, little dreamer, the truth she had been wobbling around her whole quiet life without knowing it: the very thing she was shyest about — that soft, see-through, honest little glow — was never an embarrassment at all. It was a lantern. It had just been waiting, all this time, for a dark enough night and a small enough firefly to need it.

You don't have to be the loudest. You don't have to be the brightest. You just have to be honest enough to let your own gentle light show — because somewhere out there in the dark, someone is lost, and your quiet glow is exactly bright enough to lead them home.

That night, the fireflies tucked Aiyu into a cool curl of moss at the forest's edge, blanketed her in a slow blinking quilt of green and gold, and Pip curled up glowing on her cheek like a tiny warm freckle that had finally learned to shine.

"晚安, Aiyu," whispered Pip — **晚安** (wǎn'ān) = goodnight.

"晚安, Pip," whispered Aiyu.

And one by one, all across the great dark **森林**, the little lights blinked softly off to sleep.

## Goodnight Blessing

Tonight, little dreamer, may you remember that the softest light you carry is still a light. You don't have to be the brightest star in the sky — you only have to be honest enough to glow when the dark gets deep and someone small and frightened needs to find their way. Somewhere a tiny firefly is looking for home, and your gentle, wobbly, see-through warmth is exactly enough to guide them. So close your eyes, let your own quiet glow settle soft and golden, and drift off among ten thousand sleepy lanterns. 晚安, brave little light. The forest is glowing. And so are you.
