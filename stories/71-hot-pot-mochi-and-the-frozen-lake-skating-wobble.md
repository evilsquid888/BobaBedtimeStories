# Story 71: Hot Pot Mochi & the Frozen-Lake Skating Wobble

**Main Character:** Shāo Má Shǔ (Toasted Mochi) — a puffy grilled mochi dusted in soybean powder, eager but unsteady
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A rare frozen alpine lake high in the mountains on a crisp winter day
**Theme:** Falling down is just part of learning something new
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 滑冰 (huábīng) = ice skating

---

High up where the mountains poke their snowy noses into the clouds, something happened that almost never happens: the great alpine lake froze solid overnight, smooth as a mirror and twice as sparkly. And the very first creature to come bouncing across the snow to see it was Toasted Mochi.

Now, Toasted Mochi was a sight to behold. He was a plump little square of grilled rice cake, golden-brown grill stripes toasted across his puffy cheeks, and he wore a soft coat of **黃豆粉** (huángdòufěn) = soybean powder that puffed off him in tiny yellow clouds every time he hopped. He was springy. He was squishy. He was, in his own opinion, about to become the most graceful ice skater the mountains had ever seen.

"Today," Toasted Mochi announced to a passing snowflake, "I shall glide like a swan."

The snowflake, who knew Toasted Mochi, drifted away to find somewhere safer.

---

Toasted Mochi had brought skates. They were tiny and silver and he had laced them onto his squishy bottom corners with great determination, which is a tricky thing to do when you have no actual feet, only adorable rounded edges.

His friends were already there. Red Bean, round and serious, sat on a snowbank with a steaming thermos of **熱可可** (rè kěkě) = hot cocoa balanced carefully on her lap. Pineapple Cake, all buttery corners and crumbly enthusiasm, was building a snow-fort with very questionable engineering. And little Egg Tart, wobbling in her flaky golden crust, was practicing tiny careful steps near the shore, holding both edges out like a tightrope walker who had decided the ground itself was the rope.

"Watch this!" Toasted Mochi called. "Behold — **滑冰** (huábīng) = ice skating! I have studied swans. I have studied the wind. I am basically already excellent."

He took one mighty, confident, swan-like push onto the ice.

For exactly half a second, it was glorious.

Then both skates shot out in two completely different directions, like they'd each decided to visit a different mountain, and Toasted Mochi went *FWUMP* flat onto his squishy bottom. A small mushroom cloud of soybean powder poofed into the air and settled gently over his entire face.

There was a pause. Somewhere, a faraway bird laughed, although it might have just been a bird.

"...I meant to do that," he said, from inside the powder.

"Did you?" asked Red Bean.

"No," admitted Toasted Mochi. He sat up, wiped a layer of soybean dust off his face, and squinted at the smooth, gleaming, deeply unbothered ice. "But the swan in my imagination made it look so EASY. The swan never once landed on its bottom."

"That's because the swan in your imagination," said Red Bean, "has been practicing for years. You've been practicing for half a second."

Toasted Mochi did not find this comforting. He found it slightly annoying, which is often how true things feel right before you understand them.

---

He tried again.

This time he made it almost two whole skate-lengths before his back end overtook his front end, which is not how skating is supposed to work, and he spun in a fast little circle and sat down hard. *FWUMP.* More powder. More poof.

"That's two!" cheered Pineapple Cake, who had abandoned his fort to watch.

Toasted Mochi got up. He wobbled. He fell sideways before he'd even pushed off. *Fwump.* He got up. He fell backward. *Fwump.* He got up, fell forward, and bounced — because he was, after all, made of mochi — a delightful little *boing* across the ice before landing on his face.

He tried a *fourth* time, and this fall was the silliest yet: he slipped, grabbed at the air, slipped the other way, did a complicated little dance that looked almost on purpose, and then *boinged* off his own squishy bottom and rolled clean over twice. By the time he stopped, he looked less like a mochi and more like a small fuzzy snowball that had been rolled in powder and regret.

By the seventh fall, the cheerfulness had melted right off him like butter on a hot bun.

"It's no use," he mumbled, sitting in a sad puffy heap. "Swans don't fall down. The wind doesn't fall down. I have fallen down seven times and I am covered in my own powder and I look ridiculous." He sniffled. "I quit. I shall simply go home and be a non-skating mochi for the rest of my squishy life."

---

That was when Red Bean stood up, brushed off her thermos, and said something that made Toasted Mochi blink.

"Seven times," she said. "Do you know what that means?"

"That I'm terrible?"

"It means," said Red Bean, "you've learned seven different ways NOT to fall. That's seven lessons. Nobody glides on their first try. Not swans, not the wind, not even me — and I once practiced for three whole afternoons before I could stand up straight."

"You fell down?" Toasted Mochi gasped. Red Bean always seemed so calm and dignified, like a small wise pebble.

"I fell down," Red Bean said solemnly, "so many times the ice got dizzy."

Egg Tart waddled over, leaving a trail of tiny crumb-prints. "When I fall," she whispered, "I just pretend it's a sit-down break. Then I get up and the ice is my friend again."

And Pineapple Cake — well, Pineapple Cake just grinned his crumbly grin and shouted, "Here's the new rule! Every time you fall, we CHEER! Falling means you're TRYING! And trying is the bravest thing a snack can do!"

Toasted Mochi looked at his friends. Nobody was laughing AT him. They were cheering FOR him.

"...Okay," he said. "One more."

---

He pushed off.

He fell. *FWUMP.*

"YAAAAY!" roared his friends. "GREAT FALL! BEST ONE YET!"

Toasted Mochi blinked, then giggled — because honestly, a *FWUMP* and a cheer at the same time is a very funny combination — and got up.

He pushed off again. He wobbled, arms (well, edges) windmilling, and stayed up for three seconds before sitting down. *Fwump.*

"WONDERFUL!" cried Egg Tart. "Longer this time!"

And it was longer. That was the thing he started to notice. Each tumble, he understood the ice a tiny bit more. He learned that if he leaned too far forward, his face would meet the lake. He learned that if he stiffened up like a frightened cracker, he'd tip right over — but if he stayed soft and squishy and *bendy*, the way mochi is meant to be, he could ride out a wobble and stay standing.

Fall by fall, the lake was teaching him. And fall by fall, he was listening.

"Bend your knees!" called Red Bean — then she remembered. "Well. Bend your... corners."

So Toasted Mochi bent his corners. He let himself go soft and low and round, the way a dumpling settles into a steaming bowl, and suddenly the wobbles felt smaller. The ice felt less like a slippery enemy and more like a friend who was simply asking him to relax. He glided two whole skate-lengths. Then three. Then he got excited, stiffened up, and sat down again with a tremendous *FWUMP* and a powder-cloud so big that Egg Tart sneezed.

"Bless you!" he called from the floor.

"Bless YOU," Egg Tart giggled. "That was a five-pointer at least."

---

The afternoon stretched on. The sun rolled slowly across the sky, turning the snow to gold and the ice to glittering glass. The mountains glowed pink at their tips.

Toasted Mochi pushed off, and this time — this time! — he didn't fall.

He glided.

One smooth, wobbly, gloriously soft glide, soybean powder streaming behind him like a little yellow comet-tail. His friends went absolutely bananas. Pineapple Cake fell over from excitement, which meant now HE got cheered too.

"I'M DOING IT!" Toasted Mochi shrieked. "I'M HUÁBĪNG! I'M ACTUALLY SKATING!"

He carved a big looping curve. He did a tiny spin — only a little one, just a *swan-ish* one — and he laughed so hard a puff of powder shot out of his nose. He skated past Egg Tart and gave her a high-edge. He skated past Red Bean, who lifted her thermos in a quiet, proud salute.

"Come ON!" he called to his friends. "All of you! The ice is plenty big!"

And so they all clambered out onto the lake together. Egg Tart took tiny brave steps and squeaked with delight every time she didn't fall. Pineapple Cake skated like a runaway brick and crashed into the snowbank on purpose because he liked the *poof*. Red Bean glided in slow, dignified circles, sipping her **熱可可** as she went, which is honestly a very advanced skill. The frozen lake rang with giggles and *fwumps* and cheering, and the whole mountain seemed to grin down at the four little snacks making such happy, wobbly music on the ice.

And Toasted Mochi, in the middle of it all, realized he wasn't trying to skate like a swan anymore. He was skating like *himself* — soft, bouncy, a little ridiculous, and completely, gloriously free.

And here's the wonderful part. Toasted Mochi *still* fell down sometimes. *Fwump.* But now he didn't mind even a tiny bit. He'd bounce, giggle, hop right back up, and keep going. The falling and the gliding had become the same happy thing.

---

By the time the sky turned deep velvet-blue and the first stars peeked out over the peaks, Toasted Mochi was the fastest, freest, most fearless skater on the whole frozen lake — and certainly the most powdery.

He flopped down on the snowbank next to his friends, warm inside despite the cold, dusted head to toe in soybean snow.

"I almost quit," he said softly. "After only seven falls."

"And how many falls did it take in the end?" asked Red Bean.

Toasted Mochi thought about it. "I lost count somewhere around fifty."

"Fifty falls," said Red Bean, "and one beautiful glide. That's how it works. The falls don't get in the way of learning. The falls *are* the learning."

Toasted Mochi smiled and watched a single snowflake — maybe the same one from that morning — drift down and land right on his toasty nose.

"Tomorrow," he whispered, "I'm going to fall down at least a hundred times."

"Why a hundred?" asked Egg Tart.

"Because then," said Toasted Mochi happily, "imagine how well I'll skate."

---

## Goodnight Blessing

And so, little one, when something new feels wobbly and your bottom keeps meeting the floor — remember Toasted Mochi on his frozen lake, dusted in golden powder, falling and rising and falling again, until the falling turned to gliding. Every tumble is a tiny teacher. Every wobble means you're brave enough to try. So be soft, be squishy, be patient with yourself, and let each gentle *fwump* carry you closer to the glide. The ice is your friend. The stars are watching kindly. And tomorrow you'll be a little better than today.

Snuggle down now, warm and powdered-soft as mochi in the snow.

Goodnight.
