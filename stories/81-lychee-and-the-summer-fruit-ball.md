# Story 81: Lychee & the Summer Fruit Ball

**Main Character:** Lychee (荔枝)
**Guest Characters:** Mango, Passion Fruit
**Setting:** A glamorous fruit-orchard garden ball in the southern summer
**Theme:** True elegance is making everyone feel welcome.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 水果 (shuǐguǒ) = fruit

---

On the warmest, sweetest night of the southern summer, Lychee threw open her bumpy red door and announced, to absolutely no one in particular, "Darling, I am going to host the most DAZZLING ball this orchard has ever SEEN."

Then she fell into the punch bowl.

"That," said Lychee, climbing out dripping with mango juice and dignity, "was a rehearsal. A host must rehearse her entrances. Even the soggy ones." She wrung out her pearly-white insides, fluffed her bumpy crimson shell, and struck a pose under the fairy lights strung between the longan trees. "Tonight, every **水果** (shuǐguǒ) = fruit in the south will gather. There will be dancing. There will be sparkle. There will be a chandelier made entirely of dewdrops, because *of course* there will."

A firefly bumped into the dewdrop chandelier. It jingled like tiny bells.

"See?" sighed Lychee, hand on her shell. "Magnificent. I was born for this, darling. Born."

---

Now, you should know something about Lychee. She was glamorous to her very pit. She wore her rosy bumpy shell like a ballgown and her smooth white inside like the softest silk slip. She called everyone "darling," even the snails. *Especially* the snails. And she loved — truly, deeply, dramatically loved — a Good Party.

She also had a teeny, tiny, almost invisible flaw. Lychee adored *beautiful* things so much that, every now and then, she forgot to look past them. She'd organize a whole picnic around the prettiest blanket and forget to bring food. She'd decorate a hat so splendidly she couldn't fit it through the door. Her cousins called it "Lychee's little sparkle-blindness." Lychee called it "having standards, darling," and tossed her shell.

The Summer Fruit Ball was her masterpiece. She'd spent three whole days writing the guest list with a quill made from a single curled fern, pausing every few minutes to admire the way the fern caught the light.

"Mango!" she announced, scribbling. "Sporty, golden, magnificent. He'll dance every dance and lose his sweatband twice. Passion Fruit! Cheeky, wrinkly, the soul of mischief. He'll dare everyone to do something ridiculous before the moon is up. The Starfruit sisters! The glamorous grapes! The peonies of the orchard, the JEWELS of the south!"

She rolled the list up with a flourish and a satin ribbon. "Perfect. Flawless. Not a single thing forgotten."

But here is the funny thing about lists written in a hurry by someone who loves sparkle: they tend to be full of sparkle, and a little empty of everything else.

The guests arrived as the sky turned the color of peeled lychee — soft pink fading to plum. Mango bounded in first, of course, because Mango bounded everywhere.

"LYCHEE!" he boomed, golden and glowing, his sweatband already crooked. "This place looks INCREDIBLE. Bet you I can do a backflip into that punch bowl."

"You will do no such thing," said Lychee, kissing the air beside both his cheeks. "I already did. It's taken."

Passion Fruit rolled in behind him, wrinkly and purple and grinning like he knew a secret. "Heh. I dare somebody to do a backflip into the punch bowl."

"DONE," said Mango, and did. He surfaced with a slice of pineapple balanced on his head like a tiny crown and announced, "Refreshing! Ten out of ten! Who's next?"

"I am NOT getting in the punch bowl," said a glamorous grape, fanning herself.

"That's what I said," sighed Lychee. "Look how that turned out."

The whole orchard cheered. The Starfruit sisters arrived in a shimmer of five-pointed gowns. The glamorous grapes clustered together like a chandelier of their own. Somewhere, someone started a conga line, and a watermelon — far too large for a conga line — joined anyway and knocked over a daisy, who took it remarkably well. Lychee laughed so hard she nearly popped right out of her shell. The night was young, the dewdrop chandelier sparkled, the music drifted sweet over the orchard wall, and everything was, for one shimmering moment, exactly as splendid as she'd dreamed.

And then she heard it.

A small, polite cough at the garden gate.

Lychee turned. There, just outside the warm ring of fairy light, stood a little crowd of fruits. Plain ones. Humble ones. A knobbly brown Sugar Apple, lumpy as a sleepy dragon. A pair of small green Wax Apples, pale and crunchy and shy. A single quiet Loquat, soft and golden-fuzzy, holding her own little stem like a purse.

They were not on the list.

"Erm," said the Sugar Apple, very quietly. "Is this... the ball? We heard there was a ball. For all the **水果** of the south. We're, um. We're fruit too. Mostly."

Lychee's mouth opened. Then closed. Then opened again, which on a lychee looks like a very small, very fancy goldfish.

Because — oh, darling — she *had* forgotten them. Not on purpose. Never on purpose. But somewhere between the dewdrop chandelier and the fern-quill, she'd written down only the sparkly ones, the famous ones, the jewels. And these soft, plain, hopeful fruits had heard the music drifting over the orchard wall and come anyway, holding their stems like little purses, hoping there was room.

For one terrible second, Lychee almost said the thing fancy hosts say. *Oh, I'm so sorry, it's invitation only.* The words wobbled right on the tip of her shell.

But then she looked at the Loquat. The little Loquat was looking up at the dewdrop chandelier with her whole fuzzy face full of wonder, the way Lychee herself looked at chandeliers. And something in Lychee's pearly insides went *squish.*

"Darlings," she said. And her voice came out wobbly. "I am so glad you came."

---

It wasn't easy. Lychee was used to being the most dazzling thing in any garden, and being dazzling is a very loud sort of job. But making someone *feel welcome* — that turned out to be a quiet sort of job, and a much harder one.

The Sugar Apple stood at the edge, too shy to dance. So Lychee glided over, swept into a deep bow, and said, "Darling, you have the most *marvelous* texture. Like a sleepy little dragon. Will you do me the enormous honor of one dance? I shall lead. I always lead. It's a flaw."

The Sugar Apple turned the color of a sunset and said yes.

The Wax Apples hovered by the punch (now mostly Mango-flavored). They thought no one wanted to talk to two small crunchy nobodies. So Lychee marched right over and *gasped.*

"You two are PALE GREEN. Do you have ANY idea how impossible it is to be pale green and fashionable? I have been trying my whole life and I am STUCK being red. You're trendsetters, darlings. Trendsetters."

The Wax Apples crunched with delight. They had never been called trendsetters. They had mostly been called "those small green ones," and once, sadly, "are those decorations?" So Lychee fetched two of her very own bumpy-red bows, pinned one to each of them, and declared them "the pale green sensation of the season." Within minutes, three glamorous grapes were asking where the Wax Apples got their *look.*

But the Loquat — the Loquat was the hard one. She'd found a spot under the longan tree, half in shadow, and stayed there. Not sad, exactly. Just sure, deep in her soft golden heart, that a ball this grand could not possibly be for someone as small and fuzzy as her.

Lychee tried everything. A compliment. A joke. A dramatic twirl that knocked three dewdrops off the chandelier.

Nothing.

So Lychee did something she had never, in all her glamorous life, done at one of her own parties.

She sat down.

Right there on the grass, in her beautiful shell, beside the small quiet fruit in the shadows.

"Darling," she said softly, "may I tell you a secret?"

The Loquat nodded.

"Everyone thinks I was born knowing how to do this." Lychee gestured at the lights, the music, the whole sparkling night. "But the truth is, I fell into the punch bowl tonight. Headfirst. In front of nobody, thank goodness. And earlier this week I cried because I couldn't get the dewdrops to hang straight." She laughed, a little embarrassed. "Being fancy is easy. Being fancy is just shell. But making a garden feel like *home* for everyone in it — that's the real magic. And I almost forgot to do it. I almost forgot *you.*" She took the Loquat's soft little leaf-hand. "I'm sorry. Will you come dance? Not because the ball needs you to. Because *I* would like to dance with you. Truly."

The Loquat's eyes went shiny. "But I'm not... sparkly."

"Oh, darling," whispered Lychee. "Neither is the moon, really. It just glows because it knows it's loved."

And the little Loquat smiled — a wide, fuzzy, golden sunrise of a smile — and took Lychee's hand, and stood up.

---

What happened next, the orchard talked about for the rest of the summer.

Lychee led the Loquat right into the center of the dance floor, under the dewdrop chandelier, into the brightest pool of fairy light there was. And she called out, in her grandest, most theatrical voice:

"DARLINGS! May I present the GUEST OF HONOR!"

Mango skidded to a stop. Passion Fruit's grin froze.

The whole ball turned to look at the small, soft, fuzzy Loquat standing in the very heart of the light, blinking like she'd wandered into a dream.

And then — because the southern fruits are good fruits, deep down — they *cheered.* They cheered for the Loquat, and the Sugar Apple, and the pale green trendsetting Wax Apples. Mango lost his sweatband cheering. Passion Fruit dared everyone to give the humble fruits a standing ovation, and *everyone did,* which was the easiest dare he'd ever won.

"Heh," said Passion Fruit, rolling up beside Lychee. "You know, you forgot 'em. Off the list. I saw."

"I did," said Lychee, watching the Loquat twirl. She didn't make an excuse. A real host never does.

"But you fixed it," said Passion Fruit. "Made 'em the *most* welcome of all. That's slick, darling." He'd never called anyone darling before. It just slipped out.

Lychee laughed. "You know what I've decided? The most elegant thing in any garden isn't the chandelier. It isn't the gown, or the grand entrance, or even" — she winked — "the dramatic fall into the punch bowl. It's the moment the smallest guest feels like they belong at the very center of the light."

The music swelled. The fireflies dimmed and brightened like a hundred tiny heartbeats. And the whole orchard — sparkly and plain, jeweled and humble, every single **水果** of the south — danced together until the moon climbed high and the dew settled soft as sleep on every leaf.

It was, everyone agreed, the most dazzling ball the orchard had ever seen.

But the dazzle, this time, came from the inside.

## Goodnight Blessing
May you always have a place at the very center of the light, little one, just like the small soft Loquat under the longan tree. May you remember that you do not have to sparkle to be welcome, and that the kindest thing in any garden is a hand reaching out to say *come dance.* May your dreams be warm and your heart be wide enough to make room for everyone. Sleep now, sweet as a summer lychee, soft as fuzzy gold. The fireflies are blinking slow, and the moon is glowing because it knows it's loved. Goodnight.
