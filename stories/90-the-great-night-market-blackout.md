# Story 90: The Great Night-Market Blackout

**Main Character:** Grass Jelly (仙草)
**Guest Characters:** Aiyu, Starfruit, Yuè Bǐng (Mooncake)
**Setting:** A whole night market plunged into a sudden, gentle power outage
**Theme:** When the lights go out, friends become the light.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 停電 (tíngdiàn) = power outage

---

It happened right in the middle of the night market's loudest, blinkiest, most gloriously over-decorated hour.

One second, the whole street was a thousand neon signs shouting at once — pink dragons, blue stars, a giant yellow chicken flapping in lightbulbs. The next second?

*Blip.*

Dark.

Not scary-dark. More like somebody had pulled a soft gray blanket over the entire market and tucked it in.

A few squeaks. A dropped chopstick. The giant lightbulb chicken stopped flapping mid-flap, frozen forever in one ridiculous pose.

And in the very middle of it all, sitting cool as a cucumber in his black beanie, was **Grass Jelly** — an inky black-green cube of the chilliest, calmest dessert in all of Taiwan.

He didn't gasp. He didn't even blink (which was easy, because nobody could see him do it).

"Well," said Grass Jelly slowly, in his deadpan healer's voice. "**停電** (tíngdiàn) = power outage. That's the word for this, kiddos. Big island word for *the lights went night-night.*"

---

Now, you should know something about Grass Jelly. Most folks thought he was *too* calm. Suspiciously calm. The kind of calm that makes you wonder if he's secretly a wizard, or just hasn't had enough sleep to be worried.

But the truth was simpler. Grass Jelly had spent his whole life sitting quietly in a bowl, wobbling gently, watching everyone else run around being dramatic. He'd had a *lot* of practice being calm. It was basically his superpower.

Which was good, because all around him in the dark, panic was doing its best impression of a popcorn machine.

"WHO TURNED OFF THE SUN?" boomed a familiar voice. That was Shaved Ice, who was somewhere off to the left, definitely wearing sunglasses, definitely unable to see a single thing. "I CAN'T SEE MY OWN TOPPINGS. THIS IS A DISASTER OF FROSTY PROPORTIONS."

"Bro, chill," came another voice. "I literally cannot see you panicking, so it's kind of relaxing actually."

"Has anyone seen my left side?" That was Pudding, the wobbly caramel flan, wobbling somewhere nearby. "I think I jiggled it off when the lights went out. I get very jiggly under pressure."

Grass Jelly let the chorus of worried squeaks bubble for a moment. Then he raised one cool, dark, jiggly arm.

"Everybody," he said, not loudly, but in that low steady voice that somehow cut right through the noise. "Breathe in. Breathe out. Slurp first, worry later — that's not even my line, but it works."

A tiny shy voice piped up beside him. "Um. Grass Jelly? It's me. Aiyu."

---

Grass Jelly turned. There, glowing the softest, warmest amber — like a little lantern made of honey — was **Aiyu**, the translucent aiyu jelly cube. She was shy, and honest, and right now she was lit up from the inside like a nightlight someone had forgotten to turn off.

"You're... glowing," said Grass Jelly.

"Oh!" Aiyu looked down at herself and blushed, which somehow made her glow *brighter*. "I am? I didn't notice. I always thought I was just sort of... see-through and boring."

"Kiddo," said Grass Jelly — borrowing Niú Niú's word because it felt right — "you are the least boring thing in this entire market right now. You are the only thing anyone *can* see."

And it was true. In the gray dark, Aiyu's soft amber light spilled out in a cozy circle, and suddenly the dropped chopsticks and frozen chicken and squeaking desserts all stopped looking spooky. They just looked like... a night market, waiting.

"Hold still," Grass Jelly told her gently. "You're our first lantern."

Aiyu, who had never in her life been called a lantern, glowed so hard with happiness she nearly hummed.

---

But one little amber light couldn't fill a whole market. The dark stretched far down the lane, past the dumpling stalls and the boba shops and the place that sold socks shaped like fish (every night market has one).

That's when something twinkled, high up.

"WISH UPON ME!" called a dreamy voice from above. "Oh — sorry. Force of habit. Coming down!"

It was **Starfruit**, the five-pointed star-shaped fellow, dreamy and poetic and forever wishing on things. And tonight, in the dark, his pale-yellow points caught the last glow of the moon and shimmered like a real, actual, falling star.

He drifted down the lane, glowing softly at all five points, and everywhere he passed, the dark softened.

"I always wanted to be a star," Starfruit sighed happily, twirling slowly so his light swept the whole street. "I just didn't think it would happen during a **停電**. The universe is funny like that."

"Less twirling, more lighting," said Grass Jelly, deadpan, but there was a little smile hiding in his voice. "You're doing good, kiddo. Keep going."

Now there were two lights — Aiyu's warm amber circle near the middle, and Starfruit's drifting, twinkling glow sweeping down the lane. The squeaking had stopped. People were starting to *ooh* instead.

"Hey," whispered Pudding, who had found his left side again. "This is actually kind of... pretty?"

"Told you," said the Coconut Jelly surfer. "Dark's not so bad once you stop yelling at it, bro."

---

But Grass Jelly was counting in his cool, quiet head. *Two lights. Big market. Still some shadows back by the temple.*

And from those shadows, slow and graceful, came a glow unlike the others. Not amber. Not starlight-pale.

*Golden.*

Round and golden and soft, like — well, like the moon had decided to come down and stroll through the night market.

It was **Yuè Bǐng** (yuèbǐng) = mooncake, the elegant golden-brown pastry stamped with a beautiful flower pattern, glowing gently from within because of the rich lotus-and-egg-yolk moon hidden in his middle.

"Good evening, friends," said Yuè Bǐng, in his graceful, poetic, family-reunion voice. "I heard the lights went out. So I thought I would bring a little moon of my own." He glowed warmer. "Round like the moon, full of family — and tonight, the whole market is family, isn't it?"

Grass Jelly looked at the three of them — Aiyu's honey-amber, Starfruit's twinkling points, Yuè Bǐng's golden moon-glow — and for once, the cool calm healer felt something warm wobble right through his inky middle.

"Three lanterns," he said softly. "Now we're cooking."

"We're not actually cooking," whispered Aiyu. "The stoves are off too."

"It's a *figure of speech*, kiddo," said Grass Jelly, and Aiyu giggled, and the giggle echoed down the glowing lane like a bell.

---

Here is where Grass Jelly did the thing that nobody expected from the quietest cube in Taiwan.

He took charge.

"Alright," he called, his voice rolling steady through the warm dark. "Stoves are off. Signs are off. That just means it's the perfect night for the kind of dinner you can't have with the lights blaring." He raised both arms. "Everybody — bring your snacks to the middle. We're having a candlelight feast. Except the candles are *us*."

For one heartbeat, nobody moved.

Then Red Bean, the grandma bean with her ladle, called out, "Oh, I LIKE this plan!" — and the market *moved*.

Boba bounced over with a fresh batch of pearls. Tangyuan rolled in saying "we should all sit together, we, us, the whole family!" Douhua wobbled up with her silky ginger syrup glowing faintly in Yuè Bǐng's moonlight. Gua Bao started folding everyone gentle little hugs. Even Shaved Ice trudged over, grumbling that this was "the most dramatic dinner of his LIFE," which, from Shaved Ice, was the highest praise possible.

They arranged themselves in a big cozy ring. In the center sat the three glowing guests — Aiyu, Starfruit, Yuè Bǐng — shining like the warmest little campfire that never once felt hot.

And Grass Jelly, cool and dark and steady, sat right in the middle of the light, where the shadows would have been deepest.

---

It was, everyone later agreed, the coziest meal in the entire history of the night market.

Nobody could see well enough to argue about whose stall was best. Nobody's bright sign was shouting over anyone else's. There was just the soft amber, the twinkling points, the golden moon-glow, and the gentle slurp and crunch and giggle of friends eating together in a pool of light they'd made *themselves*.

"You know," said Aiyu shyly, "I think I like being a lantern better than being invisible."

"I think," said Starfruit dreamily, "I finally got my wish without even wishing it."

"I think," said Yuè Bǐng warmly, "this is what the moon sees every night, and now I understand why it never looks lonely."

And Grass Jelly — the deadpan healer, the calmest cube on the island — pulled his black beanie down a little and said, very quietly, "I think the lights can stay off as long as they want."

Just then, with a soft *blip-blip-WHIRRR*, every neon sign in the market sputtered back to life. The giant chicken flapped again. The dragons glowed pink.

The **停電** was over.

Everyone blinked in the sudden brightness.

And then — without anyone deciding to — they all groaned, just a little. Because the bright lights were nice and all... but they couldn't quite match the glow of three friends and one quiet cube in the middle of the dark.

"Same time next blackout?" said Grass Jelly.

"Same time next blackout," everyone agreed.

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, when your room goes dark tonight, remember Grass Jelly and his glowing friends. The dark isn't something to fear — it's just the quiet place where the warmest lights get to shine. You are someone's lantern, you know. When their day goes gray, your kindness glows amber and golden and bright. Snuggle down now, breathe slow, and let the soft dark tuck you in like a cozy gray blanket. Goodnight.
