# Story 58: The Lucky Cat & the Incense-Curl Wishes

**Main Character:** Tǔdì Gōng (Earth God)
**Guest Characters:** Starfruit
**Setting:** A warm Longshan-style temple at dusk, full of curling incense
**Theme:** Good wishes, like incense, rise gently and reach far.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 許願 (xǔyuàn) = to make a wish

---

At the exact moment the sun went gooey and orange and slumped behind the city like a tired egg yolk, a tiny grandfather no taller than a teapot stuck his head out of a little red temple-hat and sneezed.

"Ah — *ah* — bless this little corner of the island!" said **Tǔdì Gōng** (tǔ-dì gōng) = the Earth God, who had, in fact, sneezed himself fully awake. He blinked his rosy cheeks at the dusk. He smelled of incense and tangerines, which is the coziest smell there is, and he held a gentle wooden staff that he mostly used for scratching his back.

The temple around him was glowing. Red lanterns swayed. Curls of incense rose like lazy silver noodles, twisting toward the rafters. And on the windowsill, a golden cat statue lifted one paw up and down, up and down, *waving* at absolutely nobody.

"Evening, Lucky Cat," yawned the Earth God.

The cat did not answer. The cat never answered. The cat just kept waving, which the Earth God found extremely polite.

---

Now, you should know that this temple was where snacks came to **許願** (xǔyuàn) = to make a wish. They'd toddle in at dusk, light a stick of incense, whisper their biggest hopes, and toddle home feeling lighter. The Earth God loved wish-time best of all. He kept a little jar of lucky candies by his elbow and handed one to every wisher, because a wish goes down smoother with something sweet.

So when the doors creaked, he sat right up.

A small star-shaped someone wobbled in. Five golden-green points, a dreamy face, and an expression like a balloon that has just realized it's getting smaller. He had clearly practiced what he was going to say, and just as clearly forgotten every word of it the moment the doors creaked open.

He paused at the threshold. He smoothed down his points. He cleared his throat, which for a starfruit sounds like a tiny squeaky gate.

"Starfruit, kiddo!" the Earth God chuckled. "Come, come. You look like a fellow with a wish in his pockets."

Starfruit shuffled forward. He bowed his five points politely. He opened his mouth.

And then he closed it again.

"Go on," said the Earth God kindly.

Starfruit opened his mouth a second time. A tiny squeak came out. Then nothing.

"It's — it's nothing," Starfruit said at last, looking at his own pointy feet. "It's too small. It's silly. Everyone else who comes here wishes for *big* things. The dumplings wished to be the fluffiest in all Taipei. Shaved Ice wished to be TALLER THAN A SKYSCRAPER — he said it in capital letters, it echoed for an hour. Even Egg Waffle came in and wished for his very own carnival, then bowed three times to an audience that wasn't there." Starfruit sighed, and one of his points drooped. "My wish is so little it would be embarrassed to be a wish at all. It would probably hide behind the candle if I tried to light it. I shouldn't waste your incense on it."

The Earth God stroked his white beard. Under the table, something rumbled — but it was only a small, plump, painted-clay temple tiger rolling over in his sleep, dreaming of pork. He let out a "rawr" that was really just a big yawn, mumbled something about second dinner, and tucked his curly tail over his nose.

"Hmm," said the Earth God. "A wish too small to say, eh? Those are my favorite kind." He set down his staff and leaned in, all crinkled and kind. "You know, in all my years sitting in this little temple — and that's more years than there are noodles in a noodle shop — I've never once met a wish that was too small. I've met loud wishes and shy wishes and a few extremely damp ones from Pudding, who cries when he's happy. But never a small one. Smallness, kiddo, is just bigness wearing slippers."

Starfruit blinked.

"Tell you what — light your incense first. You can wish out loud later, or never. No rush in this temple. We move at the speed of steam here."

---

So Starfruit lit a single stick of incense.

It glowed at the tip like a tiny orange star — which felt right, him being a star and all — and a thin curl of smoke began to rise. Up it went, slow and silver, wobbling toward the rafters.

Starfruit watched it climb. "It'll just disappear," he mumbled. "Like my wish. Up into nothing."

"Watch closer," whispered the Earth God.

The smoke curled. It looped. It made a little spiral, then a bigger one. And the higher it floated, the wider it spread — until that one skinny thread had fanned out across the whole ceiling like a soft silver blanket, touching every beam, every lantern, every carved dragon's whisker.

"Huh," said Starfruit.

"One little curl," said the Earth God, "and it reached the *entire* ceiling. Small things rise far, kiddo. Farther than big loud things, sometimes. A shout bounces off the walls and comes right back. But a whisper of incense? It floats clean up to the moon if the night is quiet enough."

Starfruit watched the smoke spread. He had to admit, it didn't *look* like a thing that was disappearing. It looked like a thing that was quietly, patiently filling up an entire room without asking permission from anybody.

Starfruit's points perked up the tiniest bit.

That was when the Lucky Cat fell off the windowsill.

---

It went *CLONK*, then *bonk-bonk-bonk* across the floor, and rolled to a stop right at Starfruit's feet, still waving its paw — *up, down, up, down* — flat on its back like an overturned beetle.

"Oh dear," said the Earth God. "She gets enthusiastic at dusk."

"She's — she's *alive*?" Starfruit squeaked.

"Only at wish-time," said a small bright voice. The Lucky Cat blinked her painted eyes, hopped upright, and dusted off her golden fur with her non-waving paw. "The rest of the day I'm a statue. It's lovely. Great for the posture." She kept the other paw going — *up, down, up, down* — because she truly could not stop, the way you can't stop a hiccup. "Now. Who's got a teeny wish they're too shy to say? I LOVE those."

Starfruit went very pink, which for a starfruit means going very orange. "How did you —"

"Sweetie, I wave at this temple all day," said the Lucky Cat. "Do you know what I'm waving at? Wishes. Big ones come stomping in like buffalo. But the *small* shy ones — they hover by the door, afraid to come close. So I wave them in." She waved her paw — *up, down* — and somehow it did feel like a friendly *come here, it's okay*. "Every wave is for a wish nobody else thought was worth catching. I've been doing it so long my paw's worn a little groove in the air. Best job in the world. So out with it. Smallest wish you've got."

"Most folks," she added, leaning in like she was sharing a secret, "wish for more. More money, more height, more fluff. Almost nobody wishes for *less* than they think they deserve. The shy ones do. And the shy ones, between you and me, usually have the loveliest wishes hidden under all that worry."

Starfruit twisted his pointy fingers together.

"I just..." He took a breath. "I just want one person to look up at the night sky, see a star, and think of me. That's all. Just one. Just once." He winced. "See? Tiny. Pointless. Stars are everywhere and I'm only a fruit shaped like one."

There was a soft silence in the temple.

The incense kept curling.

The tiger snored.

And then the Earth God laughed — a slow, warm, slurpy chuckle that made all the lanterns wobble. "Pointless! Oh, kiddo. That's not a small wish. That's the kindest wish I've heard all year."

"It is?" said Starfruit.

"You didn't wish to be the *biggest* star," said the Lucky Cat, paw still going. "You didn't wish for fame, or to be taller than a skyscraper —" (somewhere far across town, Shaved Ice sneezed) "— you just wished to *matter to somebody*. A little. Quietly. Do you know how far a wish like that travels?"

---

The Earth God shuffled to the window and pushed it open. Cool dusk air drifted in, smelling of rain and dumplings and the river. He pointed his staff at the deepening sky.

"Look there," he said.

Starfruit looked. The first star of the evening had just blinked awake — a single pinprick of silver, all alone in the velvet blue.

"One star," said the Earth God. "Small. Quiet. Doesn't shout. Doesn't flex. Just shines its little shine. And tell me, kiddo — how many sailors have steered home by one star? How many sleepy children have made a wish on it? How many grandmothers have pointed at it and told a story?" He patted Starfruit's shoulder-point. "Small and far-reaching are the same thing, when the thing is *kind*. Your wish already came true the moment you made it. Because *I* looked up, and saw a star, and thought of *you*."

Starfruit's eyes went shiny.

"And me," said the Lucky Cat. "*Up, down, up, down* — that's me thinking of you, on repeat, forever. Frankly I can't think of anyone else right now. It's the paw. It's relentless."

Starfruit laughed — a real, watery, happy laugh — and all five of his points stood straight up at once, glowing faintly gold, like a real little star deciding to shine after all.

"Now then," said the Earth God, reaching for his candy jar. "Wishes go down sweeter with something sweet." He pressed a lucky candy into Starfruit's hand. It was wrapped in shiny paper printed with — what else — a tiny star.

"That's for you," said the Earth God. "For the small wish that turned out to be enormous."

---

Outside, the sky filled up with stars, one quiet shine at a time. The incense curl reached the rafters and slipped out the window and floated up, up, up, toward the moon — where (though Starfruit couldn't see it) a fluffy white rabbit paused her mochi-pounding to sniff the sweet smoke and smile.

Starfruit stayed a long while, watching the smoke rise and the lanterns sway and the Lucky Cat wave at every shy little wish that hovered by the door. And every single one, she waved gently inside.

"Same time tomorrow?" she asked, when at last he turned to go.

"Same time tomorrow," said Starfruit. And he meant it.

He stepped out into the soft blue night, looked up at the sky thick with stars, and — for the very first time — didn't feel small at all.

## Goodnight Blessing
May your wishes rise as gently as incense, my dear, and travel farther than you'll ever know. May someone, somewhere, look up at a quiet little star tonight and think warmly of you — because you matter more than you imagine, in ways too soft to see. The lanterns are dimming now, the tiger is snoring under the table, and the Lucky Cat is waving you off to sleep, paw going slow and sleepy. Tuck in close, breathe deep, and let your kindest little hope float up to the moon. No wish is ever too small to matter, and neither, sweet one, are you. Goodnight.
