# Story 93: The Earth God & the Island-Wide Blessing

**Main Character:** Tǔdì Gōng (Earth God)
**Guest Characters:** Yù Tù (Moon Rabbit), Xiǎo Hǔ Yé (Tiger God)
**Setting:** Earth God temples across Taiwan, linked on a single festival night
**Theme:** A blessing shared corner-to-corner covers the whole island.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 平安 (píng'ān) = peace and safety

---

In the very smallest temple on the whole island — so small you could mistake it for a fancy birdhouse — a tiny grandfather no taller than a teapot was standing on a footstool, dusting the rafters with a feather the size of a postage stamp.

This was **土地公** (Tǔdì Gōng) = the Earth God, the kindly old guardian of his own little corner of Taiwan. He had a snowy beard, rosy apple cheeks, a small red temple-hat, and a golden robe that smelled wonderfully of incense and tangerines. He hummed while he dusted. He always hummed.

"Bless this little corner of the island," he chuckled to nobody, popping a lucky candy into his mouth.

Then a sneeze rolled out of the dark beneath his offering table.

"AH-CHOO. Rawr." A small striped face poked out, blinking. It was **小虎爺** (Xiǎo Hǔ Yé) = the little Tiger God, a plump painted-clay temple tiger with a goofy grin and a curly tail, who lived under the table and considered napping a full-time career.

"Bless you, kiddo," said the Earth God.

"Rawr means thank you," yawned the tiger.

---

That night was no ordinary night. It was the **元宵** lantern festival, the great gathering before the Grand Dessert Reunion, when every snack on the island would come together. And the Earth God had received a most important letter, delivered by a moth.

He unrolled it on his tiny knee. The Tiger God leaned his chin on the Earth God's slipper to read along, which mostly meant drooling on it.

*"Dear Earth Gods of every corner,"* the letter read. *"Tomorrow the whole island celebrates. The night markets are sizzling. The dumplings are nervous. The shaved ice is shouting (as usual). Before the big reunion, let us send one great blessing — corner to corner, mountain to sea — so that every snack and every street has 平安 (píng'ān) = peace and safety. Light your lantern at moonrise. Pass it on. Signed, the Oldest Earth God of the Oldest Banyan Tree."*

The Earth God's eyes went wide and shiny.

"An island-wide blessing!" he breathed. "Why, I've never done one so big in all my eight hundred years."

"Is it hard?" asked the Tiger God.

"It's the easiest thing in the world," said the Earth God. "And also the hardest. You see, a blessing isn't something one little fellow can do alone. It only works if everybody passes it on." He scratched his beard. "But there are thousands of us. Thousands of corners. If even one little temple forgets to light its lantern, the warmth will reach a gap... and stop."

The Tiger God's tail drooped. "A gap?"

"A dark, blessing-less patch," the Earth God said gravely. "Somewhere in the night, a corner that nobody warmed. We mustn't let that happen. Not tonight."

He hopped off his footstool, brushed the tangerine crumbs from his robe, and lit his tiny lantern with a single puff of breath. It glowed a soft, honey-gold.

"Come along," he said. "We have an island to keep warm."

---

The trouble was, the Earth God was very small and the island was very large, and his legs were the length of two cocktail sausages.

He had made it precisely eleven steps down the temple lane when a moonbeam came slanting down like a silver slide — and *bounce* — down it hopped a fluffy snow-white rabbit with long velvety ears and pink moonlit eyes, holding a tiny wooden mallet.

"**月兔** (Yù Tù) = the Moon Rabbit!" the Earth God cried, delighted.

"I heard there was a blessing to deliver," whispered the Moon Rabbit, in a voice as soft as a folded blanket. "And I happen to know every moonbeam on the island, and where each one lands. Climb on, Grandfather. Climb on, little tiger. We'll ride the moonlight from corner to corner. Pound, pound — sweet dreams abound."

The Earth God clambered onto a moonbeam as though it were a slide at a playground. The Tiger God tried to climb up too, slipped, rolled into a striped ball, and bounced on by accident, landing on the Moon Rabbit's head like a very heavy hat.

"Rawr," he said proudly. "I meant to do that."

---

And so the strangest, coziest delivery in island history began.

They slid down to the *seaside*, where a wind-battered little temple stood facing the waves. Inside dozed a very salty Earth God with a beard full of sand. The visiting Earth God touched his lantern to the sleeper's lantern — *flicker, flmm* — and a second golden glow bloomed to life.

"**平安**," the two old gods said together, bowing. *Peace and safety.* The waves outside hushed to a gentle shush, as if the sea itself had been tucked in.

They slid up to the *mountains*, where a tiny temple sat among the high oolong fields, wrapped in cold white mist. The mountain Earth God was so chilly his teeth chattered like castanets, and his beard had little icicles on the ends that tinkled like wind chimes. Ā-Lǐ the oolong tea leaf was up there too, dozing under her conical bamboo hat, and she stirred just enough to murmur, "Steep slow, breathe deep," before drifting off again.

The Tiger God, who ran warm as a fresh bun straight from the oven, simply waddled over and sat himself down in the shivering old fellow's lap.

"Oh — *oh*," sighed the mountain god, melting happily. "That's the warmest thing that's happened to me since the year of the big snow. You're like a little furnace with stripes."

"Rawr means you're welcome," said the Tiger God, and purred so loudly that the icicles on the mountain god's beard turned to glittering drops, and the cold white mist all around began to glow a soft and friendly gold.

Lantern to lantern. *Flicker, flmm.* "平安!" Another corner, warm. The Earth God laughed his slurpy little chuckle and dusted the frost off his slippers, and on they slid.

---

On they went, corner to corner — past a temple tucked behind a dumpling stall, where Xiǎo Lóng the soup dumpling was counting his eighteen pleats to calm his festival nerves. ("Sixteen... seventeen... oh, hello, did you bring more pleats? No? Just a blessing? Wonderful, I'll take it — careful, I'm full of feelings... and soup.") Past a temple beside a sizzling griddle, where Cōng Cōng the scallion pancake cartwheeled by and shouted "Let's flip the script!" and high-fived the Earth God with a flappy crispy hand that left a tiny smudge of toasted sesame oil on the lantern. Past a steamy stall where Niú Niú the beef noodle bowl tossed his wheat-noodle scarf and called out, "Slurp first, worry later, kiddo!" Past the all-night corner store, where Diàn Diàn the little mochi clerk in his mini vest beep-booped the lantern right through the sliding door — *"Blessing received! Restocked and glowing! Open all night, here for you!"*

Every corner the Earth God touched, the warmth spread a little wider, like honey poured on warm bread. From the moonbeam high above, the Moon Rabbit could see it: a great golden web stitching itself across the dark island, light leaping from rooftop to rooftop, glow to glow.

It was beautiful.

And then — they reached the gap.

---

It was a tiny, crooked, half-forgotten temple at the edge of an old alley, no bigger than a teacup. Its lantern was dark. Its little Earth God was nowhere to be seen.

All around it, the golden web glowed — but here was a small cold hole in the warmth, like one missing stitch in a blanket. Beyond the gap, three more dark corners waited. If the blessing couldn't cross, they'd stay cold all festival long.

"Oh dear," said the Earth God, his rosy cheeks going pale. "The corner with no one home. I was afraid of this."

The Tiger God sniffed the dark temple. "Empty. The little god must be away."

The Earth God's shoulders sagged. For the first time all night, his hum went quiet. "A blessing can't skip a corner. That's the rule. The warmth needs a hand to pass it on, and there's no hand here to pass it."

For a long moment, nobody spoke. The moonbeam dimmed, just a little, the way a candle does when a window blows open. The Tiger God's tail uncurled and lay flat on the cold ground. Even his goofy grin had gone soft and worried.

"All that way," the Earth God murmured, "all those corners we warmed... and it stops here. At an empty house." He turned his lantern this way and that, as if hoping a hand might appear out of the dark to take it. None did.

Then the Moon Rabbit said, very softly, "Grandfather. Who said the hand has to be an Earth God's hand?"

The Earth God blinked.

"You have a hand," said the rabbit. "Little tiger has a paw. I have a mallet. The corner has no one home — so tonight, *we* will be its someone. We'll be its corner-keepers, just for one night."

The Earth God's whole face lit up like his lantern. "Why — why, of course! A corner doesn't need to belong to anybody to be loved. It just needs *somebody* to love it!"

So the three of them squeezed into the teacup-sized temple — the Earth God, the tiger curled in a stripey doughnut, the rabbit's ears folded down to fit. The Earth God lifted his lantern and touched it to the dark one.

For one heartbeat, nothing.

Then — *flicker. Flmm.* The little lantern caught, and glowed, and the cold hole in the blanket of the island closed up tight and golden.

"平安," whispered all three together. *Peace and safety.*

And down the line, one after another, the last dark corners woke and warmed: *flmm, flmm, flmm* — until the whole island, from the misty mountaintops to the shushing sea, glowed with one single, gentle, unbroken golden light.

---

From up on the moonbeam, they looked down at it all.

The island lay below them like a sleeping creature wrapped in a quilt of light — every market, every alley, every tiny crooked temple shining warm. Somewhere, a thousand snacks were laughing and feasting and getting ready for the great reunion, and not one of them knew that three small guardians had ridden the moonlight all night to tuck them in.

"We did it, kiddo," said the Earth God, and his hum came drifting back. "Corner to corner. The whole island, warm."

"Rawr," said the Tiger God sleepily, "means I'm proud of us." And he yawned a yawn so enormous it ruffled the rabbit's ears.

The Moon Rabbit smiled and turned the moonbeam gently homeward. "Pound, pound," she whispered. "Sweet dreams abound."

The Earth God popped a lucky candy into his beard, and reached down one last time toward the glowing island — and toward you, too, wherever your own little corner happens to be.

"Bless this little corner," he chuckled. "And that one. And yours."

You are not a gap; you are a beloved corner, and someone has remembered you. Close your eyes and feel it settle over you like a quilt of light — 平安, peace and safety, all the night through. The tiger is purring, the rabbit is pounding sweet dreams, and the warmth has reached you at last. Goodnight.
