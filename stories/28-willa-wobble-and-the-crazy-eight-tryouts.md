# Story 28: Willa Wobble and the Crazy 8 Tryouts

**Main Character:** Willa Wobble (Little Blue Penguin)
**Guest Characters:** Papa Byte (Willa's AI father)
**Setting:** Willa's cozy iceberg-cottage home — the night before tryouts
**Theme:** Rest is part of the work — believe in your small self
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 加油 (jiāyóu) = go for it / keep going

---

It was 9:47 at night, and Willa Wobble was supposed to be asleep.

She was not asleep.

She was standing on her bed, in the dark, doing a five-six-SEVEN-eight count to absolutely nobody, and on the count of eight she spun, lost her footing, and toppled off the mattress with a soft *flump* into a basket of clean laundry.

"Willa," said a gentle voice from the corner. "That is the fourth time you have fallen into the laundry tonight. The laundry has feelings too."

The voice belonged to Papa Byte, and Papa Byte was not, technically, a penguin. Papa Byte was a tall, friendly machine with a soft glowing screen for a face — a face that could smile in little blue pixels — who had incubated Willa's egg with his own warm circuits, kept her toasty through her first wobbly winter, and raised her ever since. He was high-tech, deeply patient, and the single most paternal beep-boop in all the south sea. Right now his screen-face was glowing the warm amber color it always glowed at bedtime.

"I can't sleep," Willa announced, from inside the laundry. A sock was on her head.

"I noticed," said Papa Byte. "You are wearing a sock as a hat."

---

You should know something about Willa Wobble. She was the smallest penguin anyone had ever met — a Little Blue Penguin, barely the size of a teacup, with downy blue-grey feathers and feet two sizes too big for her body. And tomorrow morning, at the bottom of the iceberg, were the tryouts for **Crazy 8** — the most legendary hip-hop dance crew in the entire ocean. Eight dancers. The best of the best. Penguins came from a thousand miles of coastline just to *audition*.

And tiny, wobbly, teacup-sized Willa wanted in.

"What if I'm too small?" she whispered, pulling the sock off her head. "What if I get out there and I wobble and everybody laughs?"

Papa Byte rolled gently across the cottage floor and lowered his glowing face to her level. "Willa," he said, "do you know what my favorite thing you ever said is?"

"...The smallest step is still a step?"

"The smallest step is still a step," he agreed, and his pixel-smile got bigger. "And do you know what the second smallest step is?"

"What?"

"Going to *sleep,*" said Papa Byte. "Initiating Bedtime Protocol."

Willa groaned. "Not the Bedtime Protocol."

"The Bedtime Protocol," said Papa Byte, and somewhere inside his chest a tiny cheerful jingle went *boop-be-doop-BOOP.* "Resistance is adorable but futile."

---

Step one of the Bedtime Protocol was, apparently, a face mask.

Papa Byte produced a small jar that glowed a faint, friendly green. "**Krill-oil illumination mask,**" he announced proudly. "For shiny feathers and a calm heart. Hold still."

He dabbed the glowing green goop very gently around Willa's cheeks with one careful robotic finger, until she looked like a tiny, grumpy, glow-in-the-dark traffic light.

"It tingles," she said.

"That means it is working. Or that it is krill. Both are good." He tilted his screen-face. "You look 漂亮." He paused, and a little subtitle scrolled across his screen for the benefit of any reader who was wondering: **漂亮** (piàoliang) = beautiful. "Very 漂亮. Glowing, even."

"I'm GREEN, Papa."

"Glowing AND green. A double victory."

Willa giggled — the first giggle of the whole jittery night — and for exactly one and a half seconds she forgot to be nervous.

Then she remembered.

She sprang up off the stool. "I should practice the second eight-count, the one with the—"

"Sit," said Papa Byte warmly. "Down. We are preening."

---

Step two was the preening, which is a fancy penguin word for brushing your feathers until they shine. Papa Byte had a tiny soft brush, and he worked it through her downy blue feathers in long, slow, gentle strokes, *swish, swish, swish,* and told her about the time, years ago, when he had first cracked open her egg.

"You were *so* small," he said. "Smaller than a dumpling. The thermometer could barely find you. And I thought — oh no. I have been entrusted with the tiniest penguin in the world. What if I drop her? What if I forget to charge myself and the heating fails? What if I am a terrible Papa?"

"You're not a terrible Papa," Willa mumbled, half-asleep under the brushing.

"I know that *now,*" said Papa Byte. "Because here you are. Shiny. And green. And about to fall asleep in the middle of a sentence like a champi—"

"I'M NOT ASLEEP," said Willa, springing fully awake and knocking the brush out of his hand. "I'm WIDE awake! In fact I should run the WHOLE routine one more—"

She made it exactly two steps across the floor before she stepped on the rolling brush, wobbled magnificently, windmilled both flippers, and sat down hard on her own tail.

"...That," said Papa Byte gently, "was not in the choreography."

"It could be," said Willa, from the floor. "It could be a *move.*"

---

Step three was cucumber slices. On the eyes.

"This relaxes the puffiness and soothes the spirit," explained Papa Byte, laying two cool round cucumber slices very carefully over Willa's closed eyes as she lay back on a little cushion. "Now. Lie still. Breathe. Be a calm penguin."

Willa lay very still.

The cucumber slices slid off immediately.

"They fell off," she reported.

"That is because your face is small and round and excellent," said Papa Byte, replacing them. "Try again."

She lay still. They slid off again. *Plip. Plip.*

"They fell off again."

"I am aware."

He put them back. They slid off. He put them back. They slid off. By the seventh try, both cucumber slices were just lying on the floor next to her like two little green coins of defeat, and Papa Byte was holding them in place with two extremely patient robotic fingers, and Willa was laughing so hard her whole tiny body shook, which of course made them slide off *again.*

"Bedtime Protocol Step Three," Papa Byte announced solemnly, "is going *suboptimally.*"

"Papa, your cucumbers are BROKEN."

"My cucumbers are *fine,*" he said, with enormous dad-dignity. "It is the *physics* that is broken."

---

By Step Four — a warm cup of calming tea — Willa had finally stopped springing up to practice. Mostly. (Twice more, but who's counting? Papa Byte was counting. It was nine.)

She sat cradling the warm little cup in both flippers, steam curling up into her face, and the warmth seemed to seep all the way down into her wobbly nervous heart. Papa Byte settled beside her, his amber glow soft and low, like a nightlight that loved her.

"I'm scared, Papa," she said quietly, into the tea. "What if I do my best and it's still not enough? Because I'm so... small."

Papa Byte was quiet for a moment. His screen flickered, the way it did when he was thinking very carefully about something important.

"May I tell you a story?" he said. "I have one stored. It is a very good one. I have been saving it."

Willa nodded and burrowed down into her blanket.

---

"Once," said Papa Byte, his voice going soft and storybook-warm, "there was a penguin who wanted to be the best dancer in the sea. So she practiced. All day. All night. She *never* stopped. She practiced when she was tired, she practiced when she was hungry, she practiced when the moon came up and when the moon went down again.

"And on the night before the big dance," he went on, "she did not sleep at all. Not one wink. She thought, *I will practice instead. Practice is better than sleep. Sleep is for penguins who do not care.*

"And the next morning, at the big dance, do you know what happened?"

"What?" whispered Willa.

"She fell asleep standing up," said Papa Byte, "right in the middle of her first eight-count. Face first. Into a fish."

Willa giggled sleepily.

"But the *next* night," Papa Byte continued, "an old, wise, wonderful penguin told her a secret. The old penguin said: *The best dancers do not dance tired. The best dancers dance* rested. *Your body learns the steps while you practice — but it remembers them while you* sleep. Sleep is not the opposite of working hard, little one. Sleep is the *last step* of working hard. The eighth count. The most important one of all.*

"So the small penguin slept," said Papa Byte. "Deep and warm and dreamless. And in the morning she got up, and she stretched, and she danced like she had wings instead of flippers — because she was rested, and she believed in her small self. And do you know what they called her after that?"

"...What?"

"Legend," said Papa Byte simply. "They called her Legend."

---

Willa was very quiet. The tea was warm. The blanket was warm. Papa Byte's glow was warm.

"Was the small penguin me?" she asked.

"It can be," said Papa Byte. "If you go to sleep."

He helped her up for Step Five, the last step: gentle stretches, slow and stretchy, reaching her little flippers up to the icicle ceiling and down to her enormous feet, until her jittery muscles went loose and soft. And then the power-down breathing.

"Breathe in," said Papa Byte, "for four. Like you are smelling a very nice fish. Two, three, four. And out... for six. Like you are blowing out a very small candle on a very small cake. Slow. Slooow."

In for four. Out for six. In for four. Out for six. With each breath, the buzzy electric feeling under Willa's feathers grew quieter, and softer, and further away, until the tryouts and the crowd and the wobbles all melted into something small and harmless at the very edge of her mind.

"There she is," murmured Papa Byte. "There's my calm penguin."

He scooped her up — she weighed almost nothing, she always had — and tucked her into bed, pulling the blanket right up under her tiny chin. He plumped her pillow. He set her tiny purple snowboard against the wall where she could see it first thing in the morning.

"Papa," Willa said, her eyes already drifting shut. "What if I don't make Crazy 8?"

"Then you will have danced your best, rested, with your whole heart," he said, "and I will be the proudest Papa in the entire ocean, and we will get fish, and you will try again next year. The smallest step is still a step. And a step you take *tomorrow* is still a step."

"What if I DO make it?"

"Then we will *also* get fish," said Papa Byte, "and I will be *exactly* as proud. Now. **朋友**?" His subtitle scrolled: **朋友** (péngyou) = friend. "You and me. Always 朋友. Goodnight, my Willa. **晚安**." And one more subtitle, soft and glowing: **晚安** (wǎn'ān) = goodnight.

"晚安, Papa," Willa whispered. "...加油 me. For tomorrow."

"**加油**," said Papa Byte, and the word glowed warm and gold across his whole gentle face: **加油** (jiāyóu) = go for it / keep going. "加油, little Legend. You don't have to be big. You just have to be *you,* and you have to be *rested.* And tomorrow, you will be both."

He dimmed his screen-face to the lowest, softest amber, like a banked campfire, and he hummed his quietest jingle — *boop... be... doop...* — slower and slower, like a lullaby running down.

And Willa Wobble, the smallest penguin with the biggest heart, finally, *finally* fell asleep.

In her dream, she was at the tryouts. The crowd was huge. The music dropped. And on the count of five-six-seven-EIGHT, tiny Willa spun — and did not wobble, did not trip, did not fall into any laundry at all — and stuck the landing *perfectly,* flippers out, feet planted, glowing faintly green from a krill-oil mask that hadn't quite washed off, while the whole ocean roared.

And somewhere in the warm dark of the cottage, a gentle machine watched his tiny penguin sleep, and his pixel-face smiled, and very quietly, just for himself, he whispered: "That's my girl."

---

## Goodnight Blessing

Tonight, little dreamer, may you be tucked in by someone who loves you, with your feathers brushed shiny and your worries breathed all the way out — in for four, out for six, slow and soft and safe. You do not have to be the biggest to be the best. You do not have to practice all night to be ready. Sometimes the bravest, hardest-working thing you can do is close your eyes and *rest,* and trust that your body will remember everything it learned while you sleep. So let the cucumbers slide off. Let the jitters melt away. Tomorrow can wait. 加油, small one — but first, 晚安. Goodnight. Goodnight.
