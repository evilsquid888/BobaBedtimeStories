# Story 63: Boba & the Train Around the Island

**Main Character:** Boba (波霸 bōbà) — Tapioca Pearl
**Guest Characters:** Pái Pái (Chicken Cutlet), Cōng Cōng (Scallion Pancake)
**Setting:** A round-the-island railway journey aboard a cozy night train
**Theme:** The journey is the best part — bounce through it.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 火車 (huǒchē) = train

---

Boba had exactly one ticket, one tiny backpack, and one enormous idea.

"I'm going to ride the **火車** (huǒchē) = train ALL the way around the island," she announced to the empty platform, bouncing on her toes — which, since she was a perfectly round, glossy black tapioca pearl, meant she bounced on her *whole self*. "Around! The whole! Island!"

The night train pulled in with a sleepy *whoooo*, glowing yellow from every window like a string of warm dumpling-lanterns laid on its side. It was an old, friendly train, the kind that rocked you gently and smelled faintly of tea and seat cushions. Steam curled off its roof into the dark blue **晚上** (wǎnshàng) = evening.

"Last call!" called the conductor, a kindly pudding who wobbled when he checked tickets. "Round-the-island night service, departing NOW!"

Boba did not walk aboard. Boba never walked anywhere if bouncing was an option.

She bounced.

*Boing — boing — BOING* — straight up the steps, down the aisle, and into a window seat with a soft little *plop*.

"Let's bounce," she whispered to herself, grinning at the dark glass. "All. The way. Around."

The doors hissed shut. The whistle blew. And the cozy night train began to roll.

For the first while, Boba had the carriage almost to herself, which was a little lonely — but Boba was not the lonely-staying type. She pressed her face to the window and watched Taiwan slide by: rice fields silver under the moon, sleeping villages, a temple with one red lantern still glowing like an ember. A river curled past, holding the moon's reflection so still it looked painted on. The train clacked out a rhythm that sounded, if you listened closely, exactly like *let's-bounce, let's-bounce, let's-bounce.*

She practiced bouncing in time with it — small bounces, polite ones, so she wouldn't wake anybody. *Clack.* Bounce. *Clack.* Bounce. It was, she decided, the single most relaxing thing a tapioca pearl could possibly do on a Tuesday night, except possibly floating in warm tea, which was a close second.

The train slowed. A bell went *ding*.

"**第一站**" (dì-yī zhàn) = "first stop!" announced the pudding conductor, wobbling so hard he nearly poured himself onto the floor.

And onto the platform, under the buzzing station light, stood the biggest, crunchiest, most confident snack Boba had ever seen.

It was a fried chicken cutlet — golden, dusted with plum powder and pepper, and frankly *bigger than her own face.* She flexed her crispy edges like a superhero cape and strode aboard as if the train had been waiting just for her (which, technically, it had).

"BIGGER THAN YOUR FACE," the cutlet boomed, "BIGGER THAN YOUR WORRIES! Name's Pái Pái. This seat taken?"

"It is now!" said Boba, scooting over. "I'm Boba. I'm riding the **火車** all the way around the island!"

Pái Pái dropped into the seat with a satisfying *crunch.* "All the way around, huh? Bold. I respect bold. I AM bold." She lowered her voice to a stage whisper that could still be heard three carriages away. "Between you and me, kiddo — I get a little nervous on trains. Big girl, small seat. But I'd never let the crowd see that. A champion always looks calm."

"You can look calm," Boba said warmly, "AND be nervous. I bounced up those steps so fast because I was a *teeny* bit scared I'd miss it."

Pái Pái stared at her. Then she laughed — a great crackly laugh that shed two flakes of golden crust. "I like you, round one. Bumpy roads ahead — let's ride 'em together."

*Ding.* "**第二站**" (dì-èr zhàn) = "second stop!"

The doors opened. Something cartwheeled in.

It cartwheeled *in,* down the aisle, off an armrest, off the luggage rack, and landed in a perfect spinning bow right in front of them — a flaky, golden, spiral-layered scallion pancake with green freckle-flecks and flappy crispy hands held high.

"TA-DA," said the pancake. "Cōng Cōng, professional flipper, part-time genius. Did you SEE that landing? Stuck it. Stuck it like sticky rice." He held up a flappy hand. "High five! Don't leave me hanging, I've got *layers* of feelings here."

Boba slapped his hand. So did Pái Pái, very gently, because her high-fives could knock a snack into next Tuesday.

"We're going around the whole island," Boba explained.

"The whole — " Cōng Cōng's freckled eyes went wide. "Around the WHOLE island? On the **火車**? Then buckle up, my crunchy comrades, because I have approximately nine hundred train jokes and we are going to need ALL of them." He struck a pose. "Let's flip the script!"

And so the cozy night train rolled on with three friends aboard instead of one — which Boba decided, watching the lights slide past, was already nine hundred times better than riding alone.

The trouble (because every good train ride needs a *tiny* bit of trouble) began somewhere around the third stop.

The train climbed up into the mountains, where the air turned cool and misty and the tracks curled around the slopes like a sleepy dragon's tail. And right at the curviest, swirliest part — the train *swayed.*

It swayed left. It swayed right. It swayed in a great slow loop-de-loop kind of way that made the teacups rattle and the luggage shuffle.

"Whoa," said Boba.

"WHOA," said Pái Pái, who immediately did NOT look calm. Her crispy edges went a bit limp. "Okay. Okay. Big train. Tiny mountain road. This is — this is FINE, I'm a champion, champions love mountains — " She squeezed her eyes shut. "Why is the floor a *boat* now?"

"Pái Pái's a little train-sick," Cōng Cōng whispered to Boba. "I've seen this before. My cousin got like this on a ferry. He turned the color of cabbage. Then he turned the color of *sad* cabbage, which is worse."

Boba looked at her big crunchy friend, who was clutching the armrest as though it were the only solid thing left in the universe. And Boba had an idea — because Boba was very good at ideas, and even better at bouncing.

"Pái Pái," she said. "Don't fight the wobble."

"Don't — *what?*"

"Don't fight it! When the train sways, sway WITH it. Like this!" And Boba began to bounce in time with the train — *left* as it leaned left, *right* as it leaned right — turning the scary swaying into a goofy little dance. "If you fight the bounce, the bounce wins. So you bounce TOO. Then it's not a wobble — it's a *party.*"

Cōng Cōng caught on instantly, because catching on instantly was his entire personality. "She's RIGHT! It's not a sick train, it's a DANCE train! Everybody do the Mountain Wiggle!" He cartwheeled down the aisle, layers flapping, and started a conga line of exactly two people, then one, then himself, but with great enthusiasm.

Pái Pái cracked one eye open. Watched Boba bounce-left, bounce-right. Watched Cōng Cōng wiggle into the luggage rack and bounce out again, undefeated.

Slowly — *very* slowly — she started to sway.

Left. Right. Left.

"Hey," she said. "*Hey.* This isn't so bad."

"BIGGER THAN YOUR FACE," Boba cheered, "BIGGER THAN YOUR WORRIES!"

Pái Pái grinned, her crispy edges crackling back to full crunch. "You're stealing my catchphrase, round one."

"I'm BORROWING it. With swaying."

By the time the train wiggled out of the mountains, the three of them were laughing so hard that the pudding conductor had to wobble over and politely ask them to be a *little* quieter, please, because some of the snacks were trying to sleep — and then he stayed for a quick Mountain Wiggle himself before wobbling on, jiggling all the way down the aisle.

"You know the funny thing?" Pái Pái said, settling back into her seat, completely un-wobbly now. "I almost didn't get on tonight. Stood on that platform thinking, *a champion shouldn't ride a train that makes her queasy.*" She crunched thoughtfully. "But if I'd stayed home, I'd have missed the best dance of my entire life."

"That," said Boba, "is the most important thing anyone has said all night."

"It really is," agreed Cōng Cōng, upside down in the luggage rack. "And I've said a LOT of things tonight."

After that, every stop was a gift.

*Ding.* A bouncy crowd of popcorn-chicken nuggets piled aboard, giggling and finishing each other's sentences, and immediately started a snack band using teaspoons and one upside-down teacup for a drum. "MORE CRUNCH, MORE FUN!" they chorused, and nobody could disagree, because it was both crunchier AND more fun. *Ding.* A pillowy gua bao bun climbed on, took one look at the laughing carriage, and folded three snacks into a single warm hug. "Come on in," she said softly, "I'll fold you a hug," and Boba bounced straight into the middle of it, because Boba never said no to a hug.

*Ding.* A wise purple chunk of taro settled into a corner seat, adjusted his reading glasses, and said, "Good company is the best scenery," which everyone agreed was extremely true and also extremely Taro. Cōng Cōng tried to fit four train jokes into the next thirty seconds and only landed two of them, but landing two out of four while cartwheeling is, frankly, a respectable score.

The carriage filled up, stop by stop, snack by snack, until it was less a train car and more a rolling, glowing, laughing party on wheels — old friends and brand-new ones squished happily together, sharing seats and stories and exactly one very large chicken cutlet's worth of crumbs.

Boba pressed her face to the window. The sky was just beginning to soften at the edges, a thin ribbon of pink along the sea, because the night train had carried them clear around the island while they danced and laughed and made new friends.

"We're almost back where we started," she said softly.

Cōng Cōng flopped down beside her, all cartwheeled out. "Aw. Does that make you sad, round one? The trip's nearly over."

Boba thought about it. She thought about the whistle and the wobble and the Mountain Wiggle, the high-fives and the conga line and the warm yellow windows, all the snacks who'd been strangers at stop one and were family by sunrise.

"You know what?" she said. "I thought the best part would be *getting* all the way around. Arriving. Finishing." She watched the pink sea slide by. "But the best part wasn't the finish. The best part was — *all of it.* The whole bouncy in-between."

Pái Pái, half-asleep and entirely un-seasick, mumbled, "Smart pearl."

"The journey," said Taro from his corner, eyes twinkling, "is the part you remember."

The cozy night train rolled gently into the very first station, where it had all begun, just as the sun spilled gold across the platform. The whistle gave one last sleepy *whoooo.* And every snack in the carriage — old and new, crispy and round, big and small — bounced out together into the morning, already planning when they could ride the **火車** all the way around again.

Because the best trips, Boba had learned, aren't measured in stops.

They're measured in friends.

## Goodnight Blessing

Tonight, little traveler, may your dreams roll along like a cozy night train — gentle, glowing, and full of warm yellow windows. May every wobble turn into a dance, and every stranger turn into a friend by morning. Remember that the journey is the very best part, so there is no rush to arrive; just sway along, and let the rocking carry you. Snuggle down now, like a sleepy snack in a window seat, and let the soft *clackety-clack* tuck you in. The island will still be turning when you wake. Goodnight, little bouncer. Goodnight.
