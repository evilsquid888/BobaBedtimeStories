# Story 57: Tea Leaf & the Cloud Sea of Alishan

**Main Character:** Ā-Lǐ (Alishan High-Mountain Oolong Tea Leaf)
**Guest Characters:** Douhua (Tofu Pudding)
**Setting:** The misty high peaks and sunrise cloud-sea of Alishan
**Theme:** Patience lets the best things unfurl.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 烏龍茶 (wūlóng chá) = oolong tea

---

High up where the mountains poke their noses through the clouds, on a tea bush so old it had forgotten its own birthday, there lived a slender jade-green tea leaf named Ā-Lǐ. He wore a tiny conical bamboo hat tilted just so, and he smelled — and this is true — of orchids that had wandered into a misty dream. Every morning Ā-Lǐ did the same wonderfully boring thing: nothing. He sat. He breathed. He let the fog tickle his curled-up edges.

"Ā-Lǐ!" squeaked a tiny voice one frosty dawn. "Ā-Lǐ, wake UP! Are you awake? You're awake now, right? Hi! Hello! It's me!"

A brand-new baby tea leaf had sprouted overnight on the branch below — bright, bouncy, and rolled up tight as a spring. She vibrated like a kettle about to whistle.

"Good morning, little sprout," said Ā-Lǐ, slow and warm as steam. "Steep slow, breathe deep."

"Steep slow?" gasped the sprout. "There's no TIME to be slow! I want to be tea NOW!"

---

The little leaf's name was Cuì-Cuì, and Cuì-Cuì had exactly one speed: *immediately*.

"I heard," she announced, hopping in place, "that the best **烏龍茶** (wūlóng chá) = oolong tea in the whole entire world comes from THIS mountain. And I want to be the best! So how do I get picked? How do I get rolled? How do I become tea? Quick, tell me, I'm ready, let's GO!"

Ā-Lǐ chuckled, which sounded like a tiny wind chime. "First," he said, "we wait."

Cuì-Cuì made a face like a teapot that had bitten a lemon. "Wait? WAIT? That's the worst plan I've ever heard. I don't *do* waiting. Waiting is just standing still but boring-er."

"Ah," said Ā-Lǐ, unfurling one edge of himself ever so slightly, "but waiting on a mountain is not the same as waiting in a line. On the mountain, the **雲海** (yún hǎi) = sea of clouds rolls in. The mist feeds us orchid smells. The cold makes us sweet. Rush all that, and you'll only turn out one way."

"Delicious?" guessed Cuì-Cuì hopefully.

"*Bitter,*" said Ā-Lǐ.

Cuì-Cuì stuck out her little leaf-tongue. "Bleh. Okay but I'm STILL not waiting."

And — *boing!* — she flung herself off the branch entirely.

---

Now, a tea leaf cannot exactly walk. But Cuì-Cuì could roll, and tumble, and bonk, and she did all three down the mountainside until she rolled smack into something soft, white, and extremely wobbly.

"*Whoa-ho-ho!*" went the wobbly thing, jiggling like jelly in an earthquake. It was Douhua, the silky tofu pudding, sloshing gently in a little bowl of warm ginger syrup, taking her morning stroll along the mountain path. She wobbled to a stop and beamed. "Why hello there, small green speedy person. You're in quite a hurry for someone with no feet."

"I'm trying to become the best oolong tea in the world!" Cuì-Cuì announced. "But the OLD leaf up there says I have to *wait*, and waiting is for clocks and grandpas. So I'm doing it MYSELF. I'm going to find the picker and the roaster and the roller and I'm going to get made into tea before breakfast!"

"Goodness," said Douhua, who never did anything before breakfast except *be* breakfast. "And how is that going?"

Cuì-Cuì looked down at herself. In all the tumbling, she'd gotten muddy, bent, and a little crunchy at the edges. Worse — she felt *hot* inside, like a kettle left on too long. A grumpy, sharp, prickly feeling.

"I feel... weird," she admitted. "Sour. Pinchy. Like a lemon wearing a frown."

"Mm," hummed Douhua, settling beside her with a comfortable *gloop*. "You know, little leaf, I am made of the gentlest stuff on this whole island. Softer than a cloud's pillow. And do you know the secret?"

"You... never exercise?"

"I am *never* in a hurry," said Douhua serenely. "Soybeans that are rushed turn out lumpy and cross. But soybeans that are coaxed — slowly, kindly, with patience and warm ginger — turn out silky enough to make a grown man weep into his spoon." She gave a proud little jiggle. "I am the wobbliest pudding in Taiwan, and I got that way by *taking my time.* Watch."

Douhua held very, very still. The morning light slid across her glossy white surface. A single drop of ginger syrup trembled at her edge, caught the sun, and glowed like a tiny gold lantern. It was, Cuì-Cuì had to admit, the most beautiful and peaceful thing she'd ever seen a dessert do.

"...Okay," said Cuì-Cuì, very quietly. "That was kind of amazing."

---

Just then, a familiar wind-chime chuckle floated down the slope. Ā-Lǐ had drifted down on a curl of mist, riding it like a tiny green surfer on a foggy wave.

"You FOLLOWED me?" said Cuì-Cuì.

"A good tea leaf never lets a friend roll off a mountain alone," said Ā-Lǐ kindly. "Now. You feel sour and pinchy, yes? Hot inside? That, little one, is what bitter feels like *before* it happens. You shook yourself up. You scraped your edges. You did to yourself in ten minutes what a careless roaster does to bad tea." He tipped his bamboo hat. "But here is the wonderful secret of **烏龍茶**: bitter is not the end of the story. It is only the *middle* of the story for a leaf in too big a hurry."

"So I'm RUINED?" wailed Cuì-Cuì, who did everything, including despair, at top speed.

"Not at all," said Ā-Lǐ. "You're just early. And early can wait. Come — sit with me. The sun is nearly up, and you have never seen what we wake up *for.*"

Douhua wobbled closer with an encouraging slosh. "Go on, little leaf. I'll wait too. I'm extremely good at it."

So Cuì-Cuì — for the very first time in her short, zoomy life — sat down. And held still. And did the wonderfully boring thing called *nothing.*

---

At first it was torture. Her edges itched. Her insides fizzed. She counted to ten about forty times.

"It's not WORKING," she hissed.

"Shh," whispered Ā-Lǐ. "Don't watch the waiting. Watch the *mountain.*"

So she did. And slowly — so slowly she almost missed it — the **雲海** began to move.

The whole valley below had filled overnight with clouds, a great soft ocean of them, white and rolling and endless, lapping silently against the green peaks like islands in a milky sea. As Cuì-Cuì watched, the very first light of dawn touched the far edge of that cloud-ocean. And the clouds turned *pink.* Then peach. Then a gold so warm it looked good enough to drink.

The mist that drifted past Cuì-Cuì's face carried the smell of orchids and rain and faraway flowers, and — oh — she felt the cold morning air settle into her leaves, sweetening her, the way Ā-Lǐ had promised. The hot pinchy feeling melted away like frost. In its place came something calm. Something deep. Something that smelled, just faintly, like the most delicious thing in the world.

"...Oh," breathed Cuì-Cuì. "*Oh.* This is what I was rushing past."

"This," said Ā-Lǐ softly, "is what makes the best tea on the mountain. Not the picking. Not the rolling. *This.* The waiting, with your face turned to the cloud sea."

And then the sun crested the horizon — and the whole world of clouds burst into rosy-gold fire, gentle as a goodnight kiss — and Cuì-Cuì felt her tight little curled-up self begin, at long last, to *unfurl.*

It didn't hurt. It was the opposite of hurting. She opened slowly, leaf by leaf, like a tiny green hand waving good morning to the day. And out of her newly opened heart drifted a swirl of fragrance so sweet and so lovely that Douhua gave a happy wobble and Ā-Lǐ closed his eyes and smiled.

"You smell *wonderful*," said Ā-Lǐ. "You smell like patience."

"I smell like ME," said Cuì-Cuì, amazed, "but the best possible me."

---

Some weeks later, when the time was exactly, perfectly, patiently right, a tea farmer with gentle hands picked Ā-Lǐ and Cuì-Cuì from the bush together. They were rolled into tight happy curls, warmed with care, and dropped at last into a fat clay pot of hot spring water.

And oh, the *unfurling!* They swirled and opened and danced, releasing their fragrant clouds into the steam, turning the water a glowing honey-gold. Somewhere, a sleepy person lifted a cup of the finest **烏龍茶** to their nose, breathed in orchids and mist and mountain dawn, and sighed the happiest sigh in all of Taiwan.

"We did it," whispered Cuì-Cuì, swirling joyfully. "We're tea! And we're not even *a little* bitter!"

"Slow and sweet," agreed Ā-Lǐ, dancing in the warm gold water beside her. "Steep slow, breathe deep, little friend."

And far down the mountain, Douhua wobbled in her bowl and smiled at the sunrise, taking her time, the way the very best things always do.

## Goodnight Blessing
May you, little dreamer, never be in a hurry to be wonderful. May you sit with your face toward the morning and let the good things in you open up slowly, sweetly, in their own perfect time. The clouds are tucking the mountains into bed now, and the steam from the teacup is curling up like a soft gray cat. Breathe in slow. Breathe out slow. Let tomorrow unfurl all on its own. Goodnight.
