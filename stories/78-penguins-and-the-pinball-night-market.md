# Story 78: Willa Wobble & the Night-Market Pinball Quest

**Main Character:** Willa Wobble (Little Blue Penguin)
**Guest Characters:** Boba
**Setting:** A retro pinball-and-pachinko arcade tucked in a night market
**Theme:** The smallest player can rack up the biggest heart-points.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 小 (xiǎo) = small

---

Of all the penguins who ever waddled off a snowboard and onto a warm Taiwanese night, Willa Wobble was the very smallest. She was a Little Blue Penguin, which is exactly as tiny as it sounds — about the height of a teapot, a fluff of slate-blue feathers, and two flippers so short she clapped them like a baby seal at a magic show.

Tonight Willa had wobbled straight through a curtain of glowing lanterns and into the most marvelous place she had ever seen: an arcade tucked between a dumpling stall and a fish-ball cart, blinking with a thousand colored bulbs. Bells dinged. Marbles rattled. A sign over the door buzzed: **小** (xiǎo) = SMALL GAMES, BIG FUN.

Willa pressed her beak to the glass of an enormous pinball machine and gasped a gasp so small it barely fogged the window.

"Oh," she whispered. "Oh, I would LOVE a turn."

"Then bounce on in!" said a glossy black voice behind her. "I happen to be excellent at being a ball."

---

Willa spun around. There, beaming up at her, was a perfectly round, perfectly bouncy tapioca pearl: Boba, leader of the night-market snacks, glistening like a polished marble under the arcade lights.

"You're a marble?" Willa asked.

"I'm a pearl, thank you very much," said Boba, doing a little hop. "But I am ALSO marble-shaped, which makes me the luckiest pinball partner in all of Taiwan. Most players need a ball. Most players don't have a *friend* who *is* one."

Willa's tiny heart did a tiny somersault. "You'd really roll around inside a machine for me?"

"Kiddo, I was *born* to roll." Boba bounced onto the worn wooden counter, where a soft glow surrounded the whole arcade. "Come on. Let's bounce!"

The arcade keeper was Diàn Diàn, the convenience-store mochi, who tonight wore a striped referee's cap over his usual little vest. He beeped warmly as they approached.

"Beep-boop! Welcome, welcome," said Diàn Diàn. "One game tonight only — the **彈珠台** (tánzhū tái) = pinball table — the Grand Heart-Point Quest! Roll the ball through the night-market levels. Collect glowing Heart-Points by being kind, brave, or clever. Whoever lights up the big golden lantern at the top wins the Champion's Lucky Candy!" He pointed a flipper toward a hand-painted machine taller than a refrigerator, every bumper shaped like a beloved snack.

A line of much, *much* bigger players had already gathered.

There was Pái Pái, the fried chicken cutlet, bigger than her own face and twice as confident. There was Shaved Ice, a wobbling tower of toppings in sunglasses, who announced, "I AM SO LARGE I WILL FILL THE ENTIRE MACHINE WITH GLORY." And there was Zòng Zi, the sticky-rice pyramid, bouncing in place, ready to ride.

Willa looked at the others. Then she looked down at herself. Small flippers. Small beak. Small everything. Even her name — Wobble — sounded like something you'd call a wobbly little jelly, not a champion.

"They're all so big," she whispered to Boba. "Maybe small isn't good for pinball. Maybe small isn't good for *anything*. Back home on the snowboard slopes, I always got left behind because my legs were too short to keep up. The other penguins didn't mean to be unkind. I was just... easy to lose."

Boba rolled a slow, knowing circle around her webbed feet, leaving a tiny glistening trail. "Hmm," he said. "You know, kiddo, everybody thinks the night market is all about the BIG things — the big steaming pots, the big sizzling grills, the big shaved-ice towers that announce themselves in capital letters." He tilted toward Shaved Ice, who was at that very moment shouting her own name. "But the secret of the night market is the *xiǎo* things. The little candies. The little folded dumplings. The little lucky charms tucked into every corner. The market would be empty without them."

He bounced once, twice, three times, each higher than the last. "Or," he finished, "maybe small is the *secret*. Watch this."

---

Pái Pái went first. She gripped the flipper levers in her crunchy edges and SLAMMED them with all her champion strength. "BIGGER THAN YOUR FACE, BIGGER THAN YOUR WORRIES!" she boomed.

WHANG! The ball rocketed up — and instantly got *stuck*. Pái Pái was so big and so strong that she'd flipped too hard, jamming herself between the bumpers like a cutlet wedged in a too-small lunchbox.

"A little help?" she squeaked, suddenly not so loud.

Diàn Diàn beeped and gently un-wedged her. "No Heart-Points if you get stuck, I'm afraid. Next player!"

Shaved Ice went next, declaring herself UNSTOPPABLE. She was correct that she was unstoppable. She was incorrect that this was good. She was simply too *tall* — her topping-tower bonked the ceiling, her syrup dripped on the buttons, and within ten seconds she had melted halfway down the machine in a puddle of dramatic glory.

"I REGRET NOTHING," she sighed soggily, "EXCEPT POSSIBLY EVERYTHING. ALSO, IS ANYONE HOLDING A FREEZER?"

Diàn Diàn fetched a little towel and dabbed at her sadly, beeping. "No Heart-Points for ceiling-bonking either, I'm afraid. But ten out of ten for confidence."

Zòng Zi did better. He bobbed and bounced and floated through three whole levels, hollering "Wrapped tight, ready to ride!" at every bumper. But the night-market machine had narrow little tunnels — secret passages between the levels — and big bundled Zòng Zi simply could not squeeze through them. He tried wiggling. He tried sucking in his sticky rice. He tried asking very politely. None of it worked, and he came out the bottom with a sheepish bobble.

"Those tunnels are *xiǎo*," he announced. "Far too xiǎo for a festival pyramid like me." He got two Heart-Points and a friendly pat, which he wore proudly all the same.

Then Diàn Diàn turned to the smallest player in the room. "Willa Wobble. Your turn."

---

Willa's flippers trembled. The machine loomed over her like a glowing mountain. Every big player had struggled. What chance did the *xiǎo*-est penguin of all have?

Boba bounced up to her beak. "Hey. You know what's small enough to fit through every tunnel in this whole machine?"

"...A small penguin?" Willa guessed.

"A small penguin," Boba agreed. "And you know what *else* is small and round and bouncy and ready to roll?"

"You!"

"ME!" cried Boba. "Now scoop me up, partner. Let's flip the script — er, let's flip the *table*."

Willa cupped Boba in her two tiny flippers, took the tiniest deep breath, and gave him a gentle, careful, perfectly soft little tap onto the playfield.

And here is the wonderful thing about being small, dear reader, the thing nobody had told Willa Wobble her whole little life:

*Small things fit everywhere.*

Boba rolled — not too fast, because Willa hadn't slammed him like Pái Pái — and slipped neatly into the first narrow tunnel. *Ding!* A Heart-Point lit up gold. Then another tunnel, too tight for anyone else. *Ding-ding!*

And here is the second wonderful thing about being small: because Willa stood so low to the ground and looked at the world from down near everyone's feet, she noticed things the tall players sailed right past. She could lean her cheek against the glass, peer sideways through the little gaps between the bumpers, and *see* the secret paths nobody else had ever spotted — a hidden ramp behind the dumpling, a sneaky chute under the tea-egg, a whole glowing shortcut tucked beneath the noodle bowl.

"Left! Now soft-right! There's a teeny tunnel by the dumpling bumper!" she called.

"Diving in!" Boba whooped, threading through a passage barely wider than himself. *DING DING DING!*

---

But the machine had one last trick. Near the very top sat a level the snacks called the **小巷** (xiǎo xiàng) = little alley — a maze of impossibly thin corridors winding up to the golden lantern. No ball had ever made it through. The corridors were just too small.

"Too small for a *normal* ball," murmured Willa, her pink-edged eyes going wide. "But Boba isn't normal. And neither am I."

She pressed her whole tiny body against the glass, squinting into the maze, mapping every twist with her excellent small-penguin eyes. The corridors zigged where you expected a zag. They doubled back. They pinched so thin that a single grain of sticky rice would have jammed them solid.

But Willa did not rush. Rushing was a big-player mistake. Instead she breathed slow, the way the misty mountain tea leaf Ā-Lǐ might have, and she moved Boba a feather's breadth at a time — *tip, tip, tap* — left along the first wall, a careful pause, then *tap* up the hidden ramp. Where a big strong player would have flung him into a wall in one mighty swing, Willa coaxed him gently, patiently, like helping a sleepy friend find the bathroom in the dark.

"You're doing it!" Boba called, ricocheting softly off a scallion-pancake bumper. "Slow and small wins the maze!"

Behind her, the big players had stopped competing and started cheering.

"GO, LITTLE ONE!" boomed Shaved Ice from her puddle.

"Bigger heart than your face, kid!" hollered Pái Pái, who, it turned out, was a wonderful loser and an even better fan.

"Wrapped tight, ROOTING for you!" sang Zòng Zi, bobbing.

Boba slipped through the final corridor of the *xiǎo xiàng*, popped out the top, and rolled — slow, sure, and small — right into the golden lantern.

*BONNNNG!*

The whole machine erupted. Every bulb flashed. Bells sang. The golden lantern blazed to life, and across the top scrolled glowing letters: **HEART-POINTS: CHAMPION!**

Willa Wobble, smallest penguin in all the world, had won.

---

Diàn Diàn beeped a fanfare and placed the Champion's Lucky Candy in Willa's flippers — a candy so big she had to hug it with both arms, which made everyone laugh, including her.

"How?" asked Pái Pái, kneeling down to Willa's tiny height. "I'm the biggest, the strongest, the crunchiest. How did the *xiǎo*-est win?"

Willa thought about it. Then she clapped her flippers, just once, very softly.

"Because being small isn't a *problem* to fix," she said. "It's just a different way to be. I fit where you couldn't. I saw what you couldn't. And I had a friend who likes to roll." She nuzzled Boba, who glowed extra-glossy with pride.

"Slurp first, worry later," Boba sighed happily, then caught himself. "Wait, that's Niú Niú's line. Mine is — *let's bounce!* And we bounced *beautifully*."

The snacks shared the giant candy, breaking it into pieces small enough for everyone, even pieces small enough for a teapot-sized penguin. And as the lanterns of the night market glowed and the arcade dinged its sleepy late-night dings, Willa Wobble decided that the very best thing about being small was this: there was always more night-market left to discover, and just enough room in her small heart to hold the whole wonderful, bouncing, glowing place.

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, the smallest player can light the biggest lantern, and the tiniest flipper can tap the kindest path. You do not need to be the biggest, the strongest, or the loudest — you only need to be wonderfully, exactly *you*. Tonight, let your small heart rest, full of golden Heart-Points and the soft ding-ding of dreams. May you fit perfectly into your warm little bed, the way Willa fit through every secret tunnel. Bounce gently into sleep, dear one. Goodnight.
