# Story 39: Popcorn Chicken & the Runaway Giggle

**Main Character:** Kǎ Kǎ (Popcorn Chicken)
**Guest Characters:** Egg Waffle
**Setting:** A neon Shilin Night Market snack stall
**Theme:** It's okay to slow down and count your crew.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 鹽酥雞 (yánsūjī) = salt-and-pepper popcorn chicken

---

It is a known and serious fact that **鹽酥雞** (yánsūjī) = salt-and-pepper popcorn chicken cannot be trusted to hold still during a good joke.

The Kǎ Kǎ crew lived in a paper boat of waxed bag at the corner of the Shilin Night Market, twenty-two golden, crispy, pepper-dusted nuggets who all talked at once and finished each other's sentences and wore tiny fried basil leaves on their heads like little green hats.

"Knock knock," said one nugget.

"WHO'S THERE," said the other twenty-one, bouncing.

"Boo."

"BOO WHO?"

"Don't cry, it's just—" and then nobody finished it, because the entire crew exploded into giggles, and giggling popcorn chicken does not stay in the bag. They *spring*. They *ping*. They *boing* in twenty-two directions like crispy little fireworks.

By the time the laughing settled, the waxed bag held exactly one nugget.

The smallest one.

---

Her name was Kǎ Kǎ too — they were all Kǎ Kǎ, it was simpler that way — but the crew called her Little Kǎ, because she was the size of a thumbnail and always the last one to finish a sentence.

She peeked over the rim of the bag at the enormous, glittering, neon-soaked night market. Strings of lights buzzed pink and gold. Steam rose from a hundred stalls. Somewhere a vendor shouted, "More crunch, more fun!" which was *their* catchphrase, and it made Little Kǎ's tiny heart go *crunch* with worry.

Twenty-one nuggets. Scattered. And the stall closed at midnight, when the vendor would fold up the waxed bag and carry it home — empty, if she didn't hurry.

"Okay," whispered Little Kǎ, bouncing in place to be brave. "Slow down. Count the crew. One at a time."

She did not, in fact, slow down. She was popcorn chicken. She *boinged* off the counter and into the night.

---

The first nugget had landed in a basket of fried squid two stalls over, giggling so hard he had pepper coming out his ears.

"Little Kǎ!" he wheezed. "I told the best joke. Why did the dumpling — why did the — " he started laughing again and nearly bounced into a vat of oil.

"No more jokes!" Little Kǎ grabbed his basil hat and hauled. "You'll scatter *worse*. Just hop. Stay close."

"Hop and stay close," he repeated, hiccupping. "That's two whole instructions. I can do one."

So she let him hold her tiny crispy hand, which counts as one instruction, and now there were two of them. *One found. Twenty more.*

"Two!" she said out loud, because saying the number made it feel real, made it feel like a thing she could carry. "Two is more than one. Two is a start."

"Two is also the number of jokes I know," offered the rescued nugget hopefully.

"It is *not* the time for either of them," said Little Kǎ.

They bounced past a stall of glistening **滷味** stew, past the **臭豆腐** (chòudòufu) = stinky tofu cart with its friendly purple sparkle-clouds (a golden cube there waved a clothespin at them and called, "Bold on the outside, gold on the inside, kiddos!"), past a tower of shaved ice so tall it wore sunglasses indoors and bellowed "WATCH THE DRIP, TINY ONES!" as they zipped beneath it, and that's when they heard the singing.

It was coming from above. From a stall that glowed warmer and grid-shaped than all the rest.

It was the Egg Waffle stall.

---

Egg Waffle stood under a tiny spotlight he had aimed at himself, a golden grid of fluffy bubble-cake in a shiny top hat, twirling a little cane.

"LADIES AND GENTLEMEN," he boomed at no one, because it was 11:14 at night and nobody was buying waffles, "STEP RIGHT UP for the EIGHTH WONDER of the night market—"

Then he saw Little Kǎ. And he saw, stuck in the warm grid of his own bubbly back, *eleven* squirming popcorn-chicken nuggets, all giggling, all wedged between his golden bumps like tiny crispy pearls in a waffle.

"Ah," said Egg Waffle, delighted. "My adoring audience has *physically embedded itself in me*. This is the dream, frankly."

"Those are mine," said Little Kǎ, bouncing up to his counter. "I mean — they're my crew. They giggled too hard and scattered. Eleven of them landed in your back."

"Eleven!" Egg Waffle counted. "Why, that's nearly a full audience. Do they laugh at all my jokes?"

"They laugh at *everything*," said Little Kǎ. "That's the problem."

"That," said Egg Waffle, lifting his top hat in respect, "is not a problem. That is a *gift*."

But he had a kind heart under all that showmanship, so he wiggled his grid — *bumpa-bumpa-bumpa* — and the eleven nuggets popped free, giggling, raining down into a soft pile of basil hats.

"Count them," Little Kǎ told herself. She did, touching each warm crispy head with the tip of one tiny wing. "Two plus eleven is thirteen." Her tummy did a happy crunchy flip. *Thirteen found. Eight more.*

"You know," said Egg Waffle, watching her count, "most folks would just grab a handful and run. You touch each one. Why's that?"

Little Kǎ thought about it. "Because a handful isn't a *number*," she said. "A handful could be ten. A handful could be six and I'd never know who was missing. If I don't count, I can't tell who still needs finding." She tapped the last basil hat. "Everybody deserves to be found on purpose."

Egg Waffle was quiet a moment. Then he took off his top hat entirely, which for Egg Waffle was practically a standing ovation.

"My dear small fried friend," said Egg Waffle, hopping down from his stall with a flourish, "you cannot herd thirteen giggling nuggets through a night market alone. You need a SHOWMAN. You need PRESENCE. You need a top hat." He set his top hat on her thumbnail-sized head, where it swallowed her completely. "...Perhaps a smaller hat."

He took it back. But he came along anyway.

---

They found two more nuggets riding the **波霸** boba straw of a milk-tea cup like a bouncy castle, bouncing so high they nearly hit the neon sign. (*Fifteen.*) They found one balanced on a sugar-painting artist's spoon, about to become — to its absolute delight — part of a dragon. ("Sir," said Egg Waffle gravely, "that is a member of my audience, not a dragon's tail," and the artist laughed and handed it over. *Sixteen.*)

They found three more tucked inside a steamer of **小籠包** (xiǎolóngbāo) = soup dumplings, where a very polite plump dumpling in a steamer-lid beret was carefully, *carefully* lifting them out one by one.

"Careful — I'm full of feelings... and soup," Xiǎo Lóng explained, balancing. "And also, apparently, three of your friends. They told me eighteen knock-knock jokes. I have exactly eighteen pleats. I counted along to stay calm."

"Did it work?" asked Little Kǎ.

"No," said Xiǎo Lóng warmly, "but it was very nice." *Nineteen.*

Little Kǎ lined her nineteen nuggets up along the steamer's edge and counted them twice, just to be sure, the way her whole crew used to count themselves before bed in the old days — back when they were always all together and counting was just a sleepy game, not a search. The memory made her crispy edges ache a little. Nineteen was so close to all. But close was not the same as whole. The market was thinning. The neon hummed lower. Somewhere far off, a clock began the slow climb toward midnight, and Little Kǎ felt her crispy edges go cold.

"Egg Waffle," she said quietly, "what if I can't find them? What if the bag goes home empty? The whole crew, we've never *not* been together. We're a *we*. I don't even know how to be just a me."

Egg Waffle knelt down — which for a waffle is mostly a fold — until his golden grid was level with her tiny worried face.

"Little Kǎ," he said, and for once he wasn't booming. "You've been a 'me' this whole night. A brave one. You counted, you herded, you didn't give up. That's not a missing piece of a crew. That's a whole, entire, top-hat-worthy hero." He paused. "Now. Where would *you* go, if you were a giggle that ran away?"

Little Kǎ thought. She thought about where giggles go to be the silliest.

"Somewhere," she said slowly, "with an echo."

---

The public bathrooms by the market exit had the *best* echo in all of Shilin, and that is exactly where the last two nuggets were, sitting on the edge of a sink, yelling "MORE CRUNCH, MORE FUN!" and listening to it bounce back twelve times.

"There you ARE," breathed Little Kǎ.

"Little Kǎ!" they cheered. "Listen to the echo! It says our catchphrase back! It's the funniest nugget we've ever met and it's INVISIBLE!"

"That's not a nugget, that's *tile*," said Little Kǎ, but she was laughing now too, the relief of it bubbling up crispy and warm. She had never been so happy to see two silly nuggets shouting at a wall. "Come on. Count with me. Twenty. Twenty-one. We're going home."

"Twenty-one!" the two nuggets gasped. "Where's twenty-two?"

Little Kǎ looked down at herself. At her tiny, brave, pepper-speckled self. And she smiled the biggest crispy smile of the whole night. "Twenty-two is right here," she said. "Twenty-two has been here the whole time."

And they hopped — all twenty-one of them now, plus one Little Kǎ in front — single file, slow and careful and *together*, back through the dimming stalls, with Egg Waffle bringing up the rear like a glittering golden caboose, twirling his cane.

"Twenty-two," whispered Little Kǎ as the last nugget bounced into the waxed bag. "All of you. Every single one."

The bag was full again. Warm, crispy, peppery, complete.

---

At the stroke of midnight the vendor came to fold up her stall, lifted the waxed bag, and felt how reassuringly heavy it was. "Funny," she murmured. "Could've sworn I lost some."

Inside the bag, twenty-two nuggets pressed close in the dark, giggling — softly this time, gently, the kind of giggle that doesn't scatter.

"Knock knock," whispered one.

"Who's there," whispered the crew, sleepy now.

"Little Kǎ," said the nugget.

"Little Kǎ who?"

"Little Kǎ who counted us all home," they finished together, and that was the best joke of the whole night, because it was true.

Egg Waffle tipped his top hat from his stall across the way as the lights blinked out one by one. "Bravo," he said softly to the empty market. "Bravo, small one."

And Little Kǎ, snug in the middle of her warm crispy family, did the bravest thing of all.

She slowed down.

She breathed.

And she counted herself, last of all, as one of the crew.

---

## Goodnight Blessing
May your friends always bounce back to you, even when the giggles send them flying. May you be brave enough to go looking and wise enough to slow down and count. And when the neon lights blink off one by one, may you find yourself snug in the middle of everyone who loves you, warm and crispy and home. Slurp first, worry later, little one — the morning will keep. You are counted. You are safe. Goodnight.
