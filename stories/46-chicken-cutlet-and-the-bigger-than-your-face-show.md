# Story 46: Chicken Cutlet & the Bigger-Than-Your-Face Show

**Main Character:** Pái Pái (Fried Chicken Cutlet)
**Guest Characters:** Kǎ Kǎ (Popcorn Chicken)
**Setting:** A Fengjia Night Market main stage
**Theme:** Being big is great; being kind is greater.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 雞排 (jīpái) = fried chicken cutlet

---

Every Friday at the Fengjia Night Market, the brightest stage in all the island lit up, and out strutted a snack so enormous that three umbrellas couldn't have covered her. She was a **雞排** (jīpái) = fried chicken cutlet, and her name was Pái Pái, and she was, by her own cheerful announcement, "BIGGER THAN YOUR FACE, BIGGER THAN YOUR WORRIES!"

The crowd went bananas. Or rather, they went popcorn chicken, lychee, and shaved-ice-with-extra-syrup, because those were the snacks doing the cheering.

Pái Pái flexed her crispy golden edges like a superhero cape. Plum powder puffed off her in a sparkly pink cloud. She did her famous spin — the Crunch Twirl — and a hundred little snacks gasped in delight.

"Thank you, thank you," she boomed, taking a bow so deep her pepper crumbs rained down like confetti. "Try the veal — I mean, try ME — I'm here all night!"

She was the star of the Fengjia stage. And oh, did she love it.

The thing about Pái Pái was that she truly, genuinely believed bigness was the best thing a snack could be. Big crunch. Big flavor. Big applause. The night market was loud and dazzling and packed shoulder-to-shoulder, and the bigger you were, the more you mattered — at least, that was how Pái Pái figured it.

"You can't see a small snack from the back row," she liked to say, fanning herself with one crackly edge. "But ME? You can see me from space."

Her best friends were Kǎ Kǎ, the popcorn chicken crew — a giggling cluster of tiny golden nuggets crowned with little fried-basil hats, who hopped and reshuffled and finished each other's sentences.

"You're SO big!" chirped one nugget.

"So crispy!" added another.

"We're so SMALL—" said a third.

"—but we LOVE watching you!" they all finished together, bouncing in a happy pile.

Pái Pái grinned. "Stick with me, kiddos. Some of my bigness might rub off."

The truth, dear reader — and a narrator must always tell the truth, even the crunchy bits — was that Pái Pái had never once stopped to wonder whether being seen from space was the same as being *loved*. She thought a loud cheer and a warm hug were the very same thing. They are cousins, certainly. But they are not twins, and tonight she was about to learn the difference.

She practiced her bows. She practiced her catchphrase. She practiced flinging plum powder so that it caught the lantern light and sparkled like a thousand pink fireflies. "Perfect," she told her reflection in a soy-sauce puddle. "They're going to love me. They always love me. What could be better than that?"

The soy-sauce puddle did not answer, because it was a puddle. But if it had, it might have said: *Plenty, Pái Pái. Plenty.*

Now here is where our story does its first little flip.

On this particular Friday, the night market was holding its grandest event of the year: the Golden Crunch Showcase. Every snack who wanted a turn could climb the big stage, stand in the spotlight, and share one talent with the whole bustling market. Egg Waffle was emceeing in his top hat. Hēi Táng was painting caramel swirls on the announcement banner. Even Tǔdì Gōng, the tiny jolly Earth God, had toddled down from his little red temple to watch, his pockets jingling with lucky candies.

"Bless this little corner of the island," he chuckled, settling onto a cushion. "And bless whoever goes first, because going first is the hardest."

Pái Pái, of course, planned to go first, last, and several times in the middle.

But as she warmed up backstage with her Crunch Twirls, she noticed one of the Kǎ Kǎ nuggets sitting all alone behind a stack of bamboo skewers. It was the smallest of the whole crew — a single round nugget, no bigger than a marble, with a crooked little basil hat and the biggest, shiniest, most nervous eyes Pái Pái had ever seen.

"Hey there, tiny," said Pái Pái, crouching down with a crinkle. "Why aren't you bouncing with the others?"

The little nugget looked up. "M-my crew put my name on the showcase list," he squeaked. "They said I have the best hop in the whole bunch. They want me to do my big jump. In front of... everyone."

He gulped. He looked at the stage. The lights were blinding. The crowd was a roaring, hungry, delighted sea.

"I practiced in the alley behind the squid stand," the little nugget went on, his voice getting smaller with every word. "When nobody's watching, I can jump higher than the lantern wires. I once jumped so high I bonked a moth. But the second I imagine all those eyes looking at me, my hop turns into a flop. My basil hat slides over my face. And then everyone laughs at the wrong thing." He sniffled. "I don't want to be the snack that ruins the showcase."

Pái Pái thought about that. She had spent her whole shiny life *wanting* every eye in the market pointed at her. It had never once occurred to her that the very same spotlight she adored could feel, to somebody small, like standing under a very hot, very bright, very judgy sun.

"But I can't," he whispered. "I get all wobbly. My basil hat falls off. I'm too small. Nobody will even see me up there."

And then the smallest nugget did the smallest, saddest thing: he tried to hide under a single sesame seed, which did not work at all, because he was a nugget and not, in fact, smaller than a sesame seed.

Pái Pái's big crispy heart did a funny squeeze.

"Up next!" boomed Egg Waffle into the microphone. "The talent we've ALL been waiting for — the champion of crunch, the queen of the cutlet — PÁI PÁI!"

The crowd erupted. Chants rolled across the market like thunder: "PÁI! PÁI! PÁI!"

This was it. Her favorite moment in the whole wide world. The spotlight was already swinging toward the stage, hot and golden and hers.

Pái Pái looked at the spotlight.

Then she looked at the trembling little nugget hiding behind a sesame seed.

And she made a choice that surprised even herself.

"Change of plans, kiddo," she said softly. She scooped the tiny nugget up onto her crispy shoulder. "We're going up there together."

"W-WHAT?" squeaked the nugget. "No no no, that's YOUR spotlight, you can't—"

"Sure I can," said Pái Pái, marching toward the stage. "I'm bigger than your face. Pretty sure I'm allowed to bring a friend."

She strode into the blazing light. The crowd roared — and then went confused-murmury, because the famous Pái Pái was not doing her Crunch Twirl. She was just standing there, gigantic and golden, with one teeny terrified nugget perched on her shoulder.

"Evening, Fengjia!" Pái Pái called out, her voice big enough to fill the whole night sky. "Now, you all came to see ME, the biggest, crunchiest snack on the island."

"YEAH!" roared the crowd.

"But tonight," she went on, "I want to show you something even bigger than me. And believe me — that is HARD to do." (A laugh rippled through the market; even Tǔdì Gōng's belly jiggled.) "This little guy right here has the greatest jump you've ever seen. But he's nervous. So here's the deal, everybody." She winked at the audience. "When he jumps, I want you to cheer like he's ten feet tall. Can you do that for me?"

The little nugget's eyes filled with wobbly tears. "But what if I fall?" he whispered.

Pái Pái leaned close. "Then you fall onto the biggest, crispiest, safest landing pad in the whole market," she whispered back. "Me. I've got you. Always. Now — bold on the outside, gold on the inside, right? That's what your pal Chòu Chòu always says."

The nugget gave the tiniest, bravest nod.

Pái Pái lowered her crispy edge like a launch ramp. The little nugget scrambled to the very tip. The whole market held its breath. Somewhere, a tea egg whispered, "Every crack tells a story," and this one was about to be a good one.

"Three..." counted Pái Pái.

"Two..." whispered the nugget.

"ONE!" shouted the entire crowd.

The little nugget HOPPED.

Up, up, up he flew — higher than the noodle banners, higher than the lanterns, higher than even tall, lanky Yóu Tiáo could ever dunk himself — a tiny golden comet spinning against the night, his crooked basil hat trailing behind him like a little green flag.

And at the very top of his jump, he did a flip.

A perfect, wobbling, glorious flip.

The market exploded. The Kǎ Kǎ crew leaped and shrieked with joy. Hēi Táng splattered three happy caramel swirls. Shaved Ice screamed "INCREDIBLE!" in all capital letters and immediately began to melt from excitement. Tǔdì Gōng threw lucky candies into the air like a tiny golden firework.

The little nugget came tumbling down — and landed, soft and safe, right in the middle of Pái Pái's big crispy edge.

The applause that followed was the loudest, warmest, most wonderful sound Pái Pái had ever heard. And here is the strange part, the part she would think about for many cozy nights after:

It didn't feel different because it was loud.

It felt different because, for the very first time, it wasn't only for her.

"THAT," she announced, holding the beaming nugget up high, "is the bravest jump in Fengjia history! Give it up for the SMALLEST, MIGHTIEST snack on the whole island!"

The crowd chanted a brand-new name. It wasn't "PÁI PÁI." It was "JUMP! JUMP! JUMP!" — and the little nugget glowed pinker than plum powder.

After the show, the Kǎ Kǎ crew swarmed their tiny hero in a giggling, bouncing pile, and the little nugget bounced right along with them, no longer hiding behind any sesame seeds at all.

Tǔdì Gōng waddled up to Pái Pái and pressed a lucky candy into her crispy hand. His eyes twinkled like incense smoke.

"You gave away your spotlight, my big crunchy friend," he chuckled. "And do you know what happened?"

"It came back twice as bright?" Pái Pái guessed.

"It came back twice as *warm*," said the Earth God. "Being big is a fine thing. But sharing your big with someone small — ah. That is the biggest thing of all."

Pái Pái looked out at the glittering market, at the little nugget laughing in the lights, and felt her heart go warm and crispy and full.

"Bigger than my face," she said softly, smiling. "Bigger than my worries."

And then, because she was still Pái Pái, she added with a wink, "But not bigger than my *heart*. Let's not get carried away."

The whole night market laughed, and the lanterns swung, and somewhere up above, a snow-white rabbit on the moon pounded her little mallet in time with the cheering.

## Goodnight Blessing
May you be brave like the smallest nugget, who jumped even though his knees went wobbly, and may you always have a friend with a big crispy edge to catch you. May you discover, as Pái Pái did, that the warmest spotlight is the one you share. Sleep now, little one, with plum-powder dreams and a heart full of golden cheering. Tomorrow there will be more jumps to take, more friends to lift up high. But for tonight, you are exactly the right size, and you are loved beyond all measure. Goodnight.
