# Story 62: Coconut Jelly & the Kenting Tide

**Main Character:** Coconut Jelly (椰果)
**Guest Characters:** Wō Wō (Oyster Omelet)
**Setting:** The southern beaches and coral reefs of Kenting
**Theme:** Go with the flow, but reach for friends when the tide turns.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 海邊 (hǎibiān) = seaside

---

If you have never seen a chewy little cube of Coconut Jelly riding a wave, then you have never seen anything truly chill — and I am terribly sorry, because it is the most relaxing thing in the entire universe.

Down at the warm southern tip of the island, where the palm trees lean back like they are also on vacation, Coconut Jelly bobbed on a tiny banana-leaf surfboard. He was a translucent, jiggly square — clear as a window, wobbly as pudding's nervous knees — and he wore his sunglasses even though the sun had nearly finished for the day.

"Bro," he sighed happily, "the **海邊** (hǎibiān) = seaside is the best classroom on the planet. No homework. No bedtime. Just waves and good vibes, dude."

A small wave lifted him up.

He went *wheeeee* in the laziest voice you have ever heard.

This, kiddo, is where our story begins.

---

The reason Coconut Jelly was at Kenting — besides the fact that he simply liked being there — was that he had volunteered to teach a swimming lesson. The brand-new Savory Crew, fresh off the night-market food trucks, had come south on the Island Road Trip, and not a single one of them had ever touched the ocean.

They stood on the sand in a worried little clump.

There was Niú Niú, the warm beef-noodle-soup bowl, clutching his wheat-noodle scarf like a life preserver. "Kiddo," he said slowly, "I am a *bowl.* Bowls fill with water. This feels like a design flaw."

"Careful — I'm full of feelings... and soup," whispered Xiǎo Lóng the dumpling, balanced on his bamboo steamer and already counting his pleats. "One... two... three... if I tip, I'll become *broth.*"

And at the very back, half-hidden behind a piece of driftwood, was Wō Wō the oyster omelet — glossy, wobbly, draped in her sweet-pink sauce cape, clutching her tiny seashell to her chest.

"Soft and saucy, that's me," she hummed shyly. "But the sea is so... *big.*"

Coconut Jelly pushed his sunglasses up onto his head, which was a very dramatic gesture for someone so laid-back.

"Okay, my dudes. Lesson one." He spread his wobbly arms wide. "You don't fight the ocean. You *flow* with it. The wave wants to lift you? Let it lift you. The water wants to carry you? Cool, free ride. The whole trick of the **海邊**, bro, is to stop worrying and start floating."

"That," said Niú Niú, "is the worst safety briefing I have ever heard."

"And yet," said Coconut Jelly, "I have never once drowned. Slurp on *that.*"

Niú Niú slurped. He couldn't help it. It was his catchphrase being used against him, and frankly, it was effective.

---

So the lesson began.

Coconut Jelly led them into the shallows where the water was warm as bathwater and the little waves were no bigger than a sneeze. He showed them how to bob. He showed them how to let a wave roll under them like a friendly hiccup. He showed them how to lie back and let their toes point at the first shy star.

And — wonder of wonders — it *worked.*

Niú Niú discovered that a bowl, if you simply trust it, floats beautifully, his steam-cloud halo drifting above the water like its own tiny weather system. "Well, slap me with a chopstick," he chuckled. "I'm a *boat.*"

Xiǎo Lóng floated on his steamer like the world's most nervous lily pad, and when a wave lifted him gently and set him back down without spilling a single drop, he made a sound so happy it was almost a hiccup. "Eighteen pleats," he announced proudly, "all accounted for!"

Even Kǎ Kǎ — wait, was Kǎ Kǎ here? Oh, you bet the popcorn-chicken crew was here, bobbing in a giggling cluster like a raft of crunchy ducks, shouting "MORE CRUNCH, MORE FUN!" and accidentally inventing a sport that was eighty percent splashing.

The only one who hadn't gone in past her frilly edges was Wō Wō.

She stood where the foam kissed the sand, hugging her seashell, watching everyone else laugh.

Coconut Jelly paddled over, slow and easy.

"Hey," he said softly. "No pressure, dude. But you know what I think? I think the ocean has been *waiting* for you. You're an oyster omelet. You've literally got little pearly oysters peeking out of you. You're already part sea, bro. You're practically family out here."

Wō Wō looked at her oysters. She looked at the gentle, glittering water.

"...Soft and saucy, that's me," she whispered. And she took one small, wobbly step in.

The warm water hugged her sweet-potato-starch edges. A tiny wave lifted her, set her down, and she did not melt, did not sink, did not become anyone's dinner.

She *floated.*

"I'm doing it," she gasped. "Coconut Jelly! I'm flowing!"

"That's the vibe, my dude," he grinned. "That's the whole entire vibe."

---

Now, here is where I must lean close and tell you something, the way the narrator always does right before the exciting bit. So lean in.

The ocean, you see, is a kind and beautiful thing. But the ocean keeps its own schedule. And down at Kenting, when the moon gets bossy, the tide can change its mind very, *very* fast.

It happened between one giggle and the next.

The warm shallow water suddenly went *cool.* The gentle waves got a hurry-up in them. And a long, sneaky finger of current — the kind the local crabs call the "**離岸流** (lí àn liú) = rip current," the pulling-away water — wrapped itself around the smallest, wobbliest, happiest floater on the whole beach.

Wō Wō.

"Oh!" she said, drifting backward. "Oh — I think — I'm flowing a little *too* much—"

And the tide pulled her out toward the coral reef, fast as a slurped noodle.

For one frozen heartbeat, Coconut Jelly almost said his usual thing. *Go with the flow. The ocean's got her. Don't fight it, just—*

But then he saw her little face. He saw her clutching her seashell. He saw her drifting away from everyone she loved, getting smaller, getting scared, calling his name in her soft sea-song voice — and something deep inside that chill, jiggly cube went absolutely, completely *un*-chill.

"NOT TODAY, OCEAN," he bellowed, in a voice no one had ever heard him use.

And Coconut Jelly — who had spent his entire life avoiding the word "effort" — paddled.

---

He paddled like he had never paddled in his life.

He paddled *against* the current, which is the one thing he had told everybody never to do, and he did it anyway, because some rules bend the moment a friend needs you. His wobbly arms churned. His banana-leaf board sliced the water. The chill surfer who said "bro" and meant "whenever" was now a streak of determination, and let me tell you, kiddo, it was *magnificent.*

"COCONUT JELLY!" Wō Wō cried, spinning near the reef.

"HANG ON, DUDE!" he roared. "I'M COMING, I'M COMING — DON'T YOU DARE FLOW WITHOUT ME!"

Up on the beach, the crew sprang into action. Niú Niú the boat-bowl unwound his enormous wheat-noodle scarf and flung one end out across the water like a rope. Kǎ Kǎ formed a giggling crunchy chain, nugget grabbing nugget. Xiǎo Lóng — brave, careful Xiǎo Lóng — counted his pleats *out loud* to stay calm and held the very end of Niú Niú's scarf with both tiny hands.

Coconut Jelly reached the reef just as the current tried to tuck Wō Wō behind a coral fan.

He grabbed her seashell. He grabbed her frilly edge. He grabbed *her.*

"Gotcha," he panted. "I gotcha, dude. I'm not letting go."

"You came *back* for me," Wō Wō whispered, wobbling against him.

"Always," said Coconut Jelly. "That's the part of the flow I forgot to teach, bro. Sometimes the tide turns... and you've gotta reach for the people you love and *pull.*"

He caught the noodle-scarf rope in his teeth.

"PULL, KIDDOS!" Niú Niú boomed.

And the whole Savory Crew — bowl and dumpling and giggling nuggets — heaved together, hauling two soft wobbly friends back across the cooling water, back over the gentle reef, back to the warm safe sand of the **海邊** they loved.

---

They flopped onto the beach in one big happy pile, dripping and breathless and laughing the slightly shaky laugh you laugh after something scary turns out okay.

The tide, having made its point, calmed right back down and pretended it had never done anything at all. Sneaky ocean.

"You," said Wō Wō, hugging Coconut Jelly so tight his sunglasses popped onto his nose, "are the bravest chill person I have ever met."

"Eh." Coconut Jelly shrugged, going pink at the edges, which on a clear jelly is *very* visible. "I'm still chill, dude. I just learned that being chill and *caring* aren't the same thing. You can float through the easy days... but when the tide turns?" He bumped her gently. "You paddle. For the ones you love, you paddle your little wobbly heart out."

Niú Niú wrung the seawater out of his scarf and draped it warmly around all of them like a noodly blanket. His steam-cloud halo puffed back to life, toasting everyone dry.

"Slurp first, worry later," he said, looking out at the dark, glittering water. "But for the record — today? Worry first. Slurp after. Now *come here,* all of you."

And they did, in a great cozy heap, while the southern stars came out one by one and the gentle, apologetic waves whispered *sorry, sorry, sorry* against the sand.

The scarf is warm, and so are you. Tuck in close, breathe slow, and let the soft tide of sleep carry you somewhere wonderful. Goodnight.
