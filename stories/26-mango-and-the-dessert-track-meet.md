# Story 26: Mango and the Dessert Track Meet

**Main Character:** Mango (芒果 mángguǒ) — Mango
**Guest Characters:** Boba, Mochi, Bùdīng, Zhīma, Bǎi, Bīng, Yángmáo, Dàn (announcer), Piper Paddlefoot (color commentator)
**Setting:** National Stadium, Kaohsiung (高雄)
**Theme:** Find your event, run your race
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 比賽 (bǐsài) = race / competition

---

Mango was doing lunges.

Not normal lunges. Olympic-quality, juice-spraying, sunbeam-defying lunges that left a small puddle of golden sweat at his feet and a slightly alarmed groundskeeper holding a mop in the distance. He was also wearing two sweatbands now. One on his head. One on his rosy-blush cheek. For "balance."

"Gotta stay in the zone," he muttered. "Focus. Speed. Victory. Hydration. Reapply sweatband."

It was the brightest, hottest, most ridiculously perfect morning in **高雄** (Gāoxióng) = Kaohsiung. The sun was already bullying everyone by 9 AM. The flags were limp. The air smelled like coconut sunscreen, fried squid from the food trucks outside, and the unmistakable buttery shimmer of fifty thousand humans waiting for a show. And rising above it all — curling and gleaming like a metallic dragon that had decided to nap in the shape of a stadium — was the **國家體育場** (Guójiā Tǐyù Chǎng) = National Stadium. Solar-panel scales on the roof. Track painted poppy red. Stands packed shoulder to shoulder.

Today was the **比賽** (bǐsài) of the year — the All-Taiwan Dessert Track Meet.

And Mango, for the first time in his short, sweet, extremely competitive life, was the team CAPTAIN.

---

"Okay, gather up, gather up," Mango said, herding his team into the warm-up zone. "We've got six events. We've got one hour. We've got — Bīng, please stop melting onto the long jump pit."

"I CAN'T HELP IT," wailed Bīng, a towering shaved-ice mountain whose sunglasses were already sliding off his face. "It is LITERALLY a thousand degrees. This is the WORST day. ALSO the BEST day. I CONTAIN MULTITUDES."

"He contains mostly water," Caǎo observed from under a tiny umbrella. "Forecast: drippy."

The team was a beautiful disaster.

Boba was bouncing — not warming up, not stretching, just bouncing, with the slightly manic energy of a marble that had drunk three coffees. Mochi was stretching, but Mochi's stretching looked less like an athlete and more like a piece of taffy attempting yoga. Bùdīng was vibrating. That was his entire warm-up. He was vibrating. His caramel was actually fizzing.

"I'm fine, I'm fine, I'm fine," Bùdīng was whispering to himself. "Definitely not wobbling. Wobble-free. The wobble is a CHOICE."

Zhīma rolled up looking like he had skipped warm-ups entirely in favor of, apparently, eating breakfast. "I roll. That's my warm-up."

"That is not warm-up," said Mango.

"Roll the dice," Zhīma said gravely, "and you might just roll into history."

Mango massaged his temples with two tiny mango arms. "What does that even MEAN?"

"It means he's been waiting all morning to say it," said Bǎi, peering up from where she was lacing imaginary shoes onto her wrinkly purple shell. "And by the way, I dare every single one of you to win your event. Pucker up, buttercups. We are doing this."

Yángmáo was looking dreamily at the javelin. Just… staring at it. As one of her five points slowly drifted toward the ceiling.

"YÁNGMÁO," said Mango. "We have to compete. Stop wishing on equipment."

"Sorry," Yángmáo sighed, "I just thought — what if the javelin is also wishing? It points at the sky too."

Up in the broadcast booth, a golden, slightly crispy voice cleared its throat into the microphone. The whole stadium echoed.

"**STEP RIGHT UP, FOLKS!**" boomed Dàn the Egg Waffle, top hat perched at an angle, grid pattern reflecting the lights. "WELCOME to the **太棒了** (tài bàng le) — AWESOME — All-Taiwan Dessert Track Meet! I'm your announcer, Dàn 'The Golden Voice' Jīdàngāo, and joining me from the icy realm of Penguinsnowboard is our color commentator, the legendary slope-shredder herself — Piper Paddlefoot!"

A small emperor penguin in a glittery blue press jacket waved a flipper from the booth.

"Hi Dàn. Slide into it, everybody." Piper turned to a tiny notepad. "Quick disclaimer: I have never seen a track meet before. I have only ever raced down a mountain. But I am EXTREMELY enthusiastic, and I brought snacks."

"BEAUTIFUL!" Dàn cried. "Let's open this thing up with the 100-METER DASH!"

---

### Event 1: The 100m Dash

Mango stepped onto the starting line and felt his entire mango soul light up like a sunrise.

"Catch me if you can — I'm in season," he whispered to himself.

The starter pistol cracked. Mango LAUNCHED.

He was a streak. A blur. A bright orange comet leaving a trail of golden droplets across the poppy-red track. His legs were a pinwheel. His sweatband flew off. His rosy cheek was practically vibrating with speed.

In Lane 4, Boba was bouncing. Forward. Up. Forward. Up. Forward. UP — uh oh — UP again — and then a particularly enthusiastic bounce sent him soaring two meters into the air, completely off the track, into Lane 7, where he ricocheted off a hurdle (already waiting for the next event), bounced off the digital scoreboard, and landed back in Lane 2 still going forward.

"**加油**!" (jiāyóu) the crowd screamed. "GO FOR IT!"

Mango crossed the finish line first. Boba crossed the finish line eighth, having technically completed the race in three different lanes, two dimensions, and one brief encounter with a bird.

"Mango wins the 100!" Dàn announced. "And Boba wins the unofficial Aerial Sprint Award, which we just invented thirty seconds ago!"

Piper Paddlefoot blinked. "Is that legal?"

"It is now."

---

### Event 2: The 110m Hurdles

This was Bùdīng's moment.

Or, more accurately, Bùdīng's series of moments. Ten of them. One per hurdle.

He approached Hurdle One like it personally owed him money.

"You and me," he whispered to it. "I am totally fine. I am not nervous. I am SO not nervous I'm basically asleep right now."

He took off.

Wobbled.

Hit Hurdle One with his caramel top.

Wobbled HARDER.

Cleared Hurdle Two by accident — bouncing off the first one had launched him.

Hurdle Three — he just slid right under it. The crowd gasped.

Hurdle Four — he leaped, miraculously, gracefully, almost balletically — and landed in a tiny caramel splat that re-formed itself instantly.

"**小心**! (xiǎoxīn) BE CAREFUL!" someone in the stands yelled, which was unhelpful at this point.

By Hurdle Ten, Bùdīng was no longer running a race. He was performing a one-pudding gymnastics floor routine. He cartwheeled. He somersaulted. He left a trail of dramatic caramel exclamation marks on the track. He crossed the finish line at the EXACT moment the dramatic music in his own head reached its crescendo.

The crowd stood up. The crowd lost their minds.

Bùdīng wobbled into Mango's arms, weeping caramel. "I'm fine. I'm fine. I'm — I might actually be fine. I'm fine!"

---

### Event 3: Shot Put

Zhīma rolled up to the throwing circle, took one look at the regulation shot put — a 7-kilogram metal ball — and said, very calmly:

"I AM the shot put."

He climbed onto the launcher. He winked at the crowd. He nodded once at Mango.

"Roll with it."

The judge pulled the lever.

Zhīma went **WHOOSH**. A perfect, golden, sesame-speckled sphere flying through the Kaohsiung sky. The crowd shielded their eyes from the sun and the sparkle of his sesame seeds. He sailed past the world-record marker. He sailed past the regional-record marker. He sailed past, in fact, the entire shot put zone, the long jump pit, AND the snack bar.

He landed in the food cart parking lot.

A vendor selling **芒果冰** (mángguǒ bīng) = mango shaved ice opened her cart, found a beautiful golden sesame ball sitting in her ice tray, and shrugged like this was a normal Tuesday.

"Roll with it," she said.

"NEW WORLD RECORD!" Dàn screamed. "BY APPROXIMATELY FORTY METERS!"

---

### Event 4: High Jump

Mochi looked at the bar.

The bar was set at one meter.

Mochi was a marshmallow-sized blob.

"GROUP HUG FIRST!!!" Mochi cried.

After group hug (which involved everyone, including, somehow, the line judge), Mochi sprinted at the bar. Or — Mochi's version of sprinting, which was more of a determined waddle.

At the takeoff point, Mochi did not jump.

Mochi STRETCHED.

Up, up, up — stretching like taffy, like a question being asked, like a piece of dessert chewing gum being pulled by a giant invisible hand. His pillowy white body extended toward the heavens, dusted with potato starch, smiling all the way. He cleared the bar.

Then he kept stretching.

Past two meters. Past three. Past the press box.

"Mochi?" Mango called nervously.

"I'M FINE!" came a tiny, very far-up voice. "STICK TOGETHER!!!"

It took six minutes, two ladders, and a polite intervention by Piper Paddlefoot before Mochi snapped back into mochi shape with a satisfying THWUMP. He landed in the pit, dusted himself off, and giggled until sesame oozed out of his cheeks. (That was a different friend, but the principle was the same.)

---

### Event 5: Long Jump and Steeplechase Combined

The judges were exhausted. They combined two events to save time.

Boba bounced down the runway, hit the takeoff board, and bounced approximately INTO THE NEXT POSTAL CODE.

Bǎi the Passion Fruit, meanwhile, cracked her own shell open at the steeplechase water pit, leaping into the splash zone with a battle cry of "**PUCKER UP, BUTTERCUPS!**" The crowd got soaked in sour-sweet orange spray. They loved it. A small child in the front row yelled "**好吃**!" (hǎochī) = "DELICIOUS!"

Yángmáo took the javelin. She closed her eyes. She made a wish.

Then she threw a five-pointed star-shaped wish across the sky that landed gently, gently, gently — point first — in the exact center of the target.

The whole stadium fell silent.

"Every point is a wish," she whispered.

"Folks," Dàn breathed into the microphone, "I do not know what I just witnessed but I think it was magic."

---

### Event 6: The 4x100 Relay

The Final.

Mango. Boba. Mochi. Zhīma.

Four batons. One was a boba straw. Two were chopsticks. One was a churro that Boba had ordered as a snack and accidentally entered into the official equipment manifest.

"OKAY TEAM!" Mango yelled. "We're a TEAM. We pass the baton. WE FINISH TOGETHER."

The pistol cracked. Mango ran the first leg with the speed of a mango in peak season, juice spraying like a sprinkler. He handed off to Boba, who bounced the second leg with the controlled chaos of a marble in a pinball machine. Boba launched the baton-churro at Mochi mid-bounce. Mochi caught it with a stretchy arm extended sixteen feet across the track and ran the third leg by simply pulling himself toward the finish line like a rubber band.

Anchor leg: Zhīma.

Zhīma did not run. Zhīma ROLLED.

He rolled so fast he caught fire. (Not actual fire. Heat shimmer fire. Cinematic fire. Sesame seeds glowing fire.)

He rolled across the finish line first.

The stands EXPLODED.

The team collided in the most chaotic, joyful, sticky, sesame-coated, caramel-streaked, juice-spraying group hug in the history of the National Stadium. Bùdīng wobbled in. Bǎi cracked herself open in celebration. Yángmáo glowed. Bīng melted right onto everyone — and for once, nobody minded one bit. Even Caǎo joined the hug, deadpan, beanie crooked.

"You did it," Caǎo said quietly. "No big deal."

"WE DID IT!" Mango shouted, and burst into juice.

---

### The Closing Ceremony

The trophy — the Golden Mango Cup — was enormous and ridiculous and shaped, for some reason, like a smiling boba pearl wearing a crown. Mango accepted it on behalf of the team and managed to say "Thank you for believing in us" before he started crying golden tears that were just more juice.

But the REAL prize was what came next.

The stadium chefs — a gentle army in white aprons — wheeled out a glistening, gigantic mountain of shaved ice in the center of the field. The crowd hushed.

One by one, the team climbed onto the ice mountain.

Bīng draped himself across the top like a snowy summit. Mango cubes scattered themselves over the slope. Mochi tucked into golden valleys. Boba pearls bounced into syrupy lakes. Zhīma settled into crispy nests. Bǎi spread her bright orange pulp like sunset light. Yángmáo placed her star-shaped slices like little wishes. Bùdīng wobbled into a perfect golden crown on top, dripping caramel down the mountainside in long, elegant ribbons.

"Behold," announced Dàn, with the gravity of a coronation, "the **冠軍冰** (guànjūn bīng) = the CHAMPION'S SHAVED ICE!"

The crowd let out a collective gasp of pure dessert love.

Piper Paddlefoot, in the booth, picked up a spoon roughly her own size. "Slide into it, everybody."

Fifty thousand spoons descended like a happy snowfall.

And every dessert on that mountain — every Mango cube, every Boba pearl, every wobbly Bùdīng crown — went out exactly the way they wanted to: in a roaring, golden, juice-spraying, sesame-sparkling, sticky, sweet, EVERYONE-WINS stadium of joy.

Somewhere in the middle of all those happy slurps, you could swear you heard Mango laughing.

He'd run his race. He'd captained his team. He'd lived his **比賽**.

Every event had been someone's spotlight. Every spotlight had ended in dessert. And every dessert had ended in a smile.

The stadium lights are dimming now. The crowd is going home. Curl up under your covers like a mochi tucking into shaved ice, and let the cheers carry you into dreams. Goodnight, sweet champion.
