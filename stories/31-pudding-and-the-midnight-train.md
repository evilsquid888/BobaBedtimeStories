# Story 31: Pudding and the Midnight Train

**Main Character:** Bùdīng (布丁) — Pudding
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A late-night Taiwan Railways train rocking gently through the dark countryside toward home
**Theme:** It's okay to wobble — you still arrive
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 火車 (huǒchē) = train

---

The thing you have to understand about Bùdīng is that he wobbled even when he was standing perfectly still on a perfectly flat floor in a perfectly empty room with absolutely nothing happening.

So you can IMAGINE how he felt about getting on a moving **火車** (huǒchē) = train.

"I'm fine," Bùdīng announced to no one, gripping the doorway of Car 7 with two trembling little caramel hands. "I'm totally fine. I'm completely fine. I'm a calm, brave pudding riding a train all by himself for the very first time, and I am absolutely not going to—"

The train lurched.

"—WOBBLE," squeaked Bùdīng, wobbling so hard that a single golden drip of caramel slid down his side and went *plip* on the floor.

He looked down at the *plip*.

"Sorry," he whispered to the floor. "**不好意思** (bù hǎo yìsi) = excuse me, so sorry, I'll—I didn't mean to drip on you, I just—" He stopped. He was apologizing to a train floor. The train floor did not care. The train floor had seen a thousand spills and would see a thousand more.

This did not make Bùdīng feel better.

Bùdīng, if you've never met him, is a classic caramel flan: a smooth golden dome with dark caramel dripping gloriously down his sides, two enormous nervous eyes, and a center of gravity that simply refuses to commit to anything. He jiggles when he's happy. He jiggles when he's sad. He jiggles when he THINKS about jiggling, which is most of the time.

And tonight he was going home. Alone. On the late train. With seven whole stations to go.

"Okay," he told himself, finding an empty seat by the window and very, very carefully lowering himself onto it. The seat was warm and a little squishy and it caught him like a cupped hand. "Okay. Seven stations. I just have to sit here and not fall apart and get off at the right **站** (zhàn) = station. Easy. Simple. No wobbling required."

The train pulled out of the platform.

*Da-dum. Da-dum. Da-dum.*

The whole car began to rock — left, then right, then left — a slow heavy sway like a cradle the size of a building.

Bùdīng's eyes went as wide as two moons.

"Oh no," he breathed. "Oh no oh no oh no. It MOVES. It moves SIDEWAYS. Nobody told me it moves sideways. Why does it move sideways?! I can't move sideways! I barely move FORWARD! I'm going to slosh! I'm going to slosh right out of my own caramel and there will be NOTHING LEFT of me but a sad golden puddle in seat 14 and they'll have to mop me up at the last **站** and—"

"Young man," said a voice, "you are doing an awful lot of catastrophizing for someone who just got on."

Bùdīng turned.

Across the aisle sat Dòuhuā — silky, soft, serene Dòuhuā, the tofu pudding, floating in her shallow bowl of warm ginger syrup with her eyes half-closed and a small peaceful smile on her face. She was, somehow, not spilling at all, even though she was technically MORE liquid than Bùdīng was.

"How—" Bùdīng pointed a shaky hand at her bowl. "How are you not SLOSHING?"

"Oh, I'm sloshing," said Dòuhuā gently. "I'm sloshing constantly. I just stopped fighting it."

"You stopped FIGHTING it?"

"The stream does not argue with the riverbank, little one. It bends. Watch." And as the train swayed left, Dòuhuā... swayed left too. Soft. Easy. Like she and the train had agreed on it beforehand. Her syrup tilted, kissed the rim of the bowl, and slid right back. Not a drop spilled.

Bùdīng stared. "I could NEVER. The second I relax I'll—"

The train hit a curve.

Bùdīng went *WOBBLE-WOBBLE-WOBBLE-SPLAT* against the window, peeled himself off the glass, left a perfect caramel print of his own face on it, and said in a tiny voice, "...I'm fine. I'm fine. I'm wobbling."

A few seats away, a sleepy traveler chuckled without opening his eyes.

---

The train rocked on through the dark. Out the window, the countryside slid past — black rice fields, a lonely lit-up convenience store, the silver gleam of a river under the moon, mountains crouched like sleeping cats on the horizon.

Bùdīng was not looking at any of it. Bùdīng was counting.

"One station down," he whispered, watching the platform sign blur past. "**新烏日** (Xīn Wūrì). That's one. Six to go. Don't miss it. Whatever you do, don't miss your **站**." He pulled out a little crumpled paper where his Auntie Hóng Dòu had written his stop in careful letters, smoothed it flat for the eleventh time, and read it again, even though he had it memorized, even though he had read it so many times the ink was getting nervous.

"What if I fall asleep," he said suddenly, sitting bolt upright, which made him wobble, which made him drip. "What if I fall asleep and I MISS it and I wake up at the END of the line in some strange dark town where nobody knows me and I'm just a lost little pudding with no caramel left and—"

"Hey. Hey, kid." 

The voice came from the row in front. A worn, kindly face turned around to look at him — it was Dìguā, the sweet potato, rough brown skin and a tiny hardhat tipped back on his head, golden warmth practically glowing out of him. He was on his way home from a long shift at the night cart.

"First time on the late **火車** by yourself?" Dìguā asked.

Bùdīng nodded so hard he sloshed.

"Yeah, I could tell," said Dìguā, not unkindly. "Listen. I've been riding this line for years. You know what gets folks to their stop? Not panicking. The train knows the way even when you don't. You just gotta trust the rails." He tapped the floor with one earthy hand. "**小心** (xiǎoxīn) = be careful, sure — but careful ain't the same as scared. Careful is calm. Scared is just careful that forgot to breathe."

Bùdīng blinked. "But what about my stop—"

"What's your stop?"

"**豐原** (Fēngyuán)."

"Ha! No way." Dìguā grinned a warm golden grin. "That's MY stop too. Tell you what. I'll wake you. You can panic with a backup. Deal?"

Something in Bùdīng's wobbly chest went very still and very warm for just a second.

"...Deal," he whispered. "Thank you. **不好意思**—I mean—**謝謝** (xièxie) = thank you. I always say sorry when I mean thank you. I'm working on it."

"I know you are, kid," said Dìguā, and turned back around with a yawn.

---

Now here is where something rather important happened, and I want you to pay attention, little reader, because it might come in handy for you someday too.

The train hit a long, slow, rolling stretch of track — the kind that sways you deep and easy, left and right and left and right, like a giant invisible grandmother rocking the whole car in her arms.

And Bùdīng braced. He clenched every caramel muscle he had. He went stiff as a frightened flan, fighting the sway with all his might, holding himself rigid against the rock of the **火車**—

—and that's when he sloshed the WORST he'd sloshed all night, caramel jumping right up to his very rim.

"Stop fighting it," murmured Dòuhuā from across the aisle, her eyes still closed, her bowl still serene. "You're spilling because you're stiff. Let go. Wobble *with* it."

"Wobble WITH it?" 

"Become the rocking," she said softly. "Don't be the thing the train is rocking. Be the rocking itself."

It made no sense.

It made all the sense in the world.

So Bùdīng — terrified, exhausted, brave little Bùdīng — took the biggest breath a pudding can take... and let go.

The train swayed left, and instead of fighting it, he *leaned* left. The train swayed right, and he flowed right. Left. Right. Left. Right. He stopped being a stiff scared dome bracing against the world and became something loose and easy and gentle, a little golden boat riding a little golden tide.

And the caramel?

The caramel didn't spill at all. It just... rocked. Side to side. Settled. Calm.

"I'm wobbling," Bùdīng whispered in wonder. But this time it didn't sound like an apology.

This time it sounded like a discovery.

"I'm WOBBLING," he said again, louder, delighted. "And it's—it's NICE? The train wobbles and I wobble and we're wobbling TOGETHER and nothing is spilling and—oh. *Oh.* This is the secret. This was the secret the whole time."

"Mm," said Dòuhuā, smiling with her eyes shut. "Now you're sailing."

---

*Da-dum. Da-dum. Da-dum.*

The very same rocking that had terrified him an hour ago had turned, somewhere in the dark, into the coziest feeling Bùdīng had ever felt.

The train rocked, and he rocked, and the rocking went all the way down into the soft center of him. *Da-dum. Da-dum.* Like a heartbeat. Like a lullaby with wheels. The window was cool against his caramel-printed cheek. The lights in the car had dimmed to a soft amber glow. Outside, the dark fields rolled past, gentle and endless, and the moon followed along beside the train the way the moon always does, patient and round and kind.

His enormous nervous eyes began, at last, to droop.

"Five stations," he murmured. "Four... I should... I should stay awake... I should count..."

But the rocking was so soft now. So warm. *Da-dum. Da-dum.* The whole train was singing him the oldest song there is — the one that says *you are carried, you are held, you don't have to do anything but rest now, little one.*

His eyes closed.

His caramel went still and shining.

And brave little Bùdīng, who had been so sure he'd slosh apart, slept — wobbling gently, ever so gently, in perfect time with the **火車**.

He didn't miss his stop.

Of course he didn't. Because just as the train slowed into **豐原** station, a warm earthy hand patted him softly on his golden dome.

"Hey, kid," whispered Dìguā. "We're home."

Bùdīng blinked awake, soft and sleepy and not even a little bit panicked. The doors slid open. Cool night air drifted in, smelling of rain and rice fields and somewhere, faintly, somebody's late-night noodle stand.

"I made it," Bùdīng said, wobbling down onto the quiet platform, leaving one last little caramel footprint behind him like a signature. "I rode the **火車** all by myself, and I wobbled the whole way, and I—I still arrived."

"Course you did," said Dìguā, tipping his tiny hardhat. "Wobbling's not falling, kid. Wobbling's just how the soft ones keep their balance. **晚安** (wǎn'ān) = goodnight."

"**晚安**," whispered Bùdīng.

And he wobbled off down the platform toward the warm yellow lights of home, where Auntie Hóng Dòu already had a little pot of something sweet bubbling on the stove, and a soft place waiting, and a hug shaped exactly like a pudding.

He was still wobbling.

He would always be wobbling.

And that, he finally understood, was perfectly, wonderfully fine.

---

## Goodnight Blessing

Tonight, little dreamer, may you rock gently to sleep the way the midnight train rocks — *da-dum, da-dum* — carried softly through the dark toward home. You don't have to hold yourself stiff against the swaying of the world. You don't have to be brave by being still. You can wobble. You can tremble. You can be soft and unsure and full to the brim — and the rails will still know the way, and the morning will still come, and you will still, sweetly, arrive. So let go now, little pudding. Lean into the rocking. The train has you. Sleep. 晚安.
