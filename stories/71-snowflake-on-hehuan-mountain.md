# Story 71: Tangyuan & the First Snow on Hehuan Mountain

**Main Character:** Tāng Yuán (Tangyuan)
**Guest Characters:** Red Bean, Niú Niú (Beef Noodle Soup)
**Setting:** The rare snowfall atop Hehuanshan, Taiwan's high peaks
**Theme:** Warmth is a thing you carry, not a place you find.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 雪 (xuě) = snow

---

Now, Tangyuan had heard a great many silly rumors in his round little life, but the silliest one of all came whispered up the mountain that night.

"They say," gasped a passing cloud, shivering so hard it sprinkled, "that it's going to **雪** (xuě) = snow! On Hehuanshan! For the very first time this whole winter!"

Tangyuan, who was a plump white rice ball with a warm black-sesame heart, did the only sensible thing. He bounced.

"Snow?!" he cried, jiggling with joy. "Real snow? We have to go! We always go where the family goes, and tonight the family is going UP!"

You should know that Tangyuan never said "I." He always said "we," because to a tangyuan, one is just the start of many. So when he rolled toward the trailhead, he was already humming, already counting friends, already imagining a mountain peak full of giggling.

What he did not imagine, dear reader, was how very, very cold a mountain can be.

The path up Hehuanshan curled like a noodle through the dark. The higher Tangyuan bounced, the thinner the air grew, the brighter the stars burned, and the colder the wind nibbled at his soft round edges. By the time he reached the top, the first flakes were already drifting down — fat, lazy, sparkling pieces of **雪**, settling on the grass like sugar on a steamed bun.

"Oh," Tangyuan breathed. "Oh, we have never seen anything so beautiful in all our we."

And then he heard the chattering.

Not happy chattering. Teeth chattering.

Sitting beneath a frosted signpost was Red Bean, the burgundy grandmother bean with the soft white stripe down her middle, clutching her wooden ladle like a walking stick. Her warm nurturing glow had gone a touch... blue.

"R-Red Bean!" gasped Tangyuan, rolling to her side so fast he left a little track in the snow like a comma. "What are you doing all the way up here, alone?"

"I c-came to see the snow, dear," she said, her ladle rattling. "I thought it would be l-lovely. And it is. It's just also extremely, terribly, ladle-freezingly cold, and these old beans were not built for it." She gave a brave little chuckle. "I'm a sweet soup bean. I belong in a warm bowl, not a cold cloud."

Tangyuan's sesame heart squeezed. He looked at the snow. He looked at his shivering friend. And he felt, for the very first time in his bouncy life, a little bit helpless.

Because a tangyuan cannot light a fire. A tangyuan has no blanket. A tangyuan is, frankly, a rice ball — soft, round, and about as useful in a blizzard as a marshmallow in a mitten.

"Don't worry," he said anyway, because that is what you say. "We'll think of something. We always do." He patted Red Bean with his stubby little side, though patting a freezing friend with a cold rice ball is — if we are being honest — not nearly as helpful as it sounds. "Hm," said Tangyuan. "We may need a bigger idea."

That was when the snow gave a great WHUMP, and out of a drift exploded a steaming clay bowl wrapped head-to-rim in a long wheat-noodle scarf.

"SLURP FIRST, WORRY LATER!" boomed Niú Niú, the beef noodle soup, flinging his noodle scarf dramatically over one beef-cube shoulder. A cozy cloud of steam puffed off the top of him, melting a perfect little circle of snow. "Kiddos! Fancy meeting you on the coldest rock in Taiwan. Whoo! It's chilly enough up here to freeze the bok choy off a fellow." He nodded down at his bok-choy collar, which had indeed gone a little crispy.

"Niú Niú!" Tangyuan cried, and bounced so high he very nearly became a snowflake himself. "You're warm! You're a whole bowl of warm!"

"Born warm, kiddo, raised warm, and warm I shall remain," said Niú Niú, puffing his steam-halo proudly. "You could leave me on an iceberg and I'd just melt myself a comfy little hot tub." He slurped, which is what he does when he laughs at his own jokes, which is often.

"That I am, kiddo," Niú Niú said, ladling out a comforting chuckle. "Always steaming, always toasty. It's the one thing a soup is good for." He squinted around at the swirling white. "Say... why are you two up here turning into popsicles instead of cuddling up in the night market like sensible snacks?"

"Snow," said Red Bean and Tangyuan together, and pointed.

Niú Niú looked up. A flake landed right on his friendly clay nose and melted instantly into a tiny twinkling drop, and even big hearty Niú Niú went quiet and round-eyed.

"...Well now," he said softly. "Would you look at that. The whole sky's making dumplings and dropping 'em on us."

For one perfect moment, the three of them simply watched the **雪** fall. It drifted down in fat soft spirals, gathering on the signposts and the grass and Niú Niú's noodle scarf, hushing the whole mountain into a soft white quiet. It was the prettiest thing any of them had ever seen — and the coldest. Red Bean shivered again, harder this time, and a frost-flower bloomed right on the tip of her ladle.

"It really is l-lovely," she whispered through chattering teeth. "I just wish I could enjoy it without my bottom bean turning to ice."

That did it. Tangyuan stopped bouncing. He thought, and thought, and thought — which for a rice ball involves a great deal of jiggling.

"Niú Niú," he said slowly, "you are a great big warm steaming bowl."

"The warmest," Niú Niú agreed proudly.

"And a bowl," said Tangyuan, his sesame heart suddenly glowing like a tiny coal, "is a place where soup goes."

"That is generally how it works, yes."

"And tangyuan," said Tangyuan, "live in warm soup!"

There was a pause while the wind whistled and Niú Niú's eyebrows — well, the painted swirls where his eyebrows would be — slowly climbed up his rim.

"Kiddo," he said, "are you suggesting what I think you're suggesting?"

"Make a pot!" Tangyuan shouted, bouncing again, this time with a plan. "A great big pot of togetherness! You keep us warm, we keep you company, and we all stay cozy on the coldest peak in all the land! Cozy isn't a PLACE, Niú Niú — it's a thing we CARRY. And tonight we carry it together!"

For exactly half a second, nobody moved.

Then Niú Niú threw back his rim and laughed his big slurpy laugh, so loud it knocked snow off three signposts. "HA! Now THAT'S a noodle of an idea! Climb on in, you frosty little dumplings — the broth's just right!"

He gave his noodle scarf an enormous theatrical TOSS, and it spread out wide and warm like the lip of a giant cooking pot. Tangyuan hopped up first, sliding down into the cozy steaming broth with a happy *bloop*.

"OHHH," he sighed, sinking in up to his sesame heart. "Warm. So warm. We forgot what warm felt like!"

"In you come, Red Bean!" Niú Niú called. "Mind the bok choy, she's a little crunchy tonight."

Red Bean clambered in with a grateful groan, her blue glow flushing back to a healthy burgundy the very instant the broth touched her. "Oh, my old beans," she sighed. "Oh, that's the stuff. I haven't been this toasty since my grandmother bean stewed me on a winter morning a hundred years ago." She gave her ladle a happy little wave, scattering the frost-flower into the steam.

And there they bobbed — a beef noodle soup, a sweet red bean, and a sesame-hearted rice ball — three friends who, by every rule of cooking, did NOT belong in the same bowl, all stewing happily together under the falling snow.

"You know," said Niú Niú, watching the flakes melt the moment they touched his steam-halo, "beef and tangyuan in one pot. Sweet bean and savory broth. My old noodle-soup grandfather would faint clean off the stove."

"Trust the weird," said Tangyuan, doing a slow proud spin. "It's delicious."

"That's not your line," chuckled Red Bean. "That's Bǐng Bǐng's line, the ice-cream-roll one."

"Tonight," declared Tangyuan grandly, "it belongs to everybody. That's the whole point of a pot!"

They laughed, and the laughing made more steam, and the steam rose up and up into the freezing dark — and here is the loveliest part, dear reader. As their warm cloud drifted higher, the **雪** falling through it caught the heat and the light, and each flake glittered gold for one breath before it melted. From far down in the valley, anyone looking up at Hehuanshan that night would have seen a single small warm glow on the cold black peak, sparkling like a lantern, like a heartbeat, like a tiny bowl of friendship perched at the top of the world.

Other shivering snacks saw it too. A lost Sweet Potato in a tiny frost-dusted hardhat wandered out of a drift, took one look at the steaming pot, and said "Now THAT'S smart engineering" before climbing aboard. A frostbitten Mango with a frozen sweatband bounced over and dove right into the broth, sighing "best warm-up of my LIFE — put me in, coach!" A pair of Coconut Jelly cubes slid in saying "broth hot tub, bro, no way," and bobbed about looking enormously pleased with the universe.

Soon the pot was crowded and bubbling and full of giggling, and Niú Niú just kept tossing his scarf wider and ladling out more room, because that is what warm things do — they make space. Red Bean, recovered and rosy now, fished her ladle out of the broth and began serving everyone little sips of warmth like the nurturing grandmother bean she was. "Drink up, dears, drink up — there's plenty, there's always plenty when you share it."

"That's the funny thing about warmth," murmured Tangyuan, watching the snow swirl harmlessly around their glowing little pot. "The more we give it away, the more of it we seem to have."

"Everybody hold a sesame heart!" Tangyuan called over the happy chatter. "Or a bean! Or a noodle! Tonight nobody freezes alone — tonight we are all one big delicious family, and the snow can come as cold as it likes, because we brought our own summer up the mountain in a clay bowl!"

And the snow did come, soft and silver and endless, blanketing all of Hehuanshan in quiet white. But it could not get a single one of them, because right in the middle of all that beautiful cold sat the warmest, slurpiest, most togetherest pot of soup the mountain had ever known.

By the time the first dawn light crept pink over the peaks, the snow had stopped, the friends were dozing in a gentle simmer, and Tangyuan — bobbing slow and sleepy against Niú Niú's cozy rim — finally understood the rumor's real secret.

It was never the snow that mattered.

It was who you watched it with.
