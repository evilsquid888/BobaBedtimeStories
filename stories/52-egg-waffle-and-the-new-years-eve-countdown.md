# Story 52: Egg Waffle & the New Year's Eve Countdown

**Main Character:** Egg Waffle (雞蛋糕)
**Guest Characters:** Pudding, Sesame Ball
**Setting:** Taipei 101 New Year's Eve fireworks crowd
**Theme:** The best show is the one you share with friends.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 煙火 (yānhuǒ) = fireworks

---

On the very last night of the year, in the bustling square beneath Taipei 101, Egg Waffle climbed onto an upside-down bucket, threw open his arms, and bellowed at the top of his golden, grid-shaped lungs.

"LADIES AND GENTLEMEN, BUBBLES AND BEANS, GATHER 'ROUND FOR THE GREATEST SHOW THE ISLAND HAS EVER SEEN!"

Three pigeons looked at him. One yawned.

Egg Waffle did not let this discourage him. Egg Waffle was *never* discouraged. He was a golden waffle baked into a row of little round bubbles, each one puffy and warm, and he wore a tiny top hat tilted at exactly the angle a showman's top hat should tilt. He twirled a tiny cane. He had a voice like a carnival and a heart like a firework waiting to go off.

"Tonight," he announced to the pigeons, "I will perform the BIGGEST New Year's spectacular in the history of dessert!"

The pigeons left. But Egg Waffle was already dreaming.

---

The square was filling up fast. All across Taipei, every snack and sweet and savory friend on the island had come to watch the **煙火** (yānhuǒ) = fireworks burst off the side of Taipei 101 at the stroke of midnight. The tower rose up and up, glittering blue and silver, so tall it seemed to poke a hole in the sky for the stars to peek through.

Egg Waffle wriggled his way through the crowd, dragging an enormous suitcase that went *clunk-clatter-clunk*.

"Coming through! Performer coming through! Mind the genius!"

He found a clear patch of pavement near the front, planted his bucket, and began to set up. Out of the suitcase came ribbons, a foldable hoop, three rubber chickens, a kazoo, and a sign that he had painted himself in dazzling gold letters:

**THE ONE — THE ONLY — EGG WAFFLE'S MIDNIGHT MEGA-SPECTACULAR! (Now With 40% More Spectacle!)**

"Forty percent more than what?" asked a small wobbly voice behind him.

Egg Waffle spun around. It was Pudding — soft, jiggly Pudding, the caramel flan, trembling slightly the way Pudding always trembled, whether from nerves or just from being made entirely of wobble. Beside him rolled Sesame Ball, a golden sphere coated in toasted seeds, bouncing on the spot, bold and round and ready for anything.

"Forty percent more than LAST year," said Egg Waffle grandly, even though there had been no last year. "Friends! You've arrived at exactly the right moment. I need a glamorous assistant and a daring stunt-double. Pudding, you're glamorous. Sesame Ball, you're daring."

"I'm mostly just *nervous,*" admitted Pudding.

"Even better! Stage fright reads as *passion* from the back row."

---

Egg Waffle's plan was enormous. First he would juggle the rubber chickens while playing the kazoo. Then Sesame Ball would roll through the flaming hoop (there was no flame — Egg Waffle had drawn orange flames on it with a crayon, for safety). Then Pudding would do a dramatic interpretive wobble. And finally, Egg Waffle would leap from the bucket and land in a heroic pose, *exactly* as the midnight fireworks exploded behind him, so that it looked like the whole sky had detonated just to celebrate *him.*

"It's going to be unforgettable," he whispered, eyes shining.

"It's going to be something," said Sesame Ball, which was the kindest true thing he could think of.

So they began.

Egg Waffle hurled the rubber chickens into the air and blew the kazoo. Unfortunately, blowing a kazoo and catching three chickens at once turns out to be *quite* hard, even if you are very confident. The first chicken bonked him on the hat. The second landed in a stranger's bowl of **滷肉飯** (lǔ ròu fàn) = braised pork rice. The third flew off into the crowd and was never seen again. Somewhere, a tiny voice called out, "Hey! Who threw a chicken at my dinner?"

"PART OF THE ACT!" Egg Waffle shouted. "Sesame Ball — the hoop!"

Sesame Ball took a running roll and dove for the crayon-flame hoop. He was magnificent. He was a golden cannonball of courage. He sailed straight through the center —

— and the cardboard hoop folded shut around him like a clam, and he rolled off across the square wearing it like a very embarrassing collar, shouting "I REGRET NOTHING" as he disappeared between two ladies selling sausages.

"Pudding!" Egg Waffle squeaked, sweat beading on his bubbles. "Save us! Dramatic interpretive wobble! NOW!"

Pudding stepped forward bravely. Pudding wobbled. Pudding wobbled so hard, with so much heartfelt emotion, that Pudding lost his balance entirely and went *splat* — a perfect, gentle, caramel splat — right onto Egg Waffle's hand-painted sign, smearing the gold letters into a slippery brown puddle that now read, blurrily:

**EGG WA — MIDNIGH — SPLAT.**

There was a silence.

It was not a hushed, breathless, *the-magic-is-about-to-begin* silence. It was the other kind.

---

Egg Waffle stood very still on his bucket, top hat drooping, kazoo dangling from one bubble. The crowd had already turned away, chattering and laughing, gazing up at the great glittering tower. Nobody was watching the show. Nobody had ever really *been* watching the show.

His grand spectacular had flopped harder than a scallion pancake in a hurricane.

Sesame Ball rolled back, finally free of the hoop, panting. Pudding peeled himself off the sign with a small, wet *thwup* and trembled over, looking up at his friend with worried caramel eyes.

"Egg Waffle?" Pudding said softly. "Are you okay?"

For once in his loud golden life, Egg Waffle did not have a big voice. He sat down on his bucket with a quiet little squeak.

"I just wanted to put on the biggest show in the world," he murmured. "I wanted everyone to look up and gasp and remember it forever. And instead I threw a chicken into someone's dinner."

"To be fair," said Sesame Ball, "it was a *great* throw."

Egg Waffle gave a tiny, broken laugh. Then he sighed, and the sigh fogged up his little top hat.

And that was the exact moment the loudspeakers crackled to life, and a voice rang out across the whole square, deep and warm and thrilling:

**"SHÍ! ... JIǓ! ... BĀ! ..."**

The countdown.

Ten. Nine. Eight.

---

All around them, something extraordinary happened. The entire crowd — every snack, every sweet, every tired and happy face in the city — turned toward the tower as one. And they began to count. Together.

**"QĪ! LIÙ! WǓ!"**

Seven. Six. Five.

A pineapple-cake stranger pressed in close on Egg Waffle's left. A whole giggling crew of popcorn-chicken nuggets squished in on his right, bouncing on their tiptoes. Behind him, a soup dumpling and a beef-noodle bowl and a wobbly bowl of tofu pudding all leaned in shoulder-to-shoulder-to-shoulder, packed warm and tight, like dumplings in a single cozy steamer.

A complete stranger — a big, bold fried chicken cutlet — flung one crispy arm around Egg Waffle's shoulders and roared, "COUNT WITH US, LITTLE WAFFLE!"

**"SÌ! SĀN!"**

Four. Three.

Egg Waffle blinked. He looked at the thousands of faces, none of them looking at *him* — all of them looking *up*, all of them counting the *same* numbers at the *same* moment, all of their breaths puffing little clouds into the cold night air at once.

And he realized, with a warm whoosh that started somewhere in his bubbles, that nobody up on a bucket could ever have made a crowd feel like *this.*

"Pudding," he whispered. "Sesame Ball. Count with me?"

Pudding grabbed one of Egg Waffle's bubbles. Sesame Ball pressed against his side. And together, the three friends shouted, as loud as their funny little voices could go:

**"ÈR! ... YĪ! ..."**

Two. One.

---

The sky exploded.

The **煙火** went off in great golden chrysanthemums and silver waterfalls, blooming and crackling and pouring down the sides of Taipei 101 like the whole tower had turned into a fountain of light. Red and gold and emerald and electric blue burst overhead, *boom* and *boom* and *BOOM*, each one reflected a thousand times in every shining, upturned face.

Egg Waffle gasped. The fireworks lit up his golden bubbles until he glowed like a tiny treasure. They lit up Pudding's wobbly caramel until he sparkled like amber. They lit up Sesame Ball until every toasted seed gleamed like a star.

And the crowd cheered — not for Egg Waffle, not for any one performer — but *with* each other, all together, one enormous joyful sound rising up to meet the lights.

Egg Waffle laughed, and it came out wet and happy. "*This,*" he breathed, "is the biggest show in the world. And I didn't have to do *anything.*"

"You did one thing," said Pudding, snuggling close.

"What?"

"You shared it with us."

A spark drifted down and landed gently on the brim of Egg Waffle's top hat, glowing for one perfect second before winking out. He didn't brush it away. He let it stay.

---

They watched until the very last firework faded, leaving smoke-curls drifting like silver ribbons across the stars. The crowd slowly melted away into the warm midnight, full of light and noise and the soft happy ache of a year well-ended. Egg Waffle never did pack up his suitcase. He left the rubber chickens. He left the kazoo. He left the smudged, splattered sign.

He didn't need a show anymore.

He had something better — two friends pressed close on either side, the smell of smoke and sweetness in the air, and the brand-new year glittering ahead like a sky full of unburst **煙火**, waiting.

"Same spot next year?" asked Sesame Ball, yawning.

"Same spot next year," said Egg Waffle. "But no chickens."

"No chickens," Pudding agreed sleepily.

And the three of them rolled, and wobbled, and waffled their way home beneath a sky that still smelled faintly of starlight.

---

## Goodnight Blessing
May your loudest dreams grow quiet and warm, the way a firework softens into smoke and stars. May you always find someone to count down beside, shoulder to shoulder, breath to breath. The very best things in this world are never the ones we do alone — they are the ones we share, glowing, together. So snuggle in close, little one, and let the year drift gently to its end. Tomorrow is a fresh sky, waiting to be lit. Goodnight.
