# Story 42: Tea Egg & the All-Night Store

**Main Character:** Dàn Dàn (Tea Egg)
**Guest Characters:** Diàn Diàn
**Setting:** A glowing 24-hour convenience store on a quiet rainy street
**Theme:** Small comforts keep the night feeling safe.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 便利商店 (biànlì shāngdiàn) = convenience store

---

On the rainiest night of the rainy season, when the whole street looked like someone had spilled a bowl of soup over it, the only warm light for a hundred steps in any direction glowed out of a little **便利商店** (biànlì shāngdiàn) = convenience store on the corner.

Inside, in a steamy metal cooker by the counter, sat Dàn Dàn the tea egg, bobbing gently and humming to himself.

Now, Dàn Dàn was not your ordinary egg. His marbled brown shell was cracked all over in beautiful spidery lines — like little roads, like rivers, like the wrinkles around a grandmother's smile. And Dàn Dàn could *read* those cracks. He squinted at them the way other folks read tea leaves or the bottom of a teacup.

"Hmm," he murmured, peering down at himself. "A new crack tonight. Curvy. That usually means... a visitor. Someone a little bit lost."

Right then, the door went *ding-dong*, and the rain came in.

## A Beep in the Night

The shop's clerk came bouncing over the floor to greet the visitor — except there was no visitor yet, only a gust of wet wind. The clerk was Diàn Diàn, a round little rice-mochi in a tiny convenience-store vest, glowing softly like a 24-hour sign. He beeped when he walked. He beeped when he thought. He pretty much beeped.

"Beep-boop! Welcome — oh." Diàn Diàn blinked at the empty doorway. "Just the wind. The wind never buys anything. *Terrible* customer."

"Patience," said Dàn Dàn warmly. "Every crack tells a story. And my crackle-lines say the story isn't finished yet."

Diàn Diàn rolled over to the cooker and peered in. "You and your fortune-egg business. Last week you told the rice ball she'd 'meet someone tall and crispy,' and then Pái Pái the chicken cutlet walked in dripping wet."

"And were they not delighted to share an umbrella?" Dàn Dàn said, with a twinkle in his marbled shell.

Diàn Diàn opened his mouth to argue, beeped twice, and admitted, "...They were *very* delighted, actually."

The shop hummed. The fridges purred. Somewhere a slushie machine swirled around and around like a tiny lazy hurricane. It was the coziest place on the whole rainy street, and both of them knew it.

"Besides," added Dàn Dàn, settling deeper into his warm broth with a contented *gloop*, "a tea egg is never wrong. We simply take a very long time to be right. I have been steeping in this exact same pot for three days now. You learn a thing or two about patience when you marinate."

"Three days!" Diàn Diàn beeped, scandalized. "I've restocked the rice-ball shelf eleven times since you got in that pot."

"And every single time," said Dàn Dàn serenely, "you have done it beautifully, kiddo. The rice balls have never looked so loved." Diàn Diàn turned a faint glowing pink at that, which is the mochi version of blushing, and pretended to be very busy straightening a row of gum.

## The Lost Traveler

*Ding-dong.*

This time someone really did come in — a small, soggy zongzi, a sticky-rice dumpling wrapped in green bamboo leaves, tied up with string that had gone all droopy in the rain. His name was Zòng Zi, and he was usually the bravest, most festival-spirited fellow you ever met, the kind who shouts "Wrapped tight, ready to ride!" before a dragon-boat race.

But tonight he just stood in the doorway making a small puddle, looking very small indeed.

"Beep-boop! Welcome to the **便利商店**!" said Diàn Diàn, gliding over with a tiny towel. "Hot drinks on the left, dry towels on the — oh dear, you're shaking."

"I — I think I'm lost," Zòng Zi whispered. His string drooped lower. "I floated downriver for the race and the current went the wrong way and now I don't know which bridge is mine. Every street looks the same in the rain. I can't find home."

The slushie machine swirled sympathetically.

"Come closer, little traveler," said Dàn Dàn from his warm cooker. "Come, come. Sit by the heat. Slurp first — er — that's Niú Niú's line. *Drip-dry* first, worry later."

Zòng Zi shuffled over and sat on the warm ledge beside the cooker, and right away the steam wrapped around him like a blanket. Already the night felt one drop less scary.

Diàn Diàn whizzed off and came back balancing a hot can of barley tea, which he plopped into Zòng Zi's leafy little hands. "Hug this," he instructed. "Beep-boop. Doctor's orders. The doctor is me. I am also the cashier, the shelf-stocker, the mop, and the security system, but right now I am specifically the doctor."

Zòng Zi hugged the warm can. A tiny hiccup of a laugh escaped him, surprising even himself. The barley tea was so warm and the mochi clerk so funny that for one whole second he forgot to be frightened. And one second, as Dàn Dàn liked to say, is exactly how long it takes for hope to sneak back in.

## Reading the Cracks

Dàn Dàn turned himself slowly in the warm broth — *gloop, gloop* — studying his own shell.

"Let me see, let me see," he hummed. "Here is a crack shaped like a river bending left. And here, a crack like a little stone bridge. And here — ah — here is a crack shaped exactly like a tangerine tree."

Zòng Zi sniffled. "What does it mean?"

"It means," said Dàn Dàn gently, "that we can find your way home. Not by magic — by *noticing*. Tell me. When you floated past your home bridge, what did you smell?"

Zòng Zi thought hard. His bamboo leaves crinkled. "...Tangerines! There's an old tangerine tree by my bridge. And incense. The smell of incense, always, day and night."

"A tangerine tree and incense," repeated Dàn Dàn, eyes gleaming. "Diàn Diàn — who do we know who smells of incense and tangerines?"

Diàn Diàn beeped so loudly the fridge lights flickered. "Tǔdì Gōng! The Earth God! His little red temple is by the canal — the one with the tangerine tree leaning right over the water!"

"Then *that*," said Dàn Dàn, "is the bridge our friend is looking for."

"I know the way!" Diàn Diàn beeped, bouncing so high he nearly knocked over a tower of instant noodles. "I deliver out that direction every dawn — well, I *used* to, before they gave me the night shift. Three lefts, a long canal road, and a bridge with a leaning tangerine tree. I'd bet my whole vest on it."

Zòng Zi's leaves perked up half an inch. Then drooped again. "But it's so far. And so dark. And so... rainy. What if I get lost *again* between the lefts? What if a left turns into a right when I'm not looking?"

## Small Comforts

"Ah," said Dàn Dàn, and his voice went soft as a quilt. "Here is the thing about a dark, rainy night, little one. It is never as big as it feels. The night likes to *pretend* it's a giant. But it is really just a lot of little dark patches all holding hands. And little dark patches are scared of little warm lights."

He nodded at the glowing shop around them.

"That's what a place like this is *for*. A **便利商店** is a warm light that never, ever turns off. No matter how late. No matter how wet. There is always one glowing somewhere, with a beeping mochi and a steamy egg, ready to dry your leaves and point you home."

Diàn Diàn puffed up proudly. "Open all night, here for you! That's my whole entire job."

"So," Dàn Dàn went on, "we will not send you out into one big scary dark. We will send you out from one warm light — to the next — to the next. Diàn Diàn, fetch the map. And the snacks. *Especially* the snacks. No one should travel home on an empty tummy."

"Beep-boop, on it!"

Diàn Diàn whizzed around the shop gathering supplies: a warm rice ball for the road, a hot can of barley tea to hug, and best of all, one of those clear plastic ponchos they sell by the register — which, when Diàn Diàn popped it onto Zòng Zi, made the little dumpling look like he'd been beautifully gift-wrapped a second time.

"I'm wrapped tight again!" Zòng Zi gasped, and for the first time that night, he smiled. "Wrapped tight... ready to ride!"

## The Glowing Trail

Dàn Dàn gave his final fortune. He turned in the cooker and read one last crack — a long, branching line that ran clear across his shell.

"This crack," he said, "is the way home. Follow the warm lights. From this corner, the next glow is the noodle stand under the red lantern — Niú Niú's place. He'll wave you on. Past that, the all-night tea shop where Ā-Lǐ the oolong steeps clouds. Past *that*, the soy-milk cart where Dòu Jiāng is already warming up for the dawn. And just beyond the soy-milk cart — "

"The tangerine tree," whispered Zòng Zi.

"The tangerine tree," agreed Dàn Dàn, "and the little red temple, and the bridge, and home. You see? You were never lost. You only needed the lights pointed out to you."

Zòng Zi stood up. His string had dried. His leaves were crisp again. The poncho crinkled importantly.

"Thank you," he said. "I came in feeling like the night was swallowing me. And now it just feels like... a string of cozy little lamps. With me hopping between them."

"That," said Dàn Dàn, "is exactly what the night is. We just have to remember to look at the lamps and not the dark." He winked, which for a tea egg is mostly a wobble. "Every crack tells a story, kiddo. And yours has a very happy ending."

*Ding-dong.* Out went Zòng Zi into the rain — but not a scary rain anymore. Now it was a soft, sparkly, lamp-lit rain, and the little dumpling bounced from glow to glow like a stone skipping across warm water, until his round shape disappeared in the direction of the tangerine tree.

## The Last Beep

Diàn Diàn rolled back to the counter and beeped a long, satisfied beep.

"You know," he said, "he never even bought anything."

Dàn Dàn chuckled, sinking back down into his warm, steamy broth. "Some nights, Diàn Diàn, the best thing a shop can sell is *feeling safe.* And we gave that away for free."

"Beep," agreed Diàn Diàn softly. "Best deal in the store."

The fridges purred. The slushie swirled. The rain whispered on the glass. And in his warm little cooker, Dàn Dàn watched a brand-new crack form across his shell — curvy, gentle, shaped almost exactly like a smile — and he settled in to wait for whoever the night might bring to the warm corner light next.

---

## Goodnight Blessing
May your night feel small and soft, like a string of cozy lamps holding hands in the dark. May there always be a warm light glowing somewhere for you, no matter how late or how wet the world gets outside. May you remember that being a little lost is only the first step in being gently found, and that every winding crack in your day is just a story finding its happy ending. Snuggle down now, little one, wrapped tight and ready to drift. The night is keeping watch, and it is kinder than it looks. Goodnight.
