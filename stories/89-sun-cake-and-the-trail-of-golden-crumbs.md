# Story 89: Sun Cake & the Trail of Golden Crumbs

**Main Character:** Tàiyáng Bǐng (Sun Cake) — a flaky golden Taichung pastry shaped like a little sun, sheds crumbs everywhere, sunny and shy
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** The winding lanes of old Taichung at golden hour
**Theme:** The little bits you scatter can lead friends right to you
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 太陽餅 (tàiyáng bǐng) = sun cake

---

In the old part of Taichung, when the sky goes the color of warm honey and the lanterns yawn awake, there lived the flakiest pastry you ever met. Her name was **太陽餅** (tàiyáng bǐng) = sun cake, and she was shaped like a tiny, golden sun — round and puffed, with a hundred buttery layers curling up at the edges like little flames. She glowed. She really did.

There was only one problem.

Everywhere Sun Cake went, she left a mess. A scatter of golden flakes drifted off her with every single step.

*Flutter.* A crumb. *Hop.* Three crumbs. *A polite little sneeze* — and suddenly the whole alley sparkled like someone had spilled a jar of sunshine.

"Oh no," Sun Cake whispered, going pink at the edges. "Not again."

(Reader, between you and me, she had been saying *not again* for her entire life.)

---

Sun Cake was shy. Spectacularly, toe-curlingly shy. She did not like being noticed, which is a tricky thing to manage when you are literally shaped like a glowing celestial body and you sprinkle a buttery sparkle-trail behind you wherever you go.

She had tried *everything*.

She tried tiptoeing. The crumbs came anyway.

She tried holding very, very still. The crumbs *still* came anyway, because flaky pastry simply cannot keep itself together, the same way you cannot keep yourself from giggling when somebody says the word "noodle" in a funny voice.

She even tried wearing a raincoat, in July, when there was absolutely no rain. The neighbors thought this was a bit much.

"I'm a walking spill," Sun Cake sighed, dusting flakes off her shoulders only to make four more flutter down. "Everyone can see exactly where I've been. There's no hiding when you leave a trail like a comet."

So Sun Cake did her best to stay small and quiet and out of the way. She lived alone in a cozy little nook above a tea shop, and she only ever went out at **黃昏** (huánghūn) = dusk, that soft golden hour when the lanes turned amber and the world felt sleepy and forgiving. Fewer people. Less noticing. More room for a shy little sun cake to scuttle to the market and scuttle home before anyone counted her crumbs.

It was a lonely sort of life. But it was tidy. (Well — it wasn't tidy at all, really. There were crumbs absolutely everywhere. But it was *lonely*, which Sun Cake had somehow decided was the same as safe.)

---

Now, on the other side of old Taichung, on this particular golden evening, somebody very small was very, very lost.

Her name was Méihuā — Little Plum — and she was a soft, blushing **梅子** (méizǐ) = plum, no bigger than a thumb, round and purple and brand-new to the city. She had wandered out to look at the lanterns, gotten turned around at a noodle cart, taken a wrong left at a temple, and was now standing in the middle of a winding lane she did not recognize, with the sky going dark at the edges.

"Hello?" Little Plum called. Her voice came out wobbly. "Is... is anybody there?"

The lane did not answer. Lanes rarely do.

Little Plum's lip trembled. She was so small. The city was so big. And the alleys curled and split and curled again like the inside of a snail shell, and every one of them looked exactly the same.

She sat down on a cool stone step and tried to be brave. Being brave is much harder when you are tiny and lost and the streetlights are just blinking on. She sniffled once.

And that was when she noticed it.

Right there beside her foot — a single golden flake. Tiny. Buttery. Glowing faintly in the lamplight like a flake of caught sunshine.

"Huh," said Little Plum.

She looked up the lane. There was another flake. And past that, two more. And past *those* — a whole shimmering, scattered, sparkling little path of golden crumbs, winding off around the corner and into the deepening dusk.

It was the silliest, prettiest thing she had ever seen. It was as if someone had been tiptoeing through the alleys leaving a trail of stars.

"Somebody dropped these," Little Plum whispered. "Which means... somebody *walked* this way. Which means if I follow them..."

She stood up. Her tummy did a hopeful little flip.

"...maybe they lead somewhere *warm*."

---

So Little Plum followed the trail.

Crumb by crumb, flake by flake, she rolled along through the winding lanes of old Taichung. The trail led her past a steamed-bun stall (where she paused to sniff, because she was small but she was not a fool). It led her under a string of red lanterns. It led her around a sleepy cat who opened one eye, decided she was not worth the bother, and went back to sleep.

At one fork the crumbs went both ways, and Little Plum nearly panicked — until she noticed that the left-hand crumbs were dusty and faded, while the right-hand ones gleamed bright and fresh and *new*.

"Aha," she whispered, very pleased with her own cleverness. "These are the *coming-home* crumbs. The faded ones are from this morning."

(She was exactly right, reader. Sun Cake had passed that fork twice that day, once leaving and once returning, and so the lane held two trails — an old pale one and a new shining one. Little Plum chose the bright path, the way you always should.)

A breeze came whisking down the alley then and threatened to scatter the trail entirely, lifting the flakes up into a tiny golden snow-flurry. Little Plum gasped and threw herself flat over the nearest crumbs to keep them from blowing away.

"Don't go!" she squeaked at them. "You're the only friends I've got out here!"

The breeze, as breezes do, lost interest and wandered off. The crumbs settled. And Little Plum, dusted head to toe in buttery gold and looking quite a lot like a small sugared plum now, rolled onward with a giggle.

And the whole time, the golden crumbs grew thicker. Closer together. *Fresher.* Brighter.

"Getting warmer," Little Plum giggled. "Getting warmer..."

Around one last bend, the trail of crumbs became a positive *blizzard* of golden flakes — and there, glowing under a paper lantern outside the tea shop, was the source of all that scattered sunshine.

A little round pastry, shaped like a sun, frozen mid-tiptoe with one foot in the air, looking absolutely mortified at the mess she'd made.

"Oh *no*," Sun Cake was muttering, brushing frantically at her layers. "Look at all these crumbs, what a disaster, somebody could just *follow* these straight to —"

"YOU!" cried Little Plum, beaming. "I followed them straight to *you*!"

Sun Cake froze. She had been seen. The very thing she'd spent her whole life trying to avoid had happened. Somebody had tracked her down by her trail.

She braced herself for the worst.

But Little Plum wasn't frowning. Little Plum was *glowing*, nearly as bright as Sun Cake herself, bouncing up and down on the cobblestones.

"I was so lost," the little plum babbled, "and so scared, and the city was so big, and then I saw your crumbs and they were like a path of stars and I just *followed* them and they led me right here to you and you're WARM and you GLOW and oh I was so afraid I'd be alone all night!"

And she flung her tiny purple self around Sun Cake in the biggest, softest hug a thumb-sized plum can give.

Sun Cake stood very, very still. Not her holding-still-so-no-crumbs-fall stillness. A different kind. The stunned, melty kind you feel when something you were sure was *bad* turns out to be the best thing that ever happened to you.

"My... my crumbs?" she whispered. "My crumbs *helped* you?"

"They saved me!" said Little Plum. "Best crumbs in all of Taichung."

---

Well. That night, something wonderful unspooled.

Because here is the thing about a trail of golden crumbs: once one lonely friend has followed it home, *others* notice it too.

The sleepy cat got curious and padded over. A pair of tea eggs out for an evening stroll spotted the glow and wandered in. A drowsy pineapple cake leaned out a window, saw the gathering, and came down in slippers. One by one, by ones and twos, the whole quiet little corner of old Taichung followed the shimmering golden trail straight to the warm circle of lantern light outside the tea shop.

And there, in the middle of it all, sat Sun Cake — *glowing*, surrounded, and not the least bit hidden.

"All this time," she said softly, looking at the friends gathered around her, "I thought my crumbs were giving me away."

"They were," said Little Plum, snuggled warm against her side. "They gave you away to *us*."

Sun Cake laughed — a real laugh, the kind that shook loose about forty more crumbs, and for the very first time in her whole flaky life, she did not mind one bit. She didn't sweep them up. She let them sparkle.

Because a trail of golden crumbs, she finally understood, is not a mess you leave behind.

It's an invitation.

It is every warm, buttery little bit of yourself, scattered out into the dark — saying, gently, *here I am. Follow me home. There's room by the lantern for you, too.*

And from that golden evening on, Sun Cake never tiptoed again. She walked right down the middle of the lanes at dusk, head high, glowing, shedding sunshine with every step — and her friends always, always knew exactly where to find her.

## Goodnight Blessing

Little one, you scatter bits of yourself everywhere you go — a kind word here, a giggle there, a soft glow you don't even notice. You might think you're leaving a mess. But somewhere out in the gentle dusk, someone lost and lonely is following your golden trail, finding their way straight to your warm and shining heart. So shed your sunshine freely. Let your crumbs sparkle. You are easier to love than you know. Now close your eyes, little sun cake, and glow softly into your dreams. Goodnight.
