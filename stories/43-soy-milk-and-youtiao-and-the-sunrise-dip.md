# Story 43: Soy Milk & Youtiao & the Sunrise Dip

**Main Character:** Yóu Tiáo (Youtiao Dough Stick)
**Guest Characters:** Dòu Jiāng (Soy Milk)
**Setting:** A steamy pre-dawn breakfast shop in Taipei
**Theme:** Best friends are better together — one dunk at a time.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 豆漿 (dòujiāng) = soy milk

---

It was the wobbly hour before sunrise, when the whole city of Taipei was still snoring under its blanket of fog, and inside a tiny breakfast shop on a tiny corner, one very tall, very golden, very *nervous* dough stick was hiding behind a stack of bamboo trays.

"Pssst," whispered Yóu Tiáo. "Pssssst. Is it morning yet? Is it? Because if it's morning, I am *absolutely* not here. I am somewhere else. I am, in fact, a completely different dough stick."

He was a long, crispy, twin-jointed fellow — two golden strands fried side by side, so really he was a little bit *two* dough sticks pretending to be one, which made arguing with himself extremely easy.

"We can't hide," said his left strand.

"We can TOTALLY hide," said his right strand.

"That's the spirit," said his left strand. "Wait. No."

You see, Yóu Tiáo had one job each morning. One glorious, terrifying, soggy job. And he was *dreading* it.

The shop sighed awake around him. Griddles ticked. Steam curled up to lick the ceiling. And from across the counter came a warm, milky, sleepy little voice.

"Good morning," it hummed. "Sip slow."

It was **豆漿** (dòujiāng) = soy milk — though everyone just called him Dòu Jiāng. He was a gentle, creamy-pale mug of warm soy milk, calm as a sunrise, with a soft curl of steam rising off his head like a question mark and a froth mustache he was rather proud of. He was, without a single doubt, Yóu Tiáo's very best friend in the entire whole wide breakfast world.

"There you are!" Dòu Jiāng beamed, steam-curl bobbing. "Hiding behind the trays again?"

"I'm not hiding," said Yóu Tiáo, hiding. "I'm *resting*. Vertically. Behind objects."

Dòu Jiāng floated a little closer, warm and milky and impossibly kind. "It's almost time for the Dip," he said softly.

And at that one little word — *Dip* — both of Yóu Tiáo's strands went stiff as chopsticks.

The Dip. The Sunrise Dip. The grand, ancient, world-famous morning tradition where the dough stick takes a deep breath, runs to the edge of the bowl, and dives *headfirst* into the warm soy milk with a magnificent **撲通** (pūtōng) = splash. It was supposed to be the best moment of the whole day. The soy milk turned warm and dreamy. The dough went soft and sweet. And the smell — oh, the *smell* — drifted out the door and woke up the entire sleeping city, one nose at a time.

It was Yóu Tiáo's job to do the diving.

And Yóu Tiáo was *terrified* of diving.

"What if I sink?" he wailed, both strands wobbling. "What if I go *gloop* and never come up? What if I get *too* soggy and turn into noodle soup and lose my crunch and my crunch is my whole personality?"

"You won't sink," said Dòu Jiāng.

"You don't KNOW that."

"I do, actually," said the mug, and his froth mustache curved into a smile. "Because I'll catch you."

Yóu Tiáo blinked. Twice — once per strand.

"You'll... catch me?"

"Every morning, I promise," said Dòu Jiāng, voice as soft as the first light through a window. "You dive. I catch. That's the whole trick. You're never falling into nothing, my friend. You're falling into *me*."

Now, just then, a bouncy clatter came tumbling through the door — *crunch crunch crunch GIGGLE* — and a whole crew of golden popcorn-chicken nuggets came hopping in, crowned with little fried-basil hats, all talking at once.

"It's almost SUNRISE—"
"—and we came to WATCH—"
"—the famous DIVE—"
"—we love a good dive—"
"—MORE CRUNCH, MORE FUN!"

It was Kǎ Kǎ, the popcorn chicken, all eleventeen of them, reshuffling and giggling like a basket of happy dice.

"Oh no," whispered Yóu Tiáo. "An audience. Now I'll *definitely* faint. Can a dough stick faint? Let's not find out."

Behind them strolled Cōng Cōng, the scallion pancake, doing a lazy cartwheel on his crispy edges. "Heard there was a flip happening," he said, landing with a *flap* of his pancake hands. "I never miss a chance to flip the script! So — you diving or what, noodle-buddy?"

"He's a *little* nervous," said Dòu Jiāng kindly.

"A *little*?" squeaked Yóu Tiáo. "I am nervous in BOTH strands SIMULTANEOUSLY. That's twice the nervous. That's the most nervous a person can be without inventing a third strand."

Cōng Cōng tilted his flaky head. "Y'know what helps? Don't think about the falling. Think about the *landing*."

"That is somehow worse," said Yóu Tiáo.

The shop was glowing pinker now. Outside, the streetlamps were yawning off, one by one. Way up high, the very last star was packing its bags. And all over Taipei, sleepy people were rolling over in their beds, sniffing the air, *almost* awake, *almost* ready — but not quite. They needed one more thing.

They needed the smell of breakfast.

And the breakfast smell didn't come till the Dip.

"They're waiting for us," Dòu Jiāng said gently, gazing toward the misty window. "The whole city. All those tired folks who worked so hard yesterday. They don't know it yet, but they're waiting for two silly friends to wake them up the cozy way." He turned his warm steamy face to Yóu Tiáo. "I can't do it without you. A mug of soy milk all by himself? That's just a quiet drink. But soy milk *and* a dough stick? That's *breakfast*. That's a hug you can eat. That's the smell that makes a whole island say, 'Ahhh, it's morning, and morning is good.'"

Yóu Tiáo looked at his best friend. Then he looked at the bowl of warm, golden, gently-steaming soy milk — and for the first time, it didn't look like a scary deep pool.

It looked like a soft place to land.

"You'll really catch me?" he asked, very small, in both strands at once.

"I have never *not* caught you," said Dòu Jiāng. "And I never will. Dunk me, you say — and I say, *I'm ready*."

Something warm bloomed in Yóu Tiáo's crispy middle. He squared his golden shoulders. Both of them. He took the deepest breath a dough stick has ever taken.

"Okay," he whispered. "Okay okay okay. For breakfast. For the city. For my best pal." He wobbled to the edge of the counter, toes — well, *crusts* — curling over the rim. "Left strand?"

"Ready," said his left strand.

"Right strand?"

"Born ready, baby," said his right strand.

"...You're so dramatic," said his left strand.

"On the count of three!" called Dòu Jiāng, lifting his warm milky self right beneath the dive, mustache trembling with excitement. "One — two —"

"WAIT what comes after two—"

"THREE!"

And Yóu Tiáo *jumped*.

For one shining, soaring, slow-motion second, he sailed through the steamy air, twin strands streaming behind him like a golden ribbon, both eyes squeezed shut, the whole shop holding its breath—

***撲通!***

—and he plunged straight down into the warm, sweet, embracing soy milk with the most beautiful **撲通** (pūtōng) = splash anyone had ever heard.

And he did *not* sink.

Because, just like he promised, Dòu Jiāng caught him — warm and milky and soft, rising up around him in a gentle creamy hug, holding his crispy friend safe and bobbing at the surface, soaking him just *exactly* right: crunchy on the outside, dreamy-soft within.

"I'M FLOATING!" cried Yóu Tiáo, both strands wiggling with joy. "I'M FLOATING AND DELICIOUS AND I DIDN'T DIE EVEN ONE LITTLE BIT! Dòu Jiāng — you *caught* me!"

"I told you," laughed the mug, steam puffing in happy little clouds. "Best friends are better together. One dunk at a time."

And oh — the *smell*.

It rolled up out of the bowl, warm and golden and toasty-sweet, and tiptoed out the open door, and curled down the alley, and floated over the rooftops of all of Taipei. Sleepy noses twitched. Sleepy eyes blinked open. All across the waking city, people stretched and smiled and said the same soft thing: "Mmm. *Breakfast.*"

The popcorn chicken cheered. Cōng Cōng cartwheeled. The first ray of true sunrise came pouring buttery-gold through the window and landed right on two best friends bobbing happily in a bowl.

"Same time tomorrow?" asked Dòu Jiāng.

Yóu Tiáo grinned in stereo. "Dunk me," he said. "I'm ready."

And from that morning on, he never hid behind the trays again — because he knew, deep in both his crunchy strands, that the bravest thing in the world is just a little easier when somebody you love is waiting to catch you. Every single sunrise. Warm, and golden, and right on time.
