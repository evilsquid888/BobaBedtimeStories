# Story 50: The Grand Festival of Fifty Lights

**Main Character:** Boba (波霸 bōbà) — Tapioca Pearl
**Guest Characters:** Niú Niú, Tāng Yuán, Egg Waffle
**Setting:** A massive island-wide festival linking every night market by lantern-light
**Theme:** Halfway is worth a celebration when you've made it together.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 慶祝 (qìngzhù) = to celebrate

---

Boba was bouncing so hard she nearly dented the moon.

"FIFTY!" she yelled, ricocheting off a lantern pole and landing in a startled bowl of beef noodle soup. "Niú Niú! It's been FIFTY!"

"Slurp first, worry later, kiddo," said Niú Niú, his noodle-y scarf catching her gently before she could splash. He was a warm steaming clay bowl with a friendly face on the rim, a swirl of bok choy for a collar, and a cozy steam-cloud halo that always smelled like a winter night. "Fifty what, exactly?"

"Fifty STORIES!" Boba spun in a circle. "Fifty adventures! From the very first night I bounced out of my cup, all the way to right now. Do you know what that means?"

Niú Niú thought slowly, the way he did everything. "It means... we're a little tired?"

"It means we're HALFWAY!" Boba beamed. "And halfway is worth a **慶祝** (qìngzhù) = to celebrate. A big one. The biggest one Taiwan has ever seen."

---

The idea was, even Boba admitted, slightly enormous.

She wanted to light a chain of lanterns from the very tip-top of the island all the way down to the bottom — one glowing string connecting every single night market, north to south, so that for one night all of **台灣** (Táiwān) = Taiwan would shine like a necklace of warm little suns.

"It's impossible," said a wise voice.

Boba turned. Sitting on the edge of the soup stall was Tāng Yuán, a round white rice ball with a sweet sesame center, who always spoke in family metaphors and always said *we*. "But," Tāng Yuán added with a jolly wobble, "impossible is just a word we use before we round up enough friends. And we have a LOT of friends now."

That was true. Fifty stories' worth of friends. Boba's heart squeezed like a hug.

"Then let's round them up," she said. "Let's bounce."

---

By midnight, the call had spread through every **夜市** (yèshì) = night market on the island.

They came in a flood of light and laughter. Mochi came stretching all the way from the next street over without ever quite letting go of the last one. Taro came reading proverbs off the backs of crumpled receipts, adjusting his tiny reading glasses and announcing, "Good things take time to steam," to anyone who would listen and several who wouldn't. Pái Pái the chicken cutlet came flexing her crunchy edges and shouting "BIGGER THAN YOUR FACE, BIGGER THAN YOUR WORRIES!" so loudly that three lanterns lit themselves out of sheer enthusiasm and a fourth fainted.

Chòu Chòu the stinky tofu arrived in a friendly cloud of purple sparkles, lending out his clothespin as a joke. "Free with every hug!" he called. And everybody laughed and nobody pinched their nose, because by now everyone knew: bold on the outside, gold on the inside.

Kǎ Kǎ the popcorn chicken arrived as a giggling crew of dozens, hopping and reshuffling and finishing each other's sentences — "We heard there's a—" "—party and we LOVE a—" "—MORE CRUNCH, MORE FUN!" Guā Bāo came folding everyone into pillowy hugs whether they asked for one or not. Even Dàn Dàn the tea egg rolled up, peered at the crackle-lines on his marbled shell, and murmured, "Tonight the cracks all point south. Every one of them. That's never happened before." He smiled his gentle smile. "Every crack tells a story. And tonight, they all tell the same one."

But there was a hitch.

A tall, golden, grid-shaped hitch wearing a top hat.

"LADIES, GENTLEMEN, AND ASSORTED DELICIOUS PERSONS!" boomed Egg Waffle, leaping onto a crate. He was a golden grid waffle of perfect little bubbles, a carnival showman to his very last bump. "You cannot simply *light some lanterns*. A festival of FIFTY must have RAZZLE! It must have DAZZLE! It must have... me, mostly. I have prepared a four-hour fireworks symphony, a parade of dancing dragons, and a personal speech that is only nine hundred pages long."

There was a pause.

"Nine hundred pages?" squeaked Tāng Yuán.

"I'll read fast," said Egg Waffle generously.

---

This, it turned out, was the gentle problem.

Egg Waffle wanted the festival to be GRAND. Spectacular. Loud enough to rattle the stars. And Boba understood — she really did. He was a showman; sparkle was simply how his heart said *I love you*.

But the more Egg Waffle planned, the more complicated it got. He ordered ten thousand lanterns when there was only string enough for the simple chain. He scheduled the dragon parade to march north while the lantern-lighters needed to go south. He built a stage so tall it blocked the view of the actual sky.

He also rented a marching band, a confetti cannon, and a second, *backup* confetti cannon in case the first confetti cannon got tired. He drew up a seating chart, then a standing chart, then a chart explaining the other two charts. He printed programs. The programs had programs.

By the time the moon was high, nothing was lit at all. Friends stood around in confused little clumps, holding unlit lanterns and waiting for instructions that kept changing. Niú Niú's steam had gone slightly droopy. Even the confetti cannons looked bored.

"This isn't working," Boba whispered to Niú Niú.

"Mm," he agreed, steam curling thoughtfully. "Too many cooks. Not enough soup."

Egg Waffle, up on his enormous stage, was sweating golden waffle-sweat. "Page two hundred and twelve!" he announced to a crowd that had mostly wandered off to find snacks. His voice cracked. For just a second, the showman looked very small inside his very big hat.

Boba bounced over. "Egg Waffle?"

"It has to be perfect," he said quietly, top hat drooping. "Fifty stories. That's so many. If we don't make it dazzling enough, people will think it didn't *mean* anything."

And there it was. The real worry, hiding under all the razzle-dazzle.

---

Boba sat down right next to him, which for a tiny pearl meant climbing up his grid like a ladder.

"You know what I noticed," she said, "across all fifty stories?"

"That I'm extremely handsome?"

"That too." Boba grinned. "But mostly — that the best moments were never the loudest ones. Remember Red Bean's ladle of warm soup on a cold night? Remember Aiyu being secretly brave? Remember the old couple in story one, sharing one straw without a single word? None of that was a nine-hundred-page speech. It was just... us. Together. Small and warm and real."

Egg Waffle blinked his little bubble-eyes.

"Halfway isn't worth celebrating because it's *big*," Boba went on. "It's worth celebrating because we made it *together*. The point of a **慶祝** isn't to be louder than everyone. It's to make everyone feel like they belong."

A long pause. Then Egg Waffle slowly took off his top hat and held it to his syrupy chest.

"...I may have over-waffled this," he admitted.

"You over-waffle everything," said Tāng Yuán fondly. "We love you for it. *We* always will."

Egg Waffle sniffled one proud, golden sniffle. "Then let's do it your way, pearl. Simple. Together." He brightened, just a little, just enough. "But I'm *keeping* nine of the dragons. Nine is reasonable."

"Nine is reasonable," Boba agreed solemnly.

---

So they did it the warm way.

They unrolled the single great string of lanterns from the misty northern mountains, where Ā-Lǐ the oolong leaf released swirling fragrant clouds to bless it, all the way down toward the southern sea. And instead of one giant stage, everyone took a small piece. Each friend lit just one lantern, and then leaned over to light their neighbor's, and then *that* friend lit the next.

Boba lit the very first one. It glowed like a little black moon turned golden.

Niú Niú lit the second with a happy slurp. "Warmer than soup," he murmured.

Tāng Yuán lit the third, and the fourth, and gathered three smaller tangyuan into the fold, because *we* always means more than one. "A family," he said, voice wobbling with joy, "is just a bunch of little lights agreeing to glow at the same time."

Down the island the glow ran — north to south, market to market, friend to friend. Mochi to Taro. Taro to Chòu Chòu, whose purple sparkle-cloud caught the lantern-light and turned, for one magical moment, the soft pink of a sunrise. "Even my smell is celebrating," he sniffled proudly. Chòu Chòu to Pái Pái, who lit hers so dramatically that it sparked three extra lanterns, and she took a bow, and then took a second bow for the bow.

Mango lit one mid-sprint and timed himself doing it. Kǎ Kǎ tried to light one lantern with all dozen of them at once, which resulted in a friendly pileup, a great deal of giggling, and somehow *six* lanterns lit instead of one. Pudding lit one and wobbled and nearly dropped it and everyone gasped — and then cheered so hard the steam rose off the soup stalls — when he caught it at the very last second. Because catching it was the bravest thing Pudding had done in twenty whole stories, and his little caramel face glowed brighter than the flame.

Guā Bāo lit one and immediately folded the lantern, the lighter, and two passing strangers into a single enormous hug. "Come on in," she said softly, "I'll fold you a light."

And Egg Waffle — dear, grand, over-waffling Egg Waffle — saved his nine dragons for the very end. When the last lantern in the far south finally flickered awake, when the whole island shimmered like a string of warm pearls laid across the dark, *that* was when he loosed them. Nine paper dragons spiraled up through the night, and they weren't loud at all. They were soft. They glowed. They danced over a thousand glowing markets like they were saying *thank you* to every single light below.

From a tiny red temple on a hillside, Tǔdì Gōng the Earth God stepped out to watch, no taller than a teapot, his white beard glowing in the lantern-light. He smelled of incense and tangerines. "Bless this little corner of the island," he chuckled, and handed a lucky candy to Xiǎo Hǔ Yé, the plump clay temple tiger snoozing at his feet. The tiger gave a big yawn that he was fairly sure counted as a roar of approval. "Rawr means hello," he mumbled, and went back to sleep, purring.

From the moon, Yù Tù the Moon Rabbit paused her mochi-pounding to peer down. "Oh my," she whispered, her velvety ears twitching. "Taiwan is wearing a necklace tonight."

And it was. The most beautiful necklace in the world, strung from fifty stories' worth of friends.

---

Boba bounced to the top of the tallest lantern and looked north, then south, at the long golden river of light her family had made together. Halfway, she thought. We're only halfway. And it's already the most wonderful thing I've ever seen.

"To fifty more," she whispered. "But mostly — to all of us. Together."

"Slurp first," said Niú Niú softly beside her, "celebrate always."

That wasn't quite his catchphrase, but tonight, it was perfect.

## Goodnight Blessing

Tonight, little dreamer, may you remember that you don't have to wait until the very end to celebrate how far you've come. Halfway is worth a song. A Tuesday is worth a lantern. A small warm moment with the people you love is worth more than the biggest fireworks in the sky. May your light be gentle, may your friends be many, and may you always know that you belong in the glowing string of hearts that love you. Close your eyes now, and float down the long golden river of sleep. Goodnight, little light. Goodnight.
