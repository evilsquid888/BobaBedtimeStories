# Story 49: Zongzi & the Dragon-Boat Race

**Main Character:** Zòng Zi (Sticky Rice Dumpling)
**Guest Characters:** Coconut Jelly
**Setting:** A Dragon Boat Festival river race in Lukang
**Theme:** Wrapped-up tight just means ready to ride.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 端午節 (Duānwǔ jié) = Dragon Boat Festival

---

On the morning of **端午節** (Duānwǔ jié) = Dragon Boat Festival, the whole town of Lukang smelled like warm bamboo leaves, and Zòng Zi could not stop wiggling.

He was a plump little pyramid of glistening sticky rice, wrapped up snug in fragrant green leaves and tied with string in a neat, tidy bow. Inside him — and this was his proudest secret — he carried treasures: a cube of savory pork, a soft mushroom, a sweet chestnut, and one perfect golden salted-egg yolk, round as a tiny moon.

"Today," he announced to the riverbank, "I race!"

He tried to hop toward the water. He bonked instead. Flop. He rolled three feet sideways and landed upside down in a duck's nest. The duck blinked at him.

"Sorry," said Zòng Zi, very politely, from inside the nest. "I'm new at being shaped like a triangle."

A laugh bubbled up from the river. "Bro," said a cool, chilled voice. "That was the wobbliest entrance I have ever seen, and I have seen me try to walk."

---

It was Coconut Jelly — a stack of translucent, chewy little cubes balanced into a surfer-shaped tower, glistening and jiggly, with a tiny pair of sunglasses perched on top even though the sun was barely up.

"You racing too, dude?" Coconut Jelly asked.

"I want to," Zòng Zi sighed, flopping out of the duck nest. "But look at me. I'm tied up so tight I can barely bend. Real racers are loose and limber and splashy. I'm just... a tidy little bundle."

He gave his string a sad little tug. It did not budge. His leaves did not loosen. He was, as always, wrapped up snug as a present nobody had unwrapped.

"I'll never be fast," he mumbled. "I'm too bundled."

He gazed across the water at all the other festival snacks limbering up. A youtiao was doing stretchy splits. A team of popcorn-chicken nuggets bounced in place, loose and giggly and free. Even the river itself seemed loose, rolling and tumbling wherever it pleased.

"That's the trouble," Zòng Zi said quietly. "Everybody else can flop and flow. But me? I'm knotted up with string. Tied at the top, tied at the sides. A racer should be light. I feel like a backpack that someone packed too full."

Coconut Jelly slid one chewy cube up onto Zòng Zi's leafy shoulder. "Hey. Trust the river. The river knows stuff. Now hop on a boat — the **龍舟** (lóngzhōu) = dragon boat races start when the drum goes BOOM."

And right on cue, somewhere down the misty river, a great drum went BOOM.

---

The Lukang river was packed wing-to-wing, fin-to-fin, with the silliest fleet of dragon boats you ever saw. Each long boat had a carved dragon head at the front with big googly eyes and a curly painted smile. The crews were a happy jumble of festival snacks: a team of giggling popcorn-chicken nuggets all paddling out of rhythm, a dignified Mooncake captaining a boat with perfect posture, and a youtiao who kept trying to dive headfirst into the water before the race even started.

The carved dragon heads bobbed and bonked one another like a row of friendly puppies. One sneezed a little puff of river mist. Down the bank, a Red Bean grandma was handing out warm cups of tea to every crew, patting cheeks and calling, "Eat something, eat something, you're all too thin to paddle!" — which was very funny, because some of the racers were literally made of dumplings.

"Climb aboard the *Sticky Splasher*," called Coconut Jelly, patting the side of a slim green boat. "You sit up front. You're our lucky lantern."

"Me? Up front?" Zòng Zi's leaves rustled nervously. "But I'm so heavy with treasures. If the boat tips, I'll roll right out. I'll lose my pork. I'll lose my chestnut. I'll lose my little egg-yolk moon!"

"Then hold on tight, little dude," said Coconut Jelly, settling in to steer. "That's what tight is for."

The drummer — a round Tea Egg with marbled cracks all over his shell — raised two mallets. He squinted at his own cracked patterns like he was reading a fortune. "The river says... today, the bundled one shines," he murmured mysteriously. Then he grinned. "Also the river says: BOOM."

BOOM went the drum.

And the whole giggling fleet went FLYING.

---

Water sprayed everywhere. Paddles splashed. The popcorn-chicken boat zoomed past in a fit of giggles, going slightly the wrong direction. The youtiao finally got his wish and dove into the soy-milk-colored foam with a happy "Dunk me, I'm ready!"

But the *Sticky Splasher* — oh, the *Sticky Splasher* was fast.

"WHOO!" cried Zòng Zi, the wind whistling through his bamboo leaves. For the first time all morning, he wasn't bonking or flopping. He was zooming. He sat snug at the front, low and round and steady, and somehow being a tidy little bundle made him the perfect little nose-cone for slicing through the spray.

The river streamed past in ribbons of silver and green. Dragonflies zipped alongside the boat as if cheering. Somewhere behind them the popcorn-chicken crew was singing a paddling song, except all the nuggets were singing different words, so it came out as a happy, jumbled cloud of "MORE CRUNCH — paddle paddle — MORE FUN — wheee!"

"You're a natural, bro!" Coconut Jelly hollered, steering hard.

"I'M A NATURAL!" Zòng Zi shouted back, which is a very fun thing to shout when you have spent all morning falling into duck nests.

Then the river curved.

And up ahead, churning and white and loud, came the part of the river the old snacks whispered about: the **激流** (jīliú) = rapids.

---

The rapids were a wild, bubbling, slippy-splashy stretch where the water tumbled over rocks and the boats bucked like a dragon waking from a nap. (A friendly nap. Even the rapids in this story are friendly.)

One by one, the other boats got tossed. The Mooncake boat spun in a slow, dignified circle. The popcorn-chicken crew popped clean out of their boat, shrieking with laughter, and had to be scooped up in a soup ladle by a passing Red Bean grandma. SPLOOSH. SPLASH. WOBBLE.

The *Sticky Splasher* hit the rapids hard.

The boat reared up. It crashed down. Cold water came over the front in a great green wave — right over Zòng Zi.

"My treasures!" he gasped. "I'm going to lose everything!"

But here is the thing.

He didn't.

The wave slammed into him and rolled right off his snug green leaves. His tight little string held firm. His pork stayed put. His mushroom stayed put. His chestnut and his perfect egg-yolk moon stayed exactly where they belonged — safe, dry, and cozy in the middle of him — while everything around him splashed and tumbled and got soaked.

Coconut Jelly, clinging to the back, gawked. "Dude. You didn't spill. You didn't lose a single thing!"

Zòng Zi looked down at himself, dripping but whole, treasures all tucked inside. A slow, wide, sticky-rice smile spread across his leafy face.

"I'm wrapped up tight," he whispered, amazed. "I thought that made me slow. But it's what kept everything safe."

"Trust the weird," said Coconut Jelly, grinning. "It's delicious."

"All this time," Zòng Zi went on, hardly believing it, "I thought my string was holding me back. But the string isn't a cage. It's a hug! It's been hugging my treasures the whole way down the river so nothing could shake them loose. The other boats are flopping and flowing — and losing half their crew to the splash! Me? I just hold on, snug and sure."

"Wrapped tight," Coconut Jelly agreed, "ready to ride. Now point me through this mess, little dude — you're seeing the river better than anybody."

---

The rest of the rapids could not stop them now.

Zòng Zi sat low and snug at the front, and every time a wave came, he just shrugged it right off his bamboo shoulders. The water that knocked everyone else loose simply could not get *into* him. He was sealed up cozy, like a thermos, like a hug with a knot on top.

"Lean left!" he called to Coconut Jelly, reading the river the way the Tea Egg drummer read his cracks. "Big rock — go around! Now riiiiiight!"

"You're steering better than me, and I'm the one steering!" laughed Coconut Jelly, leaning the boat exactly where Zòng Zi pointed.

A big friendly rock loomed dead ahead, round and mossy and grinning the way rocks do. "Around the smiley one!" Zòng Zi called, and the *Sticky Splasher* curved past it so close that the rock seemed to wave. A swirl tried to spin them backward; Zòng Zi felt it tugging in his snug little tummy and shouted, "Hold steady — let it pass under us!" And it did.

They shot out the far end of the rapids like a cork from a soda bottle — POP! — and there it was: the finish line, a great red-and-gold banner stretched across the river, fluttering in the festival breeze. The crowd of snacks on the banks was cheering and waving sticky-rice triangles and little bamboo flags.

"GO, BUNDLE, GO!" they roared.

The *Sticky Splasher* zoomed under the banner first, the dragon head's googly eyes spinning with joy.

They had won.

---

A drum boomed one last happy BOOM. The Tea Egg drummer beamed. "Every crack tells a story," he said, "and yours, little Zòng Zi, was the best one all day."

Coconut Jelly lifted Zòng Zi up high — leaves still dripping, string still snug, every single treasure still safe and sound inside.

"He thought being wrapped tight made him slow," Coconut Jelly announced to the cheering riverbank. "Turns out being wrapped tight made him *ready*."

Zòng Zi glowed brighter than the festival lanterns. All morning he had felt like just a tidy little bundle, too bound up to be brave. But the river had whispered the truth: every snug knot, every wrapped leaf, every careful tie — it was all there to keep his treasures safe through the wildest water of his life.

"Wrapped tight," he said happily, "ready to ride."

And then, because it was a festival, and because he was a champion, his crew did the very best thing a winning Zòng Zi can hope for. They unwrapped him — slowly, proudly, like opening a gift — and shared his warm pork and soft mushroom and sweet chestnut and that perfect golden egg-yolk moon all around the riverbank.

Every snack got a happy, delicious bite. Zòng Zi beamed the whole time, prouder than he'd ever been, because his treasures had made it all the way down the river just so he could give them away.

"Best **端午節** ever," he sighed, full of joy.

## Goodnight Blessing

May all your treasures — your kind heart, your big dreams, your silliest giggles — stay safe and cozy inside you while you sleep. And may you remember that being held close and tucked in tight does not slow you down; it only keeps you ready for tomorrow's bright adventure. The river is calm now, and the lanterns are dimming low. Sleep tight, wrapped up just right. Goodnight.
