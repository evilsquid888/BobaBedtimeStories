# Story 86: Sago Pudding & the Sandcastle That Wouldn't Stay

**Main Character:** Xī Mǐ Lù (Sago Pudding) — a cool, milky pudding full of tiny translucent sago pearls, gentle and accepting
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A breezy white-sand beach on Penghu island at low tide
**Theme:** It's okay when something beautiful doesn't last
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 沙堡 (shābǎo) = sandcastle

---

On the breeziest white-sand beach on all of Penghu island, at the exact wobbly moment when the tide tiptoes out and leaves the sand smooth as a freshly made bed, a pudding rolled out of a coconut shell and landed with a soft, undignified *splat*.

"Marvelous," said Sago Pudding, who was not even slightly hurt, because she was, in fact, a pudding.

She was the color of moonlight in a milk cup, cool and wobbly and proud of it. All through her gentle middle floated hundreds of tiny **西米露** (xī mǐ lù) = sago pearls — little translucent beads, clear as raindrops, glittery as the eyes of someone who has just been told dessert is ready. When she giggled, the pearls bounced. When she sighed, they settled. Right now she was giggling, so the whole beach seemed to sparkle.

And up the dune, sniffling into his own elbow, sat a very small, very soggy crab named Doudou.

"Oh dear," said Sago Pudding, wobbling toward him. "You look like a Tuesday."

---

"It's gone," Doudou wept, waving one tiny claw at the sea. "My whole summer is *over*. We leave tomorrow. And I never got to build it."

"Build what, exactly?" asked Sago Pudding, settling beside him with a cool, comforting jiggle.

"The grandest **沙堡** (shābǎo) = sandcastle that anyone on this whole beach has ever seen!" Doudou flung both claws into the air. "Towers! Bridges! A moat! A flag made out of seaweed! I've been dreaming about it for *days*. And now there's no time, and the tide always comes, and what is even the *point*?"

He flopped backward into the sand.

Sago Pudding did not say *there, there.* She did not say *cheer up.* She had been around long enough to know that those words land on a sad person like a cold noodle on a warm forehead — technically helpful, mostly annoying.

Instead she said, "How many towers?"

Doudou peeked over his elbow. "...Four?"

"Pfft." Sago Pudding wobbled to her full, glorious height of about eleven centimeters. "Four towers is a *cottage*. We're going to build *seven*."

Doudou sat straight up.

"But the tide—"

"Is *over there*," said Sago Pudding firmly, pointing at the shimmering line of sea where it had no business being yet. "And we are *over here*. And the sand is exactly the kind of damp that begs to be a castle. Come on, little crab. Grab a claw. The afternoon isn't going to build itself."

---

What happened next was the busiest, sandiest, most splendid two hours that beach had seen all season.

They built.

Doudou, it turned out, was a *genius* with corners. He could carve a window the size of a sesame seed with one careful claw-tip and never knock the wall down. Sago Pudding was less precise — being a pudding, she mostly *flopped* sand into shape and patted it with her wobbly self — but she was wonderful at the big, brave parts. She built a tower so tall it cast a shadow. She built a moat so deep that a passing hermit crab fell in and decided to stay, on account of the location.

"Drawbridge!" announced Doudou, laying down a flat shell.

"Throne room!" cried Sago Pudding, hollowing out a hall with one mighty *squelch*.

"Library!"

"Snack pantry!"

"Why does a sandcastle need a snack pantry?"

"Every good building needs a snack pantry," said Sago Pudding, with the calm authority of someone who is herself a snack.

They worked until their imaginations ran out, and then they kept working anyway, because that is when the *very* best ideas show up — late, and grinning, and full of nonsense. Doudou carved a tiny stable for tiny imaginary sand-horses. Sago Pudding wobbled out a banquet table the size of a coin and announced that the king of the castle was a small polite pebble named His Majesty Greg.

"His Majesty Greg requests more towers," said Sago Pudding solemnly.

"His Majesty Greg is *very* demanding," said Doudou, but he was laughing too hard to complain properly, and the sea breeze took his laughter and carried it all up and down the empty beach, so that for one shining hour the whole shore sounded like a party.

A pair of sandpipers stopped to admire the work. A small wave, far away, sent up a curious sparkle, as if even the ocean were peeking.

"Window," said Doudou, carving.
"Balcony," said Sago Pudding, patting.
"Secret passage," said Doudou, hollowing.
"Second secret passage," said Sago Pudding, "in case the first one gets boring."

They added a spiral staircase. They added a seaweed flag that snapped smartly in the breeze. They added a row of tiny shell windows that caught the late gold light and glowed like a hundred little lanterns. By the time they pressed the final, perfect grain into the seventh tower, the **沙堡** was so beautiful that a seagull flew down, looked at it, and respectfully flew away again, as if not wanting to interrupt.

"We did it," whispered Doudou. His claws were trembling. "It's perfect. It's the most perfect thing I have ever made."

And it truly was.

Which is, of course, exactly when the sea remembered it had somewhere to be.

---

The first wave was small. A polite little *shhh* across the flats, the way a wave clears its throat before saying something important.

Doudou whipped around. "No. *No.* Not yet!"

He scrambled to the moat and began flinging sand into a wall, claws blurring. "Stay back! Stay *back!*" He built a barrier. The next wave ate it. He built another. The sea ate that one too, hardly chewing.

"Sago Pudding, *help!* Pile up the sand! Block it! We have to *save* it!"

Sago Pudding did not pile up the sand.

She wobbled over very gently and sat down right beside the frantic little crab, close enough that her cool sago pearls glittered in the rising water.

"Doudou," she said softly. "Look."

"I *can't* look, I have to—"

"Look anyway."

He stopped. He looked.

And a wave — a big one this time, foamy and silver and not the least bit sorry — rolled all the way up and folded over the seventh tower, and the snack pantry, and the library, and the spiral staircase, and smoothed the whole **沙堡** back down into plain wet sand, the way you smooth a wrinkle out of a blanket.

The seaweed flag floated off to sea like a tiny green goodbye.

It was gone. All of it. As if it had never been.

Doudou sat down hard in the surf and his little eyes filled all the way up to the brim.

"It didn't even last," he whispered. "We worked so hard. And it didn't even *last.*"

---

Now. Here is where most stories would have the wise friend say something grand and sweeping. But Sago Pudding wasn't grand or sweeping. She was a pudding. So she just leaned her cool, milky self against the soggy little crab and stayed there a while, letting the waves wash warm around them both, until his breathing slowed.

Then she said, "Can I tell you a pudding secret?"

Doudou sniffled. "...Okay."

"I don't last either."

He blinked.

"I'm sweet," she said, "and I'm wobbly, and I'm *delicious* — I am told this constantly. And one day, fairly soon, somebody's going to lift a spoon and *whoop!*" She gave a happy little jiggle. "Off I go, into a grin, the end. Poof. Gone, like your castle."

"That's *terrible,*" said Doudou.

"That's a Tuesday," said Sago Pudding cheerfully. "When that spoon comes, I won't squeak and try to hide behind the fridge yogurt. Oh, no. I'll be the proudest pudding in the bowl." She straightened her wobbly self. "Because being eaten, little crab, is the whole reason a pudding gets made at all. Imagine being so good somebody can't *bear* to keep you on the shelf one second longer. That's not a sad ending. That's a *standing ovation.*"

Doudou wiped his eyes with one damp claw. "But — but then there's no more *you.*"

"Is it?" She tilted her wobbly head. "When I'm a spoonful, I'm the *best* thing in somebody's whole afternoon. The sweetest minute of their day. And then I'm a happy memory they carry around forever — warm, cool, perfect, exactly the way they remember it. Nobody remembers a snack that sat in the fridge being careful." Her pearls sparkled. "The whole point of me is that I don't last. That's what makes me a *treat* instead of a paperweight."

Doudou thought about that. He thought about it the way crabs think — slowly, sideways, and with his whole face.

"So if you lasted forever," he said carefully, "you'd just be... furniture?"

"I'd be the most *boring* pudding in Penghu," she agreed. "People would walk right past me. 'Oh, that old thing,' they'd say. 'Still there, is it.' No spoons. No grins. No standing ovation." She shuddered, and all her pearls trembled. "No, thank you. I would rather be wonderful and brief than dull and endless, any day of the week."

Doudou stared at the smooth, blank sand where seven towers used to stand.

"But the castle's *gone,*" he said. "I can't keep it."

"You couldn't keep it on the beach," Sago Pudding agreed. "But tell me. Where's the snack pantry?"

A tiny, surprised smile tugged at the corner of Doudou's mouth. "...In my head."

"And the spiral staircase?"

"In my head."

"And the part where the hermit crab fell in the moat and decided to *live* there?"

Doudou laughed — a wet, hiccuppy, helpless little laugh. "*In my head.*"

"There you are," said Sago Pudding warmly. "The sea got the sand. But the *building* — the laughing, the squelching, the snack pantry argument — that was never made of sand at all. The sea can't reach that. It's all yours. Forever." She winked. "Free of charge, and waterproof."

---

The tide kept coming in, soft and silver, lapping at their toes (Sago Pudding did not technically have toes, but she lapped along enthusiastically anyway).

And Doudou looked at the wide, smooth, glistening sand — wiped perfectly clean, blank as a fresh page — and something in his little crab heart unclenched.

"Sago Pudding?" he said slowly.

"Mm?"

"Tomorrow morning, before we leave. The tide goes back out, doesn't it? And the sand will be all smooth again. Brand new."

"Brand new," she agreed. "Not one footprint. Not one tower. Just waiting." She wobbled happily. "That's the sea's best trick, you know. It doesn't *take* the beach away. It *wraps* it up fresh every single night, like a present, and leaves it on the doorstep of the morning. The tide isn't the enemy of sandcastles, Doudou. It's the thing that lets you build a brand-new one every day for the rest of your life."

Doudou's eyes shone — happy this time. "Then tomorrow we build *eight* towers. And a *lighthouse.* And TWO snack pantries."

"Now you understand puddings," said Sago Pudding, deeply pleased, "and you understand the sea."

So they sat together on the breezy white-sand beach of Penghu while the stars came out one by one, like sago pearls scattered across a dark milk sky, and they did not feel sad about the castle at all. Somewhere out in the dark, the seaweed flag was off on an adventure of its own. The hermit crab who had moved into the moat had simply moved into a new puddle and seemed perfectly content. And His Majesty Greg the pebble lay on the smooth wet sand, gazing up at the stars, looking — Doudou was quite sure — extremely proud of the kingdom he had so briefly ruled.

"Goodnight, Your Majesty," Doudou whispered to the pebble.

"He says goodnight back," reported Sago Pudding. "And he says the snack pantry was his favorite part."

Because some lovely things are built to be brief. The sunset. The seaweed flag. A spoonful of something cool and sweet on a warm night.

And a **沙堡** that the tide will always, *always* come to claim — which is exactly, Sago Pudding knew, what makes building one worth doing again tomorrow.

---

## Goodnight Blessing

Little one, the day you had today is a sandcastle too — every giggle, every game, every gentle silly moment. The tide of sleep is rolling in now, soft and warm, to smooth it all away. But don't you worry. The fun was real, the love is yours to keep, and tomorrow the beach of morning will be fresh and blank and waiting just for you. Let the waves carry the day away. Tuck your favorite memory somewhere safe inside, where no tide can reach. And rest, my sweet, knowing that tomorrow you get to build something brand new. Goodnight.
