# Story 85: Mochi & the Stretchiest Group Hug

**Main Character:** Mochi (麻糬)
**Guest Characters:** Guā Bāo (Pork Belly Bun), Tāng Yuán (Tangyuan)
**Setting:** A reunion picnic in Daan Forest Park, Taipei
**Theme:** A hug grows bigger the more it's shared.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 擁抱 (yōngbào) = hug

---

On the softest, greenest hill in Daan Forest Park, where the grass tickled like a giggle and the afternoon sun poured down like honey, Mochi was practicing.

"Ready?" she whispered to a startled dandelion. "One... two... HUG!"

She flung her pillowy white arms wide and squished the entire flower into a snug, stretchy embrace. The dandelion did not complain. Dandelions never do. They are very good huggers.

"Perfect form," Mochi said, releasing it with a satisfied *boing*. She bounced back into shape — round, white, soft as a marshmallow that had been blessed by the moon. "Now for the BIG one."

You see, Mochi had a plan. Not a small plan. Not a medium plan. A plan so enormous it could only be measured in arms. Today the whole gang was coming to the reunion picnic, and Mochi — clingy, loyal, huggiest Mochi — was going to gather every single friend, old and new, into the largest **擁抱** (yōngbào) = hug the island had ever seen.

A world record. A *wobble* record.

"Stick together," she said dreamily. "That's the whole point of being a snack."

---

The friends arrived the way good friends always do — all at once and far too loudly.

Boba came bouncing down the path with Taro, who walked slowly and adjusted his reading glasses. Shaved Ice rolled in wearing sunglasses, declaring, "THE PICNIC HAS OFFICIALLY BEGUN." Grass Jelly trailed behind in his black beanie, deadpan as ever. And waddling up the hill, balancing a basket of warm peanuts, came Guā Bāo — the fluffy folded pork belly bun, soft and round, dusted all over in a little peanut-dust apron.

"Come on in," Guā Bāo called, opening her pillowy arms. "I'll fold you a hug."

"NO," said Mochi, a bit too fast.

Everyone blinked.

"I mean — yes. Hugs. Love them. But TODAY," Mochi announced, climbing onto a picnic blanket like a tiny squishy general, "we are building the World's Biggest Group Hug. I have done the math." She had not done the math. "We will need every arm, every wobble, and at least one *very* stretchy mochi. That's me. I'm the very stretchy mochi."

Boba bounced excitedly. "Let's bounce! I mean — let's hug!"

"A record," murmured Taro, stroking his dusty-purple chin. "An old proverb says: *the longest journey is best taken arm in arm.* Or possibly that was about turnips. My memory stretches less than Mochi does."

So the friends formed up. Mochi stretched her left arm around Boba, then around Taro, then *looped* the whole thing back to grab Grass Jelly, who allowed it with a tiny, unbothered sigh.

"Squeeze!" cried Mochi.

They squeezed. It was lovely. It was warm. It was — over almost at once, because Mochi got so excited she squeezed too hard and Boba shot out the top of the hug like a watermelon seed, *boing*, sailing in a high arc and landing softly in Red Bean's lap three blankets over.

"WHEE," said Boba, who did not mind in the slightest. "Again!"

"Sorry, sorry!" Mochi gathered everyone back in, gentler this time. "Okay. Round two. Cozy power, not squishy power."

They squeezed again. Softer. It was lovely. It was warm. It was —

"That's only five," said Shaved Ice, counting with a dramatic icy finger. "Five is NOT a world record. Five is barely a small huddle."

Mochi's round face fell. Just a little. Just enough.

---

But Mochi was not the sort to deflate for long. (Mochi physically cannot deflate. She just goes squishy and pops back. It's a gift.)

"Then we need MORE," she declared. "We need everyone. Tāng Yuán! Where's Tāng Yuán?"

Right on cue, down the hill rolled Tāng Yuán — the round white rice ball with the sweet sesame center — bobbing along like a happy little moon. Wherever Tāng Yuán goes, family follows, because Tāng Yuán cannot say a single sentence without the word *we*.

"We're here!" Tāng Yuán beamed. "We brought aunties, cousins, second cousins, and a third cousin who is technically a dumpling but we don't ask questions. **團圓** (tuányuán) = reunion is the whole point of *us*!"

Behind Tāng Yuán came a rolling, giggling avalanche of round white rice balls, dozens of them, all bumping gently into each other like marbles made of joy.

Mochi's eyes went wide and shiny. "Oh," she breathed. "Oh, that's a LOT of hug."

"Stretch, Mochi!" everyone shouted.

So Mochi stretched.

She stretched her arms long and thin and pillowy-white, looping around the whole rolling crowd of Tāng Yuán cousins. She stretched around Boba and Taro and Grass Jelly and Shaved Ice. She stretched so far that her middle got skinny as a noodle and her face got tugged into a long, surprised *oooooo*.

"Mochi," said Taro gently, "you are becoming a rubber band."

"A LOVING rubber band," Mochi wheezed, and squeezed.

For one shining moment it was *spectacular*. Mochi was stretched in a great pillowy loop the size of a kiddie pool, with the entire rolling reunion gathered snug inside her arms. A passing butterfly landed on her squished, stretched-thin face and seemed to say *well done.* Shaved Ice began counting heads at the top of his lungs. "SEVEN! TWELVE! TWENTY! TWENTY-SIX AND A HALF — the half is the dumpling cousin, he's shy —"

"Don't... lose... count," Mochi panted, going whiter and thinner and stretchier by the second. Her arms were now approximately the width of two cooked noodles. Her face had stretched so long she looked like a surprised piece of taffy. "We're... almost... a record..."

---

It nearly worked. It really, nearly, almost worked.

But here is the trouble with being the only arms holding a hug together: the bigger it gets, the more *you* have to stretch — and the more you stretch, the thinner you get, and the thinner you get, the harder it is to hold on.

"More people!" Mochi gasped. "We have to be bigger! Bigger than this! Bigger than the hill! Bigger than — than —"

*Sproing.*

One arm slipped.

Tāng Yuán's cousins rolled gently free. Boba bounced away. And poor Mochi snapped back into a tight little ball, wobbling sadly in the middle of the blanket, all alone in the very center of a hug that had come apart.

The park went quiet. Even Shaved Ice took off his sunglasses, which he NEVER does.

"I just wanted the biggest one," Mochi said, very small. "I wanted to hold *everybody*. But the more I tried to grab... the more I lost. My arms only reach so far."

Guā Bāo set down her peanut basket. Soft, warm, motherly Guā Bāo. She knelt beside Mochi and gave a gentle, knowing smile.

"Oh, sweet Mochi," she said. "You've been doing it backwards."

The friends gathered close again — not in a record, just in a circle, the way friends do when one of them is sad. Tāng Yuán's cousins rolled in quietly. Taro took off his reading glasses. Even Grass Jelly, who is famously too cool to show feelings, scooted an inch nearer, which for Grass Jelly is basically a parade.

"I'm not very good at letting go," Mochi admitted, in the tiniest voice a mochi can make. "It's sort of my whole thing. I'm the sticky one. I hold on. If I don't hold on, who will?"

"That," said Guā Bāo warmly, "is exactly what I want to show you."

---

"A hug," said Guā Bāo, "isn't something one snack holds for everybody. It's something everybody holds *together*. You don't have to stretch yourself to nothing. You just have to start it — and then *let go into everyone else's arms.*"

Mochi sniffled. "But if I let go... it falls apart."

"Try," said Guā Bāo warmly, "and see."

So Mochi did the hardest thing a clingy little mochi can do.

She let go.

She reached out and hugged just *one* friend — Guā Bāo — soft bun to soft mochi, two pillows pressed cozy and close. And Guā Bāo folded around her, the way she folds around everyone, warm as a fresh-steamed morning.

Then Guā Bāo turned and hugged Boba.

And Boba bounced over and hugged Taro.

And Taro, slow and patient, hugged Grass Jelly, who hugged Shaved Ice, who hugged so dramatically he nearly melted. Tāng Yuán hugged a cousin, who hugged another cousin, who hugged a third cousin (the dumpling — nobody asked questions), and the hug went rolling and folding and looping all on its own, friend passing it to friend, arm linking into arm.

It did not need one stretchy mochi to hold it.

It held itself.

"Oh," Mochi whispered, watching it grow. "*Oh.* It's getting BIGGER. It's getting bigger *because I let go.*"

"That's the secret of a **擁抱**," said Guā Bāo softly. "The more you share it, the more there is."

---

The hug rolled clear across the picnic hill.

It scooped up squirrels and a passing pigeon. It gathered a family of joggers who weren't even sure why they'd stopped, but were smiling anyway. It rolled past Lychee, who flung her arms wide and cried "Darling, a *group hug*, how absolutely *fabulous*," and joined right in. It rolled past Pudding, who got so nervous about being hugged that he wobbled, tripped, and fell *into* the hug — which, it turned out, was exactly where he needed to be.

"See?" laughed Guā Bāo, folding Pudding cozy. "Even falling lands you somewhere warm." The Tāng Yuán cousins formed a wobbling circle so wide it ringed the whole picnic blanket like a necklace of little moons. Boba was laughing. Taro was chuckling. Shaved Ice was loudly announcing "GROUP HUG WORLD RECORD, UNOFFICIAL BUT EMOTIONALLY ENORMOUS."

And in the very middle — not stretching, not straining, just *cozy* — sat Mochi, wrapped in Guā Bāo's pillowy fold, held by a hundred warm arms she didn't have to hold up herself.

"Is it a world record?" Mochi asked sleepily.

"We think it's the *warmest* record," said Tāng Yuán, and all the cousins agreed in one happy rumble. "And the best part of a record is you don't keep it on a shelf. You keep it in each other."

Taro slid his reading glasses back on just to take them off again, which is what he does when he is moved. "There's the proverb I was trying to remember," he said softly. "*A hug shared is a hug doubled. A hug kept is no hug at all.*" He paused. "I think I just made that up. It still counts."

"Slurp first, worry later," said a hearty voice from the path — and there was Niú Niú the noodle-soup bowl, late as always, tossing his wheat-noodle scarf and steaming with delight. He squeezed right in.

The sun went gold, then rosy, then soft as a goodnight. The whole stretchy, wobbly, rolling reunion settled down into the grass together, one enormous huddle of friends breathing slow and warm.

Mochi yawned. She had learned the biggest lesson a small soft snack can learn: a hug isn't how far your arms reach. It's how many hearts pass it along.

"Stick together," she murmured, half-asleep.

And this time, she didn't have to hold on at all. Everyone was holding *her*.

---

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, you don't have to carry every friendship all by yourself — the warmest hugs are the ones we pass from heart to heart, growing bigger each time they're shared. So let your arms rest now, soft as Mochi on the greenest hill, and trust that you are held. Somewhere out there, friends old and new are still wrapped warm together, dreaming under the same gold-and-rosy sky. You are loved more ways than you could ever count on your fingers. Snuggle in, breathe slow, and let the day fold gently around you like the coziest bun. Goodnight.
