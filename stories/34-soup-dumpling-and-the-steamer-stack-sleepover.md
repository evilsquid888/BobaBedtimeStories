# Story 34: Soup Dumpling & the Steamer-Stack Sleepover

**Main Character:** Xiǎo Lóng (Soup Dumpling)
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** A towering stack of bamboo steamer baskets at a dim-sum house, late at night
**Theme:** The top spot isn't always the best spot
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 蒸籠 (zhēnglóng) = bamboo steamer basket

---

Now, the thing about Xiǎo Lóng was that he was *almost* the most perfect little soup dumpling in the whole dim-sum house — and he knew it, which is the most dangerous kind of almost there is.

He was a plump, pleated, snow-pale dumpling, gathered at the top into eighteen tiny twists that swirled like a sleepy little hurricane. His thin skin glowed faintly gold in the lantern light, and inside him — oh, *inside* — sloshed a warm secret pool of the most delicious pork-ginger soup ever simmered. When he wobbled, he sloshed. When he sloshed, he giggled. And when he giggled, the whole basket of dumplings around him giggled too, like a tray of tiny laughing moons.

But tonight Xiǎo Lóng was not laughing. Tonight he was *plotting*.

"Friends," he announced, swirling his eighteen pleats importantly, "I have decided. I am going to the top."

---

It was very late at the dim-sum house, the hour when the cooks had gone home and the lanterns burned low and gold, and the great **蒸籠** (zhēnglóng) = bamboo steamer basket stack rose toward the ceiling like a wobbly tower of round little rooms. Ten baskets high, it stood — steam still curling up through the woven gaps, warm and dreamy, smelling of ginger and good things.

Xiǎo Lóng lived in the snug middle baskets, packed shoulder to shoulder with his pleated friends. But he had been doing some *thinking*. And his thinking went like this:

"Whoever sits in the very top basket gets served *first*. The waiter lifts the top, *whoosh*, off it goes to the table — straight to the lucky table where the customer is *waiting* with chopsticks and a spoon and a little dish of black vinegar." He sighed the dreamy sigh of a dumpling imagining glory. "The top basket is the best basket. Everyone knows it. It's the *fanciest* spot. It's the *first* spot. And I am going to *get* there."

"But it's cozy *here*," said a small dumpling beside him, snuggling into the warm steam. "We're all squished together. I like squished."

"Squished is for dumplings without ambition," said Xiǎo Lóng grandly, which was a very silly thing to say, because *all* dumplings are squished, that is rather the whole point of being a dumpling. But up he went anyway.

"You'll miss the bedtime story!" called the small dumpling after him. "The chicken foot is going to tell the one about the dragon who couldn't sneeze!"

"I'll be *served first*," Xiǎo Lóng called back, as if that settled everything. "You can tell me the dragon story when I'm famous." And he kept right on climbing, leaving the warm golden giggles behind and below.

---

Climbing a steamer stack, it turns out, is *hard* when you are mostly soup.

Xiǎo Lóng heaved. He wobbled. He sloshed dangerously. He squeezed past a basket of shrimp dumplings who were playing a sleepy game of pleat-and-seek.

"Coming through! Important dumpling! Top-bound!" he puffed, elbowing — well, he didn't *have* elbows, so he sort of *sloshed* — past them.

"Where's the fire, hot stuff?" yawned a shrimp dumpling.

"There's no fire, there's *destiny*," said Xiǎo Lóng, and kept climbing.

Up through the basket of pork buns, who were so soft and round they kept rolling out of his way with little startled "*oof*"s. Up past a basket of egg tarts, who jiggled their golden custard at him in greeting. Up, up, past a single lonely chicken foot who waved one toe and said "good luck up there, kid" in a voice that sounded like it knew something Xiǎo Lóng didn't.

The higher he climbed, the thinner the steam became. The warm ginger-cloud that had wrapped the middle baskets like a blanket grew wispy, then faint, then — by the eighth basket — barely a whisper.

"Just a little chilly up here," Xiǎo Lóng told himself, twisting his pleats tighter. "That's because I'm *near the top*. Fancy places are always a bit airy. That's how you know they're fancy."

He did not entirely believe this. But he kept climbing anyway, because that is the trouble with deciding the top is best *before* you get there — you have to keep believing it the whole way up, even when your soup starts to feel a little cold.

---

And then, with one last enormous slosh, Xiǎo Lóng heaved himself over the rim of the very topmost basket.

The TOP.

He had made it.

He spun around, eighteen pleats swirling, ready to soak in the glory of being the highest, first-served, fanciest dumpling in the whole house.

The top basket was... empty.

Just him. One dumpling. In one big, round, woven basket, all alone.

And it was *cold*.

The lid was on crooked, see, so the night air sneaked in through the gap — a thin, drafty, ginger-less breeze that wandered around the basket going *whooooo* in a bored sort of way. The wide bamboo floor was hard and bare. There was nobody to squish against. Nobody sloshing. Nobody giggling. Just Xiǎo Lóng and the *whooooo* and the long, lonely drop down to where all the warmth had stayed behind.

"Well," said Xiǎo Lóng, in a small voice. "It's certainly... *roomy*."

The basket wobbled. Up at the very top of a ten-high stack, every little tremble of the building, every settle of the wood, every distant rumble of a late-night scooter outside — *all* of it traveled up the tower and shivered through the top basket like a hiccup.

*Wobble. Wobble. WOBBLE.*

"Okay," said Xiǎo Lóng, gripping the bamboo with all his pleats. "Maybe roomy and wobbly. Roomy, wobbly, and a *teensy* bit drafty. But — but *first-served*! Any second now the waiter will — "

There was no waiter. The cooks had gone home hours ago, remember. There would be no serving until morning. There was only the long, cold, wobbly, lonely night, all the way up here at the tippy-top, exactly where he'd wanted to be.

Xiǎo Lóng's soup gave a sad little slosh.

He tried to make the best of it. He tried telling himself a bedtime story, but he only knew the *beginning* of the dragon-who-couldn't-sneeze, because he'd always climbed off to bed before the good part. He tried humming the shrimp dumplings' song, but it sounded thin and sad when there was nobody humming back. He even tried squishing against the side of the basket, the way he'd squish against a friend — but a basket does not squish you back. A basket just goes *whooooo*.

"This is the fanciest spot," he said out loud, very firmly, to absolutely nobody. His voice came out small and shivery. "This is the *best* spot. I am very, very lucky to be up here all by my — "

He stopped. He could not finish the sentence. It simply wasn't true, and his soup knew it, and his pleats knew it, and somewhere deep in his ginger-warm middle, *he* knew it too.

---

He peeked over the edge.

Way down below — three, four, five baskets down — the middle of the stack glowed warm and golden. *That* was where the steam pooled, see. It rose up from the cooling water at the bottom and gathered in the snug middle baskets, wrapping all his friends in a thick, cozy, ginger-scented cloud.

And oh, they were having the *best* time.

The dumplings were packed in close, pleat to pleat, telling sleepy stories. The shrimp dumplings had started a soft humming song, and the pork buns were swaying along, and even the egg tarts were giggling their wobbly custard giggles. Steam curled up around them like a warm quilt. Somebody told a joke, and the whole basket shook with laughter, and a little puff of ginger-steam went up like a happy sigh.

It looked like the coziest sleepover in the entire world.

And Xiǎo Lóng was *not invited*, because Xiǎo Lóng had climbed all the way up to the fancy empty top, where the best thing on offer was a cold breeze going *whooooo*.

"Oh," he whispered, and his eighteen proud pleats drooped just a little. "Oh, I think I've been a *silly* dumpling."

He had thought the top was best because it was *first*. But what good is being served first thing in the morning if you have to spend the whole *night* cold and wobbly and alone? The warmth wasn't at the top. The friends weren't at the top. The *good stuff* — the steam, the squish, the giggles, the togetherness — had been in the middle the entire time.

He'd climbed right past it.

---

Now, here is the wonderful thing about going the *wrong* way: you can almost always turn around.

Xiǎo Lóng took a deep, sloshy breath, and he climbed back *down*.

It was easier going down, mostly because every basket he passed cheered him on. The lonely chicken foot waggled a toe: "Knew you'd be back, kid!" The egg tarts jiggled hello. The pork buns rolled aside with friendly little "*oof*"s. And the shrimp dumplings, still mid-pleat-and-seek, called out, "Found you! You were *up there* the whole time, you weirdo!"

Down, down, down — until the steam grew thick and warm and ginger-sweet around him again, wrapping him up like a blanket fresh from the wash. Down until the *whooooo* of the lonely breeze faded away behind him. Down, at last, into the snug, golden, giggly middle basket where all his friends were piled together.

"You're back!" cried the small dumpling who liked squished. "How was the top? Was it *amazing*? Was it *fancy*?"

Xiǎo Lóng wobbled into the warm pile, sloshing happily, his soup heating right back up to a cozy simmer. He let out the biggest, most relieved sigh a dumpling has ever sighed.

"It was cold," he said. "And lonely. And wobbly. And the breeze went *whooooo* all night and there was nobody to squish at *all*." He snuggled deeper into the steam. "Friends, I have learned a thing. The top spot looks like the best spot from far away. But the *best* spot — the *real* best spot — is wherever the warmth gathers and the friends are packed in close. And that's not the top at all."

"It's the middle!" cheered the squished dumpling.

"It's the middle," agreed Xiǎo Lóng, grinning his pleated grin. "Where the steam stays. Where the giggles live. Right here."

---

So they had a sleepover, all of them, in the warm golden middle of the great steamer stack.

The shrimp dumplings hummed their soft song. The pork buns swayed. The egg tarts giggled until their custard nearly tipped, and the lonely chicken foot — who, it turned out, had snuck down to join the cozy basket years ago and never regretted it once — told the very best ginger-scented stories of all.

And Xiǎo Lóng, plump and pleated and warm right down to his soupy middle, squished happily between his friends with the steam curling around them like the coziest quilt in all of Taiwan, knew at last that he was exactly, *exactly* where he belonged.

In the morning, the waiter would lift a basket — maybe his, maybe not — and carry it off to a lucky table with chopsticks and a spoon and a little dish of black vinegar, and *that* would be a joyful thing, a proud thing, the happiest morning a dumpling could hope for.

But the morning could wait.

For now, there was steam, and there was squish, and there were friends. And the middle of the stack, warm and golden and packed full of love, was the very best spot of all.

## Goodnight Blessing
May your night be warm, little one, and your friends gathered close. May you never climb so high chasing the fanciest spot that you leave the coziest one behind. The steam is rising soft and golden, your friends are snuggled near, and you are tucked right in the warm, squishy middle where all the good things gather. There is no need to be first, and no need to be highest — only to be here, warm and loved and home. Close your eyes now, dear heart. The lanterns are low, the steam is sweet, and you are exactly where you belong. Sleep snug. Goodnight.
