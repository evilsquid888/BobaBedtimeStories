# Story 77: Chicken Cutlet & the Gentle-Giant Games

**Main Character:** Pái Pái (Fried Chicken Cutlet)
**Guest Characters:** Mochi
**Setting:** A friendly strongest-snack "games of strength" fair
**Theme:** True strength is gentle.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 力氣 (lìqì) = strength

---

The Strongest-Snack Games opened at sundown, and the very first thing that happened was that Pái Pái flexed so hard her crispy edges crackled like applause.

"BIGGER THAN YOUR FACE," she boomed to the crowd, striking a pose, "BIGGER THAN YOUR WORRIES!"

She was, in fact, enormous — a golden fried chicken cutlet broader than a serving tray, dusted in plum powder and pepper, with crunchy edges she flared like a superhero cape. When she walked, the night-market lanterns swayed. When she laughed, three lanterns fell off entirely.

A banner crackled overhead: WELCOME, MIGHTY SNACKS. Beneath it, an Earth God grandfather no taller than a teapot rang a tiny bronze gong.

*Bonggg.*

"Bless this little corner of the island," he chuckled, "and may the strongest snack win!"

Everyone turned and looked at Pái Pái.

Pái Pái buffed her crunchy bicep on her crunchy hip.

"Don't all cheer at once," she said. Then, because she could not help it: "Okay — DO all cheer at once."

They did. It was wonderful.

The games had three events. First, the Great Radish Lift, where snacks heaved giant marbled radishes overhead. Second, the Dumpling Tug, a rope-pull across a puddle of brown-sugar syrup (the syrup, a swirly ribbon named Hēi Táng, kept dramatically narrating: "Ooh, the tension! Ooh, the *drips*!"). Third, the famous Arm Crackle, a friendly arm-wrestle on a bamboo table, padded with soft sticky-rice cushions so nobody bonked.

Pái Pái signed up for all three. She signed up so hard her pencil snapped. Then she signed up with a *second* pencil, which also snapped, and finally with a stick of celery, which held up surprisingly well and earned a small round of applause from a nearby radish cake.

"Crispy edges, soft heart," the radish cake remarked approvingly. "Strong wrist, too."

"Trophy's basically mine already," she announced. "I've got the most **力氣** (lìqì) = strength of anybody here. Look at these edges. *Crunch.* That's the sound of victory."

Now, here is where our story turns, the way a pancake turns — gently, with a little flip.

While Pái Pái was warming up (she did this by lifting other contestants over her head and asking if they were comfortable), a small white something rolled up to the registration table. It was soft. It was squishy. It was approximately the size of a snowball that had given up on being scary.

It was Mochi.

"Hi," said Mochi, in a voice like a marshmallow clearing its throat. "Is this the strong games? I'd like to enter. I think."

The Earth God peered down kindly. "And what is your special **力氣**, little one?"

Mochi thought about this very hard. You could tell, because Mochi scrunched, and when Mochi scrunched, Mochi got even rounder.

"I'm... stretchy?" Mochi offered. "And loyal. And I give very good hugs. My motto is *stick together.*"

A few of the bigger snacks snickered. A burly pepper bun named Hú Jiāo whispered, not very quietly, "That little blob couldn't lift a sesame seed."

Mochi's pink-white cheeks went pinker. Mochi began to roll backward, slowly, the way you leave a room when you've decided the room doesn't want you.

And Pái Pái — mid-flex, one contestant still balanced on her head — saw the whole thing.

Something in her chest did a funny squeeze, like a cape tightening.

"HEY," she boomed. "Where do you think *you're* rolling, champ?"

Mochi froze. "Me? I'm not a champ. I'm a... a snack-shaped apology."

"Nope," said Pái Pái, setting the head-contestant down. "Wrong. You signed up. That makes you a competitor. And competitors don't roll away before the gong, kiddo. That's the rules." She lowered her voice to a crispy whisper. "I just made up that rule. But it's a *good* rule."

So Mochi stayed. Mochi stood very straight, which for a blob mostly meant becoming a slightly taller blob, and tried to look fierce. The effect was roughly that of an angry dumpling. It was extremely cute, and absolutely nobody was frightened.

"How do I look?" Mochi whispered.

"Terrifying," said Pái Pái, lying with her whole heart. "I'm shaking. These are nervous flexes."

Mochi beamed.

The first event, the Great Radish Lift, was a disaster for Mochi — but a glorious one.

Pái Pái went first and hoisted her radish so high a passing Moon Rabbit had to duck. The crowd roared.

Then came Mochi. The radish was four times Mochi's size. Mochi wrapped around it, stretched, strained, stretched some more — and instead of lifting the radish, the radish sort of... lifted Mochi. Mochi stuck to it like soft white glue and dangled there, swinging gently.

"I'm doing it!" Mochi called, upside down. "I'm... I'm being lifted *by* it! That counts, right?"

The crowd laughed. Some of it was kind laughing. Some of it was the other kind.

Mochi's lip wobbled.

Pái Pái strode over, peeled Mochi off the radish with a soft *thwup*, and held the little blob up to the crowd like a trophy. "GIVE IT UP," she boomed, "FOR THE STRETCHIEST DANGLE I HAVE EVER SEEN. That was ART."

And because Pái Pái was so big and so confident and so obviously meant it, the crowd actually cheered. Mochi blinked. Being cheered for was a strange and warming feeling, like sunshine on dough.

The Dumpling Tug went about the same. Mochi gripped the rope bravely, dug in, and was promptly slingshotted across the syrup puddle, landing with a sticky *splat* in a delighted Hēi Táng.

"Magnificent flight!" cried the syrup. "I make everything sweeter — including your *crash landing!*"

Mochi sat up, dripping golden caramel, and — to everyone's surprise — laughed. A real laugh. "Okay," Mochi giggled, "that was actually kind of fun."

"You're a natural," said Hēi Táng, draping a glistening amber arm around the little blob. "Some snacks spend years learning to crash gracefully. You did it on the *first try.* I'm so proud I could drip."

Mochi pulled a long sticky string of syrup off one ear, examined it, and then — because there was nothing else to be done — licked it. "Mm," said Mochi. "I taste delicious now. Is that allowed?"

"Kiddo," called Pái Pái, "that might be the most useful superpower at this entire fair."

Pái Pái, watching, felt that funny chest-squeeze again. She was winning every event. The trophy gleamed on its stand, a golden cutlet exactly her shape. By all rights she should have felt enormous and victorious.

Instead she kept looking at the little syrup-covered blob who kept getting back up.

*That's a different kind of lìqì,* she thought. *Getting flung into syrup and laughing anyway. I'm not sure I have that one.*

Then came the final event. The Arm Crackle.

Before it began, Pái Pái found Mochi sitting alone behind the lantern stand, looking very small and very thoughtful.

"Hey, champ," she said, folding her huge crispy self down to sit beside the blob. "You good?"

"I keep losing," Mochi said quietly. "Every event. Everyone's so big and strong, and I'm just... soft."

Pái Pái was quiet for a moment, which for Pái Pái was a remarkable event, like a thunderstorm holding its breath.

"You know what I noticed tonight?" she said. "Every time you get flung, you get back up. Every single time. I've lifted radishes the size of small moons, kiddo, but I've never once been thrown into a puddle of syrup and stood up *laughing.* I don't think I could." She bumped Mochi gently with one enormous crunchy elbow. "That's its own kind of **力氣**. The bounce-back kind. The soft kind. Maybe it's the strongest kind there is."

Mochi looked up. "You really think so?"

"I *crunchin'* know so."

That got a giggle. And the giggle was enough to fill Mochi back up, round and ready.

Pái Pái sat at the bamboo table, planted her crunchy elbow on the sticky-rice cushion, and waited for her challenger. One by one, the strong snacks tried her. One by one — *crackle, crackle, plop* — she set their hands down so gently it was almost polite. Hú Jiāo lasted four whole seconds and considered it a personal triumph.

At last only Mochi was left.

"I have to go now?" Mochi squeaked. "Against *you?*"

"That's how brackets work, kiddo." Pái Pái patted the cushion. "Come on. Sit."

Mochi climbed up, planted a soft little arm against Pái Pái's gigantic crispy one. Mochi's whole body barely reached Pái Pái's elbow.

The Earth God raised his tiny staff. "Ready... steady... *crackle!*"

Pái Pái pushed.

And the strangest thing happened.

Mochi didn't fall over. Mochi *stretched.* Soft and endless and impossible to pin, Mochi's arm bent and bent and bent — but never, ever broke, and never, ever gave up. Pái Pái pushed harder. Mochi stretched longer, like warm taffy, like a yawn that wouldn't end, wearing the most determined little face you ever saw.

"You're — *grunt* — supposed — to — *fall!*" Pái Pái laughed.

"I — *stretch* — instead!" Mochi puffed. "It's my only move! Stick together — even when it's hard!"

The crowd went absolutely bananas. Nobody had ever seen anything like it: the mightiest snack in the market, pushing with everything she had, and a little white blob simply... refusing to be beaten by bending instead.

And right there, at the bamboo table, Pái Pái understood something that no trophy could teach her.

She had been the strongest snack in the fair all night. But the bravest snack — the one with the truest, deepest, most unbreakable **力氣** — was the soft little one who kept getting up, kept laughing, kept *sticking together* no matter how many times the world flung it into syrup.

So Pái Pái did the strongest thing she did all night.

She stopped pushing.

She turned her huge crispy hand over, took Mochi's little stretched arm in her own, and *lifted* — gently, carefully, the way you lift something precious — until Mochi was standing tall on top of the bamboo table for the whole market to see.

"FOLKS," Pái Pái boomed, "I withdraw."

The crowd gasped.

"That's right. The strongest snack just *quit.* Because I figured something out tonight." She set Mochi gently on her own broad golden shoulder. "Strength isn't how high you can lift a radish. It's how gently you can lift up somebody who's feeling small. THIS little champion has more **力氣** in one squishy pinky than I've got in my whole crispy cape. So the trophy's hers. I'm keeping something better."

"What's that?" whispered Mochi.

Pái Pái grinned her biggest, crunchiest grin.

"A best friend, kiddo."

The Earth God laughed so hard his temple-hat wobbled, and rang the gong, and handed both of them lucky candies. The golden trophy went to Mochi, who immediately said it was *their* trophy, *we* won it, because that is simply how Mochi thinks.

And from that night on, the two were never apart — the gigantic crispy cutlet and the small soft blob who taught a giant what gentle strength means. At every market afterward, you could spot them: Pái Pái flexing for the crowd, and Mochi riding proudly on her shoulder, hugging the trophy that belonged to both of them.

Because the strongest snack in all of Taiwan had finally learned her own catchphrase was true.

Bigger than your face. Bigger than your worries.

And — gentlest of all — big enough to lift somebody up.

## Goodnight Blessing

Tonight, let your shoulders rest, let your worries melt like syrup, and hold tight to the people who stick together with you. Sweet, gentle dreams, brave heart. Goodnight.
