# Story 99: Sunrise Over the Whole Island

**Main Character:** Dòu Jiāng (Soy Milk)
**Guest Characters:** Yóu Tiáo, Boba, Ā-Lǐ
**Setting:** Dawn breaking over all of Taiwan as everyone travels to the reunion
**Theme:** A new day, gently begun, holds everything.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 日出 (rìchū) = sunrise

---

Before the roosters, before the buses, before even the sleepiest scooter coughed itself awake, there was Dòu Jiāng.

She was a gentle mug of warm soy milk, creamy-pale, with a soft curl of steam rising off her head like a question mark she had not quite finished asking. A frothy mustache of foam sat above her smile. She was, in every way, a morning person — which was a slightly funny thing to be, since most mornings had not technically begun yet when she got to work.

"Good morning," she whispered to the dark sky, because she liked to greet it early, before it got busy. "Sip slow. Today's a big one."

Today was the hundredth reunion — the grand gathering where every snack on the whole island, sweet and savory, old and new, would travel the morning roads to meet. And mornings, as everybody knew, did not start themselves.

They started with Dòu Jiāng.

She rolled up her invisible sleeves and got to warming.

---

"PSST. Dòu Jiāng. Dòu Jiāng. Are we up? We're up, right? Tell me we're up."

This was Yóu Tiáo — two of him, actually, since the long golden fried-dough stick always came as a pair of twin strands joined at the middle, like a best-friend bracelet that could talk. He was lanky and crispy and stretchy, and he bounced on his end-joints with the wiggly excitement of someone who had been waiting his whole life for breakfast, which, to be fair, he had.

"We're up," said Dòu Jiāng softly. "Sip slow."

"I can't sip slow! Today's the day! The hundredth! Dunk me, I'm ready — wait, dunk me, I'm ready — sorry, that was both of us." He cleared both his throats. "What's the plan, boss?"

"The plan," said Dòu Jiāng, and she breathed out a warm cloud, "is **日出** (rìchū) = sunrise. We wake the whole island. Soft and warm. Town by town. Nobody should travel to the reunion on an empty tummy or a grumpy heart."

Yóu Tiáo gasped so hard he nearly snapped in half. "The WHOLE island? Both of us? I mean — both of me? That's a lot of dunking."

"That's a lot of love," Dòu Jiāng corrected, gently. "Which is just dunking, but warmer."

And so the two of them set off into the last of the dark.

---

The first stop was the night market, where the lanterns were yawning themselves dim. Dòu Jiāng padded between the sleeping stalls and poured a little of herself — just a splash of warmth — into the cool morning air. The savory snacks stirred. A pepper bun rolled over. A bowl of braised pork rice cracked one eye.

"Five more minutes," mumbled somebody under a tarp.

"There are no five more minutes today," Dòu Jiāng said kindly. "There's only **早安** (zǎo'ān) = good morning, and a hundred friends waiting."

Yóu Tiáo did what Yóu Tiáo did best: he flopped, dramatically, into a wide bowl of his own warm soy-milk friend, sending up a splash and a cackle. "DUNK! Ahhh. Okay, okay. Now THAT'S a wake-up call." He shook himself off, scattering golden crumbs like confetti. "Who's next? Everybody up! Up up up! It's the hundredth, you crispy sleepyheads!"

One by one, the market blinked awake — not with alarms, not with shouting, but with the smell of warm soy and fried dough drifting down every little lane. It is very hard to stay asleep, you see, when breakfast comes looking for you personally.

A radish cake stretched his square crispy edges and announced, very seriously, "Breakfast club is now in session." A glossy ribbon of brown-sugar syrup poured himself a dramatic little swirl across a tabletop and sighed, "I make everything sweeter — even Tuesdays." And a marbled tea egg cracked one more line into his shell, peered at it, and declared, "Today's fortune: warm. Very warm. The warmest." Which, as fortunes go, was hard to argue with.

Dòu Jiāng did not hurry any of them. She just kept being warm in the corner of the lane, the way the morning is warm — patiently, and on purpose. "There's no rush," she told a nervous little soup dumpling who was counting his pleats. "The day is long. You'll get there with your soup right where it belongs."

"All eighteen pleats?" he asked.

"All eighteen," she promised.

---

Up they climbed, next, into the high mountains, where the air was thin and cold and silver with mist. Here lived Ā-Lǐ, the slender jade-green oolong tea leaf with the little conical bamboo hat. She was curled tight as a secret, sleeping in a cloud.

"Ā-Lǐ?" whispered Dòu Jiāng. "It's time. The reunion."

The tea leaf did not jump up the way Yóu Tiáo had. That was not Ā-Lǐ's way. She simply opened, slowly, the way a thought opens. As Dòu Jiāng's warmth touched her, she unfurled — one curl, then another — and from her came a swirl of fragrant cloud that smelled of orchids and high cold places.

"Steep slow," Ā-Lǐ murmured, with the patience of a mountain that has watched ten thousand sunrises and was not bored by a single one. "Breathe deep. Ahh. Is it morning already, little mug?"

"Almost," said Dòu Jiāng. "The **日出** (rìchū) hasn't crested yet. But it's coming. It always comes."

"It does," Ā-Lǐ agreed. "That is the kindest thing about it. You cannot rush a sunrise, and you do not have to. It carries everything — the whole day — without ever once seeming to hurry." She tilted her bamboo hat toward the east, where the black sky had begun, very faintly, to bruise into blue. "Shall we walk down together?"

"Together!" cheered Yóu Tiáo, who had spent the last minute trying to balance on one end-joint and had only just stopped. "Wait — careful on the mountain path, both of me. Last time we got excited on a slope we rolled all the way down and ended up dunked in a stranger's breakfast. Best worst morning of our life."

"Was it the dunking that made it best, or the worst that made it a story?" asked Ā-Lǐ, with the smallest of mountain smiles.

Yóu Tiáo thought about this so hard that both halves of him crossed their eyes. "...Yes," he decided.

"Good answer," said Ā-Lǐ.

So now they were three, winding down the mountain road as the mist thinned: a steaming mug, a pair of giggling dough sticks, and a serene unfurling leaf trailing perfume behind her like a scarf.

---

At the bottom of the mountain, on the wide road that ran toward the reunion field, they found a small commotion bouncing toward them.

"LET'S BOUNCE!"

It was Boba — the glossy, brown-black tapioca pearl, round and springy and absolutely incapable of holding still. He came rolling and ricocheting up the road, leading what looked like half the island behind him: a parade of snacks all heading the same warm direction.

"Dòu Jiāng! Yóu Tiáo! Ā-Lǐ!" Boba bounced so high with excitement he very nearly stuck himself to a passing cloud. "You're DOING it! You woke everybody up! I can smell breakfast from like three towns over. Taro's already arguing about the best route, Mochi's hugging the bus, and Shaved Ice is yelling everything IN CAPITAL LETTERS because the sun is rising and he says it's, quote, 'TOO BEAUTIFUL TO USE QUIET VOICES.'"

Dòu Jiāng laughed her soft, milky laugh. "Then it's working."

"Working?" Boba did a happy little 360 in the air. "It's the best morning EVER. Everybody's coming. Sweet ones, savory ones, old friends, brand-new faces nobody's even met yet. There's a soup dumpling counting his pleats so he doesn't spill on the way, and a giant chicken cutlet who keeps announcing she's 'bigger than the sunrise,' which — honestly? Close."

Yóu Tiáo flopped against Boba in a crumbly hug. "We did good, didn't we? Both of us did good."

"You did," said Dòu Jiāng warmly. "But mostly the morning did. We just opened the door."

Boba zipped happy circles around the whole group. "I've decided this is the official theme of today, and the theme is: EVERYBODY COMES. You know how some parties you're scared you won't fit in? This one's the opposite. The brown-sugar syrup is already painting golden hellos on the road signs. The popcorn-chicken crew is bouncing in formation and counting off — 'one! two! seventeen! we lost count again!' Even the brand-new snacks that nobody's met yet are walking in, all shy, and everybody's just going *come on in, scoot over, there's room.*"

"There's always room," Dòu Jiāng agreed softly. "That's the secret nobody tells you about mornings. They never run out."

---

They crested the last small hill together, and there it was.

The east had caught fire — the soft, friendly kind of fire, all rose and gold and apricot, spilling across the whole island at once. It touched the mountains Ā-Lǐ had come from. It touched the night market that was now a morning market. It poured down every road, and on every road were friends: bobbing zongzi and floating mooncakes, hopping popcorn-chicken crews and rolling sesame balls, a tiny Earth God riding cheerfully on someone's shoulder, handing out lucky candies to anyone who passed.

The whole island, waking up, walking toward each other.

Boba went very still — which, for Boba, was a miracle. "Whoa," he breathed.

"That," said Ā-Lǐ quietly, "is a **日出** (rìchū). It does not pick favorites. It shines on the sweet and the savory the very same. On the famous and the brand-new the very same." She turned her dewy eyes on Dòu Jiāng. "You knew that all along, didn't you, little mug? That a morning, gently begun, holds everything?"

Dòu Jiāng smiled into her foam. She had not thought of it in such grand words. She had only thought: *people are nicer when they're warm, and the day is friendlier when somebody says good morning first.* But maybe that was the same thing, said small.

"Sip slow," she said, watching the light come. "We've got all day. And all day is plenty."

Yóu Tiáo sniffled, both of him. "I'm not crying, you're crying. We're both — okay, we're all crying a little."

"It's the steam," said Dòu Jiāng kindly, even though it wasn't.

---

And so they walked the last stretch together, four friends grown into a crowd, the warm smell of breakfast going on ahead of them like a welcome mat unrolled across the whole island. Behind them the sun climbed, unhurried, exactly as fast as it always climbs, carrying the day on its back the way Guā Bāo carries a hug — gently, and with room for everyone.

The hundredth reunion was waiting just over the next rise.

But that, dear reader, is a story for tomorrow.

Tonight, it is enough to know that the morning came. It always comes. And somewhere, a small warm mug is already getting ready to greet it.

## Goodnight Blessing
May your night be soft as froth and warm as a mug held in two hands. May the dark be only the quiet part before the kind part, and may the morning come to find you rested and ready, exactly as fast as it should — not one minute sooner, not one minute late. The sunrise is already on its way to you, carrying a whole new day with room inside it for everything. Sip slow, little one. You have all the time you need. Goodnight.
