# Story 30: Sesame Ball and the Marble Canyon

**Main Character:** Zhīma (芝麻球) — Sesame Ball
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** Taroko Gorge (太魯閣), Hualien — towering marble canyon, the Swallow Grotto, a jade-green river
**Theme:** Keep your momentum — steady courage rolls through anything
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 太魯閣 (Tàilǔgé) = Taroko

---

At the top of the winding mountain road, a perfectly round golden ball took one look at the long, swooping, twisty path tumbling down into the canyon below — and grinned the biggest grin a deep-fried sphere can grin.

"Oh," said Zhīma, sparkling all over in the morning sun. "Oh, this is going to be GLORIOUS."

Zhīma was a sesame ball. Crispy golden crust, covered in a thousand tiny white sesame seeds, hollow on the inside with a warm sweet bean filling that sloshed pleasantly when he got excited. And he was excited a LOT, because Zhīma's favorite thing in the entire world was rolling, and his second favorite thing was rolling FAST, and his third favorite thing was rolling fast into something dramatic.

And reader, he had just arrived at the most dramatic place in all of Taiwan: **太魯閣** (Tàilǔgé) = Taroko, a gorge carved out of solid marble by a river over millions of years. Cliffs the color of cream and silver and pale grey stripes shot straight up into the sky. And winding down the middle was a road so curvy it looked like a noodle that had given up.

"ROLL WITH IT!" Zhīma hollered, and launched himself down the hill.

*Crunch-crunch-crunch-CRUNCH.*

He picked up speed. The marble walls blurred past — swirls of stone like frozen taffy, like somebody had taken a mountain and stirred it. Zhīma whooped. Every little sesame seed on his crust buzzed with the wind. He banked off a smooth marble boulder, *boing*, ricocheted off a guardrail, *boing-boing*, and shot around a hairpin curve so fast that a tour bus full of grandmothers gasped and then applauded.

"THANK YOU, I'M HERE ALL WEEK," Zhīma called, blowing past them.

Down, down, down he rolled, into the cool shadow of the canyon, where the famous **燕子口** (Yànzǐkǒu) = Swallow Grotto opened up around him. The cliffs leaned close on both sides, pocked with hundreds of little caves, and out of those caves zipped hundreds of tiny swallows, swooping and diving and snatching bugs out of the air. The whole gorge fluttered. It was like rolling through a snow globe full of birds.

And far, far below, at the very bottom of the canyon, a river rushed by — but not a normal river. This water was a deep, glowing, milky jade-green, the color of a melon popsicle, churning white over the rocks. It thundered. It sparkled. It was loud enough to drown out a marching band.

Zhīma skidded to a stop on a flat ledge, sesame seeds pinging off in every direction, and just stared up at the towering marble **山** (shān) = mountain all around him.

"Okay," he breathed. "Okay. That's the most beautiful thing I've ever — "

"HELP!"

Zhīma's whole crust went stiff.

The voice was tiny. Squeaky. And very, very scared. It was coming from across the gorge — from a thin spit of rock on the FAR side of the rushing jade river, where a little brown swallow chick stood flapping its baby wings and not flying anywhere at all.

"HELP! I fell out of my cave and now there's a — there's a — " The chick pointed a trembling wing.

A chunk of the cliff above had let go. A little rockslide had clattered down and blocked the narrow ledge that wound back up to the swallow caves, leaving the chick stranded on a crumbly outcrop with the roaring river below and no way back up. One wrong wobble and *sploosh* — straight into the rapids.

"**小心**!" (xiǎoxīn) = be careful! Zhīma shouted across the water. "Don't move! Don't flap! Stay EXACTLY where you are!"

"I CAN'T FLY YET!" wailed the chick. "I only just learned how to FALL!"

"Falling's the first half of flying," said Zhīma. "You're halfway there." (This was not true, but it was the kind of true that helps in an emergency.)

The trouble was the river. It was at least twenty sesame-balls wide, frothing and furious, with slick wet boulders sticking out of it and absolutely no bridge of any kind. A swallow could fly it in a heartbeat. But this swallow couldn't fly. And sesame balls, in case you were wondering, cannot swim. They float, briefly, like a very crunchy boat, and then they get soggy and that is the end of the adventure.

Zhīma looked at the river. He looked at the chick. He looked at the marble cliffs leaning in on both sides, smooth and curved and polished by a million years of water.

And then Zhīma's sesame seeds prickled, because an idea was arriving, and it was a deeply ridiculous idea, and those were always his favorites.

"Hey, little **朋友**!" (péngyou) = friend, he called. "How do you feel about CATCHING something?"

"Catching WHAT?"

"ME."

The chick stopped crying out of sheer confusion. "You're a ball."

"I'm a *cannonball*," Zhīma corrected. "There's a difference, and the difference is ATTITUDE."

Here was the plan, and reader, I want you to picture it, because it was genuinely one of the silliest plans ever attempted by a dessert. The canyon walls curved inward like the inside of a bowl. If Zhīma rolled fast enough — really, properly, gloriously fast — he could go up the curved marble wall on his side, bank along it like a marble in a bowl, arc over the river through the air, and land on the far ledge beside the chick. All he needed was speed. And to a sesame ball, speed was never the problem.

Stopping was the problem. But that was a future-Zhīma issue.

"BACK UP, TINY BIRD!" Zhīma yelled. The chick squished itself against the cliff. "And whatever you do — don't stop believing in me halfway. That's when it goes wrong. You gotta believe the WHOLE way. **加油**!" (jiāyóu) = go for it!

Zhīma turned and rolled away from the river. He rolled, in fact, in completely the wrong direction, which made the chick scream "WHERE ARE YOU GOING," but Zhīma was just getting a running start. He went up the road, around the bend, all the way back to a long straight slope — and then he turned around, lined himself up, took one deep crunchy breath, and let gravity have him.

*crunch-crunch-crunch-crunchCRUNCHCRUNCH—*

Faster than before. Faster than the tour bus. Faster than a passion fruit on a dare. The marble walls became a blur of cream and silver. The swallows scattered out of his way, shrieking encouragements in swallow-language. Zhīma's filling sloshed like an ocean. His seeds sang. He was a golden comet with sweet bean inside, and he was NOT going to stop, because the whole secret — the whole entire secret of being brave — is that you cannot stop in the middle. You roll through the scary part. You keep your momentum. The moment you hesitate is the moment you wobble, and the moment you wobble is the moment you fall.

So Zhīma did not hesitate. Not even when the river roared up to meet him. Not even when the curved marble wall loomed huge and shining on his right.

He hit the wall going full speed — and instead of crashing, he *banked*, riding the smooth curve up and up, sideways, like a marble whirling inside a bowl, the whole world tilting around him. The jade river thundered below his crust. For one impossible second Zhīma was rolling along a vertical cliff of polished stone with nothing beneath him but rushing green water, a swallow chick screaming, and the entire sky.

"WAHOOOOO!" Zhīma bellowed. "BEST! DAY! EVER!"

And at the very top of the curve, where the marble wall flattened, Zhīma launched off the rock and flew.

He flew over the river. Sesame seeds streaming behind him like a comet's tail. Spinning, golden, glorious, hollow, brave.

"CATCH ME, **朋友**!" he yelled.

The little swallow chick, who had never caught anything in its life and could barely stand up, did the bravest thing a baby bird has ever done. It spread its wobbly wings wide — and *caught* him. Well. It cushioned him. Well. It got knocked flat on its tail-feathers and Zhīma bounced gently off its tummy and rolled to a soft stop against the cliff, completely unharmed, only slightly dizzy, with all his seeds still on.

"You DID it," gasped the chick.

"WE did it," said Zhīma, wobbling upright. "You caught a cannonball. That's officially braver than flying."

But they weren't safe yet — they were both stuck on the same crumbly ledge now, with the rockslide blocking the path up. Zhīma looked at the pile of fallen rocks. Then he looked at his own perfectly round, perfectly hard, perfectly Zhīma-shaped self.

"Climb on top of me," he said.

"What?"

"I'm a round golden step-stool. Get on. Hold tight to a seed."

The chick scrambled onto Zhīma's crunchy crust. And Zhīma — slowly, carefully, no speed this time, just patience — rolled forward and wedged himself into the gap in the rockslide, lifting the chick up, up, just high enough to reach the lip of its home cave. The chick scrabbled, flapped its silly half-formed wings, and *flump* — tumbled safely into the soft warm nest where its mama swallow was waiting, frantic with worry.

"小心 next time," Zhīma called up, grinning. "Or actually — don't. Be careful when you have to. But mostly? Roll with it."

The whole cliff of swallows burst into chirping cheers. It echoed off the marble walls of 太魯閣 until the entire canyon seemed to be singing, the jade river drumming along underneath, the morning sun pouring gold over a thousand tiny caves.

And Zhīma, sitting on his ledge in the warm light, sparkling all over with sesame seeds, looked out at the most beautiful gorge in the world and felt the very best kind of tired — the kind you only get after you've rolled straight through the scary part and come out the other side.

That evening, a kind Taroko park ranger found the golden ball napping happily on a sun-warmed rock and gently carried him down to a little tea stall at the mouth of the gorge, where a grandmother was frying fresh sesame balls in a bubbling golden pan.

"Well now," she chuckled, "aren't you a perfect one. Crispy. Round. Not a seed out of place." She set Zhīma on a warm plate beside a cup of hot tea. "Someone's going to enjoy you very much."

And reader, here is the wonderful thing: Zhīma did not panic. Zhīma did not wobble. Because this was his moment — the moment every brave little sesame ball rolls toward its whole crunchy life. He'd carried a friend across a canyon. Now he got to be the warmest, sweetest, most delicious treat at the bottom of the most beautiful mountain in Taiwan.

"Roll with it," Zhīma whispered, happy and golden and proud, as the steam curled up around him like a soft blanket. "Always, always roll with it."

## Goodnight Blessing

Tonight, little dreamer, may your courage roll smooth and steady, the way a brave golden ball rolls down a marble mountain at dawn. When you come to the scary part — the part where the river roars and the gap looks too wide — don't stop, don't wobble, just keep your momentum and trust that you'll make it across. You are rounder and braver and faster than you know. Sleep now, snug as a sesame ball on a warm plate, with swallows singing softly in their caves and a jade-green river humming you down, down, down into the gentlest dreams. Goodnight, brave roller. Goodnight.
