# Story 79: Egg Waffle & the Big Talent Show

**Main Character:** Egg Waffle (雞蛋糕)
**Guest Characters:** Dàn Dàn (Tea Egg)
**Setting:** A community-stage talent show in a covered market plaza
**Theme:** Everyone's got a talent worth cheering.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 表演 (biǎoyǎn) = performance

---

Egg Waffle stood at the very top of an upside-down soup pot, twirling his top hat, and announced to the empty plaza in his very best carnival-barker boom:

"LADIES AND GENTLEDESSERTS! Step right up for the GREATEST market **表演** (biǎoyǎn) = performance the island has EVER seen!"

A pigeon clapped. Sort of. It was actually just shaking a crumb off its wing, but Egg Waffle took it.

He was a golden grid of fluffy little bubble-puffs, each one warm and round as a tiny sun, and tonight every puff of him buzzed with excitement. He had spent all week stringing fairy-lights across the covered market plaza, sweeping the stage, and painting a banner that read **TALENT SHOW TONIGHT!** in letters so big and swoopy that two of them dripped.

"By tonight," Egg Waffle declared, "this stage will be PACKED with stars."

He flipped open his shiny sign-up clipboard.

It was empty.

Completely, terrifyingly empty.

"...Ah," said Egg Waffle. "Minor wrinkle."

---

By lunchtime the clipboard was still empty, and Egg Waffle had begun to sweat caramel.

The trouble was, he discovered, that asking dessert folk to *perform* in front of a crowd was a bit like asking them to wear their socks on the outside. Everybody wanted to *watch* the talent show. Nobody wanted to *be* the talent show.

"Oh, I couldn't," said Pudding, wobbling so hard he nearly tipped over. "What if I get nervous and jiggle? I'd jiggle. I always jiggle."

"You jiggle now," Egg Waffle pointed out kindly.

"See! Imagine that, but WORSE, and with a SPOTLIGHT!" Pudding shuddered, which made him jiggle, which proved his point so thoroughly that he scurried off.

Egg Waffle tried Aiyu, the shy golden jelly, who turned so translucent with shyness you could read the menu board straight through her.

He tried Coconut Jelly, who said, "Bro, performing's not really my whole vibe, you know? I'm more of a *float-around-and-be-chill* guy."

He tried Taro, who adjusted his little reading glasses and said, very wisely, "The wisest performer is the one who knows when not to perform." Then he closed his eyes and pretended to be asleep, which was, Egg Waffle suspected, simply Taro's way of saying *no thank you* in proverb.

He even tried Lychee, who pressed a bumpy red hand to her chest and gasped, "Perform? Me? Darling, I would be MARVELOUS, of course, I always am — but a true star must be *invited*, not *asked*, and you, sweet waffle, merely *asked*." And she swept off in a huff of perfume to be invited by absolutely no one.

He even tried Shaved Ice, who he was *certain* would say yes, because Shaved Ice loved attention the way the ocean loves being wet.

"DARLING, I WOULD LOVE TO," boomed Shaved Ice, "BUT I AM MELTING WITH STAGE FRIGHT AS WE SPEAK." And indeed, a single dramatic drip rolled down his syrupy slope. "ASK ME WHEN I AM NOT A PUDDLE."

By mid-afternoon, the clipboard had exactly zero names on it, and Egg Waffle's top hat had slumped down over one eye.

"A talent show," he mumbled, "with no talent. That's just a... show. That's just a stage and some folding chairs and a banner with two drippy letters."

He sat down on the edge of his stage and let out a long, foggy sigh. The fairy-lights twinkled like they felt sorry for him.

---

That was when a small, marbled brown egg came rolling up to the stage, slow and gentle, leaving a warm steam-trail behind him.

It was Dàn Dàn, the tea egg, his shell covered in beautiful crackle-lines like a tiny cobweb map of brown rivers. He had wandered over from the all-night corner store, where he usually sat in the warm cooker reading the patterns on himself like fortunes.

"You look," said Dàn Dàn softly, peering up at Egg Waffle, "like a fellow whose crackle-lines have all knotted up at once."

"Nobody signed up," Egg Waffle groaned. "Not one single dessert. They all say the same thing: 'Oh, I don't have a talent.' But that's CRAZY, Dàn Dàn. Everybody's got *something*."

Dàn Dàn tilted, studying him.

"Hmm," he said. "Every crack tells a story. May I see your stage?"

Egg Waffle waved a flappy grid-hand at it. "Be my guest. It's empty. Like my dreams."

Dàn Dàn rolled a slow circle around the boards, humming. "You went looking for *performers*," he said. "But you should have gone looking for *people*. Find what a friend already loves to do — and there's your **表演** waiting all along."

Egg Waffle blinked. His puffs un-slumped, just a little. "...Go on."

"Walk with me," said the tea egg, "and let's read the market."

---

So they did.

They found Tangyuan, the round little rice ball, telling such a warm bedtime story to a cluster of sleepy sesame balls that even Egg Waffle forgot why he'd come.

"That's not a talent," said Tangyuan, surprised. "We just tell stories. We always tell stories. We're a *we*, you know."

"Exactly," said Dàn Dàn gently. "A talent is just a thing you do so easily you forgot it was special."

Tangyuan's whole round face went pink with pleasure. He signed up.

They found Mango doing dizzying flips off a melon crate, sweatband flashing, just for the fun of it.

"That's only goofing around," Mango protested.

"That," said Egg Waffle, suddenly understanding, "is *acrobatics*, my friend, and the crowd will go BANANAS. Or mangoes. Mango-nas."

Mango laughed so hard he fell off the crate, which was, frankly, an even better flip. He signed up too.

They found Cōng Cōng the scallion pancake cartwheeling on his crispy edges down the noodle aisle. "Let's flip the script!" he hollered, and signed his name with a flourish that flicked sesame oil onto the clipboard. (Egg Waffle decided it added character.)

They found Hēi Táng the brown-sugar syrup, who could pour himself into swirly **書法** (shūfǎ) = calligraphy across a plate, painting golden tiger-stripes that smelled like heaven. "I make everything sweeter," he said, "and apparently, *fancier*." Signed up.

They found Sweet Potato fixing a wobbly stall leg with his tiny hardhat tipped back, and when Egg Waffle suggested he show off how clever his hands were, Sweet Potato grinned a shy golden grin. "Folks don't usually *cheer* for fixing things," he said. "Then they've never seen it done *well*," said Dàn Dàn warmly. Signed up. They even found Pudding again, who had stopped trembling long enough to admit, very quietly, that he made an *excellent* wobbly drum if you tapped him just so. Egg Waffle nearly wept with pride.

By the time the sky over the covered plaza turned the soft dusky purple of bedtime, Egg Waffle's clipboard was FULL. Acrobats. Storytellers. A syrup-artist. A scallion-pancake stunt squad. A whole bouncing chorus of Kǎ Kǎ popcorn-chicken nuggets who'd agreed to sing — sort of — all at once — sort of in tune.

Egg Waffle's top hat sat tall and proud again.

"Dàn Dàn," he said, "you're a genius."

"I just read the lines," said Dàn Dàn, with a small, modest crackle.

And that was when Egg Waffle noticed the one name still missing.

"What about *you*?" he asked. "What's *your* talent?"

Dàn Dàn went very quiet. His steam-trail thinned. "Oh," he said. "I don't really... *do* anything. I just sit in the warm cooker and read my own crackle-lines. It's not a **表演**. It's just... me, being a small brown egg in the dark."

Egg Waffle looked at him for a long moment.

"Kiddo," he said, borrowing a word from Niú Niú because it felt right, "you just spent a whole afternoon finding the magic in *everybody else*. Let me find yours."

---

The show began.

The plaza was packed — folding chairs squeaking, fairy-lights glowing, steam-clouds and sauce-capes and dusty-purple shoulders all crammed in shoulder to shoulder. Egg Waffle stood center stage in his top hat, glowing like the showman he was born to be.

"WELCOME," he boomed, "TO THE WARMEST **表演** ON THE ISLAND!"

And oh, what a show it was.

Mango flipped so high the crowd gasped, then cheered, then gasped again when he landed in a pudding (Pudding was fine; in fact, being landed *on* made him too startled to be nervous, and he ended up giggling on stage after all). Cōng Cōng's stunt squad cartwheeled in a spinning golden pinwheel. Tangyuan told a story so cozy that half the front row fell adorably asleep. Hēi Táng painted a dripping golden moon across an enormous plate, and the whole crowd went "oooooh." The Kǎ Kǎ nuggets sang their song — all at once — sort of in tune — and it was, somehow, the most joyful noise anyone had ever heard.

And then, for the very last act, Egg Waffle dimmed the lights down low.

"For our final performer," he said softly, "I need everyone to be very, very quiet. Quiet like the moment right before sleep."

The plaza hushed.

A single warm spotlight clicked on. And there, in the middle of the great big stage, sat one small marbled tea egg.

"This is Dàn Dàn," said Egg Waffle gently. "And he reads the future in his own cracks. Friends — come close. Let him tell you your fortune."

One by one, the desserts crept up. And one by one, Dàn Dàn turned his beautiful cracked shell to the light and whispered:

"Pudding — your crackle says you are *braver than you wobble*."

"Aiyu — yours says the shy ones are often the bravest of all."

"Shaved Ice — yours says you will melt, yes, but only into something even sweeter."

The whole plaza had gone soft and glowy and warm, every face tilted toward the tiny brown egg in the spotlight, and nobody — not one single dessert — had ever heard anything so lovely. When Dàn Dàn finished, the silence held for one long breath.

Then the market ERUPTED. It was the biggest cheer of the whole night, by a mile, for the quietest talent of all. Chairs squeaked, sauce-capes flapped, and somewhere in the back the Kǎ Kǎ nuggets began an encore cheer all at once, sort of in tune.

Dàn Dàn glowed pink right through his cracks.

"See?" Egg Waffle whispered, beaming under his top hat. "Everybody's got something worth cheering for. You just have to read the lines."

---

Long after the chairs were folded and the banner rolled up, Egg Waffle and Dàn Dàn sat together on the edge of the empty stage, the fairy-lights twinkling above them like a private little galaxy.

"Best show on the island," said Egg Waffle happily.

"Best show on the island," agreed Dàn Dàn. He looked up at the real stars. "Every crack tells a story," he murmured. "Even mine."

And the market settled into a warm, snug, sleepy hush — the kind that comes only after everyone has been seen, and cheered, and loved.

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, somewhere inside you is a talent so easy and so *yours* that you might have forgotten it was special at all. Maybe you tell cozy stories, or flip off the couch, or notice the quiet magic in your friends. It is real, and it is worth cheering for — and so are you. Tonight, let the fairy-lights of your eyelids dim down soft and low, and let your own warm spotlight rest. You did wonderfully today, just by being you. Goodnight.
