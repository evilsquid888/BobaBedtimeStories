# Story 96: Lantern Squid & the Tide-Chart Crossing

**Main Character:** Yóu Yú Gēng (Squid Thick-Soup) — a savory, peppery squid soup in a bowl, a practical seafarer type
**Guest Characters:** None
**Setting:** The fishing harbor of an offshore island, planning the boat trip to the mainland reunion
**Theme:** Knowing the right moment to go is its own kind of wisdom
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 潮汐 (cháoxī) = the tides

---

On the smallest, saltiest island you can imagine — a freckle of rock far out in the dark blue sea — there lived a soup who knew everything about boats and almost nothing about hurrying.

Her name was **魷魚羹** (Yóu Yú Gēng) = Squid Thick-Soup, and she sloshed about in a wide blue bowl with a chipped golden rim. She was the color of a misty morning, thick and glossy and gently steaming, with curls of tender squid drifting through her like little white question marks. A whole black peppercorn rode on her surface like a tiny buoy, and whenever she laughed — which was often — she rippled all the way to the edge and back.

Squid Soup smelled of the harbor: garlic, white pepper, and the wide green ocean. And tonight, the whole island was about to lose its mind with excitement.

"THE REUNION!" shrieked a Pineapple Cake, hopping in circles. "It's on the MAINLAND! Tonight! We have to GO!"

"We have to go RIGHT NOW," agreed a wobbling Tofu Pudding, who had already packed a suitcase, two umbrellas, and a snack for the trip (which was, somewhat awkwardly, himself).

The whole harbor was throbbing with snacks. There were Oyster Omelets flapping their frilly edges, a barrel of fried Milkfish Balls bouncing like popcorn, and a very dignified Squid on a Stick who kept announcing that he was "ready to depart immediately, thank you." Everyone wanted to sail across the sea to the Grand Dessert Reunion, where snacks from every corner of the island came together once a year to hug, to feast, and to argue about whose grandmother made the best soup.

And the only way across was the old wooden ferry boat, bobbing at the end of the dock.

"To the boat!" cried the Pineapple Cake. "Pile in! Pile in! We'll be late!"

Squid Soup did not pile in.

Instead, she slid herself — bowl and all — right to the edge of the dock and peered down into the water. Then she looked up at the boat. Then she looked back down at the water. Then she said a word that nobody on a fun night ever wants to hear:

"Wait."

The harbor froze. Forty snacks turned to stare at her.

"Wait?" squeaked the Tofu Pudding, jiggling with horror. "But the reunion is TONIGHT!"

"I know," said Squid Soup calmly, her peppercorn bobbing. "And I want to get there more than anyone. My cousins will be there. My grandmother's recipe will be there. But look." She tipped her bowl gently toward the water. "Do you see how low the sea is right now? Do you see the rocks?"

Everyone looked. Sure enough, all around the little ferry boat, sharp black rocks poked up out of the shallow water like the teeth of a sleeping sea-dragon. Slick green seaweed hung off them. The boat sat low, almost resting on the bottom.

"If we launch now," said Squid Soup, "that boat will scrape its belly on those rocks before we're ten paces from the dock. We'll get stuck. Or worse — we'll spring a leak, and a soup who springs a leak does not make it to any reunion." She gave a little shudder that sent ripples across her surface.

"Then how do we EVER leave?" wailed the Pineapple Cake.

Squid Soup smiled the patient smile of someone who has spent her whole life by the sea. "We wait for the **潮汐** (cháoxī) = the tides," she said. "The sea breathes, little ones. In and out, in and out, twice a day. Right now it has breathed *out* — that's low tide, and the harbor is shallow. But soon it will breathe *in*. The water will rise and rise, and lift that boat clear up over the rocks like a feather. *That* is when we go."

The snacks grumbled. Oh, how they grumbled. Grumbling is one of the things snacks do best, right after eating and napping.

"That'll take HOURS," moaned a Milkfish Ball.

"It'll take about two," said Squid Soup. "And I have just the thing to help us wait."

She nudged her bowl over to a weathered old board nailed up beside the dock. It was covered in faded blue lines that swooped up and down like gentle hills. "This," she said proudly, "is the **潮汐表** (cháoxī biǎo) = tide-chart. The harbor grandmothers drew it long ago, reading the moon. It tells us exactly when the sea breathes in and out. See this curve? Right here at the very top — that's high tide. And tonight, high tide comes at the rising of the moon."

The Tofu Pudding squinted at the wavy lines. "It looks like a drawing of my tummy after the reunion," he said, and the whole harbor burst out laughing — even Squid Soup, who rippled so hard a little wave splashed the Pineapple Cake.

But not everyone was charmed.

A bossy bunch of Fried Sweet Potato Balls had been muttering in the corner, rolling against each other and getting themselves worked up the way crowds sometimes do. "Waiting is for slow snacks," sniffed their leader, a particularly round and crispy fellow. "We didn't come all this way to sit by a fire and listen to a soup talk about the moon."

"The moon is rather important to the sea," Squid Soup pointed out gently.

"We're not waiting," he announced, ignoring her entirely. "We're brave. We're bold. We'll *push* the boat over the rocks ourselves! Who's with us?" A few nervous Oyster Omelets fluttered, but nobody else moved. And before anyone could stop them, the Sweet Potato Balls bounced aboard, grabbed the oars, and began to shove.

The boat lurched forward — *scraaaaaape!* — and stopped dead, wedged on the first rock with a sad wooden groan. The Sweet Potato Balls heaved and grunted and rowed with all their might, but the boat only rocked back and forth like a stubborn turtle, going absolutely nowhere.

"Um," said the lead Sweet Potato Ball, going slightly redder than usual. "A little help?"

Squid Soup did not say *I told you so*, because she was kind, and because soups who say *I told you so* tend to get cold. Instead she organized a gentle rescue. "All together now — *rock it back!*" she called, and the whole harbor pushed and pulled until the boat slid backward off the rock with a wet *pop*, and the embarrassed Sweet Potato Balls tumbled out in a heap.

"Now," said Squid Soup, settling her bowl down with a contented slosh. "We wait properly. And waiting, my friends, can be the best part — if you do it right."

So they did it right. They built a little driftwood fire on the dock, and the flames threw dancing orange shadows across all the wavy lines of the tide-chart. Squid Soup warmed everyone up — quite literally, ladling out steaming sips of her peppery, garlicky self to anyone who wanted a taste, because that is the bravest and most generous thing a soup can do. The Milkfish Balls, who had been so impatient an hour ago, sighed with happiness and declared her the most comforting thing they had ever met.

"Tell us about the sea, Squid Soup," asked the Pineapple Cake, who was now snuggled up small by the fire. "How do you *know* all this tide business?"

So she told them. She told them about the **滿月** (mǎn yuè) = full moon, and how it tugs on the whole wide ocean with invisible string, pulling the water up the shore and letting it slide back down again. She told them how the harbor grandmothers had watched the moon for a hundred years to draw the tide-chart just right. She told them about the time she'd seen a foolish bread-bun captain ignore the chart and sail at low tide, and how he'd spent the whole night sitting glumly on a sandbar, watching the reunion lights twinkle without him.

"The sea isn't in a hurry," said Squid Soup, her peppercorn bobbing wisely. "It does everything exactly on time, every single day, and it has never once been late. We could learn a thing or two from the sea."

They sang harbor songs — the slow, sloshy kind, and then the silly kind with too many verses about fish. They told stories about reunions past, about the year the mochi got stuck to the ceiling and the year a typhoon turned everyone's hair to seaweed. The Tofu Pudding fell asleep and woke up three times, each time announcing very seriously that he had *not* been sleeping. And all the while, so slowly that nobody quite noticed, the sea was quietly, secretly breathing in.

"Look!" whispered the Pineapple Cake at last.

The water was rising. The sharp black rocks were sinking out of sight, one by one, swallowed up by the gentle, glittering sea. First the seaweed-covered teeth disappeared, then the big jagged one by the dock, then the last little nub that everyone had been worried about. The little ferry boat, which had been resting tiredly on the bottom, gave a soft creak and floated — really *floated* — high and proud and free, rocking softly as if it had been waiting for this moment all its life. On the tide-chart, the painted curve had climbed all the way to its peak, exactly where Squid Soup had said it would.

"It's just like she promised," whispered an Oyster Omelet, very impressed. The Sweet Potato Balls, who were still a little soggy from their adventure, said nothing at all — but they did shuffle over to stand near Squid Soup, which is how proud snacks say sorry.

"High tide," breathed Squid Soup, and her whole bowl glowed warm in the firelight. "The sea is holding its breath, just for us. Now — *now* we go."

This time, nobody scraped. Nobody got stuck. The snacks climbed aboard in a happy, orderly line, and the boat sat so high above the hidden rocks that it glided out of the harbor as smooth as a spoon through pudding. The moon laid a silver road across the water, and the ferry followed it straight toward the twinkling lights of the mainland, where — they could already hear it — a whole crowd of cousins was singing a welcome song across the waves.

"We made it!" cheered the Tofu Pudding, jiggling with joy. "And we're not even leaky!"

"We made it *because* we waited," said Squid Soup softly, watching the island shrink behind them. "Anybody can rush. The trick is knowing the right moment — and then going with all your heart."

And as the boat carried them safe and dry and all in one happy piece toward the reunion, every single snack agreed: the wisest sailor on the whole wide sea was a peppery little soup who knew exactly when *not* to leave.

The reunion, by the way, was wonderful. There was so much hugging and feasting that the Tofu Pudding fell asleep mid-bite, and Squid Soup's grandmother's recipe was declared the most warming soup of the year. But that is a story for another night.

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, the sea is breathing slow and deep tonight, in and out, in and out, just like you. There is no rock you must climb tonight, no boat you must push. The tide of sleep is rising all around your bed, soft and warm and silver, lifting you up so gently you'll never feel a single bump. You don't have to hurry anywhere. The right moment will find you, just as it found the little soup who knew how to wait. So close your eyes and let the water carry you. The reunion of dreams is waiting on the far shore, and you will arrive right on time. Goodnight.
