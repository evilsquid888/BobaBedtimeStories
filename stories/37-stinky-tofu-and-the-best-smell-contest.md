# Story 37: Stinky Tofu & the Best-Smell Contest

**Main Character:** Chòu Chòu (Stinky Tofu)
**Guest Characters:** Grass Jelly
**Setting:** A crowded Shenkeng old-street tofu lane
**Theme:** What makes you different can be what makes you loved.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 臭豆腐 (chòu dòufu) = stinky tofu

---

The first thing you should know about Chòu Chòu is that he could clear a crowded street faster than a runaway dumpling cart.

Not because he was scary. Goodness, no. Chòu Chòu was a golden, crispy cube of fried **臭豆腐** (chòu dòufu) = stinky tofu, with big puppy eyes and a heart-shaped pickled-cabbage cape that fluttered when he skipped. He was, by every honest measure, completely adorable.

The trouble was his aroma.

It rolled off him in friendly purple sparkle-clouds — *poof, poof, poof* — like a marching band made entirely of smell. And on this particular evening, as Chòu Chòu hopped down the famous tofu lane of Shenkeng old street, that marching band was playing at full volume.

"Evening, everyone!" he called cheerfully.

A noodle vendor leaned away. A grandmother pinched her nose. Three little mochi rolled backward all at once, like bowling pins.

Chòu Chòu sighed a tiny crispy sigh. "Bold on the outside, gold on the inside," he murmured. "They just need to get close enough to know it."

The problem was, nobody ever got close enough.

---

Shenkeng old street was the most crowded, most wonderful tofu lane on the whole island. Lanterns swung overhead like little orange moons. Steam curled from a hundred bubbling woks. Tourists squeezed shoulder to shoulder, snapping photos and saying *ooh* and *aah* at every sizzle.

But wherever Chòu Chòu walked, a polite little bubble of empty space followed him, as if the whole crowd were playing a game called *Don't Touch the Cube.*

He had tried *everything*. He had tried tiptoeing extra quietly, as if smells could be sneaky. He had tried holding his breath, which is very difficult when you don't have lungs. He had once tried wearing the clothespin he carried — clipping it onto his own nose — but that only made a passing turtle bun laugh so hard he fell off his stool.

"Maybe," Chòu Chòu had told himself that morning, "if I just smile *extra* hard, people won't notice."

People noticed.

He found his favorite resting spot — a cool stone step in a quiet corner — and plopped down with a *crunch*.

"Rough night?" said a calm voice.

Chòu Chòu looked up. Sitting beside him, as still and cool as a midnight pond, was a glossy black-green cube in a black beanie. It was Grass Jelly, the calmest dessert on the island, who somehow always looked like he'd just woken up from the world's best nap.

"You're not backing away," Chòu Chòu said, blinking. "Everybody backs away."

Grass Jelly took a slow sip from a tiny straw. "I don't back away from friends," he said. "Also, I can't smell much. Bonus."

Chòu Chòu laughed so hard a sparkle-cloud puffed out of his ears. "You're funny."

"I'm cool," Grass Jelly corrected, deadpan. "Funny is a side effect."

For a while they just sat together, watching the lanterns sway. It was, Chòu Chòu realized, the first time all week that anyone had sat *next* to him instead of opposite him with a polite three-foot moat. He found he liked it very much.

"Can I tell you a secret?" Chòu Chòu said quietly. "Sometimes I wonder if I should just stop coming to the street. Maybe the lane would be happier without all my..." He waggled his crispy hand at the purple sparkle-clouds. "...weather."

Grass Jelly was quiet for a long, cool moment. "When I was a little jelly," he said, "people called me *too dark, too wobbly, too strange.* They said nobody wants a dessert the color of midnight." He shrugged, slow as a tide. "Then one hot afternoon, somebody finally tried me. Said I was the most refreshing thing on the island. Turns out the things people call *strange* are usually just the things they haven't tasted yet." He sipped. "Don't quit before they taste you, crispy."

---

That was when the **鑼** (luó) = gong rang out across the lane — *BONNNG!* — and a very loud, very golden somebody climbed up onto a barrel.

It was Egg Waffle, the carnival showman, in his glittering top hat. "STEP RIGHT UP, STEP RIGHT UP!" he boomed. "Tonight, Shenkeng old street holds its very first BEST-SMELL CONTEST! The aroma judges have arrived all the way from the Earth God's temple! The winner's scent will become the OFFICIAL SMELL of the whole market — printed on flags, painted on lanterns, famous across the island!"

The crowd buzzed. Smells were *very* important on a food street, after all.

"Oh, I should enter!" gasped a delicate Mooncake, fluffing her lotus-paste perfume.

"Me! I smell of toasted sesame oil!" cried a scallion pancake, doing a hopeful cartwheel.

Chòu Chòu shrank into his cape. "I would lose so badly," he whispered. "I'd lose before the contest even started. I'd lose *yesterday.*"

Grass Jelly didn't move a muscle. "You should enter," he said.

"Are you joking? People hold their *noses* at me."

"People hold their noses at strong tea, too," said Grass Jelly. "Then they pay extra for it." He took another slow sip. "Enter. I'll stand with you the whole time. I don't move much anyway."

---

So Chòu Chòu, trembling and crackling, hopped into the line of contestants. And the moment he did, the line *un*-lined. Mooncake floated three steps left. The scallion pancake folded himself into a polite distant spiral. A whole crew of popcorn chicken bounced behind a lamppost, giggling nervously.

Only Grass Jelly stayed, cool as ever, right at Chòu Chòu's side.

"Just breathe," Grass Jelly murmured beside him. "And whatever happens — I'm not moving."

Chòu Chòu held onto those words like a warm blanket.

The aroma judges arrived in a tiny procession. Leading them, no taller than a teapot, was **土地公** (Tǔdì Gōng) = the Earth God, the jolly white-bearded guardian of the market, in his little red temple-hat. He smelled of incense and tangerines and hummed as he walked. Behind him toddled two helper judges — a wise marbled tea egg who read fortunes in cracks, and a tiny clay temple-tiger with a goofy grin who kept whispering "rawr means hello!" to everyone he passed.

"Bless this little corner of the island," he chuckled, handing out lucky candies. "Now then. Let us *sniff.*"

The judging began.

Mooncake stepped up. The Earth God breathed in. "Mmm. Elegant. Sweet. Like a family reunion under the moon." He nodded warmly. "Lovely, child."

The scallion pancake went next. "Toasty! Cheerful! A morning made of sunshine." The Earth God smiled.

The tea egg leaned over and whispered, "His crackle-lines say he will sneeze at the worst possible moment." The scallion pancake sneezed. Everyone agreed the tea egg was very wise.

And then it was Chòu Chòu's turn.

The whole street went silent. Somewhere, a cricket fainted.

Chòu Chòu squeezed his eyes shut, certain of disaster, and let out the smallest, most apologetic *poof* of his life.

The Earth God breathed in.

His eyes flew open. His white eyebrows shot up to his hat. And then — to everyone's complete astonishment — the tiny old guardian's whole face crinkled into the biggest, brightest, most delighted grin anyone had ever seen.

"OH!" he cried. "*There* it is!"

---

"There *what* is?" said Egg Waffle, nearly dropping his top hat.

The Earth God clasped his little hands. "Children," he said, "let me tell you a secret about this street." He gestured up at the swinging lanterns, the steaming woks, the shoulder-to-shoulder crowd. "Do you know why people travel from the very ends of the island to Shenkeng old street? Do you know what they smell first, from three corners away, before they even *see* the lanterns?"

Nobody breathed.

"They smell *this,*" said the Earth God, and he pointed one cheerful finger straight at Chòu Chòu. "Bold. Mighty. Unmistakable. The moment a hungry traveler catches that scent on the wind, their tummy says *we have arrived somewhere special.* That smell is the heartbeat of this whole street. That smell is *why you are all famous.*"

A murmur rippled through the crowd. The Mooncake blinked. The scallion pancake's mouth fell open. The popcorn chicken peeked out from behind the lamppost.

"But — but everyone backs away from him," squeaked a little mochi.

"Because they forgot," said the Earth God gently, "that the boldest smell is the one that *calls everyone home.*" He toddled over to Chòu Chòu and patted his crispy corner with a tiny warm hand. "You, my golden friend, are not the smell people run from. You are the smell that made this street worth running *to.*"

Chòu Chòu's puppy eyes went very wide and very shiny. A single proud sparkle-cloud floated up and — for the first time ever — the crowd leaned *in* to catch it.

The little temple-tiger gave a great big yawning "RAWR!" of approval and flopped onto Chòu Chòu's lap for a snuggle. "Rawr means hello," he purred. "Also: you smell like dinner, and that's the nicest thing I know how to say."

---

"By the power vested in me by tangerines and incense," declared the Earth God, lifting his little staff, "the OFFICIAL SMELL of Shenkeng old street is — **臭豆腐!** Stinky tofu! And its champion is Chòu Chòu!"

*BONNNG!* went the gong.

And the whole street erupted.

The popcorn chicken came bouncing out in a giggling avalanche, chanting "More crunch, more fun! More crunch, more fun!" The scallion pancake cartwheeled a victory lap. The Mooncake, being graceful and kind, floated over to shake Chòu Chòu's little hand. "I was wrong to drift away," she said warmly. "You smell like *adventure.*"

Tourists crowded close — *actually* close — holding out paper boats, begging for a bite of the famous golden cube. Chòu Chòu was so happy he could barely crunch.

Through all of it, Grass Jelly stayed right where he'd been the whole time. He gave a slow, cool nod and one tiny sip of his straw.

"Told you," he said.

Chòu Chòu flung his pickled-cabbage cape around his friend in a crispy, sparkly hug. "You stayed when nobody else would," he sniffled happily. "You're the coolest cube I know."

"Obviously," said Grass Jelly. But the corner of his mouth — just the very corner — curled into the smallest warm smile.

---

That night, when the lanterns dimmed and the last happy tourist wandered home with a belly full of golden tofu, the market hung a brand-new flag above the lane. On it was painted a cheerful little cube with puppy eyes, a heart-shaped cape, and three friendly purple sparkle-clouds.

Underneath, in proud gold letters, it read: *Follow your nose home.*

And from that night on, whenever a tired traveler caught a bold, mighty whiff drifting through the dark, they smiled and thought: *Ah. Somewhere wonderful is close.*

Chòu Chòu had been the same all along. The only thing that changed was that everyone finally got close enough to know it.

"Bold on the outside," he whispered to the stars, snuggling against Grass Jelly's cool, calm side. "Gold on the inside."

"And loved," yawned Grass Jelly, "in the middle."

---

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, the things that make you different are not the things to hide — they are the things that lead people straight to your heart. Tonight, let your boldest, truest self glow soft and warm, just like a golden cube under the lanterns. Somebody out there is following the scent of who you are, all the way home to you. So breathe slow, snuggle deep, and be proud of every wonderful piece of you. You are bold on the outside, and gold all the way through. Goodnight.
