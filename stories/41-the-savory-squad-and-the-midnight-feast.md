# Story 41: The Savory Squad & the Midnight Feast

**Main Character:** Niú Niú (Beef Noodle Soup)
**Guest Characters:** Boba, Shaved Ice
**Setting:** A pop-up street-food alley that only appears at midnight
**Theme:** Sweet and savory are better together.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 宵夜 (xiāoyè) = late-night snack

---

There is a certain alley in Taiwan that does not exist during the day. Look for it at noon and you will find only a quiet brick wall, a sleepy cat, and a pigeon who pretends he knows nothing. But when the clock tiptoes past midnight, the wall yawns, stretches, and pops open like a dumpling — and out spills the most delicious secret on the whole island: the Midnight Alley.

That night, a steaming clay bowl came rolling down the cobblestones, tossing his long wheat-noodle scarf over one beefy shoulder.

"Slurp first, worry later!" boomed **牛牛** (Niú Niú) = Beef Beef, the cozy bowl of beef noodle soup. His steam-cloud halo bobbed above him like a tiny warm weather system, and his bok-choy collar fluttered as he walked. "Tonight, kiddos, the Savory Squad opens the greatest **宵夜** (xiāoyè) = late-night snack feast this alley has ever smelled!"

Behind him marched his crew, each one crispier and saltier than the last. The whole alley smelled of five-spice and toasted sesame. It was perfect. It was glorious. It was about to go completely sideways.

Because the desserts had the exact same idea.

Niú Niú rolled to the center of the alley and skidded to a stop — *splash* — right in front of a glossy black-brown sphere bouncing the other way.

"Let's bounce — oh!" said **波霸** (Bōbà) = Boba, the curious tapioca pearl, springing back in surprise. "Niú Niú? What are *you* doing here? This corner is for the dessert feast. We booked it. We hung streamers. We brought a *fountain*."

Behind Boba towered a wobbling skyscraper of toppings wearing tiny sunglasses, even though it was, obviously, midnight.

"THIS ALLEY," announced **剉冰** (Cuòbīng) = Shaved Ice, in his usual all-capital-letters voice, "IS A SWEET ALLEY. EVERYONE KNOWS THE NIGHT MARKET RUNS ON SUGAR. WE ARE THE STARS. YOU ARE THE... SOUP."

Niú Niú's steam puffed a little hotter. "Now hold on, kiddo. You can't have a night market without something savory. Where's the *meat*? Where's the *crunch*? Folks come hungry at midnight, and you can't fill a belly with snowflakes and bubbles."

"You cannot END a night," sniffed Shaved Ice, "WITHOUT SOMETHING SWEET. NOBODY GOES HOME HAPPY ON A MOUTHFUL OF SOUP."

And just like that — *poof* — the great Sweet-vs-Savory Argument of the Midnight Alley began.

It was, the narrator must admit, a very silly argument. (Most of the best ones are.) Nobody was being *mean*, exactly. It was more the kind of squabble where everyone is so sure they are right that they forget to actually listen — the way you and your little brother might argue over whose turn it is to pick the bedtime story, when really there is plenty of night for two stories.

"Savory comes first," said Niú Niú, very reasonably, "because a tired belly needs *filling* before it needs *spoiling.*"

"Sweet comes LAST," said Shaved Ice, just as reasonably, "BECAUSE THE LAST TASTE IS THE ONE YOU FALL ASLEEP REMEMBERING."

"Those," whispered Boba to nobody, "are both actually pretty good points." But the others were too busy puffing up their chests to hear.

Niú Niú's Savory Squad set up their stalls on the left side of the alley. There was Cōng Cōng the scallion pancake, cartwheeling on his crispy edges and shouting "Let's flip the script!" There was little Xiǎo Lóng the soup dumpling, balanced nervously on his bamboo steamer, counting his eighteen pleats under his breath whenever the noise got too loud. And there was the whole giggling crew of Kǎ Kǎ, the popcorn chicken, hopping and reshuffling and chirping "More crunch, more fun!" all at once like a tiny salty popcorn orchestra.

On the right side, the desserts dug in. Boba bounced up a pyramid of pearls so wobbly that every time someone sneezed, three of them rolled off and had to bounce back into place. Shaved Ice grew taller and taller, piling on mango and red bean and a drizzle of brown-sugar syrup until he could barely see over himself.

"I AM A MOUNTAIN OF JOY," he declared.

"You're going to topple," warned Boba.

"MOUNTAINS," said Shaved Ice grandly, "DO NOT TOPPLE." (Mountains, the narrator notes, do not usually melt either, and yet.)

Streamers fluttered. A bubble-tea fountain glugged proudly, and a banner went up that read, in glittery letters, SWEET RULES THE NIGHT — which made Cōng Cōng cartwheel over and hang up his own banner directly beneath it reading SAVORY RULES IT BACK, which is not a sentence, but he was very pleased with it anyway.

"We'll have the BIGGER crowd," declared Shaved Ice.

"We'll have the *hungrier* crowd," said Niú Niú, ladling his broth.

"We'll see," said Boba, who, between us, was already starting to feel a little bad about the whole thing.

So both sides set out their finest **宵夜** and waited for the midnight crowd to choose a winner.

And waited.

And — here is the silly part — *nobody came.*

You see, word had spread through the sleepy lanes of the city that the Midnight Alley had two feasts that night. And the night-snackers of Taiwan, who are wise about these things, simply could not decide. A bowl of beef noodle soup *or* a tower of shaved ice? Crispy scallion pancake *or* bouncy boba? Impossible! Cruel! It was like being told to pick a favorite star in the whole sky.

So the crowd did the only sensible thing: they stayed home, scratching their heads, growing hungrier and grumpier by the minute.

A single brave customer *did* wander in, around one o'clock — a yawning taxi driver who looked left at the steaming soup, then right at the glittering ice tower, then left, then right, faster and faster, until he got so dizzy he simply sat down on a stool, ordered a glass of plain water, and fell fast asleep. Both sides cheered, then realized neither of them had actually sold anything, and went quiet again.

Midnight stretched into the small hours. The streamers drooped. The broth simmered with no bellies to fill. Shaved Ice, who had built himself far too tall, began — very slowly, very dramatically — to *melt.*

"I AM... A PUDDLE OF MY FORMER GLORY," he announced, sliding sideways.

Xiǎo Lóng had counted his pleats so many times he'd started over twice and accidentally counted the same fold four times, which is a thing that only happens when a soup dumpling is *very* worried. Cōng Cōng had flipped until he was dizzy and was now lying flat on his back, going "weeee" in a small, sad voice. The Kǎ Kǎ crew had stopped giggling — which, if you knew the Kǎ Kǎ crew, was the most alarming sign of all. Even Niú Niú's cozy steam-cloud had thinned to a sad little wisp, no bigger than the breath you make on a cold morning.

"This is no good, kiddo," Niú Niú sighed, looking across the alley at Boba. "A feast with nobody to feed isn't a feast at all. It's just... leftovers waiting to happen."

Boba bounced over the line that divided sweet from savory — the first one all night brave enough to do it. "I know," he said quietly. "I've been thinking. When I come home tired from bouncing around all day, you know what I want? First something warm and savory to fill me up. And *then*..." He glanced back at the melting dessert side. "...something sweet to send me off to bed happy."

Niú Niú's friendly face brightened. His steam puffed up warm and round again. "Both," he said. "Not one *or* the other."

"Both," agreed Boba.

"BOTH?" came a wobbly voice. Shaved Ice oozed over, half his height gone, his sunglasses crooked. "YOU MEAN... I DON'T HAVE TO WIN? BECAUSE, BETWEEN US, I AM NOT WINNING. I AM LEAKING."

So the two sides did something braver than arguing. They pushed their stalls together.

Cōng Cōng cartwheeled across the alley and high-fived Boba with a floppy pancake hand — *smack!* Kǎ Kǎ's giggling crew hopped over and tucked themselves between the dessert bowls like crunchy little hats. Xiǎo Lóng, blushing, set his steamer right next to the brown-sugar fountain, careful, *so* careful, not to spill. And Niú Niú rolled his big warm bowl into the very center of the alley, where sweet met savory, and tossed his noodle scarf high into the air.

"NEW PLAN, kiddos!" he boomed. "We don't run two feasts. We run *one.* The greatest **宵夜** the Midnight Alley has ever seen — savory *and* sweet, side by side. You come hungry, you leave happy, and you don't have to pick a single thing."

Boba bounced onto the rim of Niú Niú's bowl like a town crier. "SPREAD THE WORD!" he called into the dark lanes. "One feast! Everything! All of it! Nobody has to choose!"

And oh, did the crowd come *then.*

They poured into the Midnight Alley from every sleepy direction — yawning night-owls, hungry travelers, students who should have been studying, grandmas who could not sleep, even the sleepy cat and the pigeon who pretended to know nothing. (The taxi driver woke up, looked around at the new feast, and ordered one of *everything.*)

They slurped Niú Niú's broth until their cheeks glowed, then bounced over for Boba's pearls. They crunched Cōng Cōng's pancakes, then cooled their tongues on what was left of Shaved Ice. Xiǎo Lóng got to show off all eighteen of his perfect pleats to an admiring grandma, who said they were the neatest folds she had ever seen, and he turned so pink with pride he nearly spilled. The Kǎ Kǎ crew got their giggles back at full volume, hopping into bowls and out again and chirping "More crunch, more fun!" until the whole alley rang with it. They went savory, then sweet, then savory again, laughing the whole time, and not one single person had to choose.

"SLURP FIRST," roared Niú Niú happily, "DESSERT LATER!"

"I make everything sweeter!" sang the brown-sugar syrup, drizzling a swirl right onto a popcorn-chicken nugget. (Surprisingly delicious. Trust the weird.)

And one by one, in the joyful midnight glow, the snacks of the Savory Squad and the desserts of the dessert crowd got their proudest wish of all: to be *eaten.* Niú Niú's beef cubes vanished into a happy belly with a satisfied *aah.* Boba's pearls bounced down a giggling straw like fireworks finally getting to light the sky. Each one beamed, glad and gobbled and gloriously full of purpose, because there is no happier ending for a midnight snack than a happy, full friend.

When the last belly was full and the last lantern dimmed, Niú Niú and Boba sat together at the center of the alley — one warm, one sweet — watching the sleepy crowd shuffle home, hand in hand, smiling.

"Sweet and savory," Niú Niú murmured, his steam-halo cozy once more.

"Better together," yawned Boba.

And as the wall slowly yawned shut to hide the alley until tomorrow's midnight, even the grumpy pigeon had to admit: it had been the most perfect feast in the history of feasts. Because a perfect night, it turns out, has room for everything — and everyone.

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, the day has had its salt and the evening has had its sugar, and now it is time for both to rest. May your dreams be warm like noodle broth and soft like a bubble of boba, with room enough for every flavor of who you are. You do not have to choose between being one thing or another — you, my darling, are allowed to be wonderfully, deliciously *both.* Snuggle in close, where it is cozy and full and kind. Goodnight.
