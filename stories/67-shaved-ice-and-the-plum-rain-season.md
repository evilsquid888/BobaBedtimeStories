# Story 67: Shaved Ice & the Plum Rain Season

**Main Character:** Shaved Ice (剉冰, towering fluffy ice tower of toppings, dramatic, melts, sunglasses, ALL CAPS)
**Guest Characters:** Aiyu
**Setting:** A drippy night market during Taiwan's plum-rain season
**Theme:** Even drama queens shine brightest helping others.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 下雨 (xià yǔ) = to rain

---

"IT IS RAINING," announced Shaved Ice, from beneath the awning, in the voice of someone announcing the end of the world. "It is **下雨** (xià yǔ) = it is *raining*, and it has been **下雨**-ing for ELEVEN DAYS, and I am pretty sure the sky has forgotten how to be a sky."

She struck a tragic pose. She was very good at tragic poses. Shaved Ice was a towering, fluffy mountain of crushed snow, crowned with wobbling jewels of mango, red bean, and grass jelly, with two tiny sunglasses perched bravely on top despite the fact that there had not been a single ray of sun in nearly two weeks.

"Nobody," she declared, "wants a COLD treat in soggy weather. Nobody! I am a summer star, and this is the **梅雨** (méi yǔ) = *plum-rain* season, and I am simply going to MELT into a puddle of forgotten dreams."

A drip landed on her nose. "See? It has begun."

---

The night market sat under the gray, generous sky, and the rain came down soft and silver and endless. Plum-rain season was like that — not a stormy, shouty rain, but a patient one, the kind that hums to itself all day long. The lanterns glowed pink and orange through the wet. Puddles caught the light and held it like little ponds full of stars.

But the food stalls were quiet. The customers had stayed home with their umbrellas and their warm slippers. And in the middle of it all, Shaved Ice slowly, dramatically, sloped to one side.

"I'm sliding," she gasped. "I'm sliding into oblivion. Tell my mango I loved it. Tell my red bean it was always my favorite — no, don't tell grass jelly that, he'll be smug."

"You're not sliding, darling. You're slouching." A gentle, honest voice drifted up from below. "Slouching is fixable."

Shaved Ice peered down past her own snowy slopes. There, glowing faintly golden in a little bowl, sat Aiyu — a translucent, amber-gold cube of aiyu jelly, wobbling shyly in a puddle of lemon-water. Aiyu was so see-through you could read a menu right through her, and she always looked a little embarrassed about it.

"Aiyu!" Shaved Ice flung out her arms. "You found me in my final hour!"

"It's eight in the evening," said Aiyu kindly.

"My final HOUR," repeated Shaved Ice, with feeling. "Nobody wants us, Aiyu. The rain won't stop. Cold treats are doomed. We are out of season, out of luck, and very nearly out of mango."

Aiyu wobbled closer. She was shy — so shy she usually let other people do the talking — but tonight there was a tiny, brave spark in her amber middle. "Can I tell you something true?" she asked. "I'm cold and wobbly too. But I've been watching everyone hide indoors, all soggy and gloomy. And I keep thinking... what they really want isn't *sun*. It's *cozy*."

Shaved Ice paused mid-collapse. "Cozy," she said slowly, as if tasting a strange new flavor.

"Cozy," nodded Aiyu, blushing right through her own transparent cheeks. "And you, Shaved Ice — you are the most *dramatic* person I have ever met. Which means... you might also be the most *wonderful* at parties."

---

Now, here is a thing about drama queens, dear reader, and you may write it on your wall: a drama queen who is sad is a puddle, but a drama queen with a PROJECT is a firework.

Something lit up behind Shaved Ice's tiny sunglasses.

"A party," she whispered. Then, louder: "A PARTY." Then, at a volume that startled a passing dumpling clean off his bamboo steamer: "THE COZIEST RAINY-DAY PARTY THIS MARKET HAS EVER SEEN!"

"That's the spirit," said Aiyu, hopping a little. Or wobbling. With Aiyu it was hard to tell.

"But I'll need help," Shaved Ice said, suddenly serious, sunglasses slipping. "I can be a star. I cannot be a star *and* a stagehand. That's just physics."

So the two of them set off through the drizzle, splashing from stall to stall, gathering the soggy and the gloomy.

They found **Niú Niú** (the warm clay bowl of beef noodle soup) huddled under his awning, his noodle-scarf damp and drooping. "Slurp first, worry later, kiddo," he sighed, "but nobody's come to slurp."

"Then you, my steaming friend, are PERFECT," cried Shaved Ice. "We need warmth! We need steam! We need a cozy cloud of your gorgeous noodle-fog!"

Niú Niú perked right up, his steam-halo puffing proud. "I do make excellent fog," he admitted.

They found **Hú Jiāo** the pepper bun, who was so glum he'd stopped popping out of his clay oven. "Fresh from the fire... full of *damp*," he mumbled. But when Shaved Ice explained the plan, his rosy cheeks went rosier, and he sneezed a happy little burst of pepper. "A FIRE!" he gasped. "We light the oven! Everyone gathers round the warm! It's what ovens are FOR!"

They found Mango (sporty, sweatbanded, sulking under a leaf) and Red Bean (warm and grandmotherly, ladle in hand) and Grass Jelly (cool, calm, deadpan, in his little black beanie, pretending he hadn't been bored to tears).

"A rainy party," said Grass Jelly flatly. A long pause. "...Fine. I'm in. But only because I have a beanie and beanies are *cozy season's* whole thing."

And so the plan came together, the way the best plans do — messily, loudly, and with everybody talking at once.

---

The market filled with delicious busyness. Hú Jiāo and Niú Niú built a warm steamy corner where the heat curled up to meet the rain and made the air feel like a hug. Red Bean set her ladle going, warming sweet soup. Mango raced (because Mango did everything as a race) to string the lanterns lower, so they glowed close and golden, turning the whole drippy market into something secret and snug.

And in the very center stood Shaved Ice — but not as a melting mountain anymore. Oh no.

"BEHOLD," she boomed, "the NEW menu! It is **下雨** season, my darlings, so we LEAN IN! We do not fight the rain — we INVITE it!"

She'd had an idea so dramatic it could only have come from her. Instead of icy-cold towers nobody wanted in the cold, she invented the **燒冰** (shāo bīng) = *warm-and-cold ice* — a clever little tower of soft shaved ice nestled right beside warm sweet red-bean soup and a drizzle of hot brown sugar, so that every bite was cool-then-cozy, snow melting against warmth, like a snowball that loved you back.

"It's the rain and the warm, TOGETHER," she explained, gesturing grandly. "Cold on top, cozy underneath! Just like THIS WHOLE NIGHT!"

And Aiyu — shy, transparent, brave little Aiyu — hopped right into the dessert herself, glowing gold in the lantern light. "And aiyu jelly with honey-lemon," she added in her small voice, "because... because lemon tastes like sunshine. And on a day with no sun, somebody ought to bring the sunshine themselves."

The whole market went quiet at that. Then it went *loud* — the good kind of loud, the cheering kind.

---

They came. They actually came! One by one, doors creaked, umbrellas bloomed, and the residents of the night market splashed out into the drizzle, drawn by the warm glow and the laughter and the impossible, marvelous smell of warm-and-cold dessert.

Soggy little Pudding wobbled in and immediately tripped into a puddle and got applauded for it. The popcorn-chicken crew bounced through the rain shouting "MORE CRUNCH, MORE FUN!" and shook themselves dry like a fistful of tiny golden dogs. Youtiao dove headfirst into a passing puddle just to see if it counted as a dunk (it did not, but he tried). Everyone laughed.

And Shaved Ice — well. Shaved Ice was in her element at last. She told stories. She did her tragic poses on purpose now, for laughs. She introduced each dessert like a queen presenting knights. She did, at one point, melt a *little* — but she only struck a pose and cried, "Even STARS must dim to let others shine!" and everyone cheered, and Red Bean topped her back up with a fresh scoop of snow.

Near the end of the night, when the rain had gone soft and dreamy and the lanterns swam in their puddles, Aiyu wobbled up beside her friend.

"You did it," Aiyu said. "You threw the coziest party the market's ever seen."

"*We* did it," said Shaved Ice — and for once, she said it quietly. "You knew. You knew the secret. It was never about the sun, was it? It was about being the warm thing for someone else."

Aiyu glowed, gold and shy and glad. "Even drama queens," she said, "shine brightest when they're shining *for* somebody."

Outside, it kept on **下雨**-ing, soft and silver and kind. And nobody minded at all.

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, the rain is only the sky giving the whole world a long, gentle bath, so that everything wakes up clean and dewy and new. Tonight, let the soft *pitter-pat* on the window be your lullaby, and know that even the grayest days hold a cozy party waiting to be made. You don't need sunshine to be someone's warmth — a kind word, a shared snack, a hand held in the drizzle will do. So snuggle deep beneath your blanket-mountain, like a happy little tower of snow, and let the plum-rain hum you down to sleep. May your dreams be warm-and-cold and just exactly right. Goodnight.
