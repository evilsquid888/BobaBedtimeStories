# Story 65: Douhua & the Beitou Hot Spring

**Main Character:** Douhua (Tofu Pudding)
**Guest Characters:** Hú Jiāo (Pepper Bun)
**Setting:** A steamy Beitou hot-spring bathhouse in the hills
**Theme:** Be soft, be still, let your worries melt.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 溫泉 (wēnquán) = hot spring

---

The little bus puffed up the green hills of Beitou, and inside it, everyone was a complete and total mess.

Mango had a leg cramp from racing the bus on foot. Shaved Ice was MELTING and announcing it loudly. And Hú Jiāo, the pepper bun, was so wound up that little puffs of smoke kept shooting out of his sesame-topped head, and every few seconds he sneezed a tiny cloud of pepper that made everybody go *ah-CHOO* like a row of dominoes.

"Are we THERE?" Hú Jiāo demanded, bouncing in his seat. "Is it relaxing yet? I need to relax IMMEDIATELY. I am extremely behind on my relaxing!"

From the front, a silky, wobbly voice answered like a slow ripple on a pond.

"Relaxing," said Douhua, the tofu pudding, "is not a race, dear pepper bun. You cannot win it by going faster."

"Watch me," said Hú Jiāo. And he sneezed again. *Ah-CHOO.*

---

Douhua was a gentle white wobble in a bowl of warm ginger syrup, so smooth and so jiggly that she rippled when she breathed. She had spent the whole island road trip being the calm one — the friend who hummed when others shouted, who paused when others panicked. And tonight she had brought the whole tired, frazzled gang to the one place she loved most in the entire world.

The bus doors hissed open, and there it was.

A **溫泉** (wēnquán) = hot spring, tucked into the misty Beitou hills like a secret. Steam curled up from stone pools in soft, ghostly ribbons. The air smelled of warm minerals and old quiet trees. Wooden lanterns glowed amber through the fog. Somewhere, water trickled with a sound like a lullaby that had been playing since before anyone was born.

"Ohhh," said Mango, his sweatband already drooping.

"OH," said Shaved Ice, who for once spoke in a normal, hushed voice instead of ALL CAPS, because even he could feel that this was a place for whispering.

But Hú Jiāo just zoomed forward, smoke trailing behind him like a tiny worried comet.

"Great great great," he said, "where's the relaxing pool, I'll do all my relaxing right now, get it over with, very efficient." He skidded to the edge of the steaming water. "Fresh from the fire, full of heart! I am ALREADY full of heart! Why is my heart going so FAST?"

"Because," Douhua said softly, wobbling up beside him, "you are trying to relax the way you do everything else. With your whole engine running."

Hú Jiāo's rosy cheeks puffed. "I don't have an engine."

A little jet of steam shot out of his ears.

"...That's just the oven warming up," he mumbled.

Douhua smiled the kind of smile that doesn't move much but warms a whole room.

---

The pool master that night was a familiar friendly face — **土地公** (Tǔdì Gōng) = the Earth God, a tiny white-bearded grandfather spirit no taller than a teapot, in a little red temple-hat. He shuffled around in fuzzy slippers, handing everyone warm towels and a lucky tangerine candy.

"Bless this little corner of the island," he chuckled, patting Hú Jiāo's crusty head. "Ho ho! This one's hot enough to bake bread on. Soak, soak, little bun. The water knows what to do."

Then he toddled off to nap under his table, where a chubby clay temple-tiger named Xiǎo Hǔ Yé was already snoring big yawn-roars that fogged up the windows.

One by one, the friends slipped into the steaming pools. Mango sighed as his cramp dissolved. Shaved Ice floated on his back like a happy cloud. Mochi went so soft and stretchy he nearly slid off the bench. Even Taro lowered himself in with a wise, contented "Ahhh," his reading glasses fogging completely over.

Everyone melted.

Everyone except one little pepper bun, who sat in the water as stiff as a brick, gripping the edge with both hands, eyes wide open.

"Am I doing it?" he asked. "Is this it? I don't feel relaxed. I feel like I'm sitting very still while NOTHING HAPPENS, which is honestly a little stressful, when's the fun part —"

*Ah-CHOO.* A whole pepper-cloud burst out of him and floated across the **溫泉**, and the entire pool went *ah-CHOO, ah-CHOO, ah-CHOO* down the line.

"Bless you," said Douhua. "All of you."

---

Douhua wobbled over and settled into the water beside the little bun. The ginger syrup in her bowl swirled and steamed, mixing gently with the mineral mist.

"Hú Jiāo," she said, in her soft, poetic, ginger-syrup way. "Do you know why I am soft?"

"Because you're tofu pudding?"

"Because I learned to be," she said. "When I was made, they poured me hot. Bubbling. Furious. If I had stayed that hot and hard, I would only ever be... lumps. But I let myself rest. I let myself cool just enough. And in the stillness — *that* is when I became silky. The soft only comes after the still."

Hú Jiāo blinked. A single, smaller puff of steam came out of him. "That's... kind of beautiful and kind of weird."

"Most true things are," Douhua agreed, with a tiny ripple of a laugh.

"But I can't just *stop*," Hú Jiāo whispered. "If I stop, all the worries pile up. The festival's coming and I'm not crispy enough and what if my crust cracks wrong and my pepper-heart isn't juicy enough and what if I sneeze at the worst possible moment —"

"You will," said Douhua warmly. "You absolutely will sneeze at the worst possible moment. That is simply who you are. And we will love you anyway, and we will all go *ah-CHOO* together, and it will be the funniest part of the whole festival."

Hú Jiāo opened his mouth to argue. Then closed it. Then, very slowly, his shoulders came down about half an inch.

"...Huh," he said.

---

"Now," said Douhua. "We breathe. The water teaches it. Listen."

The hot spring went *trickle... trickle... trickle.* Slow. Patient. The mist rose and fell like the whole hillside was sleeping and waking, sleeping and waking.

"Breathe in like the steam rising," Douhua murmured. "Hold it soft. And let it go like the mist drifting down the mountain."

Hú Jiāo breathed in.

A little jet of pepper threatened to come out — but he caught it, soft, and let the breath go slow instead. The smoke around his head thinned. Then thinned more. The fierce orange glow in his cheeks faded to a cozy, gentle pink, like the last warm coal in a fireplace just before bed.

"Again," whispered Douhua.

In like steam. Out like mist.

In like steam. Out like mist.

Hú Jiāo's grip on the edge loosened. His back, which had been clenched like a fist all day, all week, all *trip*, finally — softly — unclenched. He sank down until the warm water reached his chin. His eyes went heavy.

"Whoa," he breathed. "I think... I think my worries are... leaking out my edges."

"That's the steam doing its work," Douhua said. "Letting them go. You don't have to throw your worries away, little bun. You just have to stop holding them so tight. The warm water will carry them off for you. That's what a **溫泉** is for."

A worry-shaped wisp of pepper-steam drifted up off of Hú Jiāo, floated lazily over the pool, and dissolved into the lantern-light. Then another. Then another. Each one melting away into the misty dark like it had never been heavy at all.

"It's working," Hú Jiāo whispered, amazed. "I'm being soft. I'm being STILL." He paused. "...I almost said that in capital letters, but I made it quiet. Did you see? Did you SEE — see. Did you see."

"I saw," said Douhua, rippling with the gentlest laugh. "Beautifully done."

---

For a long, lovely while, nobody said anything at all.

The whole gang floated in the warm Beitou steam — Mango snoozing, Shaved Ice drifting, Mochi melted into a happy puddle of himself, Taro fogged-up and dreaming. The Earth God snored softly. The clay tiger's tail twitched. The lanterns glowed. The water trickled its forever lullaby.

And in the middle of it all, one little pepper bun who had never once in his whole hot-headed life sat still — sat still. His pepper-heart, which had been pounding so fast all trip, slowed down to match the trickle of the spring. Tick. Trickle. Tick. Trickle. Soft. Warm. Easy.

"Douhua?" he said, very sleepily.

"Mm?"

"Thank you for not telling me to relax."

"I would never," she said. "Telling someone to relax is the least relaxing thing in the world."

Hú Jiāo giggled — a real, soft, melty giggle, with no smoke at all. And then, because the warm **溫泉** had finally done its gentle work, the little pepper bun who could never sit still drifted off into the coziest, steamiest, most worry-free nap of his entire life, his rosy cheeks glowing soft as embers banked for the night.

Douhua tucked a warm towel over him so he wouldn't get a chill.

"Soft," she whispered, "comes after still. Sweet dreams, little fire. You've earned your rest."

And the steam rose, and the lanterns glowed, and the whole tired, happy, melted-down gang of friends slept in the warm hills of Beitou, with all their worries gently steaming away into the gentle mountain night.

---

## Goodnight Blessing
Little one, the day was loud and full and fast, and that is alright — you do not have to win at resting. Let your shoulders come down. Let your worries melt away into the warm dark like steam off a quiet spring. Breathe in soft as rising mist, and let it go slow as it drifts back down the mountain. You are soft, you are still, and you are exactly enough. Sleep now, warm and easy. Goodnight.
