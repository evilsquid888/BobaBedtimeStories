# Story 87: Douhua & the Still Temple Pond

**Main Character:** Douhua (Tofu Pudding)
**Guest Characters:** Hú Jiāo (Pepper Bun)
**Setting:** A serene lotus pond in a quiet old temple garden at dusk
**Theme:** In stillness, you can hear what matters.
**Mandarin Spotlight:** 安靜 (ānjìng) = quiet / peaceful

---

The old temple garden was the kind of place where even the crickets used their indoor voices. Lanterns blinked on one by one, the sky went the soft purple of plum jelly, and at the center of it all sat a lotus pond so smooth it looked like a mirror that had decided to take a nap.

Floating in the middle of that pond, in a tiny ginger-syrup boat shaped like a soup spoon, was Douhua.

She was a silky white wobble of tofu pudding, so soft she jiggled when a moth flew past. She drifted with her eyes half-closed, humming a tune that had no words because words felt like too much effort for such a lovely evening.

"Ahhh," she sighed, wobbling gently. "**安靜** (ānjìng) = quiet. The pond is quiet. The moon is quiet. I am quiet. We are all being quiet *together*, which is the very best kind of quiet."

That was when something came crashing through the bamboo gate like a meatball fired from a slingshot.

"INCOMINGGGG!"

*SPLOOSH.*

---

A round, sesame-topped bun with rosy cheeks and a crackly clay-oven crust belly-flopped straight into the lily pads, sending water everywhere. He surfaced, spluttering, with a lotus leaf stuck to his head like a very fancy hat.

"Oh no oh no oh NO," he gasped. "Did I miss it? Is it gone? Did the worry get me? Wait — where am I? Who are *you*? Why is it so — so —"

He looked around, blinked, and lowered his voice to a horrified whisper.

"— so *quiet*?"

This was Hú Jiāo, the pepper bun, fresh from the fiery clay oven three streets over. He was juicy and peppery and warm, with a scallion heart that beat very fast, and right now he was vibrating like a kettle about to whistle.

"AH-CHOO!" He sneezed, and a tiny puff of black pepper shot out of his nose. "Sorry. I sneeze pepper when I'm nervous. And surprised. And Tuesdays. Is it Tuesday?"

Douhua paddled her spoon-boat over very slowly, because Douhua did everything slowly, the way honey leaves a jar.

"It is not Tuesday," she said softly. "It is dusk. And you, little pepper friend, are *loud*."

"I KNOW!" wailed Hú Jiāo, then clapped his own hands over his mouth. "I know," he whispered. "I can't help it. I've got a worry, you see. A great big worry, and the worry makes me loud, and the louder I get, the bigger the worry gets, and the bigger it gets the louder I — AH-CHOO!"

Another puff of pepper. A frog on a nearby leaf sneezed back politely.

---

Douhua tilted her wobbly head. "What is the worry?"

"That's just it!" Hú Jiāo flailed, sending a juicy spray of pepper-broth into the air. "I don't *know*! It's a sneaky worry. It hides. Every time I try to look at it, I'm too busy being loud, and it scoots away. So I run faster and yell louder hoping to *catch* it, and that's how I ended up cannonballing into your beautiful pond. Which, by the way — lovely pond. Ten out of ten. Very wet. Smells faintly of ginger. I approve."

He paused to peel the lotus leaf off his head and inspect it like a tiny green map.

"I've tried *everything*," he went on, voice climbing. "I ran three laps around the temple. I sang the whole night-market jingle backwards. I tried to outrun it down Tangerine Alley and knocked over a noodle cart — sorry, Niú Niú! — and *still* the worry kept up with me, panting along behind like a little shadow that's been eating too many gua bao. You can't outrun a shadow, you know. I've checked. Many times. Tonight, specifically."

Douhua looked at him for a long, gentle moment. Then she did something nobody had ever done to Hú Jiāo before.

She said nothing at all.

The silence stretched. A lantern flickered. A lotus petal drifted down and landed on the water without a sound.

Hú Jiāo squirmed. "Why aren't you talking? Are you mad? Did I break the pond? Should I say something? I feel like I should say something. Hello? HELLO?"

"Shhh," whispered Douhua, and she said it so kindly, so warmly, that it didn't feel like being hushed at all. It felt like being tucked in. "Climb aboard, little one. Sit with me. We will be **安靜** together. And we will not chase your worry."

"We won't?" Hú Jiāo clambered into the spoon-boat, which dipped and wobbled. "Then how will we *catch* it?"

Douhua smiled her soft, silky smile.

"We will not catch it. We will let it come to us."

---

So they sat.

For the first time in his whole toasty life, Hú Jiāo tried to be still.

It was *terrible*.

His crust itched. His pepper-heart raced. His feet wanted to tap, his hands wanted to flap, and a thousand thoughts buzzed in his head like a market full of bees. *What if I'm bad at being quiet? What if the quiet doesn't work? What if a fish bites my bottom? Is that a fish? That's PROBABLY a fish — what if the moon falls in the pond — what if I forget how to breathe — wait, am I breathing? I should check —*

He fidgeted. He jiggled the boat. He drummed his little fingers on the spoon's edge, *tappity-tappity-tap*, until a sleepy turtle surfaced nearby, gave him a long flat stare, and sank slowly back down again, clearly unimpressed.

"Sorry," Hú Jiāo whispered to the turtle. The turtle did not answer. The turtle was *excellent* at being quiet.

"AH —" he began.

Douhua, very gently, placed one soft jiggly hand on his shoulder.

She didn't say *stop*. She didn't say *hush*. She just wobbled, slow and calm, like a little white moon. And somehow, Hú Jiāo's "ah-choo" got lost on the way out and turned into a long, slow breath instead.

"There," Douhua whispered. "When you go silky, the world goes silky too. Listen. Not *for* anything. Just listen."

So Hú Jiāo listened.

At first he heard only his own thumping heart. But the longer he sat, the more the heartbeat slowed — and underneath it, sounds he'd been too loud to ever notice began to float up out of the dusk.

He heard a lotus bud unfold with a *whisper*, soft as a turning page.

He heard the moon's reflection ripple, *plink*, when a dragonfly kissed the water.

He heard the temple wind chimes — not clanging, but *singing*, three notes apart, gentle as a grandmother humming in the next room.

He heard the incense smoke curling up from the temple doorway, a sound so soft it was almost a smell — warm and tangerine-sweet.

He heard, far away, the whole night market breathing in its sleep: the *tick* of cooling ovens, the slow drip of a tap, a lullaby someone was humming three streets over without knowing anyone was listening at all.

"Oh," breathed Hú Jiāo. His rosy cheeks went rosier. "Oh, it's... it's like a whole secret song. It was here the *whole time*."

"It is always here," said Douhua. "But it only sings for those who are **安靜** enough to hear it."

---

They drifted. The boat turned slow circles. And the longer Hú Jiāo stayed still, the more something deep inside him — something he'd been outrunning all day — began to rise. Slowly. Gently. Like a single bubble lifting up through silky tofu.

His lip wobbled. His crackly crust crinkled.

"Douhua," he said, very small, "I think... I think the worry's coming up. I can feel it. It's right here."

"Let it come," she said. "Bubbles always float. That is their whole job."

Hú Jiāo closed his eyes. And the worry — the great big sneaky worry — finally, *finally* floated up where he could see it.

"Tomorrow," he whispered, "is the Mid-Autumn Festival. And the whole night market does a big reunion feast. Everyone has somebody. Lǔ Ròu has his rice. Guā Bāo folds everyone into hugs. Niú Niú has his noodle-scarf. And I... I just got pulled out of the oven this *morning*. I don't have anybody yet. I was so scared of sitting still and feeling all alone that I just kept running and yelling so I'd never have to hear how quiet *I* really am inside."

There it was. The worry, out in the open at last, floating on the moonlit water like a leaf.

And here is the funny thing about worries, kiddo: the moment Hú Jiāo *said* his out loud, in the quiet, it stopped being so big. Out in the silence, where he could finally see all of it, it looked rather small. Almost cuddly. Almost silly.

---

Douhua wobbled closer until they were shoulder to shoulder in the little spoon-boat.

"Little pepper friend," she said softly. "Do you hear that?"

Hú Jiāo listened. "Hear what? I don't hear anything."

"Exactly. The silence. And yet — are you alone in it?"

Hú Jiāo looked around the still, glowing pond. At the moon. At the lotus. At the silky white pudding beside him, warm as a candle.

"...No," he said slowly. A smile crept across his crust. "No, I'm not. *You're* here."

"And I will be at the reunion feast tomorrow," said Douhua. "Right beside you. And so will the frog, probably, and the wind chimes, and the moon, which shows up for *every* reunion whether it is invited or not, the show-off." She giggled, a sound like syrup glugging. "You were never alone, Hú Jiāo. You were just too loud to notice the company."

Hú Jiāo wiped a happy little tear off his sesame seeds. "But what do I *bring* to a reunion feast? Everyone brings something. Lǔ Ròu brings warmth. Niú Niú brings his scarf. What does a fresh-baked pepper bun bring?"

Douhua thought about this, slow as the moon climbing.

"You bring your fire," she said at last. "You are warm and peppery and a little bit fizzy with feelings, and the world needs a snack like that. But more than your fire, little one — you bring your *quiet*, now. You know how to listen. That is the rarest gift at any feast. Most snacks talk and talk and never hear a thing. But you? You will sit beside the lonely ones and the loud ones and the lost ones, and you will be **安靜** with them, and they will feel, for the very first time, truly heard."

Hú Jiāo's crust glowed pink. "Truly?"

"Truly," said Douhua. "Now you have two gifts. That is one more than the moon, and the moon never stops bragging."

Hú Jiāo laughed — really laughed, a warm peppery chuckle — and for once it did not feel like running away from anything. It felt like coming home.

"Slurp first, worry later, as a wise noodle bowl says," he grinned. "Or in my case... *sit* first, worry later."

"Now you understand," said Douhua. "**安靜** is not empty. It is the most full place there is. It is just full of *quiet* things — and quiet things are the ones that matter most."

They floated together on the still temple pond as the lanterns glowed and the moon climbed higher, two little snacks on a spoon-shaped boat, not making a single sound, and perfectly, wonderfully happy about it.

And Hú Jiāo did not sneeze pepper even once more that night.

## Goodnight Blessing
May your evening grow soft and silky, little one, smooth as a moonlit pond. When your worries feel loud and chase-y, may you find a quiet spoon-boat to climb into and a gentle friend to sit beside you. Let your big thoughts float up slow, like bubbles, until they drift away light as lotus petals on the water. Listen now — not for anything, just listen — to the hush that loves you, the breath in your chest, the soft and certain quiet that means you are safe and you are home. You were never alone in the stillness; the moon and the chimes and all the dreaming snacks are keeping you company. Be **安靜**, sweet one, and let the silence sing you to sleep. Goodnight.
