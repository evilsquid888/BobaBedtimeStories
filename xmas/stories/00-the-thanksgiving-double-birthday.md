# 00 — The Thanksgiving Double Birthday

**Day:** Thanksgiving, the prologue before door one
**Celebrating:** Thanksgiving, Yeh Yeh's birthday, and Cat's birthday, all at one table
**Friends:** Yeh Yeh the clay teapot (new), Lina the snowflake crisp (new), Gabby the black star shark (new), Kyle the black bear cub (new)
**Frostpeak guest:** Piper Paddlefoot ❄️, with an invitation
**Cat’s role:** birthday penguin and noodle patrol (rides her snowboard under the long noodle, catching every droop)
**Mandarin:** 感恩節 Thanksgiving · 慢慢來 take it slow · 長壽麵 long-life noodle · 生日快樂 happy birthday
**Setting:** Rosie's home on Thanksgiving: the dining room, the front hall, the steamy kitchen, and Rosie's bedroom at night

---

## The Story

It was **感恩節** (Gǎn'ēn jié) = Thanksgiving. Rosie's house smelled like cinnamon and roast pumpkin.

Today was three parties in one. Rosie had a list.

One: Thanksgiving. Two: Cat the penguin's birthday. Three: Yeh Yeh's birthday.

Mom stirred a pot. Tori folded napkins into swans. Mostly swans. One was a potato.

"Three parties, one table," said Rosie. Her straw wobbled. "What if I mix them up?"

"Then we eat cake with gravy," said Cat. "I've had worse."

Ding-dong! Lina the snowflake crisp hopped in. Poof! Sugar snow puffed everywhere.

"I brought decorations," said Lina. "Also, I am one."

Next came Kyle the black bear cub, hugging a pie as big as a drum.

One slice was missing. "I was taste-testing," said Kyle. "It tastes like more."

Then Gabby the black star shark swam in, right through the air, as star sharks do.

She smiled her widest smile. So many teeth! "Don't worry. I only bite pie."

Last came Yeh Yeh, Rosie's grandpa, a round old teapot. Tap, tap, tap.

"**慢慢來** (màn man lái) = take it slow," he chuckled. "Slow tea is the best tea."

Mom lifted the pot lid. Inside was Yeh Yeh's birthday **長壽麵** (chángshòu miàn) = long-life noodle.

One very, very long noodle. "The longer the noodle, the longer the life," said Mom.

"So nobody breaks it," said Tori. "And nobody bites it. Kyle."

Carrying it took everybody. Rosie held the front. Tori and Lina held the middle.

Gabby carried her part in her teeth, softer than a kiss. Kyle drooled politely.

Cat zoomed underneath on her snowboard, catching every droop. "Noodle patrol!"

Halfway there, the noodle snagged on a coat hook. It stretched thin. Thinner.

Everybody pulled a different way. The noodle hummed like a guitar string.

Gabby held very, very still. Her eyes went as round as dinner plates.

"If it breaks," whispered Kyle, "may I eat it?" "No, Kyle," said everybody.

"Everybody freeze!" said Rosie. "Now tiny steps. All together. Ready? Step."

Step. Step. Yeh Yeh unhooked the noodle with his stick. Not one break.

The noodle curled into Yeh Yeh's bowl like a sleepy golden snake.

"**生日快樂** (shēngrì kuàilè) = Happy birthday, Yeh Yeh!" He slurped it in one go. It took a while.

Then Tori carried in Cat's cake, a snowy coconut mountain with one blue candle.

Cat closed her eyes. "I wish my family could see my home, Frostpeak."

She blew. Off flew Lina's sugar snow, all over Kyle. He licked his nose.

Then came the thankful round. "Seconds," said Kyle.

"Friends who see my smile, not my teeth," said Gabby.

"A full table," said Mom. "One more year and one more noodle," said Yeh Yeh.

"My brave little sister," said Tori. "A family that let a penguin move in," said Cat.

Rosie looked around the table. "All of you," she said.

WHOOSH! Something shot down the banister and landed in the napkin swans.

It was Piper Paddlefoot, Cat's friend from Frostpeak, on a glittery snowboard. "Slide into it!"

Piper held out a snowy envelope and one paper lantern, folded flat.

"Come to Frostpeak for December," said Piper. "Twenty-four days. Twenty-four little doors."

Cat's birthday wish had come true before the cake was cut.

"Is there food at Frostpeak?" asked Kyle. "Always enough fish for one more," said Piper.

Tori squeezed Rosie tight. "Three parties, zero mix-ups. You've got this, little sip."

Rosie ticked her list. Thanksgiving. Birthday. Birthday. And then, at last, there was pie.
