# 🎄 24 Days of Xmas

One cozy five-minute video story for each day from December 1 to 24, plus a Thanksgiving prologue. **Rosie**, **Cat the penguin**, **Tori**, and **Mom** spend December at Frostpeak Lodge, Cat's home valley, with the [Penguinsnowboard](https://github.com/evilsquid888/Penguinsnowboard) cast. Each morning one little door on the lodge's Door Wall opens. Each dusk one more paper lantern is hung on Lantern Lane. On the 24th the whole lane is lit for **SpicySquid's birthday**.

Along the way: snowboard lessons, a wonky tree, Hanukkah with Herschel the Walrus and Noa the jelly doughnut, boots by the door, latkes beside scallion pancakes, a crown of lights, Las Posadas with friends from Costa Rica, day trips on the Market Train to the Christmas markets of Montreal and Quebec City, tangyuan for the winter solstice with Yeh Yeh, and the northern lights over Gabby the black star shark.

## How every story works

- **Five minutes, read aloud.** 500–580 story words plus a 4-line blessing (≤ 40 words); 14–20 stills; 36–48 beats of ≤ 18 words. Same budget and pipeline as Life Lessons.
- **The family is in every video.** Rosie, Cat, Tori, and Mom are each drawn at least once (the builder refuses a story without them).
- **The door.** December stories open at the Door Wall: today's door, who opens it, and the small object or carved picture behind it.
- **The Market Train.** On four days (9, 12, 17, 18) the door holds a tiny golden train ticket: the snow train runs to the Christmas markets of Montreal or Quebec City and is home by nightfall, lantern in hand. Those stories sprinkle a little French, in the same format as the Mandarin.
- **The adventure.** One new place in Frostpeak Valley (or the city), one or two Frostpeak guests with their catchphrase and physical gag, a laugh in every scene, and usually something going funnily wrong.
- **The lantern.** At dusk the day's paper lantern is hung on Lantern Lane. Somebody says **"One more light."**
- **Goodnight.** The last shot is the lodge bunk room at night, with two small things from the day on the nightstand, under the Goodnight Blessing.

These are holiday fun stories, not lessons: fun things happen, and it is warm. No morals said out loud, no steps, no Rose breath. Playful and cozy; nothing scary, sad, or dark; jokes never at Rosie's expense; nobody gets eaten. Holidays are hosted by the friends who keep them, explained in a line or two, and the family joins in as welcome guests (details in the [Character Guide](CHARACTER_GUIDE.md)).

## Writing a new day

Stories are built from compact specs so the narration lives in one place and the storyboard can never drift from it.

```
xmas/
├── specs/NN_slug.py     ← write this: the narration, shots, and prompts for one day
├── tools/cast.py        ← every character's token, ID phrase, and reference design
├── tools/places.py      ← the recurring places: Door Wall, Lantern Lane (day and night), bunk room
├── tools/build.py       ← spec → stories/NN-slug.md, with the pipeline rules enforced
├── tools/qa.py          ← series QA across every spec
└── stories/NN-slug.md   ← generated; edit the spec, then rebuild
```

```
python xmas/tools/build.py "xmas/specs/NN_*.py"
python lifelessons/check_storyboard.py xmas/stories/NN-*.md
python xmas/tools/qa.py --terms --repeats
```

The first two must print no FAIL and no warn lines. The third is the series QA (structure, continuity, prompt hazards, repeated jokes, terms to proofread): no FAIL, and read each warn. On Windows set `PYTHONUTF8=1` first. The exemplar is [`specs/00_thanksgiving.py`](specs/00_thanksgiving.py).

A spec is one `STORY` dict:

- `num`, `slug`, `title`; `meta`, the header lines (Day, Holiday thread, Guests, Cat’s role, Mandarin, Setting).
- `palette`, the colors in the video style anchor; `cast`, the tokens drawn in this story; `cast_note`; `key_shot`, the number of the still used as the sample key scene.
- `places`: each with `name` (follows the camera angle in the still), `scene` (the 3–6 word version for beats), `props` (4–6 things that could only be there), `light` (a named source), `accents` (two colors), `atmo` (one atmospheric touch), and `motion` (the same touch as a bare clause, the ambient motion in every beat there).
- `shots`: each with `title`, `place`, optional `"time": "night"`, `angle` (a camera angle ending in a preposition), `who` (every character in frame by TOKEN with a pose and a feeling), and 1–3 `beats`.
- A beat is `(narration, framing, action, who, feeling, camera, audio)`. The narration is the story: read in order, the beats are **The Story**, and the last shot's beats are the blessing. `who` is the token (or list of tokens) the action is about. `camera` is one of: pushes in, pulls back, pans left, pans right, tilts up, tilts down, tracks beside, holds still.
- `blessing` (four lines).

Rules the builder enforces, each learned the hard way in the Life Lessons pipeline:

- Stills name characters by token only, and only characters in the still appear in its beats.
- `CAT the little blue penguin` in every still with Cat; the story says "Cat the penguin" once.
- No quoted words, signs, or lettering in stills: doors show carved pictures, cards show doodles.
- No negations in stills (no, not, never, without). Say what is there.
- A night shot needs a night word in its still; a day shot must have none (night, moon, bedtime, star, evening, dusk, asleep, sleep, night-light). By day a star is "a five-pointed shape" and dusk is "late golden sun".
- Rosie stays a cup: she stands, hops, holds things in front of her cup, sits on an edge. In a pose-risky still, spell her out (`ROSIE, a small clear cup of pink milk tea with a dome lid and a striped straw,`).
- Round characters rock, bounce, and wobble; exits are held poses; one action per beat.
