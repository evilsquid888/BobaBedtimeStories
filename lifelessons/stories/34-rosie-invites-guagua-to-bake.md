# 34 — Rosie Invites Guagua to Bake

**Life skill:** Make a clear invitation through grown-ups and accept a declined date.
**Magic sentence:** "Would you like to bake with me on Saturday?"
**Coaches:** Tama Onigiri 🇯🇵 + Tico the Toucan 🇨🇷
**Cat’s role:** helper
**Setting:** the garden pergola snack cart (practice), then the school pickup shelter (real outing).

---

## The Story

Rosie wanted to invite Guagua to bake muffins, with Mom helping and enough paper cups for everyone.

At the pergola cart, she practiced naming Saturday, rather than saying a mysterious and enormous sometime.

Tico wore a muffin cup on his head. "Beak the news! This invitation includes an extremely small chef."

Tama was a well-prepared triangular rice ball.

Tico's rainbow beak was impressively large.

Cat the penguin sorted muffin cups. One stuck to her beak and became a very short chef's hat.

Tama checked the pretend plan, then asked Tico, **"Would you like to bake with me on Saturday?"**

"An invitation offers a choice," Tama explained. "A friend can like you and still have another plan."

**Rosie's Steps**

1. Check the plan with your grown-up.
2. Name the activity and day.
3. Ask your friend once.

4. Listen to the answer.
5. Let grown-ups arrange any plans.

Rosie practiced hearing yes and hearing no, while Mom explained that grown-ups would organize the visit details.

Tico removed his muffin hat. "Saturday is open. My headwear is already fully booked."

Mom accompanied Rosie to the school pickup shelter, where Guagua waited with his grown-up beneath the covered roof.

Cat carried the recipe picture to school pickup, without assuming Guagua would accept the invitation.

Rosie held a paper muffin cup beside her lid, remembering the baking plan Mom had approved.

Step one: Rosie checked with Mom again. Saturday's baking invitation was still possible at their home.

Step two: she named the activity and day. Step three: **"Would you like to bake with me on Saturday?"**

Step four: she listened. Guagua looked pleased, then remembered he was visiting his grandparents that day.

"I can't on Saturday," Guagua said. Rosie had pictured him stirring the bowl beside her at home.

She felt disappointed. Her straw drooped. That's okay; the busy day was not an answer about their friendship.

Rosie took a Rose breath.

Smell the rose: in, two, three, four.

Blow out the candle: out, two, three, four.

"Okay. Would you like our grown-ups to find another day?" Rosie asked, leaving room for his choice.

"Yes, please," said Guagua. His grown-up smiled at Mom, and they agreed to check their schedules later.

Step five: Rosie let the grown-ups arrange any visit, instead of promising a time she had not checked.

She thanked Guagua for answering and saved an extra muffin cup for whichever day they might choose.

Tama packed the cups. Tico examined his hat. "Fortunately, this fashion works every day."

Cat held up the muffin cups. "They can wait until another day. My beak would appreciate a proper hat."

Rosie giggled. Their friendship still had room for another day.

Tori met Rosie beside the shelter's rain gutter planter, where a leaf held one bright leftover raindrop.

"You invited kindly and heard his answer. You've got this, little sip," she said, admiring the muffin cup.

Rosie felt proud. A good invitation made room for the other person's life as well as her own wish.

**加油** (jiāyóu) = go for it.

"One step at a time, one sip at a time," said Rosie.

## 🌹 Rosie's Steps (Recap)

1. Check the plan with your grown-up.
2. Name the activity and day.
3. Ask your friend once.
4. Listen to the answer.
5. Let grown-ups arrange any plans.

**If something surprising happens:** Take a Rose breath or a comfortable pause, then say: "Okay. Would you like our grown-ups to find another day?"

## 💬 Practice Together (for grown-ups)

- Role-play the situation using a paper muffin cup or a safe pretend substitute; read the five steps together.
- Swap roles so your child can see you model **"Would you like to bake with me on Saturday?"** and hear the other person's answer.
- Introduce this story's small surprise; practice "Okay. Would you like our grown-ups to find another day?" and allow a comfortable breath or pause.
- On a real outing, stay nearby, offer choices, and praise the specific step your child tried.
