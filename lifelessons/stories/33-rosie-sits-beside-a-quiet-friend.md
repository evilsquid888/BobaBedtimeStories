# 33 — Rosie Sits Beside a Quiet Friend

**Life skill:** Offer support to an upset friend without demanding details or touch.
**Magic sentence:** "Would you like company or some space?"
**Coaches:** Hóng Dòu 🇹🇼 + Estrella 🇨🇷
**Setting:** the building's fern stairwell bench (practice), then the butterfly garden pavilion (real outing).

---

## The Story

Rosie was meeting Lulu at the butterfly garden, but wondered what to do if her friend felt quiet.

On the stairwell bench, Hóng Dòu held a picture book while Estrella folded her flippers close.

"Must I make somebody happy?" Rosie asked. "Offer care without controlling their feelings," Hóng Dòu said.

Hóng Dòu was a grandmotherly red bean.

Estrella was a turtle with kind eyes.

Cat the penguin napped on a fern-print cushion.

Estrella looked quiet. Hóng Dòu asked, **"Would you like company or some space?"** She listened and sat nearby.

"Ask before a hug or advice," Estrella explained. "Sometimes sitting quietly is the help a friend chooses."

**Rosie's Steps**

1. Notice your friend's feeling.
2. Ask what would help.
3. Listen to their answer.

4. Respect their choice.
5. Get a trusted grown-up if needed.

Hóng Dòu placed her ladle down. "Even my soup advice is waiting for an invitation today."

Mom took Rosie to the butterfly pavilion, where nectar pots stood beside mesh curtains and shaded benches.

Cat settled onto a bench cushion, becoming the garden's only blue visitor with absolutely no plans to flutter.

Lulu sat beneath a butterfly picture. She looked down, keeping her little hands together beside her jelly body.

Step one: Rosie noticed Lulu looked upset. She did not guess why or ask her to stop crying.

Step two: **"Would you like company or some space?"** Step three: she waited to hear Lulu's answer.

"Company, but no talking yet," Lulu said. Rosie sat nearby with a comfortable space between them.

Lulu's usual place was beside Rosie, but today she asked for a little more room on the bench.

Rosie felt unsure. Her straw drooped. That's okay; Lulu's need for space was about this moment.

Rosie took a Rose breath.

Smell the rose: in, two, three, four.

Blow out the candle: out, two, three, four.

"Is this space comfortable?" Rosie asked after shifting along the bench, keeping her picture book closed.

Lulu nodded. Rosie stayed quietly beside her while Mom remained nearby, watching over both little friends.

Step four: Rosie respected Lulu's choice, offering neither questions nor an uninvited hug.

Step five: when Lulu wanted Mom, Rosie called her over. Mom listened, and Rosie stayed nearby.

Hóng Dòu nodded. Estrella rested her flippers. The ladle remained silent: a remarkable achievement for soup equipment.

Cat opened one eye. "Quiet company is my specialty. My bench and I have an excellent understanding."

Lulu gave a small smile. Rosie stayed beside her, glad that helping could be gentle and unhurried.

Tori met Rosie beside the pavilion's butterfly mosaic, where one tiny blue tile looked rather like Cat.

"You listened to her choice. You've got this, little sip," Tori said in a soft friendly voice.

Rosie felt proud of giving care without trying to rush her friend's feelings into a different shape.

**加油** (jiāyóu) = go for it.

"One step at a time, one sip at a time," said Rosie.

## 🌹 Rosie's Steps (Recap)

1. Notice your friend's feeling.
2. Ask what would help.
3. Listen to their answer.
4. Respect their choice.
5. Get a trusted grown-up if needed.

**If something surprising happens:** Take a Rose breath or a comfortable pause, then say: "Is this space comfortable?"

## 💬 Practice Together (for grown-ups)

- Role-play the situation using a small picture book or a safe pretend substitute; read the five steps together.
- Swap roles so your child can see you model **"Would you like company or some space?"** and hear the other person's answer.
- Introduce this story's small surprise; practice "Is this space comfortable?" and allow a comfortable breath or pause.
- On a real outing, stay nearby, offer choices, and praise the specific step your child tried.
