# 37 — Rosie Packs Her Own Outing Bag

**Life skill:** Pack from an agreed checklist and ask for help with missing essentials.
**Magic sentence:** "Can we check my bag together?"
**Coaches:** Tama Onigiri 🇯🇵 + Pock Ramune 🇯🇵
**Setting:** the guest bedroom luggage bench (practice), then the nature center welcome deck (real outing).

---

## The Story

Rosie wanted to pack her own bag for a nature-center outing, using the picture list Mom had made.

At the luggage bench, she gathered a bottle, a hat, and a folded cloth in a tray.

Pock fizzed beside the backpack. "My essential item is enthusiasm. Unfortunately, it does not replace a water bottle."

Tama was a well-prepared triangular rice ball.

Pock was a soda bottle fizzing happily.

Cat the penguin napped on a soft empty packing cube.

Tama gathered one item at a time, then asked Pock, **"Can we check my bag together?"** before closing it.

"A checklist remembers while you concentrate," Tama said. "Your grown-up helps choose its items."

**Rosie's Steps**

1. Look at your picture checklist.
2. Gather one item at a time.
3. Put each item in your bag.

4. Ask about anything missing.
5. Check the bag with your grown-up.

**準備** (zhǔnbèi) = preparation. Rosie tried **"Can we check my bag together?"** Pock Ramune pretended.

1. Look at your picture checklist.
2. Gather one item at a time.
3. Put each item in your bag.

4. Ask about anything missing.
5. Check the bag with your grown-up.

Rosie practiced matching each picture to a real item, keeping the bag open until Mom had checked it.

Tama unpacked three spare hats. "I packed a little extra, just in case the trail has more heads."

Mom and Rosie reached the nature center's welcome deck, where trail pictures hung beside the water station.

Cat napped on a folded blanket on the bench, packed for the outing with exceptionally good priorities.

Before their supervised walk, Rosie checked the backpack she had packed at the luggage bench.

Step one: she looked at the checklist. Step two: she remembered gathering each item separately from her tray.

Step three: the items were inside her bag. **"Can we check my bag together?"** she asked Mom.

They compared the pictures with the bottle, hat, and cloth before choosing the short trail they would follow.

The water bottle was in the bag, but it was empty. Packing it had not filled it.

Rosie felt surprised. Her straw drooped. That's okay; checking could find something useful before they needed it.

Rosie took a Rose breath.

Smell the rose: in, two, three, four.

Blow out the candle: out, two, three, four.

"Please help me get this ready," Rosie said, showing Mom the empty bottle instead of hiding the mistake.

Mom helped fill it at the water station and checked the lid. Rosie added a filled-bottle picture to remember.

Step four: Rosie asked about the missing water. Step five: Mom checked and closed the bag with her.

Rosie kept the pack comfortably fitted while Mom chose their safe route and stayed beside her on the deck.

Pock fizzed happily. Tama repacked the hats. "Prepared for three heads. Proud of one careful cup."

Cat yawned beneath the blanket. "Please check my list. Penguin, scarf, cushion. Everything essential has arrived."

Rosie giggled, then held Mom's hand as agreed. Their outing could begin with a bag that was ready.

Tori met Rosie at the nature center's carved-leaf gateway, where she admired the little picture checklist.

"You checked and asked for help. You've got this, little sip," she said, pointing at the bottle picture.

Rosie felt proud of packing carefully, and of noticing what still needed to be ready.

**加油** (jiāyóu) = go for it.

"One step at a time, one sip at a time," said Rosie.

## 🌹 Rosie's Steps (Recap)

1. Look at your picture checklist.
2. Gather one item at a time.
3. Put each item in your bag.
4. Ask about anything missing.
5. Check the bag with your grown-up.

**If something surprising happens:** Take a Rose breath or a comfortable pause, then say: "Please help me get this ready."

## 💬 Practice Together (for grown-ups)

- Role-play the situation using a small picture checklist or a safe pretend substitute; read the five steps together.
- Swap roles so your child can see you model **"Can we check my bag together?"** and hear the other person's answer.
- Introduce this story's small surprise; practice "Please help me get this ready." and allow a comfortable breath or pause.
- On a real outing, stay nearby, offer choices, and praise the specific step your child tried.

## 🌙 Goodnight Blessing

Goodnight, ready bag and little picture list.
Goodnight, bottle, filled for another adventure.
Goodnight, Cat, completely essential.
May careful beginnings lead to gentle days.
