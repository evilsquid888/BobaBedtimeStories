# 49 — Rosie Helps the Parade Team Choose Jobs

**Life skill:** Allocate team roles fairly by hearing preferences and checking agreement.
**Magic sentence:** "Which job would each of us like?"
**Coaches:** Gallo 🇨🇷 + Pinto 🇨🇷
**Setting:** the neighborhood club's folding wagon store (practice), then the parade float preparation barn (real outing).

---

## The Story

Rosie and Tang were preparing a parade float. Flowers and ribbons needed careful little hands.

At the wagon store, Gallo wore a ribbon around his palm hat. Pinto balanced a foam flower on his spoon.

"Better together, mae," Pinto said. "Provided we know who is doing what before the ribbon becomes lunch."

Gallo was a cheerful rice grain in a palm hat.

Pinto was a glossy black bean carrying a spoon.

Cat the penguin napped on a folded wagon-seat cushion.

Gallo showed the pretend jobs and asked Pinto, **"Which job would each of us like?"** They listened before choosing.

"Different jobs have different needs," Pinto explained. "Hear preferences, then agree on a fair arrangement."

**Rosie's Steps**

1. Name the jobs that need doing.
2. Ask each person what they prefer.
3. Listen to every answer.

4. Agree on a fair way to choose.
5. Check that everyone knows their job.

**合作** (hézuò) = teamwork. Rosie tried **"Which job would each of us like?"** Pinto pretended.

1. Name the jobs that need doing.
2. Ask each person what they prefer.
3. Listen to every answer.

4. Agree on a fair way to choose.
5. Check that everyone knows their job.

Rosie practiced naming the jobs, asking each helper, and checking that the chosen task was clear.

Gallo pointed at his ribbon hat. "Rise and pinto! I have accidentally volunteered my head as the decoration department."

Mom brought Rosie and Tang to the barn, with its dragon float, flower crates, and ribbon spools.

Cat dozed on a padded wagon seat: a small passenger providing a very well-rested service.

Mom named two jobs: sorting foam flowers and pairing ribbon bundles. Grown-ups handled the float.

Step one: Rosie named both jobs. Step two: **"Which job would each of us like?"** she asked Tang.

Step three: she listened. Tang wanted to pair ribbons. Rosie wanted ribbons too, especially the bright scarlet ones.

Step four: Rosie suggested turns at both jobs, if Mom said that would work for the preparation.

Mom explained that the ribbons needed one helper to finish a batch before swapping. An immediate switch would mix them.

Rosie felt disappointed. Her straw drooped. That's okay; a fair plan also needed to fit the real task.

Rosie took a Rose breath.

Smell the rose: in, two, three, four.

Blow out the candle: out, two, three, four.

"Can we swap after the first batch?" Rosie asked, checking with both Tang and Mom before choosing a role.

Tang began with ribbons, Rosie with flowers. Mom would show them when their batches were ready.

Step four: everyone agreed to the batch-by-batch swap. Rosie listened to how her first flower-sorting job worked.

Step five: she checked her job: match flowers in this crate. Tang explained his ribbon pairing too.

Gallo straightened his hat. Pinto lifted his spoon. "Better together. Each department has located its breakfast ingredient."

Cat peeked from the padded wagon seat. "My assigned job is passenger. I have studied the role thoroughly."

Rosie laughed and matched a flower. After their first batches, both helpers would try the other job.

Tori met Rosie at the dragon-head display and admired the neatly sorted flowers.

"You listened and made a workable team plan. You've got this, little sip," she said with a proud nod.

Rosie felt pleased. Different jobs could still contribute to one shared celebration.

**加油** (jiāyóu) = go for it.

"One step at a time, one sip at a time," said Rosie.

## 🌹 Rosie's Steps (Recap)

1. Name the jobs that need doing.
2. Ask each person what they prefer.
3. Listen to every answer.
4. Agree on a fair way to choose.
5. Check that everyone knows their job.

**If something surprising happens:** Take a Rose breath or a comfortable pause, then say: "Can we swap after the first batch?"

## 💬 Practice Together (for grown-ups)

- Role-play the situation using a little role-picture card or a safe pretend substitute; read the five steps together.
- Swap roles so your child can see you model **"Which job would each of us like?"** and hear the other person's answer.
- Introduce this story's small surprise; practice "Can we swap after the first batch?" and allow a comfortable breath or pause.
- On a real outing, stay nearby, offer choices, and praise the specific step your child tried.

## 🌙 Goodnight Blessing

Goodnight, little team and carefully chosen jobs.
Goodnight, flowers and bright ribbon bundles.
Goodnight, Cat, professional passenger.
May working together leave room for every voice.
