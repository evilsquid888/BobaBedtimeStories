# 06 — Rosie Asks to Play at the Park

**Life skill:** Joining play with a new kid at the park — and handling "no thanks" kindly.
**Magic sentence:** "Hi! Can I play with you?"
**Coaches:** Mango (Taiwan) + Dai Daruma (Japan) + Tato the Patacon (Costa Rica)
**Setting:** Rosie's cozy kitchen (practice), then a sunny neighborhood park with swings, a slide, and a sandbox — where Cat the penguin naps at the bottom of the slide

---

## The Story

Rosie the Rose Milk Tea had a big new plan. She wanted to play with a new kid at the park.

"But the kids are already playing," she said. "I don't know the words." Her pink-striped straw drooped.

WHOOSH! Mango zoomed into the kitchen, sweatband on, dripping golden juice. "Catch me if you can — I'm in season!" He stopped. "Wait. Why are we here?"

"To help Rosie," said Dai Daruma. He wobbled — boing — and stood right back up.

Tato the Patacon tripped over the rug and landed flat. "Smash now, worry never! I meant to do that."

Rosie giggled.

"Joining play has steps," said Mango.

**Rosie's Steps**
1. Watch first. See what the kids are playing.
2. Walk close. Smile.
3. Say, **"Hi! Can I play with you?"**
4. If they say yes, ask, "What are the rules?" Then take turns.
5. If they say no, say, "Okay!" Then find something else to play.
6. When you are done, say, "Thanks for playing!"

"Sometimes kids say no," said Dai. "Usually they are busy with their own game. It is not about you. Fall down seven, bounce up eight!"

"Practice time!" said Mango. He bounced a ball. "I'm a kid at the park."

Rosie practiced:
1. Watch first. See what the kids are playing.
2. Walk close. Smile.
3. Say, **"Hi! Can I play with you?"**
4. If they say yes, ask, "What are the rules?" Then take turns.
5. If they say no, say, "Okay!" Then find something else to play.
6. When you are done, say, "Thanks for playing!"

"Yes!" said Mango. They took turns. Mango bounced it off his own head. "Point for the ceiling!"

"Now practice a no," said Dai. "Tato, say 'no thanks.'"

"YES!" shouted Tato. "Oops. I love playing too much. My mouth won't say no!" Everyone laughed, even Dai.

The next day, the whole team went to the **公園** (gōngyuán) = park. Cat the penguin came too. Cat was staying with Rosie's family. She napped like a cat, which is how she got her name. She fell asleep at the bottom of the slide.

Two egg tart twins were on the swings. Step one. Rosie watched. Step two. She walked close and smiled. Step three. "**Hi! Can I play with you?**"

"No thanks," said one twin. "We're busy."

This was not like practice. Tato always said yes! Rosie felt surprised and a little sad. Her straw drooped all the way down. That's okay.

Rosie did a Rose breath. Smell the rose — in, two, three, four. Blow out the candle — out, two, three, four.

Step five. "Okay!" she said in a friendly voice.

"That was brave," whispered Mango. "Brave is a sport."

In the sandbox sat Lulu the Almond Jelly, all by herself. Step one: Rosie watched. Step two: she walked close and smiled. Step three: "**Hi! Can I play with you?**"

"Yes! I'm making a castle."

Step four. "What are the rules?" asked Rosie.

"I fill the bucket. You pat it flat."

They took turns. The castle grew four towers. Tato sat on one. "Now it's a flat tower. Very fancy."

Lulu's mom called. Step six. Rosie said, "Thanks for playing!"

"Bye, **朋友** (péngyǒu) = friend!" said Lulu.

Tato slid down the slide and bumped Cat. "Five more minutes," she mumbled.

That night, Tori called. "You made a friend! **加油** (jiāyóu) = you can do it — and you DID."

"One step at a time," Rosie said sleepily. "One sip at a time."

## 🌹 Rosie's Steps (Recap)
1. Watch first. See what the kids are playing.
2. Walk close. Smile.
3. Say, **"Hi! Can I play with you?"**
4. If they say yes, ask, "What are the rules?" Then take turns.
5. If they say no, say, "Okay!" Then find something else to play.
6. When you are done, say, "Thanks for playing!"

**If something surprising happens:** Rose breath, then say: "Okay!" — and try something else or someone else. A "no" is not about you.

## 💬 Practice Together (for grown-ups)
- Role-play with a ball: you are the kid at the park. First say "Yes!" so your child can ask "What are the rules?" and take turns. Then say "No thanks, I'm busy" (kindly!) so they can practice a Rose breath and a friendly "Okay!" Swap roles so they hear how "no" sounds from the other side.
- Praise the "no" practice even more than the "yes": "You said 'Okay!' in such a friendly voice, and you tried again. That was brave." Let your child be Dai Daruma — tip over and bounce back up.
- Put a stuffed toy "Cat the penguin" napping at the bottom of a pretend slide (a cushion) for a giggle — and let a second toy, playing alone, be the one who says yes.
- At a real park, do step one together out loud: "What are they playing?" Kids playing alone (sandbox, digging, a ball by the wall) are often the easiest first try. Afterward, talk about what went well, even if someone said no.

## 🌙 Goodnight Blessing
Goodnight, little sip, with your six small steps.
Goodnight, swings, swaying slow in the dark.
Goodnight, Cat, dreaming of slides.
Some days yes, some days no. You are wonderful either way.
