# 06 — Rosie Asks to Play at the Park

**Life skill:** Joining play with a new kid at the park — and handling "no thanks" kindly.
**Magic sentence:** "Hi! Can I play with you?"
**Coaches:** Mango (Taiwan) + Dai Daruma (Japan) + Tato the Patacon (Costa Rica)
**Setting:** Rosie's cozy kitchen (practice), then a sunny neighborhood park with swings, a slide, and a sandbox

---

## The Story

Rosie the Rose Milk Tea had a big new plan. She wanted to play with a new kid at the park.

"But the kids are already playing," she said. "I don't know the words." Her pink-striped straw drooped a little.

WHOOSH! Mango zoomed into the kitchen, sweatband on, dripping golden juice on the floor. "Catch me if you can — I'm in season!" He ran three laps around the table. Then he stopped. "Wait. Why are we here?"

"To help Rosie," said Dai Daruma. He was round and red, with big bossy eyebrows and one painted eye. He wobbled — boing — and stood right back up.

Behind him, Tato the Patacon tripped over the rug and landed flat. "Smash now, worry never!" he said from the floor. "I meant to do that."

Rosie giggled. Her straw wiggled a tiny bit.

"Joining play has steps," said Mango. "Six of them. Listen up, team!"

**Rosie's Steps**
1. Watch first. See what the kids are playing.
2. Walk close. Smile.
3. Say, **"Hi! Can I play with you?"**
4. If they say yes: ask, "What are the rules?" Then take turns.
5. If they say no: say, "Okay!" in a friendly voice. Then find something else or someone else to play with.
6. When you are done, say, "Thanks for playing!"

"Step five is my favorite," said Dai. "Sometimes kids say no. Usually they are busy with their own game. It is not about you. You say 'Okay!' and you try again." He tipped over — whump — and wobbled back up. "Fall down seven, bounce up eight!"

Rosie wrote the steps on a card. Then she read them out loud:

1. Watch first. See what the kids are playing.
2. Walk close. Smile.
3. Say, **"Hi! Can I play with you?"**
4. If they say yes: ask, "What are the rules?" Then take turns.
5. If they say no: say, "Okay!" in a friendly voice. Then find something else or someone else to play with.
6. When you are done, say, "Thanks for playing!"

"Practice time!" said Mango. He bounced a ball. "I'm a kid at the park."

Rosie watched. Mango was bouncing a ball. She walked close. She smiled. "**Hi! Can I play with you?**"

"Yes!" said Mango.

"What are the rules?" asked Rosie.

"I bounce it to you. You bounce it to me."

They took turns. Bounce. Bounce. Mango bounced it off his own head. "Point for the ceiling!"

"Thanks for playing!" said Rosie.

"Now practice a no," said Dai. "Tato, you are the kid. Say 'no thanks.'"

Tato nodded hard. Rosie watched, walked close, and smiled. "**Hi! Can I play with you?**"

"YES!" shouted Tato. "Oops. I love playing too much. My mouth won't say no!"

They tried three more times. Tato said "YES!" every single time. Everyone laughed, even Dai.

The next day, the whole team went to the **公園** (gōngyuán) = park. The sun was warm. The slide was shiny.

Two egg tart twins were on the swings. Rosie did step one. She watched. They were swinging high and counting. Step two. She walked close and smiled. Step three.

"**Hi! Can I play with you?**"

"No thanks," said one twin. "We're busy." Swoosh went the swings.

This was not like practice. Tato always said yes! Rosie felt surprised and a little sad. Her straw drooped all the way down. That's okay.

Rosie did a Rose breath. Smell the rose — in, two, three, four. Blow out the candle — out, two, three, four.

Dai rolled up beside her. He tipped over — whump — wobbled, and bounced back up. "They are busy with their own game," he whispered. "It is not about you."

Rosie remembered step five. "Okay!" she said in a friendly voice. The twins waved as they swung.

"That was brave," whispered Mango. "Brave is a sport."

Tato gave a big thumbs-up and fell into a bush. "I'm fine! **¡Vamos!** That means 'let's go!'"

Rosie looked around. In the sandbox sat a little almond jelly named Lulu, patting sand with a tiny bucket. All by herself.

Step one: Rosie watched. Lulu was building. Step two: Rosie walked close and smiled. Step three: "**Hi! Can I play with you?**"

Lulu looked up. "Yes! I'm making a castle."

Step four. "What are the rules?" asked Rosie.

"I fill the bucket. You pat it flat. Then we switch."

They took turns. Fill. Pat. Flip. The castle grew four towers and a moat. Tato tried to help and sat on one tower. "Now it's a flat tower," he said. "Very fancy." Lulu laughed so hard her jelly jiggled.

When Lulu's mom called her, Rosie did step six. "Thanks for playing!"

"Thanks for playing!" said Lulu. "Bye, **朋友** (péngyǒu) = friend!"

Rosie's straw wiggled so hard it spun.

That night, Tori called on her tiny phone. "I heard you built a castle AND made a friend! You've got this, little sip. **加油** (jiāyóu) = you can do it — and you DID."

Rosie snuggled into bed. "One step at a time," she said sleepily. "One sip at a time."

## 🌹 Rosie's Steps (Recap)
1. Watch first. See what the kids are playing.
2. Walk close. Smile.
3. Say, **"Hi! Can I play with you?"**
4. If they say yes: ask, "What are the rules?" Then take turns.
5. If they say no: say, "Okay!" in a friendly voice. Then find something else or someone else to play with.
6. When you are done, say, "Thanks for playing!"

**If something surprising happens:** Rose breath, then say: "Okay!" — and try something else or someone else. A "no" is not about you.

## 💬 Practice Together (for grown-ups)
- Role-play with a toy or ball: you are the kid at the park. First say "Yes!" and let her ask "What are the rules?" and take turns. Then say "No thanks, I'm busy" (kindly!) so she can practice a Rose breath and a friendly "Okay!" Swap roles so she hears how "no" sounds from the other side.
- Praise the "no" practice even more than the "yes" practice: "You said 'Okay!' in such a friendly voice, and you tried again. That was brave." Let her be Dai Daruma — tip over and bounce back up.
- If "**Hi! Can I play with you?**" feels like too many words, "That looks fun! Can I play?" works just as well. Pick one and practice only that one.
- At a real park, stand together and do step one out loud: "What are they playing?" Kids playing alone (sandbox, digging, a ball by the wall) are often the easiest first try. Afterward, talk about what went well, even if someone said no.

## 🌙 Goodnight Blessing
Goodnight, little sip, with your card of steps.
Goodnight to the swings, swaying slow in the dark.
Goodnight to the castle with four sandy towers.
Some days they say yes, and some days they say no —
and you are still wonderful either way.
Fall down seven, bounce up eight… and now, sleep tight.
