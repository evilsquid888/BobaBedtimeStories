# 40 — Rosie Respects a Different Favorite

**Life skill:** Respond kindly when another person likes different food or activities.
**Magic sentence:** "We can like different things and still be friends."
**Coaches:** Bīng 🇹🇼 + Lala 🇨🇷
**Cat’s role:** hip-hop dancer
**Setting:** the pantry's picnic basket alcove (practice), then the fruit festival tasting pavilion (real outing).

---

## The Story

Rosie loved mango. She imagined Guagua would choose it too at the tasting festival.

At the pantry alcove, Bīng arranged picture cards into a tower of magnificent and entirely imaginary desserts.

Lala picked a coconut card. "Favorites can differ," she said. "My heart is mostly cake, and also very friendly."

Bīng arrived, shaved ice wearing enormous sunglasses.

Lala was a soft cake wearing cream.

Cat the penguin picked a hip-hop practice rhythm. Bīng preferred a slower one; both could have a turn.

Bīng heard Lala's different choice. **"We can like different things and still be friends,"** he said warmly.

"Explain your taste without calling theirs disgusting," Lala added. "Nobody needs to eat something just to agree with you."

**Rosie's Steps**

1. Listen to the other person's choice.
2. Say your own choice kindly.
3. Avoid insulting their choice.

4. Ask what you can enjoy together.
5. Let each person keep their preference.

**尊重** (zūnzhòng) = respect. Rosie tried **"We can like different things and still be friends."** Lala pretended.

Rosie practiced saying she liked mango while her pretend friend chose coconut, then found something they both enjoyed.

Bīng examined his enormous picture tower. "More is more! Except agreement, which is apparently not a required topping."

Mom accompanied Rosie to the tasting pavilion, checking food choices and ingredients before any little bowls were offered.

Cat used a tiny silent heel tap beside the picnic seat, leaving everyone's food choices alone.

Guagua chose a fruit different from Rosie's mango. They sat at a shaded table with their own clean spoons.

Step one: Rosie listened to his choice. Step two: she said she liked the sweetness of her mango.

Step three: she kept her words kind. **"We can like different things and still be friends."** Guagua nodded.

Step four: Rosie asked what else they both enjoyed. Guagua pointed toward the pavilion's colorful fruit-picture puzzle.

Guagua did not want to taste Rosie's mango. She had expected him to enjoy one bite beside her.

Rosie felt disappointed. Her straw drooped. That's okay; liking him did not require him to share her taste.

Rosie took a Rose breath.

Smell the rose: in, two, three, four.

Blow out the candle: out, two, three, four.

"You don't have to try mine," Rosie said, leaving both bowls separate and keeping their clean spoons apart.

Mom nodded. Rosie finished her own choice while Guagua enjoyed his, with neither friend explaining the other into agreement.

Step five: each friend kept their preference. Then they tried the fruit-picture puzzle together.

They matched round shapes and long shapes, enjoying an activity that did not need matching favorite flavors.

Lala admired the puzzle. Bīng inspected a card. "A wonderful topping. Unfortunately, cardboard is unsuitable for dessert."

Cat stopped her heel tap. "Different rhythms, different fruit bowls. Still the same lovely afternoon." Guagua grinned.

Rosie giggled. Their different bowls still belonged at the same picnic.

Tori met Rosie beside the pavilion's giant painted fruit sculptures, asking which puzzle shape had been trickiest.

"You kept your words kind. You've got this, little sip," she said, hearing about the two different bowls.

Rosie felt proud. Friendship had room for different favorites, including a penguin's favorite cushion.

**加油** (jiāyóu) = go for it.

"One step at a time, one sip at a time," said Rosie.

## 🌹 Rosie's Steps (Recap)

1. Listen to the other person's choice.
2. Say your own choice kindly.
3. Avoid insulting their choice.
4. Ask what you can enjoy together.
5. Let each person keep their preference.

**If something surprising happens:** Take a Rose breath or a comfortable pause, then say: "You don't have to try mine."

## 💬 Practice Together (for grown-ups)

- Role-play the situation using a small fruit picture card or a safe pretend substitute; read the five steps together.
- Swap roles so your child can see you model **"We can like different things and still be friends."** and hear the other person's answer.
- Introduce this story's small surprise; practice "You don't have to try mine." and allow a comfortable breath or pause.
- On a real outing, stay nearby, offer choices, and praise the specific step your child tried.

Goodnight, spoons, resting side by side.
Goodnight, Cat, dancing heels now quiet.
