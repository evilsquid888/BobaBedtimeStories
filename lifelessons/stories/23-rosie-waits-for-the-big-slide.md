# 23 — Rosie Waits for the Big Slide

**Life skill:** Wait in a visible queue and check uncertainty without pushing.
**Magic sentence:** "Is this the end of the line?"
**Coaches:** Taro 🇹🇼 + Tico the Toucan 🇨🇷
**Setting:** the townhouse side passage (practice), then the indoor adventure play hall (real outing).

---

## The Story

Rosie wanted to try the play hall's big slide, which curved like a ribbon over padded flooring.

In the side passage, Tico arranged baskets into a pretend line. His beak occupied two basket spaces.

"Does waiting take forever?" Rosie asked. Taro adjusted his glasses. "Sometimes it only feels like several breakfasts."



Tico arrived, his rainbow beak impressively large.

Cat the penguin napped on a folded picnic cushion.

Taro asked Tico, **"Is this the end of the line?"** He stood behind him and waited for his turn.

"Leave enough space for bodies," Taro explained. "Your turn arrives without needing to nudge it from behind."

1. Find the end of the line.
2. Ask if you are unsure.
3. Stand behind the last person.

4. Wait with space between people.
5. Go when it is your turn.

Rosie practiced finding the last chalk footprint, then waited while Tico took his pretend turn on the stool.

Tico gave a dramatic little bow. "Beak the news! This slide is short enough to finish before lunch."

Mom stayed beside Rosie inside the play hall, where foam towers rose behind a broad curving slide.

Cat claimed a padded cubby cushion, avoiding the athletic equipment with the precision of an experienced napper.

Guagua waited near the last footprint, holding his yellow backpack close instead of letting it swing.

Step one: Rosie looked for the line's end. Step two: **"Is this the end of the line?"**

Guagua nodded. Step three: Rosie stood behind him, leaving room for his backpack and her own little cup.

Step four: they waited. Rosie watched a ribbon sway overhead while the line moved one space forward.

Guagua stepped aside to hand his backpack to Mom. Rosie wondered whether that meant his turn had ended.

Her straw drooped. She felt confused. That's okay; a small move could mean more than one thing.

Rosie took a Rose breath.

Smell the rose: in, two, three, four.

Blow out the candle: out, two, three, four.

"Are you still waiting for your turn?" Rosie asked, keeping her place rather than moving ahead of him.

"Yes, just putting my backpack away," said Guagua. He returned to his footprint, and Rosie waited behind him.

Step five: when the attendant showed her it was her turn, Rosie moved to the slide's starting place.

She followed the play hall's slide instructions, sitting properly and checking the landing area before setting off.

Taro waited at the bottom with Mom. Tico's beak peeked over a foam tower like a colorful flag.

Cat watched Rosie arrive on the soft landing mat. "An excellent invention. The floor delivers you to cushions."

Rosie laughed and stayed clear of the slide's landing, giving the next child room to finish their turn.

Tori met Rosie beside the play hall's shoe cubbies, helping her match a very small pair of shoes.

"You waited and checked kindly. You've got this, little sip," she said, straightening Rosie's little waiting card.

Rosie felt proud of the ride and the space she had left for the person in front.

**加油** (jiāyóu) = go for it.

"One step at a time, one sip at a time," said Rosie.

## 🌹 Rosie's Steps (Recap)

1. Find the end of the line.
2. Ask if you are unsure.
3. Stand behind the last person.
4. Wait with space between people.
5. Go when it is your turn.

**If something surprising happens:** Take a Rose breath or a comfortable pause, then say: "Are you still waiting for your turn?"

## 💬 Practice Together (for grown-ups)

- Role-play the situation using a little waiting card or a safe pretend substitute; read the five steps together.
- Swap roles so your child can see you model **"Is this the end of the line?"** and hear the other person's answer.
- Introduce this story's small surprise; practice "Are you still waiting for your turn?" and allow a comfortable breath or pause.
- On a real outing, stay nearby, offer choices, and praise the specific step your child tried.

## 🌙 Goodnight Blessing

Goodnight, patient feet and swooping little slide.
Goodnight, footprints, waiting for tomorrow.
Goodnight, Cat, delivered to cushions.
May your turn come gently in its time.
