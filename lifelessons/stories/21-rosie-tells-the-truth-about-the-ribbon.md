# 21 — Rosie Tells the Truth About the Ribbon

**Life skill:** Tell the truth about an accidental break and accept help repairing it.
**Magic sentence:** "I did it, and I need help fixing it."
**Coaches:** Aiyu 🇹🇼 + Niko Nigiri 🇯🇵
**Setting:** the sewing nook under the stairs (practice), then the neighborhood puppet theater workshop (real outing).

---

## The Story

Rosie was helping decorate a puppet's ribbon tail, which looked like a very cheerful trail of spaghetti.

At the sewing nook, she worried about making a mistake with something that belonged to the workshop.

Aiyu's clear golden jelly glowed beside the fabric basket. Hiding things from her seemed especially unlikely.

Aiyu's golden jelly glowed gently.

Niko was a remarkably tidy salmon nigiri.

Cat the penguin napped on a basket of soft fabric scraps.

Niko loosened a practice ribbon and told Aiyu, **"I did it, and I need help fixing it."**

"We need the true story to choose the right repair," Aiyu explained, holding the ribbon where Niko could see.

**Rosie's Steps**

1. Stop when something goes wrong.
2. Tell your trusted grown-up what happened.
3. Say what you did.

4. Ask for help repairing it.
5. Help with the agreed repair.

Rosie practiced telling Mom that she had pulled a ribbon loose, then asked how she could help.

Niko sorted the pretend repairs into tidy trays. "Everything has a place. Even my extremely organized mistakes."

At the puppet workshop, Mom helped Rosie find a low table beneath bright hanging cloth puppets.

Cat napped on a folded spare curtain, looking like a blue mountain in a very small landscape.

Rosie chose a ribbon for a dragon puppet. Its enormous cloth eyebrows seemed delighted by the choice.

Step one: she tugged too hard, and the ribbon came away from the puppet's tail. Rosie stopped.

Step two: she found Mom beside the repair tray and explained that the ribbon had come loose.

Step three: **"I did it, and I need help fixing it."** She showed Mom the ribbon.

Mom asked which ribbon Rosie had pulled. There were two similar blue ribbons beside the puppet.

Rosie felt nervous. Her straw drooped. That's okay; telling the truth could include being unsure about a detail.

Rosie took a Rose breath.

Smell the rose: in, two, three, four.

Blow out the candle: out, two, three, four.

"I'm not sure which blue one. Can we check together?" Rosie asked, keeping both ribbons on the table.

Mom compared the loose end with the tail. One matched the empty loop; the other belonged elsewhere.

Step four: Rosie asked for help reattaching the right ribbon. Mom handled the needle and explained the repair.

Step five: Rosie held the fabric steady as agreed, keeping her tiny hands away from Mom's sewing needle.

Aiyu glowed warmly. Niko checked the finished tail. "Neatly repaired. The dragon may resume its spaghetti duties."

Cat peeked from her curtain pile. "If this is a puppet show, my part is the quiet mountain."

The dragon's repaired ribbon swayed. Rosie felt lighter now that the true story had helped them fix it.

Tori joined Rosie at the theater's little ticket window, beneath a garland of cloth puppet shoes.

"You told us what happened. You've got this, little sip," she said, admiring the dragon's repaired tail.

Rosie smiled. A mistake was easier to mend when she did not have to carry a secret too.

**加油** (jiāyóu) = go for it.

"One step at a time, one sip at a time," said Rosie.

## 🌹 Rosie's Steps (Recap)

1. Stop when something goes wrong.
2. Tell your trusted grown-up what happened.
3. Say what you did.
4. Ask for help repairing it.
5. Help with the agreed repair.

**If something surprising happens:** Take a Rose breath or a comfortable pause, then say: "I'm not sure which blue one. Can we check together?"

## 💬 Practice Together (for grown-ups)

- Role-play the situation using a loose puppet ribbon or a safe pretend substitute; read the five steps together.
- Swap roles so your child can see you model **"I did it, and I need help fixing it."** and hear the other person's answer.
- Introduce this story's small surprise; practice "I'm not sure which blue one. Can we check together?" and allow a comfortable breath or pause.
- On a real outing, stay nearby, offer choices, and praise the specific step your child tried.
