# 15 — Rosie Plays Ring Toss at the Night Market

**Life skill:** Playing a game stall at the night market — watching first to learn the rules, waiting your turn, asking the price, paying, listening to the rules, taking your turn, missing (or winning) gracefully, thanking the stall owner, and letting the next person play.
**Magic sentence:** "Excuse me, how much for one game?"
**Coaches:** Bīng the Shaved Ice (Taiwan) + Dai Daruma (Japan)
**Setting:** The little lane behind Rosie's building at golden hour (practice: chalk circles on the paving, glass bottles on an old crate, a bucket of rope rings), then the game street of the night market — a ring-toss stall under a canopy of red lanterns, a goldfish tank glowing next door, and Cat the penguin asleep on the pile of plush prizes

---

## The Story

Rosie the Rose Milk Tea loved the **夜市** (yèshì) = night market.

"I want to play ring toss," she said. "All by myself." Her pink-striped straw drooped. "But what do I say to the auntie?"

"MORE IS MORE!" Bīng the Shaved Ice slid down the lane in his sunglasses. "Throw all the rings at once! Win everything!"

"That is one plan," said Dai Daruma, wobbling after him. "Here is a better one."

By the scooters, Cat the penguin, who was staying with Rosie's family, dozed on a sack of rice.

Bīng set up a pretend stall on an old crate: bottles in a row, a bucket of rings. He put on a paper visor. "Welcome to Bīng's Ring Palace."

"Game stalls have steps," said Dai.

**Rosie's Steps**
1. Watch one game first, so you know the rules.
2. Wait in line for your turn.
3. Say, **"Excuse me, how much for one game?"** Then pay.
4. Listen to the rules. Then take your turn.
5. If you miss, say, "Good try! Maybe next time."
6. Say "Thank you!" and let the next person play.

"**多少錢** (duōshǎo qián) = how much," said Dai. "Step five is the secret step. Most people miss."

He tossed a ring. It bounced off a bottle. He tipped over — boing — and stood right back up. "Fall down seven, bounce up eight!"

Rosie practiced:
1. Watch one game first, so you know the rules.
2. Wait in line for your turn.
3. Say, **"Excuse me, how much for one game?"** Then pay.
4. Listen to the rules. Then take your turn.
5. If you miss, say, "Good try! Maybe next time."
6. Say "Thank you!" and let the next person play.

Her pretend coin was a bottle cap. Her ring landed on a bottle! Bīng handed her a pinwheel. Then he took it back. "That one is the decoration."

That night, big sister Tori took Rosie to the night market. Cat came too. Red lanterns glowed.

Corn sizzled on a grill. At the ring-toss stall, bottles sparkled in rows. On the pile of plush prizes at the back, Cat was fast asleep.

Step one. Rosie watched a boy play. Three rings, three misses, one big grin. Step two. She waited in line.

Step three. "**Excuse me, how much for one game?**"

"Three rings for fifty, sweetie," said the auntie. Rosie paid.

Step four. "Any bottle counts," said the auntie. "***Ganbatte*** = do your best!" whispered Dai.

Rosie tossed. The first ring bounced off. The second ring rolled under the table. The third ring landed on Dai's head.

Three misses. Rosie felt disappointed. Her straw drooped all the way down. That's okay.

Rosie did a Rose breath. Smell the rose — in, two, three, four. Blow out the candle — out, two, three, four.

Step five. "Good try! Maybe next time," said Rosie.

The auntie smiled. "That was a very polite miss." She gave Rosie a tiny keychain with a bell.

Then the auntie reached for the plush pile. "And a penguin for you—" "That one is taken!" said Rosie. "Five more minutes," mumbled Cat.

Step six. "Thank you! **謝謝** (xièxie)!" Rosie stepped aside for the next player.

Tori hugged her on a little red stool. "You missed AND you were kind. You've got this, little sip."

Bīng arrived with shaved ice the size of a lantern. "More is more!"

"One step at a time," said Rosie sleepily. "One sip at a time."

## 🌹 Rosie's Steps (Recap)
1. Watch one game first, so you know the rules.
2. Wait in line for your turn.
3. Say, **"Excuse me, how much for one game?"** Then pay.
4. Listen to the rules. Then take your turn.
5. If you miss, say, "Good try! Maybe next time."
6. Say "Thank you!" and let the next person play.

**If something surprising happens:** Rose breath, then say: "Good try! Maybe next time." Missing is part of the game. Being kind about it is the real win.

## 💬 Practice Together (for grown-ups)
- Build a ring-toss stall at home or in the yard: three empty bottles on a box, rope rings (or bracelets, or paper-plate rings), and a pretend coin. Take turns being the stall owner and the player, and say each step out loud the first few times.
- Make sure your child misses on purpose sometimes. Practice the Rose breath and "Good try! Maybe next time." in a cheerful voice, and let them watch you miss and say it too. Be Dai Daruma — tip over, bounce back up.
- Praise the step-six part most of all: stepping aside so the next person can play. "You missed, you said a kind thing, and you let the next kid have a turn. That was the best part."
- At a real night market or fair, do step one together: stand to the side and watch one full game before getting in line. Agree in advance on how many games to play (one or two), and hold the coins until it's time to pay. A stuffed penguin napping on the prize pile makes a fine Cat.

## 🌙 Goodnight Blessing
Goodnight, little sip, with your six small steps.
Goodnight, red lanterns, swaying over the lane.
Goodnight, Cat, dreaming on a pile of prizes.
Some rings miss. Kindness never does. Sleep tight.
