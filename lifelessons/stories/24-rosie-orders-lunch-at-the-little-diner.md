# 24 — Rosie Orders Lunch at the Little Diner

**Life skill:** Choose and order one item at a sit-down restaurant.
**Magic sentence:** "May I have the vegetable noodles, please?"
**Coaches:** Tama Onigiri 🇯🇵 + Don Café 🇨🇷
**Setting:** the conservatory breakfast bench (practice), then the canal-side noodle diner (real outing).

---

## The Story

Rosie wanted to order her own lunch at the canal-side diner, where bowls smelled warm and gingery.

At the conservatory bench, she studied a picture menu and wondered how to make the server hear her.

Tama produced a toy bowl from his bag. "I packed a little extra. This lunch is delightfully washable."

Tama was a well-prepared triangular rice ball.

Don Café was a wise coffee bean.

Cat the penguin napped on a linen-covered bench cushion.

Don Café pretended to serve. Tama waited, then asked, **"May I have the vegetable noodles, please?"**

"A server may ask another question," Don Café explained. "You can answer, or ask your grown-up for help."

**Rosie's Steps**

1. Sit with your grown-up.
2. Choose from the available food.
3. Wait until the server is ready.

4. Say your order clearly.
5. Answer questions or ask for help.

**午餐** (wǔcān) = lunch. Rosie tried **"May I have the vegetable noodles, please?"** Don Café pretended.

Rosie practiced looking at the menu and making her choice before Don Café returned to the pretend table.

Tama offered his toy bowl a spoon. "Very polite lunch. It has never interrupted anybody with a slurp."

Mom and Rosie sat beside the diner's canal window, with a picture menu between their little bowls.

Cat dozed on a spare cushion beside Mom, her yellow scarf folded neatly beyond the table edge.

The server brought water. Rosie watched steam rise behind the counter, making soft clouds above the noodle pots.

Step one: Rosie stayed at the table with Mom. Step two: she found vegetable noodles on the menu.

Step three: she waited until the server turned toward her. The server smiled and held a small order pad.

Step four: **"May I have the vegetable noodles, please?"** Rosie kept her voice clear and comfortably steady.

"Thick noodles or thin noodles?" the server asked. Rosie had practiced choosing vegetables, but not noodle shapes.

She felt uncertain. Her straw drooped. That's okay; she could ask about words she did not yet understand.

Rosie took a Rose breath.

Smell the rose: in, two, three, four.

Blow out the candle: out, two, three, four.

"Could you show me the two choices, please?" Rosie asked, keeping the picture menu in front of her.

The server pointed to two pictures. Mom helped Rosie compare them, and Rosie chose the thin noodles.

Step five: Rosie answered, "Thin noodles, please." The server repeated the order, and Rosie checked it was right.

When lunch arrived, Mom checked that it had cooled enough. Rosie thanked the server before trying a careful bite.

Don Café admired the steam. Tama opened his bag. "An emergency napkin. Extremely useful equipment."

Cat blinked from her cushion as a spoon clinked. "I ordered the soft seat. Service has been excellent."

Rosie giggled, then looked toward the canal. Her bowl was warm, her choice was hers, and lunch could begin.

Tori joined them beside the diner's ceramic bowl display after lunch, admiring Rosie's careful ordering voice.

"You asked about the new choice. You've got this, little sip," she said, giving Rosie a happy nod.

Rosie felt proud. She could ask about unfamiliar words and still choose her own lunch.

**加油** (jiāyóu) = go for it.

"One step at a time, one sip at a time," said Rosie.

## 🌹 Rosie's Steps (Recap)

1. Sit with your grown-up.
2. Choose from the available food.
3. Wait until the server is ready.
4. Say your order clearly.
5. Answer questions or ask for help.

**If something surprising happens:** Take a Rose breath or a comfortable pause, then say: "Could you show me the two choices, please?"

## 💬 Practice Together (for grown-ups)

- Role-play the situation using a picture menu or a safe pretend substitute; read the five steps together.
- Swap roles so your child can see you model **"May I have the vegetable noodles, please?"** and hear the other person's answer.
- Introduce this story's small surprise; practice "Could you show me the two choices, please?" and allow a comfortable breath or pause.
- On a real outing, stay nearby, offer choices, and praise the specific step your child tried.
