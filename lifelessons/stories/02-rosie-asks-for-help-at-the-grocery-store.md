# 02 — Rosie Asks for Help at the Grocery Store

**Life skill:** Asking a store worker for help finding an item.
**Magic sentence:** "Excuse me, can you help me find the honey?"
**Coaches:** Taro 🇹🇼 (wise, patient purple taro) + Gallo & Pinto 🇨🇷 (cheerful rice-and-bean duo who LOVE grocery shopping)
**Setting:** Rosie's cozy kitchen, then a bright, friendly neighborhood grocery store

---

## The Story

Rosie the Rose Milk Tea had a very important job.

Her big sister Tori had a scratchy throat. Mom wanted to make her warm honey-lemon tea. But the honey jar was empty. Not one drop. Not even a sticky smell.

"We'll go to the **超市** (chāoshì) = supermarket after lunch," said Mom. "Rosie, will you be in charge of finding the honey?"

Rosie's pink-striped straw drooped. The grocery store was big. It had so many aisles.

*Ding-dong!* In rolled Taro, the wise purple taro, his reading glasses sliding down his tiny nose. Behind him bounced Gallo, a toasty rice grain in a woven palm hat, and Pinto, a shiny black bean with a tiny wooden spoon.

"Rise and pinto!" sang Gallo. "Did somebody say GROCERY STORE? The carts! The shelves! The little free cheese samples! Hmm-hmm!"

"He once waved at a watermelon for ten minutes," said Pinto. "He thought it was his cousin."

Rosie giggled. Her straw wiggled a little.

"I feel nervous," Rosie said. "What if I can't find the honey?"

"Then you ask for help," said Taro. "Store workers know where everything lives. Helping is part of their job. Good things take time to steam. So we will learn it one step at a time."

Rosie loved steps. She sat up tall.

**Rosie's Steps**
1. Look for a worker with a name tag and a store apron or vest.
2. Walk up and wait until they look at you.
3. Say the magic sentence: **"Excuse me, can you help me find the honey?"**
4. Listen, and look where they point.
5. Say, "Thank you!"
6. Go find it, and stay where your grown-up can see you.

"Honey is **蜂蜜** (fēngmì) = honey," Taro added. "Bees make it. Bees are hard workers, just like store workers. Only fuzzier."

Now it was time to practice.

Gallo wrote HELLO, I'M GALLO on a sticky note and stuck it on his front. Pinto tied a paper napkin around him like an apron.

"There," said Pinto. "Now he is a store worker. A very small one."

Gallo pretended to stack cereal boxes on the table. Taro read each step out loud, and Rosie did it.

1. Look for a worker with a name tag and a store apron or vest.
2. Walk up and wait until they look at you.
3. Say the magic sentence: **"Excuse me, can you help me find the honey?"**
4. Listen, and look where they point.
5. Say, "Thank you!"
6. Go find it, and stay where your grown-up can see you.

Rosie found the name tag. She walked up and waited. Gallo turned and looked at her. Rosie said, **"Excuse me, can you help me find the honey?"**

"Aisle five, next to the peanut butter!" said Gallo, pointing. Rosie looked where he pointed. "Thank you!" she said. Then she walked to the cupboard, the pretend aisle five.

They practiced three times. On the third time, Gallo pointed so hard he tumbled off the table into the grape bowl.

"I'm fine!" he called. "Grapes are very soft!"

After lunch, Mom pushed the cart into the grocery store. The coaches rode in the little cart seat.

Rosie looked up at the big signs over each aisle. *Aisle 1: Fruits and Vegetables. Aisle 2: Bread. Aisle 3: Rice and Beans.*

Gallo gasped. "Pinto! They named an aisle after US!"

"They do that in every store, **mae**," said Pinto. (Mae means "buddy" in Costa Rica.)

No sign said honey. It was time to ask.

Step 1. Rosie looked for a worker. She saw a man in a green apron with a name tag. But he was high up on a ladder, stacking big boxes. His arms were full.

This was not like practice. Rosie felt nervous. Her straw drooped. That's okay.

Rosie did a Rose breath. Smell the rose: in, two, three, four. Blow out the candle: out, two, three, four. Her straw lifted a little.

Then she said, "That's okay. I can find a different worker with a name tag."

Taro nodded. "Wise choice. A worker on a ladder needs to watch his boxes."

Near the apples, Rosie saw a woman in a green vest. Her name tag said MAYA.

Step 2. Rosie walked up and waited. Maya looked down and smiled.

Step 3. Rosie said, **"Excuse me, can you help me find the honey?"**

"Sure!" said Maya. "Honey is in aisle five, with the peanut butter and jam." She pointed.

Step 4. Rosie listened, and looked where Maya pointed. There was a big number 5.

Step 5. "Thank you!" said Rosie.

"You're welcome!" said Maya.

Step 6. Rosie rolled to aisle five, with Mom and the cart right behind her. The honey jars glowed like tiny golden suns. One jar was shaped like a bear. Rosie picked that one.

Gallo tried to hug the bear. "Not a cousin," said Pinto gently.

At home, Tori sipped her warm honey-lemon tea. "Mmm. Rosie, you found this?"

"I asked a worker for help," said Rosie proudly. "The first one was busy. So I did a Rose breath and found another one."

Tori wrapped her in a big hug. "You've got this, little sip. And you DID it!"

"**謝謝** (xièxie) = thank you," Rosie told her coaches.

Rosie's straw wiggled all the way up. "One step at a time," she said, "one sip at a time."

## 🌹 Rosie's Steps (Recap)
1. Look for a worker with a name tag and a store apron or vest.
2. Walk up and wait until they look at you.
3. Say the magic sentence: **"Excuse me, can you help me find the honey?"**
4. Listen, and look where they point.
5. Say, "Thank you!"
6. Go find it, and stay where your grown-up can see you.

**If something surprising happens:** Rose breath, then say: "That's okay. I can find a different worker with a name tag."

## 💬 Practice Together (for grown-ups)
- **Play store at home.** Put a sticky-note name tag and a kitchen apron on a grown-up or stuffed animal. Have your child walk up, wait for eye contact, and say **"Excuse me, can you help me find the honey?"** Point to a "store shelf" (a cupboard) and name a number, like "Aisle five." Swap in other items (bread, bananas, crayons) once the sentence feels easy.
- **Practice the busy worker.** Sometimes pretend to be "busy" (on the phone, or stacking pretend boxes). Coach a Rose breath and the backup sentence. Praise choosing another worker, or waiting quietly until the worker looks at them.
- **Praise the specific step:** "You waited until she looked at you. That was step 2!" or "You looked right where she pointed!" Specific praise helps the steps stick.
- **Real-world tip:** Pick a quiet time at a familiar store. Stand nearby where your child can see you, and let them do all the talking. Before you go in, remind them: workers wear name tags and store aprons or vests, and they stay where you can see them the whole time.
