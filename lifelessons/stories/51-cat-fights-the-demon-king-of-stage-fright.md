# 51 — Cat Fights the Demon King of Stage Fright

**Life skill:** Handle stage fright before performing: name the feeling, breathe, find a friendly face, say the first line, and keep going.
**Magic sentence:** "Hello, Jitters. I see you, and I'm going on anyway."
**Coaches:** Parker the Shiba Inu 🐕 + Bīng 🇹🇼 + Boba 🇹🇼
**Cat’s role:** lead actor facing stage fright, gentle kung fu bows, asks a friend to stand beside her
**Friends:** Miles the Monkey and Cleo the Tiger (new), Rosie and Tori in the front row
**Setting:** the school auditorium's backstage prop corner (practice), then the school auditorium stage on opening night (real outing).

---

## The Story

Cat the penguin had the biggest part in the school play about Hamilton. She played Hamilton himself.

Miles the monkey played Burr, who waits. Miles had never waited for anything.

Cleo the tiger played Eliza. Her whisper rattled the stage lights.

At dress rehearsal, the curtain opened. Cat's feathers fluffed. Her first line hid behind her beak.

Tiny purple fluffballs tumbled out of the wings. The Jitters! They chattered their teeth and knocked their knees.

Cat felt **緊張** (jǐnzhāng) = nervous. That's okay. Nervous means you care.

Parker the Shiba Inu played General Washington. He had forgotten lines in forty plays.

Bīng played King George. "More is more! A king needs at least three crowns."

Boba played Lafayette. "Let's bounce!" He bounced straight into the drum.

"Stage fright has steps," Parker said. "Jitters are little. They only look big in a spotlight."

**Cat's Steps**

1. Notice the wobble. Say, "Hello, Jitters."

2. Penguin breath: in for four, out for four.

3. Find one friendly face.

4. Say your first line to that face.

5. Keep going. A mistake is part of the show.

Parker pretended to be a Jitter. He wore a laundry basket and wobbled.

Cat bowed, kung fu style, and tried **"Hello, Jitters. I see you, and I'm going on anyway."**

1. Notice the wobble. Say, "Hello, Jitters."

2. Penguin breath: in for four, out for four.

3. Find one friendly face.

4. Say your first line to that face.

5. Keep going. A mistake is part of the show.

Bīng melted a little from pride. "The best rehearsal ever. Also the drippiest."

Opening night. Rosie and Tori sat in the front row. Tori waved her green straw.

The curtain rose. The Jitters tumbled in, and this time they brought their king.

The Demon King of Stage Fright was a puffy purple curtain in a paper crown.

His knees knocked loudest. "Nobody remembers a line in front of me," he boomed. He hid behind himself.

Cat's beak wobbled. That's okay. The king was a surprise, and surprises are allowed.

"Will you stand beside me?" Cat whispered. Asking for help is a step too.

Parker stepped up. "Right beside you. That is what a general is for."

Step one: "Hello, Jitters." The fluffballs froze mid-wobble.

Step two: a Penguin breath. In, two, three, four. Out, two, three, four.

Step three: Cat found Rosie's face. Rosie's straw wiggled hello.

"Charge?" asked Cat. "Kung fu style," said Parker. Cat bowed to the Demon King, calm and polite.

The Demon King had never been bowed to. He shrank to the size of a sofa.

Parker let out one enormous bark. The Jitters blew into a tidy purple pile.

Step four, to Rosie's face: **"Hello, Jitters. I see you, and I'm going on anyway."**

The Demon King shrank to a plum. **勇氣** (yǒngqì) = courage. Cat said it again, louder.

**"Hello, Jitters. I see you, and I'm going on anyway."** The king squeaked, "May I have a seat?"

He sat in the front row and clapped.

Step five: Miles swung on the rope. "I waited for it! Four seconds!" He landed in the drum.

Cleo sang her lullaby in a whisper. Two lights rattled. Everyone kept going.

The play ended. The crowd stood and cheered.

Tori hugged Cat, hat and all. "You've got this, little penguin."

"Jitters are little," said Cat. "They only look big in a spotlight."

"One step at a time, one line at a time," said Rosie.

## 🌹 Cat's Steps (Recap)

1. Notice the wobble. Say, "Hello, Jitters."
2. Penguin breath: in for four, out for four.
3. Find one friendly face.
4. Say your first line to that face.
5. Keep going. A mistake is part of the show.

**If something surprising happens:** Take a Penguin breath, then say: "Will you stand beside me?"

## 💬 Practice Together (for grown-ups)

- Give the worry a silly name and a silly shape (a fluffball, a wobbly curtain). Naming it out loud shrinks it; practice saying "Hello, Jitters" in a mirror.
- Role-play a tiny show at home: a bedsheet curtain, one line, one friendly face in the audience. Swap roles so your child plays the wobbly Jitter too.
- Practice the Penguin breath together, in for four and out for four, before any speaking moment: show-and-tell, a recital, a birthday thank-you.
- Rehearse the surprise: a new face in the crowd or a forgotten word. Praise asking for help ("Will you stand beside me?") as a real step, and praise keeping going after a mistake.

## 🌙 Goodnight Blessing

Goodnight, little penguin, your big line is said.
Goodnight, Jitters, tucked in a tidy purple pile.
Goodnight, tiny king, clapping in the front row.
May your dreams have a friendly face in every crowd.
