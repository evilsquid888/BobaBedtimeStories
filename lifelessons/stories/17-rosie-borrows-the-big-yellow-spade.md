# 17 — Rosie Borrows the Big Yellow Spade

**Life skill:** Ask before borrowing, accept an answer, and return a borrowed tool.
**Magic sentence:** "May I borrow your spade, please?"
**Coaches:** Tama Onigiri 🇯🇵 + Tato the Patacon 🇨🇷
**Cat’s role:** helper
**Setting:** the courtyard potting shed (practice), then the community allotment (real outing).

---

## The Story

Rosie wanted to plant sunflowers, but her little spoon only made dents in the soil.

A yellow spade waited beside Guagua's garden bed, shiny enough to look like buried sunshine.

Could she take it? Her straw drooped, while a worm inspected her spoon like a restaurant critic.

Tama was a well-prepared triangular rice ball.

Tato was a sturdy golden plantain patty.

Cat the penguin sorted spare seed pots. One pot fitted her beak rather too well.

Tama asked Tato, **"May I borrow your spade, please?"** He waited, then dug and returned it.

"A tool belongs to someone even when they are elsewhere," Tato explained, patting his sturdy plantain feet.

**Rosie's Steps**

1. Find the owner.
2. Ask before taking the tool.
3. Wait for the answer.

4. Use it as agreed.
5. Return it and say thank you.

**借用** (jièyòng) = borrowing. Rosie tried **"May I borrow your spade, please?"** Tato the Patacon pretended.

1. Find the owner.
2. Ask before taking the tool.
3. Wait for the answer.

4. Use it as agreed.
5. Return it and say thank you.

Rosie practiced with a cardboard spade and returned it handle first, carefully checking the pretend flowerbed.

Tama unpacked three spare spoons. "I packed extra, just in case the worms invite us for dinner."

Mom came to the allotment, where pea vines curled around poles and the earth smelled fresh.

Cat carried empty pots to the rack, looking like a blue wheelbarrow with excellent manners.

Guagua stood beside his sunflower bed, brushing soil from the straps of his yellow backpack.

Step one: Rosie found Guagua, the owner, instead of guessing that an unused tool was free.

Step two: **"May I borrow your spade, please?"** She kept her hands beside her cup.

Step three: she waited. Guagua pointed to his unfinished row. "After I finish planting these."

Rosie had expected yes right away. She felt impatient, and her pink-striped straw drooped toward the dirt.

That's okay. Waiting did not mean Guagua disliked her; his spade was still doing its job.

Rosie took a Rose breath.

Smell the rose: in, two, three, four.

Blow out the candle: out, two, three, four.

"Can you tell me when you're finished?" Rosie asked, setting her own spoon beside her seed packet.

"Yes," said Guagua. Rosie watered another bed with Mom while Guagua finished his last little hole.

Guagua offered the spade. Step four: Rosie dug only in her own bed, as they had agreed.

Step five: she brushed away the loose soil and returned the spade. "Thank you for sharing."

Tato admired the straight row. "Strong tools, gentle manners. My feet vote for both."

Cat offered Guagua the brush. "May I borrow it next? My feet have become garden beds."

Rosie pressed the first sunflower seed into its small, patient beginning.

Tori met Rosie beside the allotment's rain barrel, where their reflections bobbed in the water.

"You asked, waited, and gave it back. You've got this, little sip," she said with a proud nod.

Rosie's straw stood tall. Sharing felt friendly when everyone knew what would happen to their things.

**加油** (jiāyóu) = go for it.

"One step at a time, one sip at a time," said Rosie.

## 🌹 Rosie's Steps (Recap)

1. Find the owner.
2. Ask before taking the tool.
3. Wait for the answer.
4. Use it as agreed.
5. Return it and say thank you.

**If something surprising happens:** Take a Rose breath or a comfortable pause, then say: "Can you tell me when you're finished?"

## 💬 Practice Together (for grown-ups)

- Role-play the situation using a yellow spade or a safe pretend substitute; read the five steps together.
- Swap roles so your child can see you model **"May I borrow your spade, please?"** and hear the other person's answer.
- Introduce this story's small surprise; practice "Can you tell me when you're finished?" and allow a comfortable breath or pause.
- On a real outing, stay nearby, offer choices, and praise the specific step your child tried.
