# 28 — Rosie Cheers After the Bowling Game

**Life skill:** Respond to losing a game with respectful words and self-care.
**Magic sentence:** "Good game. I liked playing with you."
**Coaches:** Mango 🇹🇼 + Dai Daruma 🇯🇵
**Cat’s role:** hip-hop coach
**Setting:** the covered bicycle storage bay (practice), then the miniature bowling alley (real outing).

---

## The Story

Rosie was going miniature bowling with Guagua, and she hoped to knock down every pin at once.

In the bicycle bay, Mango lined up foam pins, then accidentally bowed to them as if greeting rival athletes.

"What if I don't win?" Rosie asked. Dai rocked gently. "The friendship is not printed on the score."

Mango's sports sweatband was bright red.

Dai was a determined red daruma bun.

Cat the penguin practiced a hip-hop victory step. Then she practiced a friendly losing-game bow.

Mango finished a pretend game behind Dai's score. **"Good game. I liked playing with you,"** he said.

Cat taught Rosie a friendly finish: face your friend and offer kind words. "You can feel disappointed and still be kind."

**Rosie's Steps**

1. Wait until the game is finished.
2. Notice and name your feeling.
3. Say something kind to the players.

4. Take a break if you need one.
5. Choose whether to play again.

**比賽** (bǐsài) = competition. Rosie tried **"Good game. I liked playing with you."** Dai Daruma pretended.

Rosie practiced naming disappointment and congratulating the other player, without pretending that losing felt wonderful right away.

Dai examined a foam pin. "This fellow falls down once and gets carried upright. Luxury training program."

Mom stayed nearby at the miniature bowling alley, where light balls waited beside short lanes and tiny shoes.

Cat waited by the shoe shelf, reserving her dance steps for the clear seating area.

Guagua's first ball knocked down several pins. Rosie's knocked down two, which fell with impressively dramatic little clacks.

Step one: Rosie waited until all turns were complete. Guagua had the higher score when the game ended.

Step two: "I feel disappointed," she said quietly to Mom, holding her little bowling shoes beside her cup.

Step three: **"Good game. I liked playing with you."** Guagua thanked her and smiled at their shared lane.

Guagua asked to start another game straight away. Rosie still felt tight inside and was not ready.

Her straw drooped. That's okay; saying kind words had not made her need for a pause disappear.

Rosie took a Rose breath.

Smell the rose: in, two, three, four.

Blow out the candle: out, two, three, four.

"I need a short break before I decide," Rosie said, placing the ball back where it belonged.

Mom went with her to the seating area. Guagua waited, using the time to change his shoes.

Step four: Rosie took her break with Mom and watched the colored lane reflections settle into gentle stripes.

Step five: she chose another game, noticing her ball's movement instead of chasing the score.

Mango nodded. Dai stayed upright beside a foam pin. "We are practicing another beginning."

Cat gave both players the same tiny cheer-step. "Friendship gets a celebration, whatever the score." Guagua grinned.

Rosie thanked Guagua. Another game could wait until she felt ready.

Tori joined them at the alley's little shoe-return bench, where Rosie paired her shoes carefully together.

"You were kind and took your pause. You've got this, little sip," she said with a warm nod.

Rosie felt proud of finishing kindly, even though she had not finished in first place.

**加油** (jiāyóu) = go for it.

"One step at a time, one sip at a time," said Rosie.

## 🌹 Rosie's Steps (Recap)

1. Wait until the game is finished.
2. Notice and name your feeling.
3. Say something kind to the players.
4. Take a break if you need one.
5. Choose whether to play again.

**If something surprising happens:** Take a Rose breath or a comfortable pause, then say: "I need a short break before I decide."

## 💬 Practice Together (for grown-ups)

- Role-play the situation using a soft practice bowling ball or a safe pretend substitute; read the five steps together.
- Swap roles so your child can see you model **"Good game. I liked playing with you."** and hear the other person's answer.
- Introduce this story's small surprise; practice "I need a short break before I decide." and allow a comfortable breath or pause.
- On a real outing, stay nearby, offer choices, and praise the specific step your child tried.

## 🌙 Goodnight Blessing

Goodnight, little bowler and kindly spoken words.
Goodnight, pins, standing quietly again.
Goodnight, Cat, cheering feet now resting.
May play bring friendship beyond the score.
