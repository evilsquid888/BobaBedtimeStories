# 48 — Rosie Starts the Homework Mountain

**Life skill:** Begin a large-feeling task by planning one small work period and a break.
**Magic sentence:** "I'll start with this part, then take a break."
**Coaches:** Niko Nigiri 🇯🇵 + Tama Onigiri 🇯🇵
**Setting:** the family's folding screen study nook (practice), then the school homework club's reading booth (real outing).

---

## The Story

Rosie's homework felt like a mountain: math problems and a paragraph to read before club time ended.

In the study nook, Tama arranged practice pages into a hill. Niko carefully moved the pencil off its summit.

"Must I finish everything at once?" Rosie asked. "First, find a manageable beginning," Niko said.

Niko was a remarkably tidy salmon nigiri.

Tama was a well-prepared triangular rice ball.

Cat the penguin napped on a cushion beneath the study desk.

Tama checked the task and chose two problems. **"I'll start with this part, then take a break,"** he said.

"Agree on the plan with your grown-up," Niko explained. "A beginning can be small and still be useful."

**Rosie's Steps**

1. Check the task with your grown-up.
2. Choose one small part to begin.
3. Agree on a short work period.

4. Try the chosen part.
5. Check progress before your agreed break.

**開始** (kāishǐ) = beginning. Rosie tried **"I'll start with this part, then take a break."** Tama Onigiri pretended.

1. Check the task with your grown-up.
2. Choose one small part to begin.
3. Agree on a short work period.

4. Try the chosen part.
5. Check progress before your agreed break.

Rosie practiced pointing to her first small part, then setting the picture timer with Mom's help.

Tama unpacked a spare pencil. "Mountain equipment. Much easier to carry than an emergency desk."

Mom joined Rosie at homework club, where task trays waited beside warm lamps and cushioned reading booths.

Cat settled onto a spare reading cushion, taking responsibility for the club's important horizontal work.

The tutor showed Rosie her assignment. Rosie noticed both the math and reading, instead of hiding the longer page.

Step one: she checked with Mom and the tutor. Step two: Rosie chose the first two problems.

**"I'll start with this part, then take a break."** Step three: she agreed on a short work period with Mom.

Step four: Rosie tried the first problem, keeping the reading page for later.

A second page was tucked underneath. Rosie spotted it and suddenly felt that her small plan had become useless.

Her straw drooped. She felt overwhelmed. That's okay; another page did not erase the beginning she had chosen.

Rosie took a Rose breath.

Smell the rose: in, two, three, four.

Blow out the candle: out, two, three, four.

"Which part belongs in this work period?" Rosie asked, showing Mom and the tutor the extra page.

The tutor explained that it was optional practice. Rosie could keep her agreed first part and check again afterward.

Step four continued: she tried two problems, asking the tutor about a number she did not understand.

Step five: she checked her progress with Mom when the timer finished, then took the break they had agreed.

Niko tidied the tray. Tama inspected his pencil. "Our mountain equipment has climbed one respectable little slope."

Cat blinked from the cushion. "I started with this nap. My next small part may be another nap."

Rosie giggled, stretching her tiny arms beside her cup. The assignment was still there, but she had begun.

Tori met Rosie at the book cubbies, beside the completed first part in its tray.

"You found a beginning and followed your plan. You've got this, little sip," she said warmly.

Rosie felt proud of beginning. A mountain could have smaller slopes.

**加油** (jiāyóu) = go for it.

"One step at a time, one sip at a time," said Rosie.

## 🌹 Rosie's Steps (Recap)

1. Check the task with your grown-up.
2. Choose one small part to begin.
3. Agree on a short work period.
4. Try the chosen part.
5. Check progress before your agreed break.

**If something surprising happens:** Take a Rose breath or a comfortable pause, then say: "Which part belongs in this work period?"

## 💬 Practice Together (for grown-ups)

- Role-play the situation using a small picture timer or a safe pretend substitute; read the five steps together.
- Swap roles so your child can see you model **"I'll start with this part, then take a break."** and hear the other person's answer.
- Introduce this story's small surprise; practice "Which part belongs in this work period?" and allow a comfortable breath or pause.
- On a real outing, stay nearby, offer choices, and praise the specific step your child tried.

## 🌙 Goodnight Blessing

Goodnight, little beginning and carefully chosen steps.
Goodnight, pencil, resting after your climb.
Goodnight, Cat, preparing another nap.
May tomorrow begin with one manageable part.
