# 01 — Rosie Makes a Friend on the Train

**Life skill:** Starting a friendly conversation with another kid on a train (Taipei MRT).
**Magic sentence:** "Hi! I'm Rosie. What's your name?"
**Coaches:** Boba (Taiwan) + Momo Mochi (Japan)
**Setting:** Rosie's kitchen, then a Taipei MRT train on a sunny Saturday morning

---

## The Story

Rosie wanted to ride the **捷運** (jiéyùn) = MRT train with her big sister, Tori. She also wanted to make a friend on the train.

"But how?" asked Rosie. Her pink-striped straw drooped. "What's the first step?"

BOING! Boba bounced onto the kitchen table and landed right in the sugar bowl. "Let's bounce!" he said. "Oops. Now I'm a sugar pearl."

Momo Mochi squished in behind him. "In Tokyo, I ride the *densha* — the train — every day," she said. "Making a friend has steps."

**Rosie's Steps**
1. Find a kid who is not busy: no headphones, not sleeping.
2. Smile and give a small wave.
3. Use an inside voice: **"Hi! I'm Rosie. What's your name?"**
4. Listen to their name. Say, "Nice to meet you!"
5. Ask one easy question or say one nice thing.
6. At your stop, say, "Bye! Have a nice day!"

"Sleeping means busy," said Momo. She pointed at the fruit bowl. Cat the penguin was napping in it, between two oranges. Cat was the family's houseguest from Frostpeak. She napped like a cat — that's how she got her name.

"Five more minutes," mumbled Cat.

"Very busy," giggled Rosie.

Then it was time to practice. Momo pretended to be a kid on a train. She sat on a chair and swung her feet. Rosie did her steps:
1. Find a kid who is not busy: no headphones, not sleeping.
2. Smile and give a small wave.
3. Use an inside voice: **"Hi! I'm Rosie. What's your name?"**
4. Listen to their name. Say, "Nice to meet you!"
5. Ask one easy question or say one nice thing.
6. At your stop, say, "Bye! Have a nice day!"

"I'm Momo!" said Momo. They practiced three times. By the third time, Rosie's straw was wiggling.

On Saturday, everyone rode the MRT. Cat came too, in Tori's tote bag. They let people get off first. Ding-dong went the doors.

The train rocked gently. Cat was snoring before the doors even closed.

A big boy had headphones on. Busy. Boba pretended to snore. Momo squished his mouth shut.

Then Rosie saw a small round Sweet Potato Ball, swinging her feet. Not busy.

Rosie smiled and gave a small wave. "**Hi! I'm Rosie. What's your name?**"

The Sweet Potato Ball looked down at her shoes. She did not say anything.

Rosie felt nervous. Her straw drooped. That's okay.

Rosie did a Rose breath. Smell the rose — in, two, three, four. Blow out the candle — out, two, three, four.

She waited. Some kids need more time. Then she said one nice thing. "I like your backpack!"

The Sweet Potato Ball peeked up. "It has a star," she whispered. "I'm Guagua."

"Nice to meet you!" said Rosie. "Where are you going?"

"To the **動物園** (dòngwùyuán) = zoo," said Guagua. "To see the pandas!"

"SHE'S DOING IT!" whispered Boba. It was not a quiet whisper. Momo squished him again.

Ding-dong. It was Rosie's stop. "Bye! Have a nice day!" said Rosie.

"Bye, Rosie!" Guagua waved a big wave.

On the platform, Tori hugged her. "You've got this, little sip — and you did it!"

"I made a **朋友** (péngyǒu) = friend," said Rosie.

Cat finally opened one eye. "Are we there yet?" she yawned.

That night, Rosie snuggled into bed and thought about pandas. "One step at a time," she whispered. "One sip at a time."

## 🌹 Rosie's Steps (Recap)
1. Find a kid who is not busy: no headphones, not sleeping.
2. Smile and give a small wave.
3. Use an inside voice: **"Hi! I'm Rosie. What's your name?"**
4. Listen to their name. Say, "Nice to meet you!"
5. Ask one easy question or say one nice thing.
6. At your stop, say, "Bye! Have a nice day!"

**If something surprising happens:** Rose breath, then wait a few seconds and say one nice thing: "I like your ___!"

## 💬 Practice Together (for grown-ups)
- Line up two chairs like train seats. Take turns being Rosie and being "the kid." Swap in your child's own name for "Rosie."
- Play "Busy or Not Busy?": act out headphones, napping (a stuffed penguin works great), reading, or looking out the window, and let your child say which kids to leave alone.
- Practice the hiccup on purpose: as "the kid," look down and stay quiet. Praise the Rose breath, the waiting, and the backup nice thing — not just the happy ending.
- On a real ride, your child stays right next to you, chats only on the train, and does not share where they live or go anywhere with the other kid. Praise the try, even if the other kid stays quiet.

## 🌙 Goodnight Blessing
The train is sleeping in its station now.
Ding-dong, the doors are closed. Goodnight.
A little friend is dreaming of pandas.
Smell the rose. Blow out the candle. Goodnight, little sip.
