# 45 — Rosie Offers Help Without Taking Over

**Life skill:** Ask before helping and let the other person choose how much help to receive.
**Magic sentence:** "Would you like help, or time to try?"
**Coaches:** Boba 🇹🇼 + Estrella 🇨🇷
**Cat’s role:** kung fu practitioner
**Setting:** the family's chess-board cabinet (practice), then the hotel lobby's giant puzzle corner (real outing).

---

## The Story

Rosie liked the satisfying click when a puzzle piece found its place. She wanted to help Tang.

At the chess cabinet, Boba held a piece against his body: a puzzle wearing a pearl.

"What if I know where Tang's piece goes?" Rosie asked. Estrella rested her flippers beside the little board.

Boba was a glossy pearl bouncing gently.

Estrella was a turtle with kind eyes.

Cat the penguin practiced a gentle kung fu stance. Estrella chose to watch instead of joining.

Boba reached toward Estrella's puzzle, paused, and asked, **"Would you like help, or time to try?"** She chose time.

"A friend owns their task too," Estrella explained. "Knowing the answer does not give us permission to take over."

**Rosie's Steps**

1. Notice the task your friend is trying.
2. Ask before touching their things.
3. Listen to their choice.

4. Help only in the agreed way.
5. Let them keep control of their task.

Boba examined a round chess piece. "An excellent family resemblance. Slightly less expressive around the eyebrows."

Mom accompanied Rosie to the hotel puzzle corner, where broad pieces waited beside a giant floor picture.

Cat asked before moving the puzzle tray, then waited while Tang worked on his own piece.

Tang studied a corner piece. Rosie thought she recognized its place beside the picture of a yellow umbrella.

Step one: Rosie noticed Tang matching his piece. Step two: she kept her hands beside her cup.

**"Would you like help, or time to try?"** Step three: she listened. Tang wanted one more try alone.

Rosie worked on her own part, giving Tang room without reaching across his little task.

Then Tang asked for a hint, but Rosie almost began lifting his piece to put it in place.

She felt uncertain. Her straw drooped. That's okay; permission for a hint was not permission to do everything.

Rosie took a Rose breath.

Smell the rose: in, two, three, four.

Blow out the candle: out, two, three, four.

"Would a pointing hint help?" Rosie asked, leaving Tang's piece exactly where he had put it.

Tang nodded. Rosie pointed toward the umbrella's yellow corner without touching his piece or finishing his part.

Step four: she gave the agreed hint. Tang turned his own piece and tried the matching corner.

Step five: he kept control of the task. When it clicked, Rosie smiled at what Tang had done himself.

Estrella nodded. Boba admired the umbrella picture. "Unfortunately, too flat to shelter a very round pearl."

Cat offered to steady the tray. Tang agreed. "One steady flipper, not one takeover penguin," she said.

Tang thanked Rosie. Both friends still had puzzle parts to try.

Tori met Rosie beside the enormous umbrella stand and heard about Tang's careful corner-piece discovery.

"You offered help and respected the amount. You've got this, little sip," she said with a proud nod.

Rosie felt pleased. Helping could make someone else's turn easier without changing whose turn it was.

**加油** (jiāyóu) = go for it.

"One step at a time, one sip at a time," said Rosie.

## 🌹 Rosie's Steps (Recap)

1. Notice the task your friend is trying.
2. Ask before touching their things.
3. Listen to their choice.
4. Help only in the agreed way.
5. Let them keep control of their task.

**If something surprising happens:** Take a Rose breath or a comfortable pause, then say: "Would a pointing hint help?"

## 💬 Practice Together (for grown-ups)

- Role-play the situation using a large puzzle piece or a safe pretend substitute; read the five steps together.
- Swap roles so your child can see you model **"Would you like help, or time to try?"** and hear the other person's answer.
- Introduce this story's small surprise; practice "Would a pointing hint help?" and allow a comfortable breath or pause.
- On a real outing, stay nearby, offer choices, and praise the specific step your child tried.

## 🌙 Goodnight Blessing

Goodnight, puzzle pieces finding their own places.
Goodnight, helpful hints and patient hands.
Goodnight, Cat, one steady helpful flipper.
May care leave room for your own discoveries.
