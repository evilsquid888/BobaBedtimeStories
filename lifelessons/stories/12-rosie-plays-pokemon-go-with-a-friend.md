# 12 — Rosie Plays Pokémon GO with a Friend

**Life skill:** Playing a phone game (Pokémon GO) with a friend, safely and kindly — asking a grown-up first and playing only while they walk with you, stopping your feet before you look at the screen, taking turns, cheering a friend's catch, shrugging off a miss, and stopping when it's time.
**Magic sentence:** "Nice catch! Let's find the next one together."
**Coaches:** Marcus (teen Inclusive Navigator) + Pock Ramune (Japan crew)
**Cat’s role:** kung fu balance partner
**Setting:** Rosie's living-room floor turned into a masking-tape pretend town (practice), then the riverside park on a golden afternoon — a red arched footbridge, a stone temple square with a fountain and stone turtles, a mural wall, a shaved-ice cart — with Guagua the Sweet Potato Ball, Mom holding the phone, Tori waiting on the river steps, and Cat the penguin balancing beside a sun-warmed stone turtle

## The Story

Rosie the Rose Milk Tea had a new **朋友** (péngyǒu) = friend. Guagua, from the train!

Today they would play Pokémon GO on Mom's phone.

"But phones have so many rules," said Rosie. Her pink-striped straw drooped.

"Rules are just steps," said Marcus, the teen next door. He was an Inclusive Navigator.

"I've caught about a hundred of those. Mostly by accident."

FIZZZ! Pock Ramune popped his marble. "Pop the marble — let the fun fizz out!" Soda sprayed the lamp.

Cat the penguin practiced a kung fu balance pose. Her scarf needed practice.

Marcus taped a road across the floor. A crosswalk. A bridge of books.

"Here are the steps," said Marcus.

**Rosie's Steps**
1. Ask a grown-up first. Play only while they walk with you.
2. Feet stop, then look at the phone. Look up, then walk.
3. Take turns. One holds the phone. One watches the path.
4. When your friend catches one, say, **"Nice catch! Let's find the next one together."**
5. If one gets away, say, "Oh well! Next one."
6. When the grown-up says "Time's up," close the game and say "Good game!"

"Step two is the big one," said Marcus. Feet stopped. THEN he looked. Looked up. THEN he walked.

"Practice time!" said Marcus. "Pock is your friend." Rosie practiced:

On Saturday, Mom, Marcus, Rosie, Guagua, and Cat walked to the riverside **公園** (gōngyuán) = park.

Step one. "Mom, can we play?"

"Yes," said Mom. "I walk with you. I hold the phone when we walk."

At the red **橋** (qiáo) = bridge, a dot glowed. Step two. Rosie stopped her feet. THEN she looked.

A little round one! Flick. Caught! "Nice catch!" said Guagua.

Step three. Guagua's turn. Rosie watched the path. Guagua pointed at the fountain. "A blue one!"

"That's Cat," said Marcus. "Not a Pokémon. Just a penguin." "Kung fu penguin," said Cat, bowing.

By the mural wall, the screen sparkled. A sparkly one! Rosie had wanted one all week.

Flick. "Got it!" said Guagua. Rosie felt a little jealous. Her straw drooped. That's okay.

Rosie did a Rose breath. Smell the rose — in, two, three, four. Blow out the candle — out, two, three, four.

Step four. Rosie smiled. "**Nice catch! Let's find the next one together.**"

Guagua smiled her shy smile and held out the phone. "Your turn, Rosie."

Step five. "Oh well! Next one," said Rosie. "That one had places to be," said Marcus.

"Time's up!" said Mom. Step six. Rosie closed the game. "Good game!"

"Good game," said Guagua. "**一起** (yīqǐ) = together is the best part."

Tori waited on the steps. "Feet stopped AND you cheered for your friend! You've got this, little sip."

"One step at a time," Rosie said sleepily. "One sip at a time."

## 🌹 Rosie's Steps (Recap)
1. Ask a grown-up first. Play only while they walk with you.
2. Feet stop, then look at the phone. Look up, then walk.
3. Take turns. One holds the phone. One watches the path.
4. When your friend catches one, say, **"Nice catch! Let's find the next one together."**
5. If one gets away, say, "Oh well! Next one."
6. When the grown-up says "Time's up," close the game and say "Good game!"

**If something surprising happens:** (a friend catches the one you wanted, or yours gets away) Rose breath, then say: "Nice catch!" or "Oh well! Next one." A friend's catch is good news for the team.

## 💬 Practice Together (for grown-ups)
- Build a tape town on the floor: a masking-tape road, a crosswalk, a book bridge, and a cardboard "phone." Walk it together and over-act step two — freeze your feet like a statue, THEN look at the phone; look up, THEN walk. Make it silly so it sticks.
- Play two rounds of pretend: first your child holds the phone while you "watch the path," then swap. Praise the swap out loud: "You handed over the phone without being asked — that's step three."
- Practice the feelings on purpose. Catch the "sparkly one" yourself and let your child do a Rose breath and say "Nice catch!" Then let one "get away" so they can shrug and say "Oh well! Next one." Cheer the words, not the catch.
- For the real thing: it's the grown-up's phone, the grown-up walks with the kids and carries the phone between stops, and the game only comes out when feet are stopped in a safe spot (never at a curb or on a bridge step). Give a two-minute warning before "Time's up," and end with "Good game!" every time.

## 🌙 Goodnight Blessing
Goodnight, little sip, feet stopped and eyes up.
Goodnight, red bridge, resting over the river.
Goodnight, Cat, balancing feet now still.
Nice catch today. Sleep tight, friend.
