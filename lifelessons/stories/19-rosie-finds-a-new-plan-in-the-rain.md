# 19 — Rosie Finds a New Plan in the Rain

**Life skill:** Handle a changed plan by choosing between realistic alternatives.
**Magic sentence:** "What can we do instead?"
**Coaches:** Bīng 🇹🇼 + Slowy the Sloth 🇨🇷
**Setting:** a backyard camping tent (practice), then the lakeside visitor lodge (real outing).

---

## The Story

Rosie planned a paddleboat outing with Lulu, complete with a paper captain's hat and a picnic.

Inside the backyard tent, she pictured herself steering past ducks, politely refusing every request to share lunch.

Then Mom explained that heavy rain could change their outing. Rosie gripped her hat, suddenly less captain-like.

Bīng arrived, shaved ice wearing enormous sunglasses.

Slowy was a sloth taking his time.

Cat the penguin napped on a rolled camping mat.

Slowy heard a pretend weather change and asked Bīng, **"What can we do instead?"** before choosing crafts.

"You can feel disappointed and still choose something," Bīng said. "I contain at least three feelings per scoop."

**Rosie's Steps**

1. Listen to the changed plan.
2. Name how you feel.
3. Ask about another choice.

4. Choose an available option.
5. Start the new plan together.

**選擇** (xuǎnzé) = choices. Rosie tried **"What can we do instead?"** Slowy the Sloth pretended.

1. Listen to the changed plan.
2. Name how you feel.
3. Ask about another choice.

4. Choose an available option.
5. Start the new plan together.

Rosie practiced choosing between a picture puzzle and folding boats, giving her paper captain a new job.

Slowy folded one corner very slowly. "Puuura... viiida..." Bīng called it the world's most peaceful boat launch.

At the lakeside lodge, Mom explained that today's paddleboat rides were closed because of the weather.

Lulu arrived with her own little hat. Outside, rain decorated the window with long silver racing stripes.

Cat claimed a padded boot-bench seat. Her scarf hung over the edge like a very sleepy flag.

Step one: Rosie listened to Mom explain the closure. Nobody was going onto the water today.

Step two: "I feel disappointed," she said. She had wanted to try steering, not watch raindrops race.

Step three: **"What can we do instead?"** Mom pointed toward two dry activities inside the visitor lodge.

The model-boat workshop was already full. That was the choice Rosie had been hoping to make.

Her straw drooped. Rosie felt frustrated. That's okay; she could ask about what was actually available.

Rosie took a Rose breath.

Smell the rose: in, two, three, four.

Blow out the candle: out, two, three, four.

"What other choices are ready now?" Rosie asked. Mom checked the lodge's craft table and picture shelf.

They could fold paper boats or explore the lake puzzle. Both were inside, warm, and ready for them.

Step four: Rosie chose paper boats. Lulu chose them too, and they sat together at the craft table.

Step five: they started folding. Mom helped with one tricky corner, while Rosie pressed the crease flat.

Bīng made a boat with enormous paper sails. Slowy was still choosing a very thoughtful first corner.

Cat peered over the boot bench. "If your boats need a captain, I can supervise from this cushion."

Rosie sailed her boat across the tabletop. It had traveled nowhere and somehow still had an adventure.

Tori joined them beneath the lodge's model lighthouse, examining Rosie's new boat and slightly soggy captain's hat.

"A new plan can still be yours. You've got this, little sip," she said, admiring the folds.

Rosie kept her wish for another paddleboat day, and let this rainy afternoon become something different.

**加油** (jiāyóu) = go for it.

"One step at a time, one sip at a time," said Rosie.

---

## 🌹 Rosie's Steps (Recap)

1. Listen to the changed plan.
2. Name how you feel.
3. Ask about another choice.
4. Choose an available option.
5. Start the new plan together.

**If something surprising happens:** Take a Rose breath or a comfortable pause, then say: "What other choices are ready now?"

## 💬 Practice Together (for grown-ups)

- Role-play the situation using a folded paper boat or a safe pretend substitute; read the five steps together.
- Swap roles so your child can see you model **"What can we do instead?"** and hear the other person's answer.
- Introduce this story's small surprise; practice "What other choices are ready now?" and allow a comfortable breath or pause.
- On a real outing, stay nearby, offer choices, and praise the specific step your child tried.
