# 54 — Cat, Loren, and the Wiggly Worm

**Life skill:** When something goes wildly wrong, stop chasing it: freeze, breathe, ask for the simplest fix, then fix it at the source or ask a grown-up for help.
**Magic sentence:** "What's the simplest fix?"
**Coaches:** Pock Ramune 🇯🇵 + Taro 🇹🇼
**Cat’s role:** lead car-washer and hose-chaser (a kung fu sponge chop, a belly-first pounce), then practices the steps with her best friend
**Friends:** Loren the Axolotl (new, Cat's best friend), Tori (it's her car)
**Original story:** "The Wiggly Worm" by Cat Chen, kept close to the original; the steps and coaches are added
**Setting:** the building's shady front porch (practice), then the sunny curb in front of Rosie's building, beside Tori's little lemon-yellow car (real outing).

## The Story

Water went everywhere. It was crazy. Who could have known washing a car would be so hard?

On a hot, sunny day, Cat the penguin and Loren the axolotl went to wash Tori's car.

They got everything ready. Sponges and rags. Soap and water. **洗車** (xǐchē) = car wash time!

They were having fun. They made patterns in the bubbles and scrubbed hard.

"Loren, get the windows! I'll get the trunk," Cat called, scrubbing. They got every single side.

"Let's get the hose," said Loren.

"Okay," said Cat. They turned on the tap. But nobody was holding the hose.

The hose went crazy! Water went everywhere. It flung into the air like a giant wiggly worm.

"We have to catch it!" said Cat, dodging.

"How?" asked Loren. The hose got her in the gills.

"I don't know. Ahh!" cried Cat. The hose got her too. They chased it and got soaked.

"I caught it!" Cat cheered. Then it slipped right out of her flippers.

It sprayed her in the face, as if it was mad at her for grabbing it.

"Cat!" Loren groaned. She grabbed for it. It slipped right past her.

"Ugh!" they groaned, and started laughing.

They laughed so hard, they forgot about the hose. When they looked up, it was gone!

"Where is it?" Cat wondered. Then they saw it, swinging around on the ground.

Cat looked at Loren. Loren looked at Cat. They counted to three. One, two, three, go!

Cat jumped on it. Loren grabbed the top. Then they turned it off.

That's when they realized. They could have just turned it off, instead of chasing it!

They looked at each other. "We could have just turned it off!" they said together.

Tori came out with lemonade. Her car sparkled. Cat and Loren dripped.

"Best car wash ever," Tori giggled. "Wettest washers, too."

Pock Ramune fizzed up. "When I fizz over, I don't chase it. I cap it!"

Taro pushed up his glasses. "Wild messes have steps."

**Cat's Steps**

1. Stop chasing. Freeze your feet.

2. Penguin breath: in for four, out for four.

3. Ask, "What's the simplest fix?"

4. Fix it, or ask a grown-up.

Loren played the hose, wiggling across the porch.

Cat froze, breathed, and asked, **"What's the simplest fix?"**

1. Stop chasing. Freeze your feet.

2. Penguin breath: in for four, out for four.

3. Ask, "What's the simplest fix?"

4. Fix it, or ask a grown-up.

Pock fizzed with pride, then capped himself.

Rinse time. Cat turned the tap. Pop! Off flew the nozzle.

Step one: Cat froze. Step two: Penguin breath, in for four, out for four.

Step three: **"What's the simplest fix?"** Loren pointed at the tap.

The tap was stuck. Cat felt frustrated. That's okay.

Step four: "Tori, can you help me?" Tori and Cat turned it together. **關掉** (guān diào) = off!

Tori hugged them, soggy and all. "You've got this, little penguin."

It was a wet day. But bet you never had a funner time washing a car!

## 🌹 Cat's Steps (Recap)

1. Stop chasing. Freeze your feet.
2. Penguin breath: in for four, out for four.
3. Ask, "What's the simplest fix?"
4. Fix it, or ask a grown-up.

**If something surprising happens:** Take a Penguin breath, then say: "Can you help me?"

## 💬 Practice Together (for grown-ups)

- Play "wiggly hose": one of you wiggles and flops around like a runaway hose while the other practices freezing their feet instead of chasing. Swap roles; the hose gets to be as silly as it likes.
- After any small mess (spilled water, a toy that keeps rolling away, a tangled kite string), ask together, "What's the simplest fix?" and look for where the trouble starts: the tap, the cap, the switch.
- Practice the Penguin breath, in for four and out for four, before you go looking for the fix. Calm eyes find the tap faster.
- Praise asking for help as a real step. Some taps and lids are too stiff for small flippers, and "Can you help me?" is the right fix.

## 🌙 Goodnight Blessing

Goodnight, little penguin, dry and warm.
Goodnight, Loren, gills tucked in tight.
Goodnight, wiggly hose, coiled up still.
May every wild day end in giggles.
