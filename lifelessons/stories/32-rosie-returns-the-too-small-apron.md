# 32 — Rosie Returns the Too-Small Apron

**Life skill:** Ask a shop about an exchange and accept the available options.
**Magic sentence:** "Could I exchange this for another size, please?"
**Coaches:** Feng 🇹🇼 + Niko Nigiri 🇯🇵
**Setting:** the costume shop's fitting alcove (practice), then the craft-supply shop's exchange counter (real outing).

---

## The Story

Rosie's new craft apron was too small. Its ties barely reached around her little tea cup.

At the folding counter, Mom kept the receipt safe while Rosie wondered what to say at the shop.

Feng inspected the apron. "Beautiful wrapping," he sighed. "Unfortunately, this wrapping has misunderstood the parcel."

Feng arrived, pineapple cake in fancy wrapping.

Niko was a remarkably tidy salmon nigiri.

Cat the penguin napped on a basket of folded washcloths.

Niko showed Feng the apron and receipt. **"Could I exchange this for another size, please?"** he asked.

"The worker explains the shop's options," Feng said. "We ask politely, then decide with our grown-up."

**Rosie's Steps**

1. Go with your grown-up.
2. Bring the item and receipt.
3. Wait for the worker.

4. Ask about an exchange.
5. Choose from the offered options.

**尺寸** (chǐcùn) = size. Rosie tried **"Could I exchange this for another size, please?"** Niko Nigiri pretended.

1. Go with your grown-up.
2. Bring the item and receipt.
3. Wait for the worker.

4. Ask about an exchange.
5. Choose from the offered options.

Rosie practiced keeping the apron folded and asking her question while Niko pretended to check the receipt.

Feng tied a ribbon around a washcloth. "A perfect fit. This customer has unusually cooperative corners."

Mom accompanied Rosie to the craft shop, where aprons hung beside rolls of bright cotton fabric.

Cat dozed on a padded parcel stool, wrapped comfortably in her own entirely satisfactory yellow scarf.

Rosie placed the folded apron beside Mom's receipt, waiting while the worker finished helping another customer.

Step one: Rosie stayed with Mom. Step two: they had brought both the apron and receipt.

Step three: she waited for the worker. Step four: **"Could I exchange this for another size, please?"**

The worker checked the purchase and showed them where the larger aprons usually hung beside the counter.

The larger apron in Rosie's favorite blue was sold out. A green one was still available.

Rosie felt disappointed. Her straw drooped. That's okay; asking did not promise every color would be ready.

Rosie took a Rose breath.

Smell the rose: in, two, three, four.

Blow out the candle: out, two, three, four.

"What other options are available?" Rosie asked, keeping the original apron folded neatly on the counter.

The worker explained the permitted choices. Mom helped Rosie understand them before they decided what to do.

Step five: Rosie chose the green apron, after Mom checked that its size and the exchange were right.

They completed the exchange as the worker instructed, then thanked her and put their new receipt safely away.

Niko admired the tidy fold. Feng nodded. "The parcel now has breathing room. My wrapping standards are satisfied."

Cat lifted her scarf. "My size is penguin. I recommend checking for adequate space around the nap."

Rosie laughed, holding the green apron close. It could be a good choice even in a different color.

Tori met Rosie beside the shop's spool display, where the new apron rested folded in its paper bag.

"You asked and chose from the options. You've got this, little sip," she said, admiring the green.

Rosie felt proud. The little apron could finally fit around the little cup who would wear it.

**加油** (jiāyóu) = go for it.

"One step at a time, one sip at a time," said Rosie.

## 🌹 Rosie's Steps (Recap)

1. Go with your grown-up.
2. Bring the item and receipt.
3. Wait for the worker.
4. Ask about an exchange.
5. Choose from the offered options.

**If something surprising happens:** Take a Rose breath or a comfortable pause, then say: "What other options are available?"

## 💬 Practice Together (for grown-ups)

- Role-play the situation using a small folded apron or a safe pretend substitute; read the five steps together.
- Swap roles so your child can see you model **"Could I exchange this for another size, please?"** and hear the other person's answer.
- Introduce this story's small surprise; practice "What other options are available?" and allow a comfortable breath or pause.
- On a real outing, stay nearby, offer choices, and praise the specific step your child tried.

## 🌙 Goodnight Blessing

Goodnight, little apron with room to fit.
Goodnight, folded cloth and safe receipts.
Goodnight, Cat, comfortably penguin-sized.
May tomorrow's choices feel right for you.
