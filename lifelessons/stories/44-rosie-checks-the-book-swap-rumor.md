# 44 — Rosie Checks the Book-Swap Rumor

**Life skill:** Treat an uncertain claim as uncertain and check with someone who knows.
**Magic sentence:** "I don't know if that's true. Let's check."
**Coaches:** Taro 🇹🇼 + Momo Mochi 🇯🇵
**Cat’s role:** helper
**Setting:** the model village display shelf (practice), then the town square book-swap marquee (real outing).

---

## The Story

Rosie hoped to swap a boat book, but Guagua had heard that the stall was gone.

A folded paper cover hid a toy shop on the model village shelf. It certainly looked absent.

"A cover can hide things," Taro said. Momo squished beneath a card roof. "Including a perfectly good mochi."



Momo was pink mochi with peachy cheeks.

Cat the penguin sorted returned books. A fish bookmark kept escaping its shelf like a tiny slippery librarian.

Momo guessed that a covered toy shop had vanished. Taro answered, **"I don't know if that's true. Let's check."**

"Ask how somebody knows," Taro explained. "A guess can be honest, but we still need to call it a guess."

**Rosie's Steps**

1. Pause before repeating the claim.
2. Ask how the person knows.
3. Say when you are unsure.

4. Check with someone responsible.
5. Share only what you have checked.

Momo lifted the paper roof. "Shop recovered. Mochi also recovered. Both would appreciate a slightly taller ceiling."

Mom took Rosie to the marquee, where book crates stood beside a folded cover and reading stools.

Cat carried the empty book basket to the organizer, then checked which shelf was actually closing.

Guagua pointed at the folded stall cover. "I think the book swap has closed for good," he said.

Step one: Rosie paused before telling anyone else. Step two: she asked Guagua how he knew about the closure.

"I saw the cover yesterday," he said. Step three: **"I don't know if that's true. Let's check."**

Step four: Rosie stayed with Mom and asked the organizer what was happening with the stall.

The organizer said the swap had moved to another table. Rosie had expected either open here or closed forever.

She felt confused. Her straw drooped. That's okay; the checked answer could be different from both early guesses.

Rosie took a Rose breath.

Smell the rose: in, two, three, four.

Blow out the candle: out, two, three, four.

"Could you show us where it is now?" Rosie asked, keeping her boat book beside her cup.

The organizer pointed to the book crates under the other side of the marquee. Mom went with both friends.

Step five: Rosie told Guagua the checked information: the book swap was open at the other table today.

Guagua nodded. "I was guessing from the cover." They found the boat-book basket and checked how the swap worked.

Taro adjusted his glasses. Momo examined the stool. "Reliable report: this seat has room for mochi."

Cat lifted the basket. "Rumor says this basket contains a penguin. Check again: the penguin is carrying it."

Rosie laughed. Looking carefully could change a first guess.

Tori met Rosie beside the paper-book bunting and admired her newly chosen boat book.

"You checked before passing it on. You've got this, little sip," she said, keeping her voice warm.

Rosie felt proud. She could enjoy storybooks while checking the everyday stories people told each other.

**加油** (jiāyóu) = go for it.

"One step at a time, one sip at a time," said Rosie.

## 🌹 Rosie's Steps (Recap)

1. Pause before repeating the claim.
2. Ask how the person knows.
3. Say when you are unsure.
4. Check with someone responsible.
5. Share only what you have checked.

**If something surprising happens:** Take a Rose breath or a comfortable pause, then say: "Could you show us where it is now?"

## 💬 Practice Together (for grown-ups)

- Role-play the situation using a little question-picture card or a safe pretend substitute; read the five steps together.
- Swap roles so your child can see you model **"I don't know if that's true. Let's check."** and hear the other person's answer.
- Introduce this story's small surprise; practice "Could you show us where it is now?" and allow a comfortable breath or pause.
- On a real outing, stay nearby, offer choices, and praise the specific step your child tried.
