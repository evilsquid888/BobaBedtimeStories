# 13 — Rosie Rides the Bus

**Life skill:** Riding the city bus with a grown-up — standing back from the curb, letting people off first, tapping your card, asking the driver if the bus goes where you're going, holding the pole, pressing the stop bell at the right time, and thanking the driver.
**Magic sentence:** "Excuse me, does this bus go to the zoo?"
**Coaches:** Tico the Toucan (Costa Rica) + Taro (Taiwan)
**Cat’s role:** snowboard safety coach
**Setting:** The glass bus shelter on Rosie's ginkgo-lined street (practice, with a cardboard-box bus), then the real number-twelve city bus to the zoo — with Cat the penguin checking her snowboard helmet and riding seated beside Mom

---

## The Story

Rosie the Rose Milk Tea was going to the **動物園** (dòngwùyuán) = zoo. On the **公車** (gōngchē) = bus!

"A real bus," Rosie whispered. "With a real driver." Her pink-striped straw drooped. "What if I get on the wrong one?"

"BEAK THE NEWS!" Tico the Toucan landed on the bus-stop bench. "Buses have steps! I know them ALL!"

Taro shuffled up slowly, pushing his reading glasses up. "Good things take time to steam. Buses, too."

Cat the penguin checked her snowboard helmet beside the bench. "On snow, I check my gear. Here, we check our bus steps," she taught Rosie.

"Here are the steps," said Taro.

**Rosie's Steps**
1. Wait at the bus stop. Stand back from the curb.
2. Let people get off first.
3. Step on and tap your card. Ask, **"Excuse me, does this bus go to the zoo?"**
4. Hold the pole. Sit down if there is a seat.
5. Press the stop bell when you see the stop before yours.
6. Say "Thank you!" to the driver and step off.

"Practice time!" said Tico. He climbed into a cardboard-box bus with paper-plate wheels. "I am the **司機** (sījī) = driver. ***¡Vamos!*** = let's go!"

Taro played the passenger getting off. It took a while.

Rosie practiced:
1. Wait at the bus stop. Stand back from the curb.
2. Let people get off first.
3. Step on and tap your card. Ask, **"Excuse me, does this bus go to the zoo?"**
4. Hold the pole. Sit down if there is a seat.
5. Press the stop bell when you see the stop before yours.
6. Say "Thank you!" to the driver and step off.

"NEXT STOP, THE MOON!" shouted Tico. The box fell over. "Bumpy road."

On Saturday, Rosie and Mom waited at the real bus stop. Rosie stood back from the curb. Cat held her helmet beside Mom.

A bus hissed to a stop. Step two. Rosie let the people get off first. Step three. She tapped her card. Beep!

"**Excuse me, does this bus go to the zoo?**"

"Not this one, sweetie," said the driver kindly. "The twelve goes to the zoo."

That was not like practice. Tico's bus always went to the zoo. Rosie felt flustered. Her straw drooped. That's okay.

Rosie did a Rose breath. Smell the rose — in, two, three, four. Blow out the candle — out, two, three, four.

"Okay! Thank you!" She stepped off with Mom, and they waited again.

Then came a bus with a big twelve on the front. Rosie did every step. Beep! "**Excuse me, does this bus go to the zoo?**"

"Yes it does! Hop on."

Step four. Rosie held the yellow pole until the bus stopped rocking. Then she sat by the window.

Cat sat beside Mom, holding her helmet firmly in her lap. "Ready for the ride," said Mom.

Shops slid by. A temple roof. A river bridge.

Step five. Rosie saw the stop before the zoo — a big giraffe-shaped sign! She pressed the red button. Ding!

"Helmet gathered!" said Cat. Mom helped her down after the bus stopped.

Step six. "Thank you! **謝謝** (xièxie)!" said Rosie. The driver tipped her cap.

At the zoo gate stood Tori, waving both arms. "You rode the bus! You've got this, little sip!"

"One step at a time," said Rosie. "One stop at a time."

## 🌹 Rosie's Steps (Recap)
1. Wait at the bus stop. Stand back from the curb.
2. Let people get off first.
3. Step on and tap your card. Ask, **"Excuse me, does this bus go to the zoo?"**
4. Hold the pole. Sit down if there is a seat.
5. Press the stop bell when you see the stop before yours.
6. Say "Thank you!" to the driver and step off.

**If something surprising happens:** Rose breath, then say: "Okay! Thank you!" — and wait for the right bus with your grown-up. The wrong bus is not a mistake; it is just the wrong bus.

## 💬 Practice Together (for grown-ups)
- Build a box bus: a big cardboard box, paper-plate wheels, a saucer taped by the door as the card reader, a jar lid as the stop button. Take turns being the driver and the passenger who gets off first (go slowly, like Taro!) so your child practices standing back and waiting before stepping on.
- Practice the wrong-bus surprise on purpose: as the driver, sometimes say "Not this one — the twelve goes to the zoo." Praise the Rose breath and the cheerful "Okay! Thank you!" as much as the magic sentence.
- Praise the safety steps out loud: "You stood back from the curb." "You held the pole until the bus stopped rocking." Swap in your own destination: "Excuse me, does this bus go to ___?"
- For the real thing, pick a short ride at a quiet time, sit near the driver, and let your child tap the card, ask the question, and press the bell. Agree on a landmark for "the stop before ours" ahead of time.

## 🌙 Goodnight Blessing
Goodnight, little rider, your bus card tucked away.
Goodnight, yellow poles, standing still in the dark.
Goodnight, Cat, helmet safely tucked away.
One step, one stop, one sip. Sleep tight.
