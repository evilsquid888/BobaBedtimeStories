# 26 — Rosie Keeps Her Promise to Water

**Life skill:** Remember an agreed task and tell a grown-up when help is needed.
**Magic sentence:** "I promised to help. Can we check the plan?"
**Coaches:** Niko Nigiri 🇯🇵 + Lala 🇨🇷
**Cat’s role:** helper
**Setting:** the utility room drying rack (practice), then the school seedling conservatory (real outing).

---

## The Story

Rosie had promised to water the class seedlings with Mom after school, before the afternoon games began.

At the utility rack, a picture of a watering jug reminded her what she had agreed to do.

Lala hugged a towel. "Soak it all in," she said. Excellent advice for the laundry.

Niko was a remarkably tidy salmon nigiri.

Lala was a soft cake wearing cream.

Cat the penguin drew a watering reminder. Her seedling looked like a broccoli wearing a crown.

Niko checked the picture reminder and told Lala, **"I promised to help. Can we check the plan?"**

"A promise needs a clear job and a reminder," he explained. "Asking for help keeps the job possible."

**Rosie's Steps**

1. Agree on one clear task.
2. Choose a reminder with your grown-up.
3. Check the reminder when it is time.

4. Do the task as agreed.
5. Tell your grown-up if you need help.

Rosie practiced checking the picture after a pretend school bell, then pouring a small amount into an empty tray.

Lala admired her careful pour. "Excellent. The floor has politely declined to become a swimming pool today."

After school, Mom met Rosie at the seedling conservatory, where rows of little plants waited on low shelves.

Cat checked the drip tray, then held a cloth ready while Rosie measured the water.

Cat napped on a folded towel beside an empty drip tray, contributing exactly zero water to the plants.

Step one: Rosie remembered their agreement: water the tray with Mom, using the amount they had checked.

Step two: their picture reminder was ready. Step three: Rosie looked at it when school had finished.

**"I promised to help. Can we check the plan?"** she asked Mom before lifting the little jug.

The jug was empty, and the nearest tap was too high for Rosie's tiny arms to reach.

She felt worried about finishing. Her straw drooped. That's okay; remembering did not mean doing everything alone.

Rosie took a Rose breath.

Smell the rose: in, two, three, four.

Blow out the candle: out, two, three, four.

"I need help filling the jug, please," Rosie told Mom, placing it safely on the low shelf.

Mom filled it to their agreed mark. They checked the tray together before Rosie began to pour.

Step four: Rosie watered as agreed, slowly moving between the seedlings while Mom watched the amount.

Step five: she had asked for help when the tap was out of reach, keeping their plan workable.

Niko set the jug back neatly. Lala examined a damp leaf. "An excellent drink. Better manners than my towel."

Cat wiped one splash. "I promised to dry this spot. The towel has been expecting my arrival."

Rosie joined the game. The seedlings had their promised drink.

Tori met Rosie beside the conservatory's potting sink, where the little jug stood upside down.

"You remembered and asked for help. You've got this, little sip," she said, admiring Rosie's picture reminder.

Rosie felt proud. The seedlings had water, and there was still time left for fun.

**加油** (jiāyóu) = go for it.

"One step at a time, one sip at a time," said Rosie.

## 🌹 Rosie's Steps (Recap)

1. Agree on one clear task.
2. Choose a reminder with your grown-up.
3. Check the reminder when it is time.
4. Do the task as agreed.
5. Tell your grown-up if you need help.

**If something surprising happens:** Take a Rose breath or a comfortable pause, then say: "I need help filling the jug, please."

## 💬 Practice Together (for grown-ups)

- Role-play the situation using a small watering jug or a safe pretend substitute; read the five steps together.
- Swap roles so your child can see you model **"I promised to help. Can we check the plan?"** and hear the other person's answer.
- Introduce this story's small surprise; practice "I need help filling the jug, please." and allow a comfortable breath or pause.
- On a real outing, stay nearby, offer choices, and praise the specific step your child tried.

## 🌙 Goodnight Blessing

Goodnight, watered seedlings and remembered promises.
Goodnight, jug, resting beside the sink.
Goodnight, Cat, towel folded after helping.
May small caring tasks grow into trust.
