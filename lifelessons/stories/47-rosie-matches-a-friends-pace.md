# 47 — Rosie Matches a Friend's Pace

**Life skill:** Agree on a comfortable shared pace rather than rushing a slower companion.
**Magic sentence:** "Let's choose a pace that works for both of us."
**Coaches:** Mango 🇹🇼 + Slowy the Sloth 🇨🇷
**Cat’s role:** snowboarder
**Setting:** the covered footbridge lookout (practice), then the botanical garden's sundial path (real outing).

---

## The Story

Rosie wanted to see the garden's giant sundial with Guagua, who liked stopping to notice tiny details.

At the footbridge lookout, Mango posed beside pebble markers. Slowy was still considering the first pebble.

"Must we move alike?" Rosie asked. Mango tapped his sweatband. "Even sports have more than one speed."

Mango's sports sweatband was bright red.

Slowy was a sloth taking his time.

Cat the penguin remembered learning to snowboard. Her friend had chosen a gentler slope; they rode it together.

Mango listened to Slowy's comfortable speed. **"Let's choose a pace that works for both of us,"** he said.

"Together means checking on each other," Slowy explained. "We can enjoy the path without making it a race."

Guagua spotted a tiny beetle beside a sculpture. Rosie was already picturing the sundial farther along the path.

Step one: Rosie noticed they were not moving together. Step two: she asked Guagua about his pace.

"I like a little time to look," he said. **"Let's choose a pace that works for both of us."**

Step three: they agreed to move with Mom between the sculptures, leaving time for Guagua's little discoveries.

At the next sculpture, Guagua wanted a pause longer than Rosie had expected. Her sundial wish felt impatient.

Rosie felt restless. Her straw drooped. That's okay; sharing a pace could need more than one conversation.

Rosie took a Rose breath.

Smell the rose: in, two, three, four.

Blow out the candle: out, two, three, four.

"Can we check our plan again?" Rosie asked, staying with Mom and Guagua beside the sculpture.

They agreed to pause here, then continue to the sundial together. Mom helped them keep their shared plan comfortable.

Step four: Rosie stayed with Mom and her friend, rather than going ahead alone to the sundial.

Step five: they checked again after the pause. Guagua was ready, and they enjoyed the giant bronze dial together.

Mango admired its shadow. Slowy nodded. "Puuura... viiida... An excellent clock. It understands that shadows need time."

Cat paused beside the beetle picture. "We choose a comfortable pace on snow too. These beetles prefer tiny steps."

Guagua grinned. The shared path held discoveries worth slowing for.

Tori met Rosie at the bronze leaf arch and admired their little route picture.

"You made room for another pace. You've got this, little sip," she said, listening to the beetle story.

Rosie felt proud. The sundial had waited, and the path had offered more than one discovery.

**加油** (jiāyóu) = go for it.

"One step at a time, one sip at a time," said Rosie.

## 🌹 Rosie's Steps (Recap)

1. Notice whether you are together.
2. Ask what pace feels comfortable.
3. Agree on a shared pace.
4. Stay with your grown-up and friend.
5. Check again if someone needs a pause.

**If something surprising happens:** Take a Rose breath or a comfortable pause, then say: "Can we check our plan again?"

## 💬 Practice Together (for grown-ups)

- Role-play the situation using a little route picture or a safe pretend substitute; read the five steps together.
- Swap roles so your child can see you model **"Let's choose a pace that works for both of us."** and hear the other person's answer.
- Introduce this story's small surprise; practice "Can we check our plan again?" and allow a comfortable breath or pause.
- On a real outing, stay nearby, offer choices, and praise the specific step your child tried.
