# 25 — Rosie Says No to a Hug

**Life skill:** Express a personal boundary and choose whether to offer another greeting.
**Magic sentence:** "No hug, thank you. I'd like to wave."
**Coaches:** Mochi 🇹🇼 + Estrella 🇨🇷
**Setting:** the balcony hammock corner (practice), then the family reunion courtyard (real outing).

---

## The Story

Rosie loved seeing family, but sometimes she wanted a wave instead of a hug around her little cup.

On the balcony, Mochi stretched his arms like soft ribbons, then carefully folded them beside his own body.

"A hug needs two yeses," Estrella said. "Your greeting can be warm even when your arms stay home."

Mochi arrived, a white mochi stretching gently.

Estrella arrived, a turtle with kind eyes.

Cat the penguin napped on a woven hammock cushion.

Estrella asked about a hug. Mochi replied, **"No hug, thank you. I'd like to wave."** She waved back.

"Another greeting is optional," Mochi explained. "You can also say no and keep your hands beside your cup."

**Rosie's Steps**

1. Notice whether you want a hug.
2. Say your choice clearly.
3. Offer another greeting if you want.

4. Repeat your choice if needed.
5. Tell your grown-up if it is ignored.

**練習** (liànxí) = practice. Rosie tried **"No hug, thank you. I'd like to wave."** Estrella pretended.

2. Say your choice clearly.

Rosie practiced choosing a wave, then a simple hello, while Estrella respected each answer and gave her space.

Mochi waved so enthusiastically that his arm resembled a noodle. "My hello comes in the extra-long size."

Mom brought Rosie to the reunion courtyard, where family photographs stood beside paper fans and round tables.

Cat napped beneath the greeting arch on a bench cushion, greeting everybody with impressive stillness.

A visiting auntie opened her arms toward Rosie, smiling warmly. Mom stayed nearby and let Rosie answer.

Step one: Rosie checked her feeling. She wanted to say hello, but she did not want a hug.

Step two: **"No hug, thank you. I'd like to wave."** Step three: she gave a small friendly wave.

The auntie lowered her arms and waved back. Rosie felt comfortable standing beside Mom at the greeting arch.

Later, another relative did not hear Rosie's first answer and leaned closer, still expecting a hug.

Rosie felt tense. Her straw drooped. That's okay; her answer still counted even if somebody had missed it.

Rosie took a Rose breath.

Smell the rose: in, two, three, four.

Blow out the candle: out, two, three, four.

"I don't want a hug. Mom, please help," Rosie said, repeating her choice and moving close to Mom.

Mom calmly explained the boundary. The relative stepped back and offered a hello from a comfortable distance.

Step four: Rosie had repeated her choice. Step five: she had asked her grown-up to help protect it.

She joined the picture table, choosing where to stand while Mom stayed close and the family gave her room.

Estrella waved. Mochi folded his arms. "Today my noodles are practicing excellent manners."

Someone offered Cat a cuddle. She blinked and tucked her scarf closer. "A respectful wave suits this cushion."

Rosie laughed softly. The courtyard felt friendly, and she could greet people while keeping her own comfortable space.

Tori met Rosie beside the reunion's paper-fan rack, offering a choice between a wave and a side hug.

Rosie chose a wave. "You've got this, little sip," Tori said, answering with her own bright wave.

Rosie felt proud. Her voice could name her choice, and her family could listen.

**加油** (jiāyóu) = go for it.

"One step at a time, one sip at a time," said Rosie.

## 🌹 Rosie's Steps (Recap)

1. Notice whether you want a hug.
2. Say your choice clearly.
3. Offer another greeting if you want.
4. Repeat your choice if needed.
5. Tell your grown-up if it is ignored.

**If something surprising happens:** Take a Rose breath or a comfortable pause, then say: "I don't want a hug. Mom, please help."

## 💬 Practice Together (for grown-ups)

- Role-play the situation using a little greeting puppet or a safe pretend substitute; read the five steps together.
- Swap roles so your child can see you model **"No hug, thank you. I'd like to wave."** and hear the other person's answer.
- Introduce this story's small surprise; practice "I don't want a hug. Mom, please help." and allow a comfortable breath or pause.
- On a real outing, stay nearby, offer choices, and praise the specific step your child tried.

## 🌙 Goodnight Blessing

Goodnight, waving hands and thoughtful greetings.
Goodnight, Cat, snug in your scarf.
May your choices be heard with care.
