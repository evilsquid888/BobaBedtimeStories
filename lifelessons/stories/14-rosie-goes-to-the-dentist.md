# 14 — Rosie Goes to the Dentist

**Life skill:** A dentist checkup — checking in at the front desk, waiting for your name, climbing into the big chair, opening wide, raising your hand when you need a break, and saying thank you and picking a sticker.
**Magic sentence:** "Hi, I'm Rosie. I'm here for my checkup."
**Coaches:** Niko Nigiri (Japan) + Don Café (Costa Rica)
**Cat’s role:** kung fu breathing companion
**Setting:** The family bathroom on a sunny morning (practice: the bathtub is the big chair, the sink is the front desk), then a bright, friendly dentist's office with a tooth-shaped fish tank and a cloud-and-balloon ceiling — where Cat the penguin keeps calm company with a picture book

## The Story

Rosie the Rose Milk Tea had a checkup on Friday at the **牙醫** (yáyī) = dentist.

"What's the next step?" she asked. Her pink-striped straw drooped.

"A checkup has steps," said Niko Nigiri, snapping on tiny gloves. "Let's do this neatly... or deliciously!"

Don Café tipped his straw hat. "And waiting. Good things brew slowly."

Niko tied on a paper mask. "The bathtub is the big chair." A folded towel was the headrest. The shower head was the light.

**Rosie's Steps**
1. Walk to the front desk and say, **"Hi, I'm Rosie. I'm here for my checkup."**
2. Sit in the waiting room until you hear your name.
3. Climb into the big chair and lie back.
4. Open wide and say "Ahh."
5. If you need a break, raise your hand.
6. Say "Thank you!" and pick a sticker.

"Open wide is **張開** (zhāngkāi)," said Niko. "Your lid pops open, and I count your pearls."

"Step five is my favorite," said Don Café. "A hand in the air means stop. ***Tranquilo*** = calm."

"Practice time!" The sink was the front desk. Don Café sat on it with a magazine. He was the whole waiting room. Mom called from the doorway, "Rosie?"

Rosie practiced:
1. Walk to the front desk and say, **"Hi, I'm Rosie. I'm here for my checkup."**
2. Sit in the waiting room until you hear your name.
3. Climb into the big chair and lie back.
4. Open wide and say "Ahh."
5. If you need a break, raise your hand.
6. Say "Thank you!" and pick a sticker.

Niko peeked in with the mirror. "Three pearls. Very neat." Rosie picked a sticker. So did the rubber duck.

On Friday, Rosie and Mom went to the real dentist. Cat the penguin came too. She practiced gentle kung fu breathing. Even her scarf seemed calmer.

Step one. Rosie walked to the front desk. "**Hi, I'm Rosie. I'm here for my checkup.**"

"Welcome, Rosie!" said Ana, the dental helper.

Step two. Rosie sat in the waiting room. A fish tank shaped like a tooth bubbled.

Cat sat beside Mom with a picture book. "Fish with excellent brushing habits," she said, studying the tooth-shaped tank.

"Rosie?" called Dr. Sam. Step three. Rosie climbed into the big chair. Clouds and balloons floated across the ceiling. Ana clipped on a paper bib.

Then — WHIRR. The chair tilted back by itself.

That was not like practice. The bathtub never moved! Rosie felt startled. Her straw drooped. That's okay.

Rosie did a Rose breath. Smell the rose — in, two, three, four. Blow out the candle — out, two, three, four.

Step five. Rosie raised her hand. The chair stopped at once.

"Good signal," said Dr. Sam. "The chair leans back so I can see. It stops whenever you say."

"Ready now," said Rosie.

Step four. "Ahh." Her dome lid popped open. The light swung over. Dr. Sam peered in with his little mirror. "Three pearls," he said. "All shiny. No cavities!"

Step six. "Thank you! **謝謝** (xièxie)." Rosie picked a **貼紙** (tiēzhǐ) = sticker with a gold star.

"**勇敢** (yǒnggǎn) = brave," said Mom. "You raised your hand, then opened wide."

Ana found a blue feather in the sticker box. "A souvenir from my scarf," said Cat, offering a tissue.

Outside, Tori waited under the big tree with two new toothbrushes. "You've got this, little sip. And three shiny pearls!"

"One step at a time," said Rosie. "One sip at a time."

## 🌹 Rosie's Steps (Recap)
1. Walk to the front desk and say, **"Hi, I'm Rosie. I'm here for my checkup."**
2. Sit in the waiting room until you hear your name.
3. Climb into the big chair and lie back.
4. Open wide and say "Ahh."
5. If you need a break, raise your hand.
6. Say "Thank you!" and pick a sticker.

**If something surprising happens:** Raise your hand (that is step five — it always works), do a Rose breath, then ask: "Can you tell me what happens next?"

## 💬 Practice Together (for grown-ups)
- Turn the bathtub (or a reclining chair) into the dentist's chair: a folded towel for a headrest, a lamp or the shower head for the light, a hand mirror for the little mirror, and a sink or table for the front desk. Play the dentist, the front-desk helper and the person calling the name, and let your child say every step out loud.
- Practice step five on purpose: tilt the "chair" (lean them back slowly), make the mirror come close, or make a whirring sound, and praise the raised hand every single time: "You raised your hand, and I stopped. That's exactly how it works."
- Rehearse the real sequence of a checkup in order: bib clipped on, chair leans back, light swings over, "open wide," counting teeth, maybe a tickly toothbrush, then a sticker. Kids who know what comes next are much calmer in the chair. A stuffed penguin napping on a heavy blanket makes a fine Cat.
- Before the real visit, call ahead and tell the office your child is practicing; many dentists will show the chair tilting and let your child hold the mirror first. Afterward, name the steps they did: "You checked in, you waited for your name, and you raised your hand."
