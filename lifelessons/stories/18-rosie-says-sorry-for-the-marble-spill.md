# 18 — Rosie Says Sorry for the Marble Spill

**Life skill:** Own an accidental mistake, apologize, and help repair it.
**Magic sentence:** "I'm sorry. How can I help fix it?"
**Coaches:** Hóng Dòu 🇹🇼 + Dai Daruma 🇯🇵
**Setting:** the apartment's mosaic landing (practice), then the covered marble club pavilion (real outing).

---

## The Story

Rosie was visiting a marble club, where glass marbles gleamed like tiny planets in a bowl.

She practiced carrying her pouch on the mosaic landing, keeping it close in front of her cup.

"What if I bump something?" she asked. A felt marble stuck to her straw like an unexpected hat.

Hóng Dòu arrived, a grandmotherly red bean.

Dai arrived, a determined red daruma bun.

Cat the penguin napped on a cushion beside the shoe bench.

Hóng Dòu nudged a felt marble bowl. **"I'm sorry. How can I help fix it?"** she asked Dai.

Dai showed her the sorting tray. "An accident needs care, not a contest to find the biggest excuse."

**Rosie's Steps**

1. Stop and check what happened.
2. Tell the person what you did.
3. Say you are sorry.

4. Ask how to help.
5. Help with their permission.

**練習** (liànxí) = practice. Rosie tried **"I'm sorry. How can I help fix it?"** Dai Daruma pretended.

1. Stop and check what happened.
2. Tell the person what you did.
3. Say you are sorry.

4. Ask how to help.
5. Help with their permission.

Rosie practiced naming the bump and offering help, while Dai played a marble collector with solemn eyebrows.

His collection contained one enormous pompom. "Rare specimen," he announced. "Very difficult to lose under a sofa."

Mom took Rosie to the covered pavilion, where marble tracks curved above soft mats and low tables.

Cat napped on a spare felt mat, contributing one round tummy to the club's collection of circles.

Tang balanced a sorting bowl beside his track. His five candied strawberries sparkled in the filtered sunshine.

Step one: Rosie's pouch bumped the bowl. She stopped; marbles pattered across the soft mat below.

Step two: "My pouch knocked your bowl," she told Tang, checking that nobody had been hurt.

Step three: **"I'm sorry. How can I help fix it?"** Her voice stayed gentle and clear.

Tang looked at the scattered colors. "I need a minute," he said, holding his empty bowl.

Rosie felt worried. Her straw drooped. That's okay; an apology did not require Tang to smile immediately.

Rosie took a Rose breath.

Smell the rose: in, two, three, four.

Blow out the candle: out, two, three, four.

"I'll wait here until you're ready," Rosie said, keeping her feet away from the scattered marbles.

Tang took a breath too. Then he pointed to the blue ones. "Could we sort these first?"

Step four: Rosie checked, "You want the blue marbles in this bowl?" Tang nodded and moved closer.

Step five: they collected the marbles together, taking care around the smooth little balls on the mat.

Hóng Dòu held the bowl steady. Dai examined his pompom. "Still rare. Still too fluffy for racing."

A marble stopped beside Cat's scarf. She opened one eye. "My nap has acquired a tiny audience."

Tang laughed softly, then thanked Rosie for helping. The track was ready, and so were its colorful planets.

Tori waited beneath the pavilion's hanging bamboo baskets, with a little cloth pouch ready for Rosie's marbles.

"You owned your bump and helped repair it. You've got this, little sip," she told her.

Rosie felt proud of the repair, and she clipped her pouch securely before watching Tang's next race.

**加油** (jiāyóu) = go for it.

"One step at a time, one sip at a time," said Rosie.

## 🌹 Rosie's Steps (Recap)

1. Stop and check what happened.
2. Tell the person what you did.
3. Say you are sorry.
4. Ask how to help.
5. Help with their permission.

**If something surprising happens:** Take a Rose breath or a comfortable pause, then say: "I'll wait here until you're ready."

## 💬 Practice Together (for grown-ups)

- Role-play the situation using a marble sorting bowl or a safe pretend substitute; read the five steps together.
- Swap roles so your child can see you model **"I'm sorry. How can I help fix it?"** and hear the other person's answer.
- Introduce this story's small surprise; practice "I'll wait here until you're ready." and allow a comfortable breath or pause.
- On a real outing, stay nearby, offer choices, and praise the specific step your child tried.
