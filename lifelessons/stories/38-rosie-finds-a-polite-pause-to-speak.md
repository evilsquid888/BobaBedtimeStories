# 38 — Rosie Finds a Polite Pause to Speak

**Life skill:** Wait for a conversational pause while recognizing when help is urgent.
**Magic sentence:** "Excuse me. May I ask something?"
**Coaches:** Caǎo 🇹🇼 + Tico the Toucan 🇨🇷
**Cat’s role:** kung fu practitioner
**Setting:** the breakfast room window seat (practice), then the observatory daytime reception lounge (real outing).

---

## The Story

Rosie wanted to ask about the observatory's model telescope, but worried about interrupting a grown-up conversation.

At the window seat, Tico practiced talking to a puppet. His beak seemed to require its own audience.

"Ordinary questions can wait for a pause," Caǎo explained. "Urgent help can be asked for straight away."

Caǎo arrived, grass jelly wearing a beanie.

Tico's rainbow beak was impressively large.

Cat the penguin practiced a kung fu bow, then waited for Tico's nod before speaking.

Caǎo waited beside Tico, then asked during a pause, **"Excuse me. May I ask something?"** Tico nodded.

"If you are hurt or unsafe, tell your trusted grown-up immediately," Caǎo added, keeping the teaching plain.

**Rosie's Steps**

1. Check whether you need urgent help.
2. For an ordinary question, wait nearby.
3. Look for a pause.

4. Ask politely to speak.
5. Say your question when invited.

Tico bowed to the puppet. "Beak the news! My interviewee has finally agreed that I may stop talking."

Mom took Rosie to the lounge, where a model telescope stood beside picture brochures and a globe.

Cat studied a planet model while Rosie waited for a comfortable pause at the reception desk.

Mom spoke with the receptionist about their visit. Rosie wanted to know whether the model telescope could be touched.

Step one: Rosie checked her question. Nobody was hurt or unsafe; this was an ordinary question about a model.

Step two: she waited nearby with Mom. Step three: she listened for a pause in the conversation.

Step four: **"Excuse me. May I ask something?"** She stayed close enough for Mom to hear her clearly.

Mom held up one finger, showing she needed a brief moment to finish checking their visit details.

Rosie felt impatient. Her straw drooped. That's okay; the signal meant wait briefly, not that her question was unimportant.

Rosie took a Rose breath.

Smell the rose: in, two, three, four.

Blow out the candle: out, two, three, four.

"Please let me know when you're ready," Rosie said softly, keeping the picture brochure in front of her cup.

Mom finished the detail, then turned toward Rosie. "I'm ready now. What would you like to ask?"

Step five: Rosie asked about touching the model. Mom checked with the receptionist and explained the exhibit's rules.

Rosie followed those rules, enjoying the telescope with Mom nearby. Her question had found its own comfortable moment.

Caǎo nodded. Tico regarded the globe. "A fine listener. It allows me a world of pauses."

Cat waited, then asked about the model. "Is that Saturn? Its ring looks like my scarf on laundry day."

Rosie laughed quietly. Both questions had found a listening moment.

Tori met Rosie beside the lounge's model telescope stand, where the little brochure rested on a low shelf.

"You found a pause and asked clearly. You've got this, little sip," she said, admiring Rosie's patient voice.

Rosie felt proud. Ordinary questions could wait, and urgent needs could still be spoken right away.

**加油** (jiāyóu) = go for it.

"One step at a time, one sip at a time," said Rosie.

## 🌹 Rosie's Steps (Recap)

1. Check whether you need urgent help.
2. For an ordinary question, wait nearby.
3. Look for a pause.
4. Ask politely to speak.
5. Say your question when invited.

**If something surprising happens:** Take a Rose breath or a comfortable pause, then say: "Please let me know when you're ready."

## 💬 Practice Together (for grown-ups)

- Role-play the situation using a picture brochure or a safe pretend substitute; read the five steps together.
- Swap roles so your child can see you model **"Excuse me. May I ask something?"** and hear the other person's answer.
- Introduce this story's small surprise; practice "Please let me know when you're ready." and allow a comfortable breath or pause.
- On a real outing, stay nearby, offer choices, and praise the specific step your child tried.
