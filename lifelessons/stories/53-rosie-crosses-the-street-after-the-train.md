# 53 — Rosie Crosses the Street After the Train

**Life skill:** Cross a street safely as a pedestrian after stepping off a train: stop at the curb, look left, right and left again, listen, wait until every car has stopped, make eye contact with the driver and wait for the nod, then walk together with your group while looking both ways.
**Magic sentence:** "Stop. Look left, right, left. Walk together when it's clear."
**Coaches:** Slowy the Sloth 🇨🇷 + Niko Nigiri 🇯🇵 + Marcus (Inclusive Navigator)
**Cat’s role:** practical helper (snowboard laid flat as the practice curb, then the group's caboose at the back of the line with a plain round red paddle)

**Setting:** the building's underground garage ramp (practice), then the street crossing outside Lantern Hill Station's side exit (real outing).

## 🌹 Rosie's Steps (Recap)

1. Stop at the curb, toes behind the edge.
2. Look left, right, then left again. Listen too.
3. Wait until every car has stopped.
4. Look at the driver's face. Wait for the nod.
5. Walk, don't run, and keep looking both ways.
6. Cross together with your group.

**If something surprising happens:** Rose breath, then say: "Not yet. We wait for the next clear." A car that keeps rolling, or a driver looking the other way, means wait again; the next clear always comes. If the signal starts flashing while you are already crossing, keep walking calmly to the far side. Do not turn back and do not run.

## 💬 Practice Together (for grown-ups)

- Make a practice curb with a tape line or a flat board, and a pretend car with a toy on a string. Read the six steps together while your child stops with toes behind the edge, looks left, right, left, and listens.
- Take turns being the driver: look away at first, then look at your child and nod. Praise the waiting, especially the backup sentence "Not yet. We wait for the next clear."
- Say **"Stop. Look left, right, left. Walk together when it's clear."** out loud with your child every time you cross a real street, and keep looking both ways while you walk.
- For a real outing, pick a quiet crossing with a signal, cross as a group with your child in the middle, and praise walking instead of running. Agree ahead of time: if the signal flashes midway, keep walking calmly to the far side.
