# 29 — Rosie Asks for One Direction at a Time

**Life skill:** Ask for instructions to be repeated or broken into smaller parts.
**Magic sentence:** "Could you tell me one step at a time?"
**Coaches:** Dora the Dorayaki 🇯🇵 + Taro 🇹🇼
**Setting:** the model railway hobby cupboard (practice), then the transport museum model-building table (real outing).

---

## The Story

Rosie wanted to build a model bridge with Tang, but the kit had several pieces that looked alike.

Dora unfolded a long instruction sheet. It covered the hobby table like a pancake carpet.

"What if I forget the middle?" Rosie asked. Taro adjusted his glasses and found the first little picture.

Dora was a pancake sandwich wearing a beret.



Cat the penguin napped on a felt scenery cushion.

Dora gave several pretend directions. Taro asked, **"Could you tell me one step at a time?"** Then repeated one.

"Asking helps the other person teach clearly," Dora explained. "A long instruction can become several small useful ones."

**Rosie's Steps**

1. Listen to the instruction.
2. Say which part you need help with.
3. Ask for one step at a time.

4. Repeat the first step back.
5. Try it and check the next step.

**問題** (wèntí) = question. Rosie tried **"Could you tell me one step at a time?"** Taro pretended.

1. Listen to the instruction.
2. Say which part you need help with.
3. Ask for one step at a time.

4. Repeat the first step back.
5. Try it and check the next step.

Rosie practiced repeating the first direction before touching a track piece, then checking what should happen next.

Dora pointed to her carpet of paper. "A good story has chapters. Apparently, so does this tiny bridge."

Mom took Rosie to the transport museum, where wooden bridges filled shallow sorting trays.

Cat dozed on a spare padded stool, providing a very convincing model of a stationary passenger.

Tang held the bridge kit. The workshop helper explained several things about sorting, fitting, and placing the supports.

Step one: Rosie listened, keeping the wooden pieces in their tray instead of guessing which to join.

Step two: "I need help remembering the supports," she said, pointing at the little wooden bridge piece.

Step three: **"Could you tell me one step at a time?"** The helper nodded and showed the first picture.

Two supports looked almost the same. Even the slower instruction did not tell Rosie which one to choose.

She felt puzzled. Her straw drooped. That's okay; she could ask for a clearer detail as well.

Rosie took a Rose breath.

Smell the rose: in, two, three, four.

Blow out the candle: out, two, three, four.

"Could you point to the piece you mean?" Rosie asked, leaving both supports side by side in the tray.

The helper pointed to the wider piece and turned it gently. Its flat edge belonged against the base.

Step four: "First, fit the wide support here," Rosie repeated. The helper checked her meaning before she tried.

Step five: she fitted that piece, then asked for the next step. Tang held the base steady beside her.

Taro admired the support. Dora folded a page. "Chapter one: the bridge learns to stand."

Cat blinked from her stool. "My model passenger requests a station with pillows. Please build that step next."

Rosie laughed, checking the second support with Tang. The whole bridge was easier when each instruction had room.

Tori joined Rosie beside the museum's miniature station canopy, where their finished bridge stood in a display tray.

"You asked for the size of step you needed. You've got this, little sip," she said warmly.

Rosie felt proud. Her questions had helped the bridge reach the other side.

**加油** (jiāyóu) = go for it.

"One step at a time, one sip at a time," said Rosie.

## 🌹 Rosie's Steps (Recap)

1. Listen to the instruction.
2. Say which part you need help with.
3. Ask for one step at a time.
4. Repeat the first step back.
5. Try it and check the next step.

**If something surprising happens:** Take a Rose breath or a comfortable pause, then say: "Could you point to the piece you mean?"

## 💬 Practice Together (for grown-ups)

- Role-play the situation using a wooden bridge piece or a safe pretend substitute; read the five steps together.
- Swap roles so your child can see you model **"Could you tell me one step at a time?"** and hear the other person's answer.
- Introduce this story's small surprise; practice "Could you point to the piece you mean?" and allow a comfortable breath or pause.
- On a real outing, stay nearby, offer choices, and praise the specific step your child tried.
