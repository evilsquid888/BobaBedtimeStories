# 07 — Rosie Asks the Librarian

**Life skill:** Asking a librarian for a book, using a quiet library voice, waiting your turn, and checking out a book.
**Magic sentence:** "Excuse me, where can I find books about sea turtles?"
**Coaches:** Dora the Dorayaki (Japan crew) + Slowy the Sloth (Costa Rica crew)
**Setting:** Rosie's back garden on a bright morning (practice) — a picnic table under a lemon tree, billowing laundry, a crate for a Help desk, the mailbox for checkout, and a striped hammock where Cat the penguin naps — then the grand old town library with a spiral iron staircase, a giant globe, a sea-turtle mobile and a bubbling fish tank by the Information desk, and the porch swing at home by lantern light

---

## The Story

Rosie had a brand-new library card. She kept it in her cup sleeve and patted it forty times a day.

"I want a **書** (shū) = book about sea turtles," she told her big sister Tori. "But I don't know how to ask at the **圖書館** (túshūguǎn) = library."

RING-RING. At the gate stood Dora the Dorayaki, two fluffy pancakes in a red beret. In the lemon tree, Slowy the Sloth lowered one foot very slowly.

"Hellooooo," said Slowy. His foot came down. "...Rosie."

"He started saying hello at the gate," said Dora. "He just finished."

Rosie giggled. Cat the penguin, who was staying with Rosie's family, opened one sleepy eye in the hammock, decided nothing was happening, and closed it again.

"A good story is the best filling," said Dora. "Here is how to ask for one."

**Rosie's Steps**
1. Use your library voice — soft and quiet.
2. Find the Help or Information desk.
3. If someone is at the desk, wait in line behind them.
4. Look at the librarian and say, **"Excuse me, where can I find books about sea turtles?"**
5. Follow the librarian to the shelf.
6. At checkout, show your card and say "Thank you!"

"In Japan we say ***shizuka ni*** = quietly," said Dora.

"Liiiike... thiiiis?" whispered Slowy. And so slow that Rosie's tea swirled twice.

Then they practiced. Dora put on tiny glasses and played the librarian. Slowy stood in line. Rosie practiced:
1. Use your library voice — soft and quiet.
2. Find the Help or Information desk.
3. If someone is at the desk, wait in line behind them.
4. Look at the librarian and say, **"Excuse me, where can I find books about sea turtles?"**
5. Follow the librarian to the shelf.
6. At checkout, show your card and say "Thank you!"

"Shelf 597," said Dora. Rosie checked out a pretend book at the mailbox.

The real library was big and quiet. A fish tank bubbled beside the Information desk. Dora, Slowy, and Cat came too. Cat curled up in the book-return cart and fell asleep.

A grandpa was at the desk. Rosie waited in line behind him. When he left, she stepped up.

But the librarian was on the phone. She smiled and held up one finger.

That was not in the practice. Rosie felt nervous. Her straw drooped. That's okay.

"One finger can mean please wait," whispered Dora.

Rosie did a Rose breath. Smell the rose — in, two, three, four. Blow out the candle — out, two, three, four.

She nodded and counted the fish. Seven orange fish. One snail, almost as slow as Slowy.

The librarian hung up. "Thank you for waiting! How can I help you?"

"That's okay!" said Rosie. Then, in her library voice: **"Excuse me, where can I find books about sea turtles?"**

"Great choice," said the librarian. "Follow me." On shelf 597 was a book with a green sea turtle on the cover.

Then the librarian peeked into the book cart. "Oh! A penguin. I almost shelved her under P."

"Five more minutes," mumbled Cat.

At checkout, Rosie showed her library card. Beep!

"Thank you!" said Rosie. Then, very softly, "**謝謝** (xièxie) = thank you."

Behind her, Slowy finally reached the desk. "Excuuuuse... meeeee..." The lights blinked for closing time.

On the porch swing, Tori gave her a big lychee hug. "You waited AND you asked. You've got this, little sip."

"One step at a time," Rosie said sleepily. "One sip at a time."

## 🌹 Rosie's Steps (Recap)
1. Use your library voice — soft and quiet.
2. Find the Help or Information desk.
3. If someone is at the desk, wait in line behind them.
4. Look at the librarian and say, **"Excuse me, where can I find books about sea turtles?"**
5. Follow the librarian to the shelf.
6. At checkout, show your card and say "Thank you!"

**If something surprising happens:** If the librarian is busy and holds up one finger, that means "one minute, please." Nod, do a Rose breath, and wait quietly (you can count something nearby). When the librarian is ready, say: "That's okay!" — then say the magic sentence.

## 💬 Practice Together (for grown-ups)
- Set up a "Help" desk at home or in the yard (an upturned crate or a box works great). Take turns being the librarian, the person in line, and Rosie. Practice the library voice first: "Can only the person next to you hear it?"
- Practice the one-finger signal on purpose: pretend to be on the phone, hold up one finger, and praise your child for nodding and waiting quietly. Count something together (fish, books, buttons) — 30 seconds at first, then a whole minute.
- Hide a stuffed penguin in a laundry basket "book cart" for a giggle, then praise the specific steps: "You waited behind the person in line." "You used your library voice." "You said thank you at checkout."
- Real-world tip: visit at a quiet time (weekday mornings are calm). Let your child swap in any topic they love — "Excuse me, where can I find books about ___?" — and let them carry and show their own library card.

## 🌙 Goodnight Blessing
Goodnight, little reader, soft voice and slow breath.
Goodnight, sea turtles, swimming through the pages.
Goodnight, Cat, almost shelved under P.
One step, one sip, one story. Sleep tight.
