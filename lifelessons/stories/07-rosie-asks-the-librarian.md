# 07 — Rosie Asks the Librarian

**Life skill:** Asking a librarian for a book, using a quiet library voice, waiting your turn, and checking out a book.
**Magic sentence:** "Excuse me, where can I find books about sea turtles?"
**Coaches:** Dora the Dorayaki (Japan crew) + Slowy the Sloth (Costa Rica crew)
**Setting:** Rosie's kitchen for practice, then the big, quiet town library with a bubbling fish tank by the Information desk.

---

## The Story

Rosie had a brand-new library card. She kept it in her cup sleeve and patted it about forty times a day.

"I want to go to the **圖書館** (túshūguǎn) = library," Rosie told her big sister Tori. "I want a **書** (shū) = book about sea turtles. But I don't know how to ask."

"I know two friends who can help," said Tori.

The next morning, the doorbell rang. On the step stood Dora the Dorayaki, two fluffy honey-brown pancakes with a red-bean smile and a comfy beret. Behind him stood Slowy the Sloth, mossy and smiling, lifting one foot very, very slowly.

"Hellooooo," said Slowy. His foot came down. "...Rosie."

"He started saying hello at the corner," said Dora. "He just finished."

Rosie giggled. Her straw wiggled.

Dora sat Rosie at the kitchen table. "A good story is the best filling," he said. "And the library is full of stories. Here is how to ask for one." He wrote on a card:

**Rosie's Steps**
1. Use your library voice — a soft, inside-quiet voice.
2. Find the desk that says "Help" or "Information."
3. If someone is at the desk, wait in line behind them.
4. Look at the librarian and say the magic sentence: **"Excuse me, where can I find books about sea turtles?"**
5. Follow the librarian, or look for the shelf number they write down.
6. Take your book to the checkout desk, show your library card, and say "Thank you!"

"In Japan we say ***shizuka ni*** = quietly," said Dora. "Soft enough that only the person next to you can hear."

"Liiiike... thiiiis?" whispered Slowy. It was soft as moss. It was perfect. It was also so slow that Rosie's tea swirled twice before he finished.

"Slowy is the champion of library voices," said Dora. "Maybe just a little faster."

Then they practiced. Dora put on tiny glasses and sat behind a cereal box with **HELP** written on it. He was the librarian. Slowy was the person in line. Rosie read the card out loud.

**Rosie's Steps**
1. Use your library voice — a soft, inside-quiet voice.
2. Find the desk that says "Help" or "Information."
3. If someone is at the desk, wait in line behind them.
4. Look at the librarian and say the magic sentence: **"Excuse me, where can I find books about sea turtles?"**
5. Follow the librarian, or look for the shelf number they write down.
6. Take your book to the checkout desk, show your library card, and say "Thank you!"

Rosie waited behind Slowy. Slowy was still asking for a banana book. Then it was her turn. She looked at Dora's face and used her library voice.

**"Excuse me, where can I find books about sea turtles?"**

Dora wrote **597** on a sticky note. "Sea turtle books live on shelf 597," he said. Rosie found a pretend book, carried it to the toaster, which was the checkout desk, showed her card, and said, "Thank you!"

"Perfect," said Dora. "Let's go do it for real."

The real library was big and quiet. By the front door, a fish tank bubbled softly next to a desk with a sign: **INFORMATION**.

A grandpa was at the desk. Rosie stood in line behind him and waited. When he left, she stepped up.

But the librarian was on the phone. She looked at Rosie, smiled, and held up one finger.

Rosie's straw drooped. That was not in the practice. Rosie felt nervous. That's okay.

"One finger means 'one minute, please,'" Dora whispered. "It means wait."

Rosie did a Rose breath. Smell the rose, in for four. Blow out the candle, out for four. She nodded to the librarian. Then she waited quietly and counted the fish. Seven orange fish. One striped fish. One snail, who was almost as slow as Slowy.

The librarian hung up. "Thank you for waiting! How can I help you?"

"That's okay!" said Rosie. Then she looked at the librarian's face and said, in her library voice:

**"Excuse me, where can I find books about sea turtles?"**

"Great choice," said the librarian. "Follow me." She walked Rosie right to shelf 597. There was a book with a green sea turtle on the cover.

"It looks like Estrella!" Rosie whispered.

At the checkout desk, Rosie showed her library card. The librarian beeped the card and the book. "It's due back in three weeks," she said.

"Thank you!" said Rosie. Then, very softly, "**謝謝** (xièxie) = thank you."

Rosie's straw wiggled all the way up.

Meanwhile, Slowy had reached the Information desk. "Excuuuuse... meeeee..." he whispered.

The librarian waited kindly.

"...where... can... I... find..."

The lights blinked. A gentle voice said, "The library will close in five minutes."

"...books... about... sloths?"

The librarian smiled. "Shelf 599. Come back tomorrow. I'll save you a good one."

"Puuura... viiida," said Slowy happily.

At home, Tori asked, "Did you do it?"

Rosie held up her book. "I waited my turn. I waited for the one finger. And I asked, **'Excuse me, where can I find books about sea turtles?'**"

Tori scooped her up in a big lychee hug. "You've got this, little sip."

"One step at a time," she said. "One sip at a time."

That night, Rosie read about baby ***tortugas*** = sea turtles, as Slowy calls them, until her eyes got heavy and the turtles swam into her dreams.

## 🌹 Rosie's Steps (Recap)
1. Use your library voice — a soft, inside-quiet voice.
2. Find the desk that says "Help" or "Information."
3. If someone is at the desk, wait in line behind them.
4. Look at the librarian and say the magic sentence: **"Excuse me, where can I find books about sea turtles?"**
5. Follow the librarian, or look for the shelf number they write down.
6. Take your book to the checkout desk, show your library card, and say "Thank you!"

**If something surprising happens:** If the librarian is busy and holds up one finger, that means "one minute, please." Nod, do a Rose breath, and wait quietly (you can count something nearby). When the librarian is ready, say: "That's okay!" — then say the magic sentence.

## 💬 Practice Together (for grown-ups)
- Set up a "Help" desk at home (a box with a sign works great). Take turns being the librarian, the person in line, and Rosie. Practice the library voice first: "Can only the person next to you hear it?"
- Practice the one-finger signal on purpose: pretend to be on the phone, hold up one finger, and praise your child for nodding and waiting quietly. Count to 30 together at first, then try a whole minute.
- Praise the specific steps: "You waited behind the person in line." "You used your library voice." "You said thank you at checkout."
- Real-world tip: visit at a quiet time (weekday mornings are calm). Let your child swap in any topic they love — "Excuse me, where can I find books about ___?" — and let them carry and show their own library card.

## 🌙 Goodnight Blessing
Soft voice, slow breath, pages turning low,
the library of your dreams is open — off you go.
The sea turtles are swimming where the quiet waters glow,
and every little question is a brave seed you can grow.
Rest now, little sip, one step and one sip slow.
Goodnight, sweet reader. Goodnight.
