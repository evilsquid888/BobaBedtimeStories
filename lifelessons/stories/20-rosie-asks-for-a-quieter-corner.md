# 20 — Rosie Asks for a Quieter Corner

**Life skill:** Notice sensory discomfort and request a supported quiet break.
**Magic sentence:** "It's too loud for me. Can we take a quiet break?"
**Coaches:** Caǎo 🇹🇼 + Don Café 🇨🇷
**Cat’s role:** helper
**Setting:** a music room's instrument alcove (practice), then the science museum sound gallery (real outing).

---

## The Story

Rosie wanted to see sound waves at the science museum, especially the tray that made grains dance.

In the instrument alcove, Don Café wore padded headphones over his straw hat, creating a spectacular mushroom shape.

"What if the sound feels too big?" Rosie asked, touching the little rose petal on her lid.

Caǎo arrived, grass jelly wearing a beanie.

Don Café was a wise coffee bean.

Cat the penguin made picture cards for quiet choices. Her ear-defender drawing looked suspiciously like waffles.

Caǎo told Don Café, **"It's too loud for me. Can we take a quiet break?"** They moved together.

"Bodies give useful messages," Don Café said. "We can listen to them before our feelings boil over."

**Rosie's Steps**

1. Notice how your body feels.
2. Tell your trusted grown-up.
3. Ask for a quiet break.

4. Go together to the agreed place.
5. Decide together what happens next.

Rosie practiced telling Mom and pointing to the agreed quiet chair, rather than disappearing around a corner.

Caǎo tested a soft mallet against a felt pad. "Excellent concert. My beanie applauds at a sensible volume."

Mom stayed beside Rosie in the sound gallery, where tubes curved above trays of gently vibrating grains.

Cat pointed out the quiet corner to Mom, then carried their bag there without rushing Rosie.

Rosie tried the first exhibit. The dancing grains looked like a tiny breakfast learning a new dance.

Step one: a nearby sound tube boomed. Rosie noticed her cup felt tense and her straw stiffened.

Step two: she told Mom what she felt, standing close enough for Mom to hear her clearly.

Step three: **"It's too loud for me. Can we take a quiet break?"** Mom nodded right away.

The first quiet chair had a cleaning basket on it. Their practiced place was temporarily unavailable.

Rosie felt unsettled. Her straw drooped. That's okay; Mom was still beside her, ready to help.

Rosie took a Rose breath.

Smell the rose: in, two, three, four.

Blow out the candle: out, two, three, four.

"Can you help me find another quiet place?" Rosie asked, staying with Mom beside the alcove entrance.

Mom found a clear bench farther inside. They moved there together, away from the booming sound tube.

Step four: Rosie sat with Mom on the quieter bench, holding the padded headphones in front of her cup.

Step five: they decided to leave the sound gallery and visit the gentle light exhibit next.

Caǎo nodded. Don Café straightened his hat. "Some afternoons brew better when we turn down the bubbles."

Cat handed Rosie her bag. "Would you like company?" Rosie nodded. A helper could use a quiet voice.

Rosie settled beside Cat. Her ears and her straw felt easier.

Tori met them beside the museum's enormous glass prism, where lemon-colored light made patches on the floor.

"You listened to your body. You've got this, little sip," she said, offering Rosie a warm smile.

Rosie felt proud. She could enjoy an outing and ask for a change when something felt uncomfortable.

**加油** (jiāyóu) = go for it.

"One step at a time, one sip at a time," said Rosie.

## 🌹 Rosie's Steps (Recap)

1. Notice how your body feels.
2. Tell your trusted grown-up.
3. Ask for a quiet break.
4. Go together to the agreed place.
5. Decide together what happens next.

**If something surprising happens:** Take a Rose breath or a comfortable pause, then say: "Can you help me find another quiet place?"

## 💬 Practice Together (for grown-ups)

- Role-play the situation using a pair of padded headphones or a safe pretend substitute; read the five steps together.
- Swap roles so your child can see you model **"It's too loud for me. Can we take a quiet break?"** and hear the other person's answer.
- Introduce this story's small surprise; practice "Can you help me find another quiet place?" and allow a comfortable breath or pause.
- On a real outing, stay nearby, offer choices, and praise the specific step your child tried.
