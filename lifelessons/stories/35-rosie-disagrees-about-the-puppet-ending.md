# 35 — Rosie Disagrees About the Puppet Ending

**Life skill:** State a different preference politely and negotiate a shared creative choice.
**Magic sentence:** "I have a different idea. Can I tell you?"
**Coaches:** Dora the Dorayaki 🇯🇵 + Tato the Patacon 🇨🇷
**Cat’s role:** hip-hop performer
**Setting:** the unused fireplace puppet nook (practice), then the community center puppet rehearsal room (real outing).

---

## The Story

Rosie and Tang were planning a puppet show, but Rosie wanted a different ending from Tang's idea.

At the puppet nook, Dora balanced a toy crown over her beret, creating a rather complicated royal pancake.

"Can I disagree and still be kind?" Rosie asked. Tato nodded, planting his sturdy little feet.

Dora was a pancake sandwich wearing a beret.

Tato was a sturdy golden plantain patty.

Cat the penguin tried a hip-hop puppet bow. The dragon's tail attempted to steal the spotlight.

Tato listened to Dora's pretend ending. **"I have a different idea. Can I tell you?"** he asked.

"Different ideas do not make either person bad," Dora explained. "Tell your idea without insulting theirs."

**Rosie's Steps**

1. Listen to the other idea.
2. Say that you have another idea.
3. Explain it kindly.

4. Ask about a shared choice.
5. Follow what you agree together.

**想法** (xiǎngfǎ) = idea. Rosie tried **"I have a different idea. Can I tell you?"** Tato the Patacon pretended.

1. Listen to the other idea.
2. Say that you have another idea.
3. Explain it kindly.

Rosie practiced explaining her ending while Dora listened, then heard Dora's version before they tried a shared choice.

Tato crowned a sock puppet. "A brave ruler. Particularly experienced in finding shoes."

Mom brought Rosie to the rehearsal room, where Tang had arranged a cloth dragon beside the little stage.

Cat helped set the puppet basket beside the stage, then left the ending to the two friends.

Tang suggested an ending where the dragon won every race. Rosie imagined the dragon helping somebody else finish.

Step one: Rosie listened to Tang's whole idea, keeping the puppet still until he had finished explaining.

Step two: **"I have a different idea. Can I tell you?"** Tang nodded and turned toward her.

Step three: Rosie explained her helping ending kindly. She did not call Tang's racing idea silly or wrong.

Tang liked his racing ending and did not want to replace it with Rosie's entire new ending.

Rosie felt frustrated. Her straw drooped. That's okay; listening did not mean automatically agreeing with her.

Rosie took a Rose breath.

Smell the rose: in, two, three, four.

Blow out the candle: out, two, three, four.

"Could we use a little of each idea?" Rosie asked, looking between the dragon and the finish-line ribbon.

Tang suggested that the dragon finish its race, then return to help a slower puppet reach the ribbon.

Step four: Rosie checked the shared choice. They both liked a race that ended with some helping.

Step five: they rehearsed that ending together, giving each puppet room and taking turns moving the ribbon.

Dora applauded quietly. Tato examined the crowned sock. "Our ruler has approved it. By unanimous agreement of one foot."

Cat made the dragon bow once. "Our dragon enjoys both endings. Especially the part where its tail gets applause."

Tang laughed. The ending had room for both their imaginations.

Tori joined Rosie beside the rehearsal room's hanging backdrop rack, admiring the neatly coiled finish-line ribbon.

"You disagreed with care. You've got this, little sip," she said, letting Rosie explain their shared ending.

Rosie felt proud. A different idea could join a friendship rather than push one person out of it.

**加油** (jiāyóu) = go for it.

"One step at a time, one sip at a time," said Rosie.

## 🌹 Rosie's Steps (Recap)

1. Listen to the other idea.
2. Say that you have another idea.
3. Explain it kindly.
4. Ask about a shared choice.
5. Follow what you agree together.

**If something surprising happens:** Take a Rose breath or a comfortable pause, then say: "Could we use a little of each idea?"

## 💬 Practice Together (for grown-ups)

- Role-play the situation using a small cloth dragon puppet or a safe pretend substitute; read the five steps together.
- Swap roles so your child can see you model **"I have a different idea. Can I tell you?"** and hear the other person's answer.
- Introduce this story's small surprise; practice "Could we use a little of each idea?" and allow a comfortable breath or pause.
- On a real outing, stay nearby, offer choices, and praise the specific step your child tried.
