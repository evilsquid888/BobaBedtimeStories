# 43 — Rosie Asks Before Taking a Picture

**Life skill:** Get permission before photographing someone and respect a changed answer.
**Magic sentence:** "May I take your picture?"
**Coaches:** Aiyu 🇹🇼 + Pock Ramune 🇯🇵
**Setting:** the sunroom's camera shelf (practice), then the aquarium's kelp-window viewing bay (real outing).

---

## The Story

Rosie wanted a picture of Lulu beside the aquarium's long green kelp ribbons.

At the camera shelf, Pock posed beside an empty frame and fizzed until a bubble balanced on his marble.

"What if Lulu doesn't want a picture?" Rosie asked. Aiyu glowed gently, her golden jelly clear as a lens.

Aiyu's golden jelly glowed gently.

Pock was a soda bottle fizzing happily.

Cat the penguin napped on an open padded camera case.

Pock checked with his pretend grown-up and asked Aiyu, **"May I take your picture?"** He waited for her answer.

"A smile is not the same as permission," Aiyu explained. "Ask first, and let the person choose."

**Rosie's Steps**

1. Check with your grown-up first.
2. Ask the person before taking a picture.
3. Wait for a clear answer.

4. Take it only if they agree.
5. Check permission before sharing it.

**照片** (zhàopiàn) = photo. Rosie tried **"May I take your picture?"** Pock Ramune pretended.

1. Check with your grown-up first.
2. Ask the person before taking a picture.
3. Wait for a clear answer.

Rosie practiced hearing yes, no, and not yet. She kept the pretend camera lowered until the answer was clear.

Pock tried a serious portrait face. One bubble rose. "My enthusiasm appears to have escaped through the top."

Mom brought Rosie to the aquarium and agreed she could use Mom's phone for a private picture.

Cat dozed on a cushion beside the kelp window, providing an exceptionally stationary blue audience.

Lulu stood near the glass, admiring the fish. Rosie kept the phone lowered in front of her little cup.

Step one: Rosie checked with Mom. Step two: **"May I take your picture?"** she asked Lulu before touching the button.

Step three: she waited. Lulu said yes and chose a comfortable spot beside the broad aquarium window.

Rosie held the phone steady for step four. Mom stayed close and watched the little screen.

Just before the picture, Lulu lowered her smile. "Actually, I don't want one today," she said quietly.

Rosie felt surprised. Her straw drooped. That's okay; Lulu could change her mind before the button was pressed.

Rosie took a Rose breath.

Smell the rose: in, two, three, four.

Blow out the candle: out, two, three, four.

"Okay. We can skip the picture," Rosie said, lowering the phone again and keeping her hands still.

Lulu looked relieved. Mom accepted the phone, and they watched the fish together instead of arranging another pose.

Step four meant taking a picture only with permission, so Rosie chose not to take one of Lulu.

Step five: Mom explained that any sharing needed permission too. Nothing about a camera made somebody's choice disappear.

Aiyu nodded. Pock inspected the empty frame. "A portrait of nobody. Privacy has excellent lighting."

Cat blinked beside the glass. "Please ask my scarf before photographing it. It is having a very quiet day."

Rosie giggled. The aquarium visit still felt special, even with no picture of her friend to carry home.

Tori met Rosie beneath the giant wooden fish sculpture, with Mom's phone safely tucked away.

"You respected her changed answer. You've got this, little sip," she said, listening to Rosie's small story.

Rosie felt proud of leaving the button alone, and of sharing a comfortable afternoon.

**加油** (jiāyóu) = go for it.

"One step at a time, one sip at a time," said Rosie.

## 🌹 Rosie's Steps (Recap)

1. Check with your grown-up first.
2. Ask the person before taking a picture.
3. Wait for a clear answer.
4. Take it only if they agree.
5. Check permission before sharing it.

**If something surprising happens:** Take a Rose breath or a comfortable pause, then say: "Okay. We can skip the picture."

## 💬 Practice Together (for grown-ups)

- Role-play the situation using a small pretend camera or a safe pretend substitute; read the five steps together.
- Swap roles so your child can see you model **"May I take your picture?"** and hear the other person's answer.
- Introduce this story's small surprise; practice "Okay. We can skip the picture." and allow a comfortable breath or pause.
- On a real outing, stay nearby, offer choices, and praise the specific step your child tried.
