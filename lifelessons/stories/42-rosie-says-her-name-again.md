# 42 — Rosie Says Her Name Again

**Life skill:** Correct a mistaken name clearly and ask for respectful use of your name.
**Magic sentence:** "My name is Rosie. Please call me Rosie."
**Coaches:** Neko-Pan 🇯🇵 + Flan 🇨🇷
**Setting:** the window alcove beside the coat pegs (practice), then the dance studio's ribbon reception (real outing).

---

## The Story

Rosie was collecting her dance-studio name card. She wanted people to call her Rosie.

At the coat pegs, Flan practiced introducing himself. His card slipped behind his caramel top.

"My name isn't Wobbles," he said. Rosie giggled, but wondered what she would say if somebody got hers wrong.

Neko-Pan was a cream bun waving cheerfully.

Flan arrived, coconut custard wobbling hopefully.

Cat the penguin napped on a folded coat on the stool.

Neko called his pretend guest Rosemary. Flan answered, **"My name is Rosie. Please call me Rosie."** Neko tried again.

"A kind correction helps people learn," Neko explained. "You can say your name slowly instead of pretending a mistake fits."

**Rosie's Steps**

1. Get the person's attention.
2. Say your name clearly.
3. Offer a simple pronunciation.

4. Ask them to try again.
5. Thank them for listening.

**名字** (míngzi) = name. Rosie tried **"My name is Rosie. Please call me Rosie."** Flan pretended.

2. Say your name clearly.
3. Offer a simple pronunciation.

4. Ask them to try again.
5. Thank them for listening.

Rosie practiced her sentence and said her name in two clear parts: ROH, then zee. Flan listened carefully.

Neko examined his own card. "Neko-Pan. Two parts, one bun. Please keep the filling attached during introductions."

Mom brought Rosie to the reception, where ribbon wands hung above baskets of dance shoes.

Cat napped beside the shoe basket on a spare cushion, declining every opportunity to become a tap-dancing penguin.

The host looked at the register. "Welcome, Rosemary." Rosie knew it was her turn.

Step one: Rosie got the host's attention. Step two: **"My name is Rosie. Please call me Rosie."**

Step three: she offered the two clear parts, ROH and zee. Mom stayed nearby, letting Rosie explain for herself.

Step four: she asked the host to try again. The host checked the register and repeated Rosie.

A ribbon wand rattled against its basket, and the host missed the second part of Rosie's pronunciation.

Rosie felt shy about repeating herself. Her straw drooped. That's okay; her name still deserved a careful listen.

Rosie took a Rose breath.

Smell the rose: in, two, three, four.

Blow out the candle: out, two, three, four.

"I'll say it once more, slowly," Rosie said. She paused until the little rattling ribbon basket was still.

The host listened again. "Rosie. Thank you for helping me get it right." Mom smiled beside the desk.

Step five: Rosie thanked the host for listening. Together they checked that her name card belonged to her.

Rosie clipped the card to her cup sleeve, proud that a gentle correction could make the greeting more comfortable.

Neko nodded while Flan checked his card. "Names and fillings deserve their proper owners."

Cat opened one eye beside the shoes. "My name is Cat. My occupation is penguin. A complicated résumé."

Rosie laughed softly. The host smiled too, and called her Rosie when she handed over the ribbon wand.

Tori met Rosie at the rainbow ribbon rack and admired her neatly clipped name card.

"You helped someone learn your name. You've got this, little sip," she said, giving Rosie a warm wave.

Rosie felt proud. Her name had its own small place in a comfortable greeting.

**加油** (jiāyóu) = go for it.

"One step at a time, one sip at a time," said Rosie.

## 🌹 Rosie's Steps (Recap)

1. Get the person's attention.
2. Say your name clearly.
3. Offer a simple pronunciation.
4. Ask them to try again.
5. Thank them for listening.

**If something surprising happens:** Take a Rose breath or a comfortable pause, then say: "I'll say it once more, slowly."

## 💬 Practice Together (for grown-ups)

- Role-play the situation using a blank name card or a safe pretend substitute; read the five steps together.
- Swap roles so your child can see you model **"My name is Rosie. Please call me Rosie."** and hear the other person's answer.
- Introduce this story's small surprise; practice "I'll say it once more, slowly." and allow a comfortable breath or pause.
- On a real outing, stay nearby, offer choices, and praise the specific step your child tried.
