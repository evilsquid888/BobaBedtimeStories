# 30 — Rosie Gives a Thoughtful Compliment

**Life skill:** Offer a sincere specific compliment without requiring a response.
**Magic sentence:** "I like the way you chose those colors."
**Coaches:** Neko-Pan 🇯🇵 + Lala 🇨🇷
**Setting:** the costume trunk dressing corner (practice), then the neighborhood quilt exhibition (real outing).

---

## The Story

Rosie was visiting Lulu's quilt display and wanted to say something kinder than just a hurried hello.

In the dressing corner, she compared fabric colors while Neko-Pan balanced a paper crown between his little ears.

Lala admired the crown. "Very grand. A pastry royal family should definitely have a snack-shaped throne."

Neko-Pan arrived, a cream bun waving cheerfully.

Lala arrived, a soft cake wearing cream.

Cat the penguin napped on a velvet cushion inside the trunk.

Lala looked at Neko's fabric arrangement. **"I like the way you chose those colors,"** she said once.

"Say something you mean," Neko explained. "A compliment is a little gift, not a question demanding a reply."

**Rosie's Steps**

1. Notice something you really like.
2. Choose a kind specific comment.
3. Wait for a comfortable moment.

4. Say your comment once.
5. Let the person answer in their way.

Mom brought Rosie to the quilt exhibition, where colorful squares hung from wooden clips beside fabric samples.

Cat napped on a spare sample cushion, approving the exhibition's most comfortable piece with her entire round tummy.

Lulu stood at the maker's table beside the small quilt she had helped choose colors for.

Step one: Rosie noticed how Lulu's blues and yellows made the quilt look like sunshine beside water.

Step two: she chose her comment. Step three: she waited until Lulu had finished adjusting a clip.

Step four: **"I like the way you chose those colors."** She said it once, with a warm little smile.

Lulu smiled but did not say anything. She seemed shy with the little quilt and the visitors nearby.

Rosie felt uncertain. Her straw drooped. That's okay; a quiet answer could still be an answer.

Rosie took a Rose breath.

Smell the rose: in, two, three, four.

Blow out the candle: out, two, three, four.

"You can just smile. That's okay," Rosie said gently, leaving Lulu time rather than repeating the compliment louder.

Lulu relaxed and pointed at one yellow square. Rosie looked at it with her, sharing the quiet moment.

Step five: Rosie accepted Lulu's smile. She did not ask for thanks or decide that her words had failed.

A little later, Lulu said, "This yellow came from my old scarf." Rosie listened, enjoying the small story.

Lala admired the finished quilt. Neko straightened his crown. "A royal announcement: these squares have excellent manners."

Cat opened one eye on the sample cushion. "I like the way this fabric has chosen to support me."

Rosie giggled, and Lulu laughed too. The little quilt glowed warmly beneath the exhibition's bright wooden clips.

Tori met Rosie beside the exhibition's basket of leftover fabric strips, where Lulu chose one for her keepsake.

"You noticed something real and gave her room. You've got this, little sip," Tori said quietly.

Rosie felt proud. Her compliment could be complete before anyone said a word back.

**加油** (jiāyóu) = go for it.

"One step at a time, one sip at a time," said Rosie.

## 🌹 Rosie's Steps (Recap)

1. Notice something you really like.
2. Choose a kind specific comment.
3. Wait for a comfortable moment.
4. Say your comment once.
5. Let the person answer in their way.

**If something surprising happens:** Take a Rose breath or a comfortable pause, then say: "You can just smile. That's okay."

## 💬 Practice Together (for grown-ups)

- Role-play the situation using a patchwork fabric sample or a safe pretend substitute; read the five steps together.
- Swap roles so your child can see you model **"I like the way you chose those colors."** and hear the other person's answer.
- Introduce this story's small surprise; practice "You can just smile. That's okay." and allow a comfortable breath or pause.
- On a real outing, stay nearby, offer choices, and praise the specific step your child tried.
