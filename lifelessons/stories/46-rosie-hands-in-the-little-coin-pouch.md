# 46 — Rosie Hands In the Little Coin Pouch

**Life skill:** Turn found belongings over to an appropriate worker with grown-up support.
**Magic sentence:** "I found this. Where should I hand it in?"
**Coaches:** Feng 🇹🇼 + Don Café 🇨🇷
**Setting:** the apartment's umbrella drip tray corner (practice), then the railway station's covered concourse (real outing).

---

## The Story

Rosie wondered what to do if she found belongings that somebody had accidentally left behind.

At the umbrella corner, Feng displayed a practice pouch. Its little ribbon looked as though it expected applause.

Don Café tipped his hat. "A beautiful pouch can belong to someone else even when nobody is holding it."

Feng arrived, pineapple cake in fancy wrapping.

Don Café was a wise coffee bean.

Cat the penguin napped on a folded raincoat on the woven stool.

Feng pointed out the pouch to his pretend grown-up. **"I found this. Where should I hand it in?"** he asked.

"Finding is not owning," Don Café explained. "Stay with your grown-up and let a responsible worker help."

**Rosie's Steps**

1. Stop beside your grown-up.
2. Point out the found item.
3. Ask a worker where to hand it in.

4. Let your grown-up help.
5. Leave the item with the responsible worker.

**失物** (shīwù) = lost belongings. Rosie tried **"I found this. Where should I hand it in?"** Don Café pretended.

1. Stop beside your grown-up.
2. Point out the found item.
3. Ask a worker where to hand it in.

4. Let your grown-up help.
5. Leave the item with the responsible worker.

Rosie practiced pointing at the pouch, letting Mom handle it, and asking the pretend worker where it belonged.

"Excellent wrapping," Feng said. "Respectfully appreciated from this entirely appropriate distance."

Mom and Rosie reached the railway concourse, with picture maps, luggage benches, and a staffed counter.

Cat dozed beside Mom's bag on a luggage-bench cushion, uninterested in the departing trains.

A little coin pouch lay beside their bench. Rosie noticed its blue ribbon but did not open it.

Step one: she stopped beside Mom. Step two: Rosie pointed out the pouch lying near their bag.

Mom went with Rosie to the staffed counter. Step three: **"I found this. Where should I hand it in?"**

The station worker thanked them and asked Mom where they had found it, keeping the pouch closed.

The worker asked whether Rosie could describe the bench. There were several similar benches across the tiled concourse.

Rosie felt unsure. Her straw drooped. That's okay; helping did not mean inventing a detail she couldn't remember.

Rosie took a Rose breath.

Smell the rose: in, two, three, four.

Blow out the candle: out, two, three, four.

"Mom, please help me show which bench," Rosie said, staying with her beside the service counter.

Mom pointed out the nearby bench and its picture-map column. The worker noted the location for the owner.

Step four: Rosie let Mom help. They followed the station worker's instructions for handing it in.

Step five: the pouch stayed with the worker. Rosie and Mom neither searched it nor kept it.

Don Café nodded. Feng admired its ribbon once more. "Respectfully appreciated. My wrapper and I are satisfied."

Cat blinked beside the luggage bag. "If you find my nap, please return it directly to this cushion."

Rosie giggled. Somewhere, the pouch's owner could ask the station for help, and their little belongings would be waiting.

Tori met Rosie at the model-train display, where Mom explained their careful handover.

"You helped something find its way back. You've got this, little sip," she said, smiling at Rosie.

Rosie felt proud of caring for something that had never belonged to her.

**加油** (jiāyóu) = go for it.

"One step at a time, one sip at a time," said Rosie.

## 🌹 Rosie's Steps (Recap)

1. Stop beside your grown-up.
2. Point out the found item.
3. Ask a worker where to hand it in.
4. Let your grown-up help.
5. Leave the item with the responsible worker.

**If something surprising happens:** Take a Rose breath or a comfortable pause, then say: "Mom, please help me show which bench."

## 💬 Practice Together (for grown-ups)

- Role-play the situation using a closed little coin pouch or a safe pretend substitute; read the five steps together.
- Swap roles so your child can see you model **"I found this. Where should I hand it in?"** and hear the other person's answer.
- Introduce this story's small surprise; practice "Mom, please help me show which bench." and allow a comfortable breath or pause.
- On a real outing, stay nearby, offer choices, and praise the specific step your child tried.
