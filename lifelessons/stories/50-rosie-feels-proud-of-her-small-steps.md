# 50 — Rosie Feels Proud of Her Small Steps

**Life skill:** Accept praise and recognize personal effort without comparing achievements.
**Magic sentence:** "Thank you. I practiced, and I'm proud."
**Coaches:** Dai Daruma 🇯🇵 + Lala 🇨🇷
**Cat’s role:** kung fu practitioner
**Setting:** the family keepsake chest alcove (practice), then the neighborhood small-steps celebration gallery (real outing).

---

## The Story

Rosie brought a picture to a small-steps celebration. It showed a question she had learned to ask.

Dai rocked proudly beside the keepsake chest's ribbon. Lala admired a drawing of a carefully closed backpack.

"Is my little step enough?" Rosie asked. "A small step can take real effort," Dai said gently.

Dai was a determined red daruma bun.

Lala was a soft cake wearing cream.

Cat the penguin practiced her gentle kung fu bow. This time, her scarf stayed neatly out of her beak.

Lala praised Dai's practice. He listened and replied, **"Thank you. I practiced, and I'm proud."** Then named his effort.

"You can enjoy a kind comment," Lala explained. "You do not have to make your achievement smaller to be polite."

**Rosie's Steps**

1. Listen to the kind comment.
2. Say thank you.
3. Name something you worked on.

4. Say how your effort feels.
5. Ask about someone else's effort too.

Dai inspected the ribbon. "My achievement is staying upright. The ribbon's achievement is staying out of my eyebrows."

Mom took Rosie to the gallery, where bright rosettes surrounded picture frames and little keepsake tables.

Cat hung her practice picture beside the frames, proud of learning balance without comparing it to Rosie's work.

Lulu admired Rosie's picture. "You practiced that question and used it for real," she said, smiling warmly.

Step one: Rosie listened to Lulu's kind comment. Step two: **"Thank you. I practiced, and I'm proud."**

Step three: she named her effort: asking her question out loud, even when she had first felt shy.

Step four: "I feel proud," Rosie said, holding her picture carefully in front of her cup.

Nearby, another picture showed a much larger project. Rosie wondered whether her own achievement was too small to celebrate.

Her straw drooped. She felt unsure. That's okay; somebody else's big project did not erase her own effort.

Rosie took a Rose breath.

Smell the rose: in, two, three, four.

Blow out the candle: out, two, three, four.

"My step matters to me," Rosie said, looking again at the question she had practiced and learned to use.

Mom nodded. Rosie could admire another person's work and still keep her own picture in the celebration.

Step five: Rosie asked Lulu about her effort, giving their different achievements room beside each other.

They listened to each other's little stories, leaving room for different efforts and different kinds of proud feelings.

Dai nodded. "Fall down seven, bounce up eight. Occasionally celebrate standing on a very nice mat."

Cat bowed beside her picture. "I practiced keeping steady, and I'm proud. My scarf finally learned the bow too."

Rosie put her picture beside Lulu's. Both belonged in the display.

Tori met Rosie beneath the ribbon arch, where their family admired her little keepsake picture.

"Fifty stories, and so many small brave steps. You've got this, little sip," Tori said with a warm smile.

Rosie felt proud of learning and asking. Tomorrow's steps would have room to happen gently.

**加油** (jiāyóu) = go for it.

"One step at a time, one sip at a time," said Rosie.

## 🌹 Rosie's Steps (Recap)

1. Listen to the kind comment.
2. Say thank you.
3. Name something you worked on.
4. Say how your effort feels.
5. Ask about someone else's effort too.

**If something surprising happens:** Take a Rose breath or a comfortable pause, then say: "My step matters to me."

## 💬 Practice Together (for grown-ups)

- Role-play the situation using a little keepsake picture or a safe pretend substitute; read the five steps together.
- Swap roles so your child can see you model **"Thank you. I practiced, and I'm proud."** and hear the other person's answer.
- Introduce this story's small surprise; practice "My step matters to me." and allow a comfortable breath or pause.
- On a real outing, stay nearby, offer choices, and praise the specific step your child tried.

## 🌙 Goodnight Blessing

Goodnight, little sip and all your small steps.
Goodnight, pictures, keeping warm memories.
Goodnight, Cat, proud of your steady practice.
May you rest knowing your effort matters.
