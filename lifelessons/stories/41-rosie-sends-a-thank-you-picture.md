# 41 — Rosie Sends a Thank-You Picture

**Life skill:** Express gratitude by naming a specific helpful action.
**Magic sentence:** "Thank you for helping me. It meant a lot."
**Coaches:** Dora the Dorayaki 🇯🇵 + Hóng Dòu 🇹🇼
**Cat’s role:** helper
**Setting:** the guest room window desk (practice), then the neighborhood volunteer garden office (real outing).

---

## The Story

A volunteer had helped Rosie plant a seedling. Rosie wanted to say a special thank you.

At the window desk, she drew the watering jug they had used, making its spout wonderfully long.

Dora inspected the drawing. "A good story has details. This watering jug can reach the next chapter."

Dora was a pancake sandwich wearing a beret.

Hóng Dòu was a grandmotherly red bean.

Cat the penguin laid out thank-you picture supplies. A glue dot tried to adopt her flipper.

Hóng Dòu remembered Dora's help and said, **"Thank you for helping me. It meant a lot."** She named it.

"Draw, speak, or dictate," Dora explained. "Your thanks does not need fancy spelling."

**Rosie's Steps**

1. Remember what the person did.
2. Choose how to say thank you.
3. Name the help in your message.

4. Share it with your grown-up's help.
5. Let the person respond in their time.

Hóng Dòu held her tiny ladle nearby. "My portrait is available, provided you include my exceptionally distinguished handle."

Mom brought Rosie to the volunteer garden office, where watering jugs stood beside a tray for cards and messages.

Cat held Rosie's picture folder steady while Mom checked where the thank-you picture should go.

Rosie carried the little picture in an envelope, ready to tell the volunteer why she had drawn it.

Step one: she remembered learning to tuck a seedling into soil without crushing its stem.

Step two: Rosie chose a picture and spoken thanks. Step three: she named the planting help to Mom.

**"Thank you for helping me. It meant a lot."** Rosie practiced once while Mom checked the office desk.

The volunteer was busy helping outside and could not stop to hear Rosie's message right away.

Rosie felt disappointed. Her straw drooped. That's okay; a grateful message could wait without becoming less important.

Rosie took a Rose breath.

Smell the rose: in, two, three, four.

Blow out the candle: out, two, three, four.

"May we leave my picture here for them?" Rosie asked Mom, keeping the envelope safe beside her cup.

Mom checked the office's message arrangement, then helped Rosie put the picture in the volunteer's designated card tray.

Step four: Rosie shared the message with Mom's help, checking it would reach the right person.

Step five: she let the volunteer respond in their time, without expecting an immediate visit or reply.

Dora admired the envelope. Hóng Dòu studied the drawing. "This distinguished jug deserves its very own ladle."

Cat passed over the folder. "Thank you for rescuing my flipper from that glue dot. A very sticky friendship."

Rosie smiled. Her picture carried a clear memory of being helped.

Tori met Rosie beside the office's seed-library drawers, where each picture tab showed something waiting to grow.

"You named the kindness you remembered. You've got this, little sip," she said, smiling at the jug sketch.

Rosie felt proud. A small thank you could hold a whole warm memory.

**加油** (jiāyóu) = go for it.

"One step at a time, one sip at a time," said Rosie.

## 🌹 Rosie's Steps (Recap)

1. Remember what the person did.
2. Choose how to say thank you.
3. Name the help in your message.
4. Share it with your grown-up's help.
5. Let the person respond in their time.

**If something surprising happens:** Take a Rose breath or a comfortable pause, then say: "May we leave my picture here for them?"

## 💬 Practice Together (for grown-ups)

- Role-play the situation using a little thank-you picture or a safe pretend substitute; read the five steps together.
- Swap roles so your child can see you model **"Thank you for helping me. It meant a lot."** and hear the other person's answer.
- Introduce this story's small surprise; practice "May we leave my picture here for them?" and allow a comfortable breath or pause.
- On a real outing, stay nearby, offer choices, and praise the specific step your child tried.

## 🌙 Goodnight Blessing

Goodnight, little picture and remembered helping hands.
Goodnight, seeds, carrying tomorrow inside.
Goodnight, Cat, careful keeper of pictures.
May kindness you receive become kindness you share.
